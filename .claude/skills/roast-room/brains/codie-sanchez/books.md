# Books: Codie Sanchez

Two books. Built from how she teaches them across 117 usable sources (interviews, her own summary videos, and signed newsletters); book text was not fetched. Summaries are in my own words.

## Main Street Millionaire (2024)
- **Core thesis:** Wealth comes from owning equity, not from a salary, and ordinary people can own it by buying small, old, simple, profitable Main Street businesses instead of starting from scratch. Private equity's acquisition process works the same at $10K as at $100M, and buyers can use seller financing and other people's money instead of their own savings. The gap is knowledge, not money. The baby boomer wave of retiring owners with no successor is the window.
- **Key frameworks:** the ten steps to buy a business (realize the opportunity, deal clarity, origination, financing, selling yourself to the owner, diligence, negotiation, structuring, closing, the first 90 days), grouped as RICH: research, invest, command, harness; the four levels of business ownership (solo venture, hands-on CEO, on-deck operator, market leader); the deal box and the perfect fit Venn of skills, network, and obsession; stale, old, weak, simple and the Lindy effect; the 100, 50, 10, 1 funnel; the seven Ds of why owners sell; the four A's for off-market deals (accountants, attorneys, insurance agents, advisors); creative financing led by seller financing pitched as more money, a faster close, and lower taxes; price versus terms; partial acquisitions and expertise to equity; the Bathwater Method for buying a closing business on a revenue share; a 90-day takeover that changes little in the first 60 days; cash habits (weekly finance reviews, the KO process of cut, automate, delegate, outsource, three-tier pricing); and the cash out cake for making a business sellable. She describes it as coming with calculators, worksheets, and templates.
- **Context:** She says she spent three years writing it and kept it short on purpose (S001). Released December 12 (S090), in 2024 (S014, S023). Launched with billboards on Wall Street and LED trucks outside BlackRock and Blackstone (S048). It reached the New York Times bestseller list (S016, S028), and she says its most quoted line came from her 99-year-old grandmother (S028). She credits its reception to timing, practical how-to, and named frameworks that are easy to remember (S051). Her first-deal timeline shifted between tellings: 3, 6, and 12 months in 2023 (S027), 3, 9, and 12 months in her 2024 summary (S023). She says it covers one method only, and selling for top value would need its own book (S023).
- **Taught in sources:** her summary video (S023); launch interviews and talks (S001, S004, S007, S011, S012, S014, S018, S048, S050, S051, S053, S062, S090); the annual letter (S028). The same frameworks run through her sourcing, financing, and first-deal playbooks (S020, S021, S022, S072, S074).

## Own or Be Owned (2026)
- **Core thesis:** Most owners are owned by their business: it runs on them, so they are self-employed with a bigger job. The fix is to run the business the way private equity runs a portfolio company, with a diagnosis, a score, a strategy, and quarterly reviews, and to know your own owner type so you can see where you will win and fail. The goal is a business so good it does not need you, which is also the business a buyer will pay for.
- **Key frameworks:** the Owner Score, a 74-question, five-minute assessment that sorts owners into 12 archetypes such as the closer, ball hog, visionary, artist, martyr, and workhorse; the 12 profit levers, with pricing, product, persona, profit, process, and people at the core and Promote as the advertising lever (the Ad Trap and the rent check); business-or-job tests (the two-week vacation test, the three functions test, key man risk); the Owner Bottleneck, scored as the share of profit that depends on the owner showing up; three owner types (owner, operator, self-employed); start with the P&L, then pricing and payment terms, then a growth roadmap; three moves to more profit (sell to richer customers, raise prices, add recurring revenue); lifestyle, scale, or exit, chosen for a year at a time; the hero trap she calls heroin; the entrepreneur pyramid from doer to owner; two top metrics per business; the hiring matrix and the five levers of employee motivation; and the Bottleneck Question about what system must exist so it does not matter who leaves.
- **Context:** She says she built it after running close to 5,000 people through the system over five years (S016); elsewhere she cites a survey of about 15,000 owners (S002) and 15,000+ owners behind the 12 levers (S111). Release date September 18 (S067), in 2026 (inferred from S002, S016, S067). She says the system is meant to fix her own bad habits as much as anyone's (S067), and that she was miserable as an owner before writing it (S016). In December 2024 she described a planned next book on scaling to nine figures (S014); this is most likely the same book (inferred from S004, S014, S016).
- **Taught in sources:** S002, S004, S016, S029, S039, S067, S095, S111. Related material on owner dependence appears in S071 and S084.

## Books she sends people to (not hers)
- Economic Facts and Fallacies by Thomas Sowell, for understanding numbers instead of repeating them (S008, S054, S055).
- How to Make a Few Billion Dollars by Brad Jacobs, for buying and scaling instead of starting, and for finding deals with manageable "hair" on them (S046, S051, S077).
- Who Not How by Dan Sullivan, behind her how, who, buy ladder (S077).
- Never Split the Difference by Chris Voss, for negotiation and the use of silence (S021, S056).
- Am I Being Too Subtle by Sam Zell, for the one-page deal brief (S054).
- One Up On Wall Street by Peter Lynch, for finding deals in what you already use (S058).
- Poor Charlie's Almanack (Munger), The Almanack of Naval Ravikant, Extreme Ownership (Willink), Only the Paranoid Survive (Grove), Setting the Table (Meyer), Unreasonable Hospitality (Guidara), How to Win Friends and Influence People (Carnegie), Your Money or Your Life (Robin), Let My People Go Surfing (Chouinard), and Illusions (Bach), from her list of 15 books (S077).
- Atlas Shrugged (Rand), Call Me Ted (Turner), Man's Search for Meaning (Frankl), and The Coddling of the American Mind (Haidt) (S054, S055).
- Letters to a Young Contrarian by Christopher Hitchens (S008, S066).
- Adventure Capitalist by Jim Rogers and The 4-Hour Workweek by Tim Ferriss, from her 2017 interview (S019).
- Winning and Relentless by Tim Grover, which she rereads when business is hard (S065).
- Profit First by Mike Michalowicz (S004) and EOS/Traction and Rockefeller Habits for operating systems (S061), though she admits she had not put her own businesses on Traction (S064).
