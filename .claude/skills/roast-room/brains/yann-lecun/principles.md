# Principles: Yann LeCun

Built from 92 usable talks, interviews, lectures, hearings, and essays (S001 to S099, six excluded as listed in `sources.md`; dated ones run 2017 to 2026, many keynotes undated). 30 decision rules, most-used first, weighted toward AI technology and openness decisions. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources across all extraction notes that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Do not build an agent that cannot predict the consequences of its actions.** "basing agentic systems on LLMs is a recipe for disaster because how can a system possibly plan a sequence of actions if it can't predict the consequences of its actions" (S008) "I cannot imagine that we can build agentic systems without those systems having an ability to predict in advance what the consequences of their actions are going to be." (S058) Sources: 15 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S008, S009, S020, S033, S040, S047, S048, S058, S060, S097). Confidence: high.

2. **Scale is necessary but not enough; bigger LLMs will not close the gap.** "scaling is necessary, but not sufficient, and we're missing some basic ingredients to get to human level AI" (S063) "We're never going to get to human-level intelligence by just training on text." (S051) Sources: 20 (S001, S008, S009, S014, S015, S016, S020, S029, S035, S037, S038, S051, S054, S056, S057, S061, S062, S063, S075, S087). Confidence: high.

3. **If it is research, publish it; secrecy makes you fall behind.** "in my opinion you can't really call it research unless you publish what you do because otherwise you can get easily fooled by yourself" (S045) "when you do research in secret you fall behind that's inevitable" (S086) Sources: 22 (S008, S009, S012, S014, S016, S021, S025, S045, S047, S053, S063, S064, S066, S067, S080, S086, S090, S094, S040, S039, S010, S005). Confidence: high.

4. **Judge the technology by what it still cannot do, not by its best demo.** "But where is my domestic robot? Where is my level five self driving car? Where is a robot that can do what a cat do?" (S012) Sources: 14 (S001, S007, S012, S019, S021, S022, S032, S040, S041, S047, S062, S065, S067, S087). Confidence: high.

5. **Do not mistake fluency for intelligence.** "The biggest difficulty is not to get fooled into thinking that uh a a computer system is intelligent simply because it can manipulate language." (S007) "A system trained on language alone will never approximate human intelligence, even if trained from now until the heat death of the universe." (S035) Sources: 10 (S007, S012, S014, S020, S023, S033, S035, S037, S087, S065). Confidence: high.

6. **Use LLMs for what language carries (text, code, math, retrieval), and not for the physical world.** "LLMs are really good at manipulating language, but basically nothing else." (S003) "it turns out that the methods that have been so successful in the context of language, namely LNNs, actually do not work uh very well for for for the real world." (S048) Sources: 12 (S001, S002, S003, S006, S007, S012, S030, S031, S040, S048, S060, S068). Confidence: high.

7. **Bet on open platforms; proprietary ones lose over time.** "for foundational technology, for infrastructure type technology, open source always wins." (S063) "open source models are overtaking uh slowly but surely uh proprietary models" (S086) Sources: 21 (S001, S008, S010, S013, S019, S021, S032, S039, S047, S056, S057, S061, S062, S063, S064, S066, S067, S085, S086, S087, S038). Confidence: high.

8. **Do not train your own foundation model as a startup; build on open engines and own a vertical.** "trick number one use open source engines because you're not going to Train Your Own Foundation model" (S055) "take an open-source platform... and then fine-tune it for a particular vertical application and become an expert in that vertical application" (S019) He warns that raising a billion to pretrain burns it in about two years (S055). Sources: 7 (S012, S019, S032, S055, S056, S061, S017). Confidence: high.

9. **Concentration of AI in a few proprietary companies is the real risk, not AI takeover.** "I see the danger of this concentration of power through proprietary AI systems as a much bigger danger than everything else." (S020) "open source AI platforms are a necessity for the preservation of cultural diversity. democracy, local sovereignty" (S010) Sources: 13 (S002, S008, S010, S018, S020, S021, S031, S033, S041, S047, S061, S084, S086). Confidence: high.

10. **Put hard safety constraints in the objective, enforced at run time, not in fine-tuning.** "LLMs are intrinsically unsafe. Okay, that doesn't mean they're dangerous because they're not that smart." (S010) "You would be extremely stupid to build a system and not build any guardrails. That would be like building a car with a 1,000-horsepower engine and no brakes." (S038) Sources: 15 (S001, S008, S010, S017, S033, S038, S041, S045, S051, S086, S087, S088, S095, S099, S009). Confidence: high.

11. **Design the behavior through the objective, not by programming the behavior.** "you don't program the behavior you just design an objective function in such a way that when the system optimizes this objective function it will actually have the behavior that you expect" (S099) "AI might be dangerous if it sets its own goals, but if we set their goals, then they just work for us" (S062) Sources: 5 (S062, S088, S095, S099, S025). Confidence: high.

12. **Use reinforcement learning last, and sparingly; plan with a model instead.** "RL is like what you do when you're desperate and there is nothing else you can do." (S082) "if you are in kind of a reinforcement learning scenario without a model of the world you'll have to run off the cliff thousands of times before you figure out that it's not a good idea" (S099) Sources: 20 (S005, S009, S029, S031, S032, S034, S047, S052, S058, S070, S072, S076, S079, S081, S082, S084, S085, S089, S095, S099). Confidence: high.

13. **Do not generate what you do not need to predict.** "forget about generative models. I mean unless you want to generate text unless you want to generate video but if you are interested in really understanding the content do not generate or generate in representation space" (S032) "If you train the system by predicting pixels, you just don't get good representations, you don't get good predictions, you get blurry predictions most of the time." (S063) Sources: 17 (S009, S015, S029, S030, S031, S032, S040, S052, S058, S060, S063, S068, S081, S082, S084, S085, S099). Confidence: high.

14. **A plausible video or digital twin is not a world model.** "world models should not be simulators. Right? They should work in abstract space. They should not be digital twins, you know, that's a buzzword." (S082) Generating a plausible video is not the same as understanding the physical world (S081). Sources: 4 (S045, S053, S081, S082). Confidence: high.

15. **Assume capability timelines are longer than claimed, including his own.** "it's almost certainly much harder than we think because in the past 70 years of history of AI, it's always been much harder than we thought" (S009) "I don't see this happening in less than five or six years. Okay. But now, is it going to happen in five or six years? And I think the this is a distribution with a tail that's very long." (S049) Sources: 12 (S009, S011, S012, S015, S019, S026, S045, S049, S054, S081, S093, S065). Confidence: high.

16. **Do not believe anyone who claims the secret, and do not fund it.** "if you think that there is some startup somewhere with five people who has discovered the secret of a GI and you should invest five billion in them you're making a huge mistake" (S015) "if a start-up tells you they have the secret to you know human level intelligence and common sense don't believe them they don't" (S026) Sources: 5 (S013, S015, S016, S026, S045). Confidence: high.

17. **Progress is gradual, not one event; plan for steady increments.** "it's not going to be an event. We're not going to discover one secret. We're we're going to make continuous progress" (S007) "It's not going to be an event. It's it's it's going to be progressive." (S046) Sources: 9 (S007, S013, S020, S021, S031, S033, S037, S046, S062). Confidence: high.

18. **No one has a monopoly on good ideas; expect them from anywhere.** "no entity has a monopoly on good ideas which is why open collaboration makes the field progress faster." (S014) "the recent story uh about deepseek really shows that good ideas can come from anywhere." (S013) Sources: 6 (S010, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016). Confidence: high.

19. **Stay ahead by moving faster in the open, not by locking things down.** "progress faster that's the way you stay ahead" (S064) "And then it's my good AI against your bad AI." (S037) Sources: 5 (S037, S039, S040, S064, S086). Confidence: high.

20. **Regulate products, not research; do not legislate open source out of existence.** "the debate is about whether research and development should be regulated and there I'm I'm clearly very strongly of the opinion that it should should not" (S021) "don't legislate open source AI out of existence because of imaginary fears of dangers that don't exist" (S061) Sources: 11 (S018, S021, S022, S031, S039, S054, S057, S061, S062, S066, S010). Confidence: high.

21. **Deploy small, measure, and correct before you scale; when a product causes real harm, stop that product.** "you deploy a system and then you have to measure the effect and you want to deploy it in a small way so that you see the effect before if there are bad effects you can correct them" (S067) "if there is a real harm you you should you know stop that product from from being deployed uh now you know should it mean that you should ban all all of AI research no" (S022) Sources: 6 (S021, S022, S062, S067, S095, S063). Confidence: high.

22. **Design AI to amplify people, with the human as the boss.** "they're not going to replace us. they're going to amplify us." (S006) "We're all going to be a boss. We're all going to be like those high-level managers who will tell our AI systems what to do" (S019) Sources: 10 (S006, S007, S011, S017, S018, S019, S046, S053, S059, S062). Confidence: high.

23. **Hire the best, give them the means, give them a long leash, and get out of the way.** "get the best people together somehow so they can interact. Give them the means to do the research on what they think is the most promising and get out of the way." (S047) "if you really want Innovation you need a longer leash you need to let people uh take risks and innovate" (S016) Sources: 8 (S001, S013, S015, S016, S025, S047, S064, S067). Confidence: high.

24. **Do not play catch-up where incumbents hold the compute; work on the next thing.** "Don't work on LLMs. There is no point. You're not going to be able to rival what's going on in industry. Work on something else." (S040) "if you are in Academia don't work on LM because you're in competition with like hundreds of people with tens of thousands of gpus like there's nothing you can bring to the table do something else" (S086) He says this to researchers; the business reading is not to compete head-on with frontier labs at pretraining LLMs. Inferred from S010, S040, S055. Sources: 12 (S001, S009, S010, S011, S016, S029, S040, S047, S082, S084, S085, S086). Confidence: high.

25. **Do not abandon a method that works just because the theory is missing.** "it's not because we don't understand this from the theoretical point of view that it's not true empirically." (S093) "we have to um accept the fact that actually works" (S086) Sources: 9 (S072, S076, S077, S086, S088, S090, S092, S093, S097). Confidence: high.

26. **Get out of the trench everyone is digging, but follow evidence, not fashion.** "everybody is digging the same trench" (S001) "i think you have to be kind of a pragmatic scientist about it so there are two things right there is you know these beliefs first of all then there is empirical results" (S077) Sources: 7 (S001, S005, S010, S045, S077, S078, S090). Confidence: high.

27. **Build your own tools, then open them so others can reproduce your work.** "the tools you build for yourself as a scientist give you superpowers that's super important" (S078) "good results are not enough making them easy reproducible also makes them incredible" (S094) Sources: 6 (S054, S074, S078, S090, S093, S094). Confidence: high.

28. **Learn things with a long shelf life, and do not lock yourself into one bet on where technology goes.** "learn things that have a long shelf life because it's almost certain that you're going to technology is going to evolve really fast and you're going to have to change job" (S032) "don't make choices that sort of makes you make you prisoner of a hypothesis about where technology is going" (S056) Sources: 9 (S008, S009, S024, S032, S045, S051, S056, S074, S019). Confidence: high.

29. **Change your mind when the evidence demands it.** "I think the the essence of being a scientist is to be able to change your mind in the face of uh evidence" (S073) He reversed himself on pixel-level video prediction: "I completely changed my mind about this and I've become actually kind of philosophically opposed to this very idea." (S084) Sources: 5 (S024, S045, S073, S084, S099). Confidence: high.

30. **Pick a long-term ambitious goal and work back from it.** "pick a long-term very ambitious plan and work your way back and you're going to make progress. Useful things are going to come out of that" (S009) Sources: 1 (S009). Confidence: low.

## Updates, inconsistencies, and blind spots
- **LLM obsolescence, then and now.** Around 2022 to 2023: "within a few years perhaps as short as two years uh nobody in their right mind would use Auto regressive llms" (S088), and three to five years in another talk (S087). 2025: generative AI has about a three-year shelf life (S017). Newer (2026): "there's nothing wrong with LLMs in the sense of LLMs, you know, are the basis for a lot of uh very useful AI products that all of us use, including me." (S001). He now pictures LLMs as orchestrators inside larger systems (S040). The newer view is the one to use: useful products, wrong path to human-level AI.
- **His own world-model bet is early.** 2026: the narrator of S004 (not LeCun) reports JEPA planning still far behind VLA on simple robot tasks. AMI Labs had no public demo at scale (S040). Timeline for general world models: three to five years or more; narrow uses in one to two years (S006, S015). He says the plan "may fail" (S087) and has no plan B (S055).
- **Generative models, reversed.** He worked for years on generative and latent variable video prediction, GANs included (S072, S092, S097), then turned against it around 2021 and 2022 (S024, S073, S099). Rules 13 and 14 are the newer view.
- **He walked away once.** He stopped working on neural nets from about 1996 to 2001 because "it wasn't fun for me anymore" (S090). His rule of only working on what is fun (S074) is a personal taste, not founder advice.
- **Concedes to critics on structure.** In the Marcus debate he agreed that convolution is innate structure and that unsupervised learning may need more built in (S096), while still arguing for the minimum prior structure.
- **Productivity numbers vary.** He cites economists for gradual gains but the figures differ: about 0.6% a year (S007), about 6% of GDP (S046), about 7% (S032). Treat his macro numbers as loose. Inferred from S007, S032, S046.
- **Too slow on transfer.** He admits being too pessimistic about how fast research becomes product (S016, S032). Discount his skepticism on time-to-market for narrow, well-defined products.
- **Interpretability blind spot.** He calls interpretability not useful (S077, S090). That rule fits badly in medicine, lending, insurance, and law.
- **Bias against rivals.** He names Anthropic, Dario Amodei, and Gary Marcus with open hostility (S030, S071) and frames doom concerns as partly commercial lobbying (S001). He also says he is biased when praising FAIR's work (S064). Weigh his safety and regulation views as an interested party: an open-source advocate who ran research at a company that gave models away. Inferred from S001, S012, S030, S057.
- **Not an operator.** He stepped down from managing FAIR because he was not good at it (S001, S027), had little technical role in Llama (S051), and has no operational role at AMI Labs (S009, S040). His rules cover technology and openness, not pricing, sales, or cash.
