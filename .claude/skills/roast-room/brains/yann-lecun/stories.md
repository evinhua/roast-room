# Stories: Yann LeCun

18 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Built from the seven extraction batches over S001 to S034. Source ids in parentheses; most-used first.

1. **The 17-year-old driver.** A teenager learns to drive in about 20 hours, yet millions of hours of driving data have not produced a level 5 self-driving car. Lesson: learning fast from little data is the real test, and current AI is missing something basic (S001, S003, S007, S008, S022, S032).
2. **The four-year-old's data.** A child awake for 16,000 hours takes in about 10^14 bytes through vision, the same order as all the public text used to train the largest LLMs. Lesson: more text is not the bottleneck; the kind of data and the architecture are (S006, S011, S014, S015, S029, S031).
3. **Linux beat Sun and HP-UX.** In the 1990s proprietary Unix and Windows NT ran internet servers; today Linux runs nearly everything. He casts OpenAI and Anthropic as today's Sun and HP-UX. Lesson: platforms go open, and the open side wins in the end (S001, S019, S032).
4. **Galactica, then ChatGPT.** Meta's open science model drew a wave of attacks on Twitter in late 2022 and its small team pulled the demo. Three weeks later ChatGPT launched to wide praise. "we like we looked at each other and said like what what just happened?" (S013). Lesson: public reaction to AI is fickle and depends on who ships it (S010, S013, S023).
5. **Llama came from a pirate team of 12 in Paris.** A small FAIR Paris team built Llama 1 for its own research while the big official LLM effort was stopped. Lesson: small, insulated teams with a long leash produce the breakthroughs (S009, S013, S014, S025).
6. **Babies and impossible events.** Around nine or ten months, babies stare in surprise when an unsupported object does not fall. V-JEPA, trained only on video, shows the same spike in prediction error on impossible events. Lesson: world models can be learned from observation, and that is the evidence his approach works (S002, S008, S015, S025, S029, S032).
7. **Fifteen years of blurry video.** He spent about ten of fifteen years trying to predict video pixel by pixel and got only blurry frames, because the model averages all possible futures. Lesson: stop predicting every detail and predict in an abstract space instead, which led to JEPA (S009, S019, S031, S032).
8. **The ConvNet patents stranded at NCR.** When AT&T split, his convolutional net patents went to NCR, which did not understand them. He kept working while hoping NCR's lawyers would not notice, and celebrated when the patents expired in 2007. Lesson: locking up ideas slows everyone, including the inventor (S005, S016, S026, S027).
9. **CVPR rejects the segmentation paper.** Around 2011 his lab's convolutional net beat every other system for semantic segmentation, and reviewers rejected it anyway. By 2014 CVPR would not accept papers that did not use neural nets. Lesson: a community that does not know the paradigm will dismiss strong evidence, so take it to a friendlier venue and keep going (S005, S016).
10. **The pirate NIPS workshop and the CIFAR years.** Shut out of the main conferences, he, Hinton, and Bengio ran their own workshops and summer schools from 2004 to 2007. Lesson: when the mainstream is hostile, build your own forum until the results speak (S005, S016).
11. **Three conditions to join Facebook.** He told Zuckerberg he would stay in New York, keep his NYU job, and publish and open source everything. All three were accepted at once. Lesson: set the culture you need at the point of hire (S005, S017, S018).
12. **Arguing Meta into releasing Llama 2.** For months a group of about 40 people, including Zuckerberg, met weekly to debate release while legal and policy teams objected. He argued the risks were overblown and the upside was a whole industry. Lesson: open release can start an ecosystem that a closed one never would (S001, S012).
13. **The GenAI group under product pressure.** Meta pulled 60 to 70 researchers out of FAIR into a product-focused GenAI group in 2023. Cut off from long-term research, it did conservative work and lost key people, some of whom founded Mistral. Lesson: research needs a protected bridge into product, or the company misses the next wave (S001).
14. **FAIR Paris versus DeepMind Paris.** FAIR Paris published openly and hosted PhD students; it produced Llama 1 and alumni who started Mistral. DeepMind Paris kept secrets and could not host students, and seeded far less. Lesson: openness recruits and compounds; secrecy isolates (S009).
15. **The NYU MRI project.** An informal talk with NYU Radiology led to a FAIR collaboration to speed up MRI scans. It was published and open sourced and ended up in nearly every MRI machine sold. Lesson: open research plus a real-world partner can turn a side project into global infrastructure (S006).
16. **The paper that falls the wrong way.** He asked ChatGPT what happens when you hold a sheet of paper flat with two hands and let go with one. It answered fluently and got the physics exactly wrong. Lesson: text-trained systems give confident, wrong answers about the physical world (S015, S023).
17. **The car wash question.** Asked whether to walk to a car wash 100 meters away, most LLMs said walk, missing that the car has to go too. Lesson: fluent models still lack basic common sense, and a correct answer may only mean the question was in the training data (S030).
18. **Hate speech removal went from about a quarter to 95 percent.** Self-supervised transformers raised Facebook's automatic hate speech removal from roughly 23 percent in 2017 to 95 percent by 2022. Lesson: AI is often the fix for a social harm, not the cause (S018, S033).
