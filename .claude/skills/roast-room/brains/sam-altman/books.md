# Books: Sam Altman

Built from 51 sources (his essays from 2013 to 2025 and talks and interviews from 2014 to 2026). He has written no books. His thinking lives in essays, one long online guide, and a lecture series, listed below. Summaries are in my own words.

## Startup Playbook (2015)
- **Core thesis:** A free online guide to starting a company: idea, product, team, and execution, with making something users love as the step no one can skip.
- **Key frameworks:** a small group who love the product beats a large group who like it; founders do sales and support themselves; keep burn low and hire for aptitude and belief in the mission; founders set the culture early.
- **Taught in sources:** S001.

## How to Start a Startup, Stanford lecture series (2014)
- **Core thesis:** His opening lecture of the course he ran: you need a great idea, product, team, and execution, and the idea and product come first.
- **Key frameworks:** explain the company in one sentence; the best ideas look bad at first; answer "why now"; plan for an eventual monopoly in a large market.
- **Taught in sources:** S027 (lecture, shared with a second speaker); the same themes in S013, S014, S028.

## Startup advice essays (2013 to 2015)
- **Core thesis:** Short lists of rules for founders: growth fixes most problems, momentum matters, speed is your edge over big companies, and you should only start something you would work on for ten years.
- **Key frameworks:** growth rate as the obsession; decide in an hour; painkillers over vitamins; keep salaries low and equity high; be suspicious of complexity; start with a project, not a company.
- **Taught in sources:** Startup Advice (S013), Startup advice, briefly (S014), Super successful companies (S017), Advice for ambitious 19 year olds (S019), Startups, Role Models, Risk, and Y Combinator (S020), Valuations (S022).

## How to Hire (2013)
- **Core thesis:** Companies are the team the founders build, so hiring deserves a third to a half of a founder's time after product-market fit.
- **Key frameworks:** paid work trials over interviews; hire from personal networks first; learn a role before you hire for it; never compromise early; fire fast.
- **Taught in sources:** S018; echoed in S011, S026.

## How To Be Successful (2019)
- **Core thesis:** Habits for an exponential career: compound yourself, believe in yourself almost to delusion, own equity, take risks, focus, and work hard.
- **Key frameworks:** compound yourself; small bets with 1x downside and 100x upside; long-term thinking as the edge; be internally driven; get good at sales; own things that rise in value.
- **Taught in sources:** S002; related essays The days are long but the decades are short (S004) and Productivity (S016).

## Hard Startups (2020) and Idea Generation (2020)
- **Core thesis:** Hard, important startups attract talent and help that easy ones do not; good ideas come from watching big shifts and from ideas most people think are bad.
- **Key frameworks:** a hard startup is a tailwind; a 10-year horizon beats a 3-year one; scan for what is newly possible this year; the bad-seeming but good idea; founder and company fit.
- **Taught in sources:** S003, S012.

## How To Invest In Startups (2020)
- **Core thesis:** Startup returns follow a power law, so only back companies that could be enormous and founders who are forces of nature.
- **Key frameworks:** the $10 billion filter; rate of improvement over current ability; scale what is working; decide quickly; help founders be more ambitious.
- **Taught in sources:** S015; the power law applied to research in S038.

## What I Wish Someone Had Told Me (2023)
- **Core thesis:** Lessons from running OpenAI in one page: optimism, concentration, compounding, and speed.
- **Key frameworks:** plans in decades, execution in weeks; concentrate on few high-conviction bets; compounding advantage with scale; inaction is risk; incentives are superpowers; outcomes over process.
- **Taught in sources:** S011.

## AI essays (2021 to 2025): Moore's Law for Everything, The Intelligence Age, Reflections, Three Observations, The Gentle Singularity, Abundant Intelligence
- **Core thesis:** Deep learning gets predictably better with scale, the cost of intelligence keeps falling fast, and abundant intelligence and energy will make most goods far cheaper. Policy should share ownership of that value widely.
- **Key frameworks:** Moore's Law for everything; taxing capital rather than labor and an American Equity Fund (2021); intelligence rises with the log of compute while cost falls about 10x a year; progress is one smooth exponential curve; iterative deployment; compute as the binding constraint.
- **Taught in sources:** S005 (2021), S006 (2024), S008 (2025), S007 (2025), S009 (2025), S010 (2025). Note that his policy essays sit outside a founder's usual decisions; use them for his view of where costs and markets are heading.

## Books he cites
- **The Beginning of Infinity (David Deutsch):** he says he is rereading it in light of AI, for its idea that progress means understanding more of the world (S038). Not his book.
