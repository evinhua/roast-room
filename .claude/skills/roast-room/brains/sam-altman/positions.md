# Positions: Sam Altman

Built from six batches of verified notes over 51 sources (essays 2013 to 2025, talks and interviews 2014 to 2026). 40 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (essay month, or upload month for video; see `sources.md`). Where he changed his mind, both views sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Ambition and hard startups
- **Stance:** Pick the hard, important thing. Hard startups get a tailwind of talent and support that easy ones never get.
- "An easy startup is a headwind; a hard startup is a tailwind." (S003, 2020-02)
- "It is easier for a team to do a hard thing that really matters than to do an easy thing that doesn't really matter; audacious ideas motivate people." (S011, 2023-12)
- 2026-07: "Do something that matters. Like do something that is important and if you don't do it, if your company doesn't succeed, might not happen." (S047)
- **Read:** consistent from 2013 to 2026, and the core of this seat. Inferred from S003, S011, S028, S047.

## Choosing an idea
- **Stance:** The best ideas sound bad to most people and are big if they work. Never take your idea from a stage talk.
- "A good test for an idea is if you can articulate why most people think it's a bad idea, but you understand what makes it good." (S012, 2020-05)
- "I love the unpopular ideas. I I I have always liked ideas that are big if they work, difficult to get to work, and unpopular." (S050, 2026-03)
- Caveat he adds: "what you really want is to be right being contrarian and wrong still is wrong." (S029, 2024-05)
- And a check on lone conviction: "if you can't find anybody else that shares your belief, you should pay attention to that." (S031, 2026-07)

## Market size and growth
- **Stance:** Care about how fast the market grows and how big it gets, not today's size. As an investor, only back things that could be huge.
- "What you really want to do is identify a market that's going to grow every year and be able to ride that up elevator." (S028, 2018-08)
- "You should try to limit yourself to opportunities that could be $10 billion companies if they work." (S015, 2020-01)
- 2015-02: prefers a fast-growing market to a large slow one, especially one others dismiss (S014).

## Timing and why now
- **Stance:** Startups win when the ground shifts fastest. He calls the current AI shift the best time ever to start.
- "Any time you can think of something that is possible this year and wasn't possible last year, you should pay attention." (S012, 2020-05)
- "when the clock cycle of the industry changes this much, startups almost always win." (S026, 2025-06)
- "This is the best fucking time ever in the history of technology, ever, period, to start a company." (S026, 2025-06)
- 2023-01: downturns are good for builders because tourists leave (S044).

## Building on AI models
- **Stance:** Build something that gets better when the next model ships. Do not build a patch for a gap the next model will close.
- "you can either build a business that bets against the next model being really good or a model that bets on that happening and benefits from it happening." (S042, 2024-05)
- "bet that intelligence as a service gets better and cheaper every year and it is necessary but not sufficient for you to win." (S042, 2024-05)
- 2025-12: "Bolting AI onto the existing way of doing things, I don't think it's going to work well as redesigning stuff in this sort of like AI-first world." (S040)

## Defensibility and moats
- **Stance:** Every great business needs a plan for long-term advantage, but the moat can come after launch. Compounding scale advantages are the goal.
- "Compounding exponentials are magic. In particular, you really want to build a business that gets a compounding advantage with scale." (S011, 2023-12)
- 2023-01, for AI startups: "build like deep relationships with customers a product they love and some sort of moat that doesn't have to be technology." (S045)
- 2025-06, on OpenAI itself: "We didn't have any defensibility strategy. We just, like, had the only good thing out there." (S026)
- 2025-12: "personalization is extremely sticky. People love the fact that the model gets to know them over time" (S040)

## Competition and copying
- **Stance:** Do not copy competitors or build what everyone else is building. Compete with yourself.
- "If you compete with other people, you end up in this mimetic trap, and you sort of play this tournament, and if you win, you lose." (S025, 2019-02)
- "you're always like going to where your competitor was and you don't build up a culture of learning what it's like to innovate." (S037, 2025-06)
- "if you're starting the same startup as everybody else, it's like you get a lot of hype and you can raise a lot of money" (S031, 2026-07), but those are rarely the big outcomes.
- He still respects threats: "If Google had really decided to take us seriously in 2023, let's we would have been in a really bad place." (S040, 2025-12)

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Concentrate on a few high-conviction bets and kill good projects to feed the best one. He admits he is bad at this himself.
- "Concentrate your resources on a small number of high-conviction bets; this is easy to say but evidently hard to do. You can delete more stuff than you think." (S011, 2023-12)
- "sacrificing the good ideas to go after the great ideas is is kind of the hardest lesson for any entrepreneur or business to learn." (S038, 2026-08)
- Earlier exception, 2022-09: let about 10 percent of the company explore new directions (S043).
- **Read:** focus on one bet plus a small explore budget. Inferred from S011, S032, S038, S043.

## Conviction versus data
- **Stance:** Hold extreme conviction on one bet until the data arrives, then follow the data.
- "most of the world still does not understand the value of like a fairly extreme level of conviction on one bet." (S036, 2024-11)
- "conviction is great until the moment you have data one way or the other" (S036, 2024-11)

## Scaling what works
- **Stance:** When something is already working at small scale, push it further than anyone has tried.
- "when in doubt if you have something that seems like it's getting better with scale I think you should scale it up." (S036, 2024-11)
- "The central learning of my career so far has been that you can and should scale up things that are working." (S015, 2020-01)

## Growth and momentum
- **Stance:** Growth is the main job. Never lose momentum early.
- "Growth solves (nearly) all problems." (S013, 2013-06)
- "One of the most important jobs you have as a founder is to never lose momentum." (S028, 2018-08)
- Signal he trusts: "when something really starts growing and it's not very good, you have a guaranteed hit on your hands." (S030, 2026-06)

## Product and niche
- **Stance:** Start with a small group who love it, not a big group who like it. Word of mouth is the test.
- "It's much better to first make a product a small number of users love than a product that a large number of users like." (S001, 2015-11)
- "the degree to which you're successful approximates the degree to which you build a product that is so good people spontaneously tell their friends about it." (S028, 2018-08)
- 2026-09: "you got to like make something people want." (S048)

## Shipping early
- **Stance:** Ship before you are comfortable and learn from real users. He applies this to safety too.
- "it does kind of suck to ship a product that you're embarrassed about but it's much better than the alternative." (S029, 2024-05)
- "put things out into the world, get feedback from real customers, see where they break, see where they don't break. That is the way you make a good product." (S038, 2026-08)

## Raising money
- **Stance:** Raising is not the hard part. Raising too much early is a known bad sign. Take market terms and get back to work.
- 2014-06: resist raising and spending too much; companies that raise very large first rounds have a bad track record (S022).
- 2019-05: "I would not have YC companies raise the amount of money they raise or the valuations they do" (S046)
- For capital-heavy missions he raises without limit (see Burn and spending).

## Valuation
- **Stance:** Do not chase above-market valuations. Early conviction on the right team matters more than price.
- 2014-06: just take market terms (S022).
- 2023-01: Stripe was his second investment ever and his best multiple, at a valuation that would look tiny today (S044).

## Burn and spending
- **Earlier (2013 to 2015):** keep burn low and stay frugal. Keeping personal burn low is a standing rule for founders (S004, S019), and frugal companies keep a long-term mindset (S022, 2014-06).
- **Newer (2024 to 2026), for compute-bound bets:** "whether we burn 500 million a year or 5 billion or 50 billion a year I don't care I genuinely don't" (S029, 2024-05). "I definitely badly undershot on the compute investments." (S032, 2026-07)
- **Read:** frugal by default; spend without limit only when scale itself is the advantage and the demand is visible. Inferred from S022, S029, S032, S040.

## Debt
- **Stance:** On a startup's own debt: no recorded position. On AI infrastructure, he called debt reasonable because data centers are real assets; his worry is model progress stalling, not the debt (S040, 2025-12).

## Pricing and margins
- **Stance:** Expects margins to fall and is fine with it: more money at lower margin.
- "I think that most companies like OpenAI will make more money at a lower margin." (S024, 2025-11)
- On specific pricing tactics or discounting: no recorded position.

## Advertising and monetization
- **Stance:** Dislikes ads, and holds that paid placement must never change the answer.
- "I kind of hate ads just as an aesthetic choice" (S023, 2024-03)
- "if ChatGPT were accepting payment to put a worse hotel above a better hotel, that's probably catastrophic for your relationship with ChatGPT." (S024, 2025-11)
- He thinks the real money is elsewhere: "the way to monetize the world's smartest model is certainly not hotel booking." (S024, 2025-11)

## Paid acquisition and marketing
- **Stance:** Prefers organic growth. Paid ads: no recorded position. On marketing a new technology, sell the outcome, not the technology.
- "what you are getting from us is not electricity. It's light at night." (S030, 2026-06)

## Content and personal brand
- No recorded position on content marketing. He values founders who can evangelize: at least one founder must recruit, sell, talk to press, and raise money (S028, 2018-08).

## First hires and hiring
- **Stance:** Hiring decides the company. Hire from your network, audition candidates on real work, hire for slope.
- "If you don't hire very well, you will not be successful -- companies are a product of the team the founders build." (S018, 2013-09)
- "Spend more time recruiting. Take risks on high-potential people with a fast rate of improvement." (S011, 2023-12)
- "hire for slope, not Y intercept. And I think that's just like unbelievably great advice." (S026, 2025-06)
- 2014-01: only hire when there is no other way to do what you need (S017).

## Firing
- **Stance:** Fire fast.
- "I have never met a newbie founder that fires fast enough." (S018, 2013-09)
- 2013-06: hire slow, fire fast (S013).

## Senior executives
- **Stance:** Promote from inside. Do not start with an eminent senior administrator.
- "I think most of the time executives at a company should be promoted internally, not hired externally." (S032, 2026-07)
- 2025-06: he has not had success starting with a very senior administrator (S026).

## Equity and compensation
- **Stance:** Own equity, not time. Pay early staff a bit below market with generous equity.
- "You get truly rich by owning things that increase rapidly in value." (S002, 2019-01)
- 2013-09: slightly below market salary, very generous equity (S018).
- Personal exception, 2026-07: he holds no OpenAI equity: "I have a front row seat to the most exciting moment of human history. And like that is worth more to me than any amount of money." (S047)

## Co-founders and founder fit
- **Stance:** Founder fit with the company matters as much as product fit with the market. Back people over ideas.
- "I've come to believe that founder/company fit is as important as product/market fit." (S012, 2020-05)
- "The rate of improvement is often more important than the current absolute ability." (S015, 2020-01)

## Remote work
- **Stance:** Against remote early on; expects the most important companies to be in person.
- 2013-09: do not hire remote employees while early culture is gelling (S018).
- 2023-01: skeptical of fully remote; the most important companies of the decade will be heavily in person (S045).
- 2019-02: also against coworking for startups: "The single thing that has differentiated YC more than any other decision we've made is that we do not have a coworking space." (S025)

## Work hours
- **Stance:** Extreme effort for a period of life is required, and it is fine to choose not to.
- "Work really hard. Everyone wants a secret to success other than this; if it exists, I haven't found it yet." (S014, 2015-02)
- "I think people who pretend you can be super successful professionally without working most of the time (for some period of your life) are doing a disservice." (S002, 2019-01)
- But hours are second to direction: "It is much more important to work on the right thing than it is to work many hours." (S002, 2019-01)

## Burnout and hard times
- **Stance:** Resilience is learnable. He reframes pain rather than avoiding it, and admits fatigue.
- "I just decided that I was going to like learn to love the hard parts" (S035, 2025-07)
- "the opposite of a bad experience is no experience." (S050, 2026-03)
- 2026-07: "I'm tired. I don't know if so I've been doing this a long time. It's tiring." (S047)

## Culture
- **Stance:** Founders set culture early and it is hard to change. Values are articles of faith. Fight bureaucracy.
- 2013-09: diversity of values inside a startup is bad (S018).
- 2025-06: "the special thing about OpenAI is we've managed to build a culture that is good at repeatable innovation." (S037)

## Metrics and boards
- **Stance:** Know your growth numbers cold. A board helps by forcing monthly reporting.
- "It turns out to be really good for a company to have a board -- it focuses the company if everyone knows they have to present the key metrics to outsiders once a month." (S022, 2014-06)
- 2024-03: hire board members as slates, and weigh track record more for directors: "For a board member, I do care much more about the Y-intercept." (S023)

## Corporate structure
- **Stance (newer):** Do not innovate on legal structure. He calls OpenAI's nonprofit setup a mistake.
- "I would heavily discourage any startup that was thinking about starting as a nonprofit and adding a for-profit arm later." (S023, 2024-03)
- 2026-07: "we made a mistake to try to innovate in our structure in the beginning" (S047)

## Vertical integration
- **Earlier (undated; in 2025-10 he says he was "always" against it):** against it.
- **Newer (2025-10):** "I was always against vertical integration and I now think I was just wrong about that." (S039) For AGI he now wants to own chips, data centers, research, and product together.

## Platform versus product
- **Stance:** Respects platform plus killer app, but wants OpenAI to leave most categories to others.
- "A business strategy I've always really respected is like the platform plus killer app together." (S045, 2023-01)
- 2026-08: "I don't think we should go build every product category." (S038)

## Selling the company and IPO
- On selling a startup: no recorded position.
- IPO, 2025-12: "am I excited to be a public company CEO? 0%." (S040) 2026-09: "I would say not 2026." (S051)

## Speed of AI adoption
- **Earlier (2021 to 2023):** expected fast change; in 2021 he wrote that within five years programs would read legal documents and give medical advice (S005).
- **Newer (2026):** "Society and the economy will adapt more slowly." (S038, 2026-08) He calls his education forecast "a prediction error for me." (S030, 2026-06)
- **Read:** the technology moved as fast as he said; society moved slower. Weight the newer view when judging go-to-market timing.

## Jobs and AI
- **Stance:** Not a jobs doomer, but honest that some jobs vanish.
- "I'm not a jobs doomer at all" (S050, 2026-03)
- "I think it's important to be honest about that there will be some jobs that totally go away" (S035, 2025-07)

## Regulation and safety
- **Stance:** Light touch on current models, oversight for catastrophic-risk models. By 2026 he sets a hard safety line above business interest.
- 2024-05: "it' be a mistake to go regulate heavily regulate like current capability models" (S042)
- 2026-09: "I do not think we should train models where we cannot make a safety case for why we will be able to make strong statements about their controllability and alignment." (S051)

## Risk
- **Stance:** Most people overrate risk and underrate the risk of doing nothing.
- "Look for small bets you can make where you lose 1x if you're wrong but make 100x if it works." (S002, 2019-01)
- "Inaction is a particularly insidious type of risk." (S011, 2023-12)
- "The most expensive investing mistake in the world to make is to be a pessimist." (S025, 2019-02)

## Side projects
- **Stance:** Start as a project, not a company. Low stakes let you entertain big, odd ideas. Once in company mode, decide in an hour (S014, 2015-02).
