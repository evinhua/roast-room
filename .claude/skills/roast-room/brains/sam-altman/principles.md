# Principles: Sam Altman

Built from 133 usable sources (his blog essays from 2013 to 2025, many undated in the source table, plus talks, lectures, podcasts, and interviews from 2014 to 2026). 30 decision rules, roughly most-supported first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Build something a small number of people love before trying to scale.** "The startup graveyard is littered with people who thought they could skip this step." (S001) "you have to eventually build something so good that people will recommend it to their friends" (S057) Sources: 12 (S001, S013, S017, S020, S028, S048, S057, S062, S067, S130, S131, S133). Confidence: high.

2. **Ship early, even when you are embarrassed by it; let people use it instead of arguing.** "it does kind of suck to ship a product that you're embarrassed about but it's much better than the alternative." (S029) "talking about it doesn't seem to break through, but if people can use it and feel it" then they do (S115). Sources: 9 (S017, S029, S033, S038, S042, S100, S115, S117, S122). Confidence: high.

3. **Concentrate on a few high-conviction bets and delete the rest.** "Concentrate your resources on a small number of high-conviction bets; this is easy to say but evidently hard to do. You can delete more stuff than you think." (S011) Sources: 9 (S011, S036, S056, S083, S109, S119, S123, S126, S132). Confidence: high.

4. **Do not copy what everyone else is building, and do not take an idea from a stage.** "don't shoot for something that somebody else has already done. Shoot for something that no one has done yet." (S025) "the very best companies, the very best investment opportunities are almost never the ones that look really popular." (S047) Copying a competitor only takes you where they were (S037). Sources: 9 (S025, S026, S029, S031, S037, S047, S050, S079, S133). Confidence: high.

5. **Pick the hard problem that matters over the easy one that does not.** "It is easier for a team to do a hard thing that really matters than to do an easy thing that doesn't really matter; audacious ideas motivate people." (S011) "Do something that matters." (S047) Sources: 8 (S003, S011, S028, S047, S050, S063, S091, S122). Confidence: high.

6. **Build a business that gets better when the model gets better, and still build a real business.** "I encourage people to try to build things where you are so hoping the model gets wildly better." (S110) "bet that intelligence as a service gets better and cheaper every year and it is necessary but not sufficient for you to win." (S042) Sources: 11 (S036, S042, S045, S050, S109, S110, S114, S123, S125, S127, S134). Confidence: high.

7. **Be contrarian only when you are also right, and be willing to be misunderstood for years.** "what you really want is to be right being contrarian and wrong still is wrong." (S029) "You should trade being short-term low-status for being long-term high-status, which most people seem unwilling to do." (S060) Being dismissed kept rivals away from OpenAI for years (S031). Sources: 9 (S012, S015, S029, S031, S060, S079, S112, S122, S133). Confidence: high.

8. **Never lose momentum; growth solves nearly all problems.** "Growth solves (nearly) all problems." (S013) "one of my top few startup commandments is never let the company lose momentum" (S066) When it is lost, win small first and save the vision speeches (S132). Sources: 8 (S013, S014, S028, S066, S088, S131, S132, S134). Confidence: high.

9. **Count inaction as a risk; most things are less risky than they look.** "Inspiration is perishable and life goes by fast. Inaction is a particularly insidious type of risk." (S011) "Don't worry so much. Things in life are rarely as risky as they seem." (S004) Sources: 8 (S002, S004, S011, S019, S100, S121, S123, S129). Confidence: high.

10. **Only take high-risk bets that pay off hugely if they work.** "high risk bets are okay as long as you take the ones where if they work, it's super valuable." (S038) Sources: 7 (S015, S026, S038, S059, S080, S123, S126). Confidence: high.

11. **Rebuild AI-first, use the tools yourself, and adopt fast.** "The number one piece of tactical advice is just use the tools." (S104) "I think it's very important that companies adopt AI in a big way very quickly." (S110) A slow plan to deploy AI in two years he calls "a catastrophic mistake" (S113). Sources: 9 (S032, S040, S050, S104, S109, S110, S112, S113, S125). Confidence: high.

12. **Plan in decades, execute in weeks, and only start what you would do for ten years.** "Plans should be measured in decades, execution should be measured in weeks." (S011) Sources: 7 (S003, S011, S013, S014, S032, S068, S134). Confidence: high.

13. **Hold conviction until the data arrives, then follow the data.** "conviction is great until the moment you have data one way or the other." (S036) He admits he did not update enough on AlexNet in 2012 (S050), and says the lab should pivot to what the science allows, not the roadmap (S127). Sources: 6 (S026, S036, S050, S068, S114, S127). Confidence: high.

14. **Scale what is working, especially if it gets better with scale.** "The central learning of my career so far has been that you can and should scale up things that are working." (S015) Sources: 7 (S011, S015, S030, S036, S100, S121, S129). Confidence: high.

15. **Startups win when costs fall fast and cycle times shrink; move then.** "when the clock cycle of the industry changes this much, startups almost always win." (S026) Sources: 7 (S026, S032, S036, S043, S044, S050, S134). Confidence: high.

16. **Hire late and slow, never compromise early, and fire fast.** "I have never met a newbie founder that fires fast enough." (S018) "A single mediocre hire in the first five will often in fact kill a startup." (S132) Sources: 5 (S013, S017, S018, S131, S132). Confidence: high.

17. **Hire for slope, and spend real time recruiting.** "hire for slope, not Y intercept. And I think that's just like unbelievably great advice." (S026) (the phrase is one he credits to Paul Buchheit; the endorsement is his) "Always be recruiting and promoting talented people." (S056) Sources: 6 (S011, S015, S023, S026, S056, S084). Confidence: high.

18. **When something grows while still rough, drop everything else and serve it.** "when something really starts growing and it's not very good, you have a guaranteed hit on your hands." (S030) Sources: 4 (S030, S032, S038, S047). Confidence: high.

19. **Kill good projects to feed the great one.** "killing the good ideas, like sacrificing the good ideas to go after the great ideas is is kind of the hardest lesson for any entrepreneur or business to learn." (S038) Sources: 5 (S011, S032, S038, S040, S105). Confidence: high.

20. **Spend more time choosing what to work on than on how.** "far more important than any particular system is just figuring out the right things to work on" (S133) Pick the option with the highest rate of learning and impact (S050). Sources: 6 (S016, S050, S061, S126, S129, S133). Confidence: high.

21. **Prefer a fast-growing market to a large, slow one.** "What you really want to do is identify a market that's going to grow every year and be able to ride that up elevator." (S028) "the thing that I have seen be most predictable for a large Tam down the road is how much the people that are using it today used it and loved it" (S130) Sources: 4 (S014, S015, S028, S130). Confidence: high.

22. **Founders do the selling, and no one stands between them and users.** "All great careers, to some degree, become sales jobs." (S002) He says a company needs at least one evangelical founder who recruits, sells, and raises (S028). Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S017, S028, S132). Confidence: high.

23. **Keep burn low early; do not raise more than you need.** "Establish a culture of frugality." (S083) He warned that companies raising $30M or more in their first round do badly (S022) and later cited the same for $40M Series A rounds (S134). Newer and different for a frontier lab: "whether we burn 500 million a year or 5 billion or 50 billion a year I don't care I genuinely don't" (S029, 2024). Sources: 8 (S001, S004, S019, S022, S029, S083, S124, S134). Confidence: high for early-stage startups; the 2024 view applies to a company already on a clear trajectory.

24. **Be generous with employee equity and tough with investors.** "I have never seen a startup regret being generous with equity for their early employees." (S091) "I believe in like fighting with investors to reduce the amount of equity they get" (S132) Sources: 4 (S063, S064, S091, S132). Confidence: high.

25. **Pick cofounders you have known and worked with for a long time.** "one of the biggest predictors of success was had the co-founders known each other for a long time." (S109) Sources: 4 (S092, S109, S124, S132). Confidence: high.

26. **Never trade long-term user trust for short-term revenue.** "if ChatGPT were accepting payment to put a worse hotel above a better hotel, that's probably catastrophic for your relationship with ChatGPT." (S024) "our long-term incentive is to stay as aligned with our users as possible." (S104) Sources: 6 (S023, S024, S041, S094, S104, S105). Confidence: high.

27. **Set the incentives before you set the strategy.** Incentives are superpowers, so time spent setting them is the highest leverage there is (S122, S011). "If everyone owns a slice of American value creation, everyone will want America to do better." (S005) Sources: 4 (S005, S011, S089, S122). Confidence: high.

28. **Operate from optimism; pessimism is the costliest mistake.** "The most expensive investing mistake in the world to make is to be a pessimist." (S025) "the systemic mistake is always to assume that progress is about to S-curve off" (S123) Sources: 5 (S025, S031, S043, S122, S123). Confidence: high.

29. **In fundraising, create competition and never take a price that sets up a down round.** "Anything other than a term sheet is a no, and all the reasons don't matter." (S065) "In early-stage investing, you should not focus on downside protection." (S097) Sources: 2 (S065, S097). Confidence: medium.

30. **Grow leaders inside, but get a few great builders of organizations early.** "I think most of the time executives at a company should be promoted internally, not hired externally." (S032, 2026, newer) Earlier he praised an inexperienced CEO who quickly hires "a small number of direct reports who are really great" (S130). Sources: 3 (S026, S032, S130). Confidence: high on count, but the sources pull in two directions, so weight the newer view.
