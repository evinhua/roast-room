# Principles: Sam Altman

Built from 51 sources (his essays from 2013 to 2025 and talks and interviews from 2014 to 2026). 28 decision rules, roughly most-supported first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Concentrate on a few high-conviction bets and delete the rest.** "Concentrate your resources on a small number of high-conviction bets; this is easy to say but evidently hard to do. You can delete more stuff than you think." (S011) Sources: 5 (S011, S032, S036, S038, S040). Confidence: high.

2. **Do not copy what everyone else is building.** "don't shoot for something that somebody else has already done. Shoot for something that no one has done yet." (S025) "the most enduring companies are usually not doing the same thing as everybody else." (S026) Sources: 6 (S025, S026, S031, S037, S047, S050). Confidence: high.

3. **Pick the hard problem that matters over the easy one that does not.** "It is easier for a team to do a hard thing that really matters than to do an easy thing that doesn't really matter; audacious ideas motivate people." (S011) "Do something that matters. Like do something that is important and if you don't do it, if your company doesn't succeed, might not happen." (S047) Sources: 5 (S003, S011, S028, S047, S050). Confidence: high.

4. **Build something a small number of people love before trying to scale.** "The startup graveyard is littered with people who thought they could skip this step." (S001) "you got to like make something people want." (S048) Sources: 5 (S001, S013, S017, S028, S048). Confidence: high.

5. **Ship early, even when you are embarrassed by it.** "it does kind of suck to ship a product that you're embarrassed about but it's much better than the alternative." (S029) Sources: 5 (S017, S029, S033, S038, S042). Confidence: high.

6. **Bet that AI gets better and cheaper every year, and still build a real business.** "bet that intelligence as a service gets better and cheaper every year and it is necessary but not sufficient for you to win." (S042) Against AI magic thinking: "the laws of business don't apply to me I have this magic technology... that's obviously not true" (S036), said of founders who skip building a moat. Sources: 4 (S036, S042, S045, S050). Confidence: high.

7. **Only take high-risk bets that pay off hugely if they work.** "high risk bets are okay as long as you take the ones where if they work, it's super valuable." (S038) Sources: 4 (S015, S026, S038, S050). Confidence: high.

8. **Count inaction as a risk; most things are less risky than they look.** "Inspiration is perishable and life goes by fast. Inaction is a particularly insidious type of risk." (S011) "Don't worry so much. Things in life are rarely as risky as they seem." (S004) Sources: 4 (S002, S004, S011, S019). Confidence: high.

9. **Be contrarian only when you are also right.** "what you really want is to be right being contrarian and wrong still is wrong." (S029) "if you can't find anybody else that shares your belief, you should pay attention to that." (S031) Sources: 4 (S012, S027, S029, S031). Confidence: high.

10. **Hold conviction until the data arrives, then follow the data.** "conviction is great until the moment you have data one way or the other." (S036) He also admits he did not update enough on AlexNet in 2012 (S050). Sources: 3 (S026, S036, S050). Confidence: high.

11. **When something grows while still rough, drop everything else and serve it.** "when something really starts growing and it's not very good, you have a guaranteed hit on your hands." (S030) Sources: 4 (S030, S032, S038, S047). Confidence: high.

12. **Kill good projects to feed the great one.** "killing the good ideas, like sacrificing the good ideas to go after the great ideas is is kind of the hardest lesson for any entrepreneur or business to learn." (S038) Sources: 4 (S011, S032, S038, S040). Confidence: high.

13. **Scale what is working, especially if it gets better with scale.** "The central learning of my career so far has been that you can and should scale up things that are working." (S015) Sources: 4 (S011, S015, S030, S036). Confidence: high.

14. **Growth fixes most problems; never lose momentum.** "Growth solves (nearly) all problems." (S013) "One of the most important jobs you have as a founder is to never lose momentum." (S028) Sources: 3 (S013, S014, S028). Confidence: high.

15. **Plan in decades, execute in weeks, and only start what you would do for ten years.** "Plans should be measured in decades, execution should be measured in weeks." (S011) Sources: 4 (S003, S011, S013, S014). Confidence: high.

16. **Prefer a fast-growing market to a large, slow one.** "What you really want to do is identify a market that's going to grow every year and be able to ride that up elevator." (S028) "what you should care about is the growth rate and eventual size of a market." (S015) Sources: 3 (S014, S015, S028). Confidence: high.

17. **Startups win when the ground is moving; move fast then.** "when the clock cycle of the industry changes this much, startups almost always win." (S026) Sources: 4 (S026, S032, S036, S044). Confidence: high.

18. **Be willing to be dismissed for years; it keeps competitors away.** "for years at OpenAI it felt like we knew the biggest secret in the world. Everybody was calling us an idiot" (S031) Sources: 4 (S015, S031, S033, S034). Confidence: high.

19. **Hire for slope, and spend real time recruiting.** "hire for slope, not Y intercept. And I think that's just like unbelievably great advice." (S026) "Spend more time recruiting. Take risks on high-potential people with a fast rate of improvement." (S011) Sources: 4 (S011, S015, S023, S026). Confidence: high.

20. **Hire slowly, never compromise early, and fire fast.** "I have never met a newbie founder that fires fast enough." (S018) Sources: 3 (S013, S017, S018). Confidence: high.

21. **Promote leaders from inside; do not open with senior administrators.** "I think most of the time executives at a company should be promoted internally, not hired externally." (S032) Sources: 2 (S026, S032). Confidence: medium.

22. **Founders do the selling, and no one stands between them and users.** "All great careers, to some degree, become sales jobs." (S002) He says a company needs at least one evangelical founder who recruits, sells, and raises (S028). Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S017, S028). Confidence: high.

23. **Keep burn low early; do not raise more than you need.** He told startups to stay frugal and warned that companies raising $30M or more in their first round do badly (S022), and to keep personal burn low for options (S004, S019). Newer and different for a frontier lab: "whether we burn 500 million a year or 5 billion or 50 billion a year I don't care I genuinely don't" (S029, 2024). Sources: 5 (S001, S004, S019, S022, S029). Confidence: high for early-stage startups; the 2024 view applies to a company already on a clear trajectory.

24. **Never let payment change the answer you give a user.** "if ChatGPT were accepting payment to put a worse hotel above a better hotel, that's probably catastrophic for your relationship with ChatGPT." (S024) "I kind of hate ads just as an aesthetic choice" (S023) Sources: 3 (S023, S024, S041). Confidence: high.

25. **Rebuild AI-first and adopt AI fast, or lose to those who do.** "Bolting AI onto the existing way of doing things, I don't think it's going to work well as redesigning stuff in this sort of like AI-first world." (S040) "a startup of today still looks mostly like a startup of 10 years ago, cuz that's what the received wisdom says you're supposed to do" (S032) Sources: 3 (S032, S040, S050). Confidence: high.

26. **Operate from optimism; pessimism is the costliest mistake.** "The most expensive investing mistake in the world to make is to be a pessimist." (S025) Sources: 3 (S025, S031, S043). Confidence: high.

27. **Expect more money at lower margins as AI cuts costs.** "I think that most companies like OpenAI will make more money at a lower margin." (S024) The view rests on his belief that the cost of intelligence and energy trends toward zero (inferred from S024, S043). Sources: 1 (S024). Confidence: low.

28. **Answer a credible threat early and hard.** "when a pandemic starts every bit of action you take at the beginning is worth much more than action you take later." (S040) Sources: 1 (S040). Confidence: low.
