# Frameworks: Sam Altman

Built from 51 sources (his essays from 2013 to 2025 and talks and interviews from 2014 to 2026). 22 frameworks, roughly most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea is used (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short descriptive name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. Compounding exponentials *(unnamed; "compound yourself" in S002)*
- **What it is:** The furthest-out years matter most, so pick work and businesses whose advantage grows with time and scale. He says his edge is trusting exponential curves more than most people do, in people, companies, and models alike.
- **Parts:** Look for an up-and-to-the-right curve, not a linear one; prefer leverage (capital, technology, brand, network effects, managing people) over selling time; operate on a 10-year clock while others run on 3; let the next project make the last one look like a footnote.
- **When to use:** Any choice between a safe linear path and a bet that gets better every year it survives.
- **How to score:** 5 if the advantage gets stronger with each customer, year, or model release (network effect, data, brand, scale economics); 3 if it holds steady; 1 if every unit of growth costs the same effort as the last.
- **Quote:** "Compounding exponentials are magic. In particular, you really want to build a business that gets a compounding advantage with scale." (S011)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S011, S016, S031, S032, S046)

## 2. Iterative deployment *(his name for it)*
- **What it is:** Ship early and imperfect, watch where it breaks, improve fast, and never surprise people with a big reveal. He treats this as both the way to a good product and the way to a safe one.
- **Parts:** Release something real to real users; keep the feedback loop tight; ship in steps so customers and the world can adapt; keep the ability to pull back and change.
- **When to use:** Launch timing, big-bang versus staged rollouts, and any team that wants to "finish it first".
- **How to score:** High if the plan ships something usable within weeks and learns from it; low if the plan is a long stealth build followed by one launch.
- **Quote:** "put things out into the world, get feedback from real customers, see where they break, see where they don't break. That is the way you make a good product. That is also the way you make a safe product." (S038)
- **Sources:** 9 (S008, S023, S029, S033, S034, S038, S039, S042, S045)

## 3. Make something a small number of users love *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Love before scale. A few users who love a product will spread it; many users who mildly like it will not. Spontaneous word of mouth is the real test.
- **Parts:** Find the users who love it; make it better for them; measure whether they tell friends unprompted; scale only after that.
- **When to use:** Before spending on growth, ads, or sales hires; when a founder points to broad but shallow interest.
- **How to score:** 5 if a named group uses it constantly and recruits others; 3 if usage is steady but nobody evangelizes; 1 if the evidence is signups, a waitlist, or polite praise.
- **Quote:** "It's much better to first make a product a small number of users love than a product that a large number of users like." (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S013, S017, S028, S048)

## 4. Bet on the next model, not against it *(unnamed; "flashlight versus Uber" in S042)*
- **What it is:** Every business built on AI either gets better when the models improve or gets wiped out by it. Build the kind that rides the rising tide, and still build a real business on top.
- **Parts:** Assume intelligence as a service gets better and cheaper every year; ask whether that helps or kills you; avoid filling a gap the platform will close (the flashlight app); aim for a business the platform enables but cannot absorb (Uber); keep a moat that is not the model.
- **When to use:** Any product that depends on AI, and any decision to pour work into making today's model barely work.
- **How to score:** 5 if a model twice as good makes the product clearly better and the moat stays; 3 if neutral; 1 if the next release makes the product unnecessary.
- **Quote:** "you can either build a business that bets against the next model being really good or a model that bets on that happening and benefits from it happening." (S042)
- **Sources:** 4 (S036, S042, S045, S050)

## 5. Hard startups are a tailwind *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A hard, important mission is often easier to win than an easy one, because talent, advisors, partners, and money all want to work on something that matters. Easy startups face the same forces as headwinds.
- **Parts:** Ask whether it would be very valuable if it works; ask whether it matters if you do not do it; tell the big version of the vision while keeping a reasonable next step.
- **When to use:** Choosing between a safe small idea and a bigger, harder one; recruiting problems.
- **How to score:** High if the hard version is also valuable when it works and the team can name the next concrete step; low if it is hard for its own sake or easy and forgettable.
- **Quote:** "An easy startup is a headwind; a hard startup is a tailwind." (S003)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S011, S028, S047, S050)

## 6. Take small bets with 1x downside and 100x upside *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Most people overestimate risk and ignore the risk of standing still. Seek asymmetric bets, and count inaction as a risk too.
- **Parts:** Name the worst case; name the case where it works; compare the ratio; remember that years spent doing nothing are also a cost.
- **When to use:** A founder frozen by fear of a move whose downside is bounded.
- **How to score:** Strong if the loss is capped and survivable and the win is many times larger; weak if the downside is ruin or the upside is only a small step.
- **Quote:** "Look for small bets you can make where you lose 1x if you're wrong but make 100x if it works." (S002)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S004, S011, S019, S038)

## 7. Power law filter *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Returns follow a power law. The best bet outperforms all others combined, so only bets that could be enormous are worth the time. He applies it to investing and to AI research alike.
- **Parts:** Ask what it becomes if it works; drop ideas that cap out small even if they are safe; accept many failures in exchange for a shot at a giant outcome.
- **When to use:** Choosing which product, market, or company to commit years to.
- **How to score:** His investing threshold is a possible $10 billion company. Guidance: 5 if there is a believable path to that scale; 3 if a solid business is the best case; 1 if the ceiling is a nice small company.
- **Quote:** "You should try to limit yourself to opportunities that could be $10 billion companies if they work." (S015) Also, a line he quotes from Vinod Khosla, not his own: "there's a very big difference between a zero million dollar startup and a zero billion dollar startup, but they both have zero dollars of revenue." (S026)
- **Sources:** 4 (S015, S026, S038, S050)

## 8. Extreme conviction on one bet, until the data says otherwise *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A smaller team beats a richer one by concentrating hard on one bet. Conviction is right until real data arrives; then you follow the data, even against your old view.
- **Parts:** Pick the one thing; put most resources there; define what evidence would change your mind; switch fully when it comes.
- **When to use:** A founder spread across several bets, or one clinging to a thesis the numbers have already answered.
- **How to score:** High if focus is total and the team names the data that would change course; low if bets are spread thin or the team ignores clear data.
- **Quote:** "most of the world still does not understand the value of like a fairly extreme level of conviction on one bet." (S036) Also: "conviction is great until the moment you have data one way or the other." (S036)
- **Sources:** 5 (S011, S026, S032, S036, S050)

## 9. Kill the good to make the best work better *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When one thing starts to really work, shut down other good things to feed it. He calls this the hardest lesson and admits he is bad at it himself (S038).
- **Parts:** Spot what is working best; list the good projects competing for the same people and compute; cut them, even if funded and exciting; move the resources.
- **When to use:** A company with one rising product and several side bets.
- **How to score:** High if the plan moves people and money to the best performer; low if it keeps every good project alive "for optionality".
- **Quote:** "killing the good ideas, like sacrificing the good ideas to go after the great ideas is is kind of the hardest lesson for any entrepreneur or business to learn." (S038)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S032, S038, S040)

## 10. Growth while it is still bad means a hit *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** If something grows on its own while still rough, you have a hit. Drop other plans and serve it. He read the ChatGPT launch this way while researchers called it hype (S032), and Peter Thiel told him to ignore everything but the growing text box (S038).
- **Parts:** Watch for organic growth despite a poor product; follow what users are already doing; put the company behind it at once.
- **When to use:** A founder who wants to polish or pivot while something rough is already spreading.
- **How to score:** Strong signal if usage grows without paid push and despite obvious flaws; weak if growth only comes from spend or launches.
- **Quote:** "when something really starts growing and it's not very good, you have a guaranteed hit on your hands." (S030)
- **Sources:** 4 (S030, S032, S038, S047)

## 11. Scale what is working *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When something already works at small scale and gets better as it grows, push it to a scale no one has tried. Scale often shows properties that were invisible when small. He admits he has no theory for why (S030).
- **Parts:** Confirm it works small; list every reason not to scale it (technical, capital, culture); remove them one at a time; push.
- **When to use:** A working product or channel that the team is nervous to expand.
- **How to score:** High if the thing gets better with scale and the barriers are named; low if scaling would only multiply something that does not yet work.
- **Quote:** "when in doubt if you have something that seems like it's getting better with scale I think you should scale it up." (S036) Also: "The central learning of my career so far has been that you can and should scale up things that are working." (S015)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S015, S030, S036)

## 12. Real trend versus fake trend *(named in S028)*
- **What it is:** A real trend is a new platform the early adopters use obsessively and talk about. A fake trend sells units that sit unused. His examples: the iPhone was real; VR in 2018 was not.
- **Parts:** Look at usage, not purchases; check whether a small group uses it a lot rather than many using it a little; check whether they tell friends.
- **When to use:** Betting a company on a new platform or technology wave.
- **How to score:** Real if early users build their day around it; fake if the numbers are sales, downloads, or press.
- **Quote:** "Real trends are ones where a new technology platform comes along and the early adopters use it obsessively and tell the friends how much they love it." (S028)
- **Sources:** 2 (S012, S028)

## 13. Tectonic shifts and the changing clock cycle *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The best openings come when something is possible this year that was not last year. When an industry's pace speeds up, costs fall, and cycle times shrink, startups beat incumbents.
- **Parts:** Name the shift; name what it newly makes possible; ask why now; check whether big companies are slower on this curve than you.
- **When to use:** Judging timing and the "why now" of an idea.
- **How to score:** High if the idea depends on a capability that only just became cheap or possible; low if it could have been built five years ago.
- **Quote:** "Any time you can think of something that is possible this year and wasn't possible last year, you should pay attention." (S012) Also: "startups are they have the biggest edge when the ground is shifting the most...when costs are coming down rapidly and cycle times are coming down rapidly, that's when startups really I think just have a a massive inherent advantage." (S032)
- **Sources:** 6 (S012, S027, S032, S036, S044, S050)

## 14. Seems bad, is good *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The best ideas sit where most people think the idea is bad but you can explain why it is good. Being contrarian only pays when you are also right.
- **Parts:** State why most people dismiss it; state what they are missing; check that the reason is real, not just contrarian taste.
- **When to use:** A founder with an idea everyone dislikes, or one with an idea everyone already loves.
- **How to score:** High if the founder can explain both the common objection and why it is wrong; low if the idea is popular and obvious, or unpopular with no clear reason it works.
- **Quote:** "A good test for an idea is if you can articulate why most people think it's a bad idea, but you understand what makes it good." (S012) Also: "being contrarian and wrong still is wrong." (S029)
- **Sources:** 7 (S012, S025, S027, S029, S031, S047, S050)

## 15. Plans in decades, execution in weeks *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Hold a few strong convictions about where the future is heading and commit for ten years, while moving very fast on what to do now. He plans forward from today, not backward from a distant date (S032).
- **Parts:** A small set of long-range convictions as direction; a 10-year willingness to stay; decisions made in an hour, not a week; flexibility on everything else.
- **When to use:** Founders with a big vision and a slow cadence, or fast cadence and no direction.
- **How to score:** High if the founder would stick with it for ten years and ships weekly; low if either the long commitment or the short cadence is missing.
- **Quote:** "Plans should be measured in decades, execution should be measured in weeks." (S011)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S011, S013, S014, S032)

## 16. Hire for slope, not Y-intercept *(he credits the phrase to another investor)*
- **What it is:** Rate of improvement matters more than current skill for most roles. He reverses this for board members, where the track record matters more (S023).
- **Parts:** Meet people more than once; look for visible improvement between meetings; take risks on fast improvers; check track record only for roles that need it now.
- **When to use:** Hiring, choosing cofounders, picking which founder to back.
- **How to score:** High if the person shows fast improvement and has shipped real things; low if the case rests on pedigree alone.
- **Quote:** "The rate of improvement is often more important than the current absolute ability." (S015) Also: "There are some roles where I totally ignore track record and just look at slope, ignore the Y-intercept..." (S023)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S015, S023, S026)

## 17. Cost of intelligence falls, demand has no ceiling *(unnamed; "Three Observations" in S007)*
- **What it is:** His AI economics. Model ability rises with the log of compute and data; the cost of a given level of AI falls about 10x a year; and demand for good enough intelligence at a low enough price is effectively uncapped. He says the marginal cost of intelligence and energy will fall toward zero.
- **Parts:** Assume the input cost of intelligence drops sharply every year; plan for volume rather than scarcity; expect compute to be the binding constraint.
- **When to use:** Pricing, unit economics, and timing for any AI product; deciding whether a cost problem will solve itself.
- **How to score:** Guidance: favor plans that become more profitable as AI gets cheaper; mark down plans whose margin depends on intelligence staying expensive.
- **Quote:** "Moore's law changed the world at 2x every 18 months; this is unbelievably stronger." (S007) Also: "the demand for sufficiently high quality intelligence at a sufficiently low price is effectively uncapped." (S031)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S007, S009, S023, S031, S043)

## 18. Moat beyond the model *(unnamed; "middle layer" in S043)*
- **What it is:** For AI companies, defensibility comes from customer relationships, proprietary data loops, personalization, and brand, not from the base model. Defensibility does not have to exist on day one: a window with the only good product is time to build it (S026).
- **Parts:** Build on a strong base model; add a vertical data flywheel; deepen memory and personalization; know your plan for a long-term edge (network effect or monopoly).
- **When to use:** Any "thin wrapper" worry, and any pitch that cannot explain why a copy would fail.
- **How to score:** High if the founder can name the network effect, data loop, or lock-in that grows with use; low if the only edge is being first with today's model.
- **Quote:** "The best thing you can do to make an AI startup is... to build like deep relationships with customers a product they love and some sort of moat that doesn't have to be technology." (S045) Also: "All of the really great businesses I know have an answer to this question." (S028)
- **Sources:** 6 (S026, S027, S028, S040, S043, S045)

## 19. Capability overhang *(his term)*
- **What it is:** Models can already do far more than the products built on them. Even if progress stopped, there would be a great deal left to build, so most opportunity lies in products, not in new models.
- **Parts:** Ask what today's models can do that no product yet captures; build that; rebuild workflows AI-first rather than bolting AI onto old ones.
- **When to use:** Founders who think AI is "taken" by the big labs, or who plan to copy a lab's core product.
- **How to score:** High if the idea captures an ability nobody has productized and is designed AI-first; low if it copies a lab's chat assistant or adds AI to an old flow.
- **Quote:** "it looks to me now like the overhang is going to be massive in most of the world." (S040) Also: "Bolting AI onto the existing way of doing things, I don't think it's going to work well as redesigning stuff in this sort of like AI-first world." (S040)
- **Sources:** 4 (S026, S032, S040, S050)

## 20. Platform plus killer app *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Build the platform and also the app that shows what it can do, then leave most categories to others. He wants OpenAI to be a platform company rather than to build every product (S038).
- **Parts:** One flagship app that proves the platform; open the platform to builders; decline categories others can do better.
- **When to use:** Platform companies deciding how much to build themselves.
- **How to score:** High if one strong app proves the platform and outside builders have room; low if the company tries to own every use case.
- **Quote:** "A business strategy I've always really respected is like the platform plus killer app together." (S045)
- **Sources:** 3 (S038, S045, S048)

## 21. Sell light at night, not electricity *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When a technology is new, people do not buy the technology; they buy the clearest outcome it gives them. Early electric utilities sold light at night.
- **Parts:** Find the one tangible outcome; lead with it; reveal the platform underneath later.
- **When to use:** Positioning and marketing for a new technology product.
- **How to score:** High if the pitch names a concrete result a customer can feel; low if it sells "AI" or the platform itself.
- **Quote:** "what they started marketing, selling to people was light at night...what you are getting from us is not electricity. It's light at night." (S030)
- **Sources:** 1 (S030)

## 22. Move early on threats *(unnamed; "pandemic" analogy)*
- **What it is:** Like a pandemic, a competitive threat is cheapest to answer at the start. Act hard and early, then correct.
- **Parts:** Treat a credible threat as real before it is confirmed; respond at once; scale the response down if it proves small.
- **When to use:** A competitor with momentum, or a platform move aimed at your market.
- **How to score:** High if the response starts now and is sized to the worst plausible case; low if the plan is to wait and see.
- **Quote:** "when a pandemic starts every bit of action you take at the beginning is worth much more than action you take later." (S040)
- **Sources:** 1 (S040)

---

**Changed views to keep in mind:**
- Vertical integration: long against it, now for it in AI. "I was always against vertical integration and I now think I was just wrong about that." (S039, 2025, newer)
- Spending: in 2014 he warned startups against raising and spending too much (S022); in 2024 he said of OpenAI, "whether we burn 500 million a year or 5 billion or 50 billion a year I don't care I genuinely don't." (S029, newer). The second is about a frontier lab with a trajectory, not advice for a seed company.
- Speed of adoption: after GPT-4 he expected the economy to change faster than it did (S038, S047); his education prediction was wrong too (S030). Weight his timelines for customer behavior change as optimistic.
