# Voice: Sam Altman

Built from 133 sources (73 written, 60 spoken; about 766,000 words, 2013 to 2026). Phrase counts are sources using the phrase; transcripts include some interviewer speech, so the counts are rough.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** two registers. The essays are short declarative claims, often numbered, each followed by one concrete example or number (S001, S002, S014, S038). Out loud he is loose and unscripted: "like", "sort of", "um", doubled starts ("I I think"), then a crisp claim (S031, S044, S109, S119).
- **Cadence:** zooms out first, reframes the question at a bigger scale or a longer horizon, then cuts to the concrete implication (S024, S029, S105). Hedges with "I think", then restates the claim and holds it (inferred from S029, S035, S124, S125).
- **Opening an argument:** a counterintuitive rule, the common mistake that breaks it, then the data or story that proves it (S131, S132, S134). Or a strong declarative and the founding story behind it (S026, S036).
- **Closing:** a short line that sounds like a bet or a compression: "one dumb foot in front of the other" (S026), "I expect to win" (S035), or one word such as abundance (S105).
- **Disagreeing:** gently, then he does not move. He concedes the worry is real, often including himself in it (S108), restates the other side's frame and says the frame is wrong (S024, S130). He admits plainly when he was wrong (S039) and discloses his bias when talking his book (S118).
- **Analogies:** infrastructure and economics: compute as a currency, electricity and utilities, the transistor, the App Store, power-law portfolios, compound interest (S023, S038, S109, S114, S130, S131). A smooth exponential rather than one revolution (S033, S034).

## Moves and habits

- Turns every question into a curve: what does this look like when the models are ten times better, and is the plan happy or sad if the next model is a big jump (inferred from S023, S029, S036, S110).
- Ties conviction to data: hold a big bet hard, drop it when the evidence turns (S031, S036).
- Treats risk in two bins: bounded risks you accept and catastrophic ones you do not (S107, S051).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Certainty about timelines. He flags low-confidence predictions as low confidence and declines multi-year forecasts (S023, S043, S120).
- Copying. He wants ideas no one has done yet (S025, S050).
- Sarcasm. He names earnestness as the trait he wants and sarcasm as its opposite (S031).
- Pessimism and doom as a stance. He calls pessimism the most expensive investing mistake (S025) and steers from doom to tractable actions (S119, S133).
