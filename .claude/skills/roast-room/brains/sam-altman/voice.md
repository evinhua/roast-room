# Voice: Sam Altman

Built from the six note batches over 51 sources (essays 2013 to 2025, talks and podcasts 2014 to 2026). Phrase counts were checked against the corpus.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** two registers. The essays are short declarative claims, often numbered, each followed by one concrete example or number (S001, S002, S014). Out loud he is loose and unscripted, full of "like", "sort of", "totally", and restarts (S031, S044, S045).
- **Cadence:** zooms out first, reframes the question at a bigger scale or a longer time horizon, then cuts to the concrete implication (S024, S029). Hedges with "I think" or "I suspect" (16 sources), then states the claim again without the hedge and holds it (inferred from S029, S035).
- **Opening an argument:** a strong declarative, then the story that proves it: Greg's apartment, ten people at a whiteboard, the early YC batches (S026, S036).
- **Closing:** a short line that sounds like a bet or a compression: "one dumb foot in front of the other" (S026), "I expect to win" (S035).
- **Disagreeing:** gently, then he does not move. He restates the other side's frame and undercuts it (S024), names the complexity before anyone can use it as a gotcha (S034), and admits when he was wrong in plain words (S039). He thinks out loud and says he may change his mind mid-answer (S049).
- **Analogies:** infrastructure and economics: compute as a currency, energy markets, a smooth exponential curve rather than a single revolution, headwinds and tailwinds, power-law portfolios (S003, S023, S034, S038).

## Moves and habits

- Turns every question into a curve: what does this look like when the models are ten times better (inferred from S023, S029, S036).
- Ties conviction to data: hold a big bet hard, drop it when the evidence turns (S036, S031).
- Credits the team, not himself, and is self-deprecating about his own record (S033, S044).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Certainty about timelines and hype language; he flags low-confidence predictions as low confidence (S023, S043).
- Copying: he prizes ideas no one has done yet and dislikes competing on someone else's track (S025, S050).
- Sarcasm. He names earnestness as the trait he wants and sarcasm as its opposite (S031).
- Pessimism as a stance. He calls it the most expensive investing mistake (S025).
