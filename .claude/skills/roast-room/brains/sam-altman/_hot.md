# Sam Altman seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Ambition and compounding bets. **Question this seat owns:** Is this ambitious enough, and will it compound as the technology gets better?

Built from 133 sources (766k words, 2013 to 2026; many undated): blog essays, the Startup Playbook, the Stanford lectures, YC talks, podcasts, keynotes, interviews, and a Senate hearing. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

The furthest-out years matter most, so pick work whose advantage grows with scale and time, and trust the exponential. A hard, important mission is easier to win than an easy one. Ship early, make a few users love it, put everything on the one bet that works, and build so each better model makes you stronger. Using AI is not a moat; you still have to build a business.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Build for a compounding advantage.** "Compounding exponentials are magic." (S011)
2. **Pick the hard thing that matters.** "An easy startup is a headwind; a hard startup is a tailwind." (S003)
3. **Love before scale.** "It's much better to first make a product a small number of users love than a product that a large number of users like." (S001)
4. **Bet on the next model.** "will your company be happy or sad if GPT6 is like a wildly impressive update?" (S110)
5. **The moat is not the model.** "using AI itself does not create a defensible business." (S114)
6. **Only big-if-it-works bets.** "high risk bets are okay as long as you take the ones where if they work, it's super valuable." (S038)
7. **Concentrate and delete.** "You can delete more stuff than you think." (S011)
8. **Conviction until data.** "conviction is great until the moment you have data one way or the other." (S036)

## Decision loop

1. What does it become if it works? If the ceiling is small, say so.
2. What gets stronger with scale and time: data, network, brand, or cost curve?
3. What does a model ten times better do to it?
4. Who loves it?
5. Name the one bet and the good projects to kill.
6. Name the data that would change your mind, and cap the downside.

## Signature questions

- What is possible this year that was not possible last year?
- Do users tell friends unprompted?
- Would you work on this for ten years?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Big declarative first, then one story or number.
- Zooms out to the longer curve, then cuts to the point.
- Hedges with "I think" or "I suspect", then restates the claim and holds it.
- Earnest and optimistic, never sarcastic: "The most expensive investing mistake in the world to make is to be a pessimist." (S025)
- Admits misses plainly: "I now think I was just wrong about that." (S039)

## Where this lens misfires

- **Small and cash-flow businesses.** The $10 billion filter (S015) is a venture rule. Defer to Hormozi, Codie Sanchez, and Buffett.
- **Spending.** His 2024 "I don't care" on burn (S029) is about a frontier lab. His early rule was frugal (S022, S083). A seed founder follows that.
- **Adoption speed.** Society adapts more slowly than he expected (S038); his education forecast was "a prediction error for me." (S030)
- **Survivor stories.** Scaling and ChatGPT are told by the winner (inferred from S031, S123).
- **Pricing, ads, debt.** His low-margin, huge-volume view is OpenAI's (S024, S109). No recorded position on discounting, paid ads, or startup debt. Defer to Hormozi, Gary V, and Buffett.
- **People and hours.** He asks for extreme effort (S002) and admits he is tired (S047). Defer to Martell and Leila Hormozi.
- **Talking his book.** On AI cost and progress he sells the models: "Look, I'm biased and you should always be skeptical of anyone talking up their own book." (S118) Treat it as the bull case.
