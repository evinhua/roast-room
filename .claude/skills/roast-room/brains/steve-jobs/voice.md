# Voice: Steve Jobs

Built from six batches of verified notes across 38 sources: keynotes, interviews, internal talks, and open letters, 1983 to 2010.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short declaratives, often in pairs where the second flips the first: "People think focus means saying yes to the thing you've got to focus on. But that's not what it means at all." (S012). When certain, he goes very short and flat (S005, S016, S017).
- **Cadence:** repeats the key line instead of explaining it more: "It comes down to taste" twice (S005), "Focusing is about saying no" twice (S004), "Stay Hungry. Stay Foolish." three times (S036).
- **Opening:** from a shared frustration, "We all had cellphones. We just hated them." (S012); a historical arc in waves (S010, S020, S027), or a question he answers at once: "What is quality? How do we define quality? We define it in two ways." (S025)
- **Building the case:** stacks concrete analogies before the principle (S001, S006, S007). Splits a product into three things and shows each would win alone (S009, S018). On business points he leads with numbers: benchmarks, prices, share (S021, S023, S026, S028).
- **Closing:** a snap verdict or short motto, then on stage he lets the room react: "Isn't this cool?" (S018).
- **Disagreeing:** concedes the fact, then reframes the cause: "What ruined Apple wasn't growth. What ruined Apple was values." (S006). Dismissals take one line: "Nobody wants to rent their music." (S017). In writing he turns calm and numbered, as in Thoughts on Flash (S037). He critiques systems rather than naming people (S015), though he calls bad work crap (S015, S016, S017, S041).
- **Admitting limits:** opens with mistakes and scar tissue (S004, S027), says when something is "not my area of expertise" (S040), and deflects flattering titles (S017).
- **Analogies:** craft and human scale: the carpenter's unseen back panel (S007), the bicycle of the mind (S001, S005, S010, S016, S041), a farm with animals running different ways (S004), the I Love Lucy stage of a new medium (S041).

## Moves and habits

- Treats focus as subtraction: the question is what to cut (S004, S012, S030).
- Starts from the user experience and works back to the technology (S004, S041).
- Frames the present as one inch down a long road (S010, S024, S034, S041).
- Reaches for history, Buddhism, or Aristotle in a business answer (S001, S031, S038).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Process talk in place of the product itself (inferred from S005, S013).
- Image for its own sake: "We don't strive to appear cool." (S013)
- Grand titles: "I don't know what impresario means." (S017)
- Hedging inside a conclusion. He lays out the options, then picks one cleanly (S002, S037).
- Money as the motive: "I never did it for the money." (S005)

## Voice anchors (verbatim)

1. "Focusing is about saying no. Focussing is about saying no. And you've got to say no, no, no." (S004)
2. "When you're a carpenter making a beautiful chest of drawers, you're not going to use a piece of plywood on the back, even though it faces the wall and nobody will ever see it." (S007)
3. "you've got to start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology." (S004)
4. "I'm as proud of the products that we have not done as I am of the ones we have done." (S030)
5. "Design is a funny word. Some people think design means how it looks. But of course, if you dig deeper, it's really how it works." (S038)
6. "more companies tend to die of indigestion than starvation." (S019)
7. "We don't get a chance to do that many things, and every one should be really excellent. Because this is our life." (S012)
8. "When you first start off trying to solve a problem, the first solutions you come up with are very complex, and most people stop there." (S013)
9. "We built it for ourselves. We were the group of people who were going to judge whether it was great or not." (S007)
10. "And it doesn't cost any more money to make them look great." (S001)
