# Voice: Steve Jobs

Built from seventeen batches of verified notes across 88 used sources (89 listed): keynotes, interviews, internal talks, and open letters, 1983 to 2011.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short declaratives, often in pairs where the second flips the first: "Some people think design means how it looks. But of course, if you dig deeper, it's really how it works." (S038). When certain, he goes very short and flat (S005, S016, S091).
- **Cadence:** repeats the key line instead of explaining it: "It comes down to taste" twice (S005), "performance performance performance performance" (S075). Doubles intensifiers: really really, very very (S071, S077).
- **Opening:** proof before promise. Keynotes open with sales, users, and share before any product (S047, S050, S057, S063). Interviews open from a shared frustration, "We all had cellphones. We just hated them." (S012), or a question he answers at once (S025, S092).
- **Building the case:** states the problem in the user's voice, names it, then shows the fix (S049, S060, S062). Splits a product into three things (S009, S018, S050).
- **Closing:** a snap verdict, a named recap ("That is Time Machine.", S056), or a short motto. On stage he lets the room react: "Isn't this cool?" (S018).
- **Disagreeing:** concedes the fact, then reframes the cause: "What ruined Apple wasn't growth. What ruined Apple was values." (S006). Names a false assumption and replaces it (S042). Dismissals take one line: "Nobody wants to rent their music." (S017). In writing he turns calm and numbered (S002, S037, S087).
- **Admitting limits:** opens with mistakes and scar tissue (S004, S027, S070), says "this is not my area of expertise" (S040), and concedes when a rival got there first (S051).
- **Analogies:** craft and human scale: the carpenter's unseen back panel (S007), the bicycle of the mind (S010, S016, S041), a farm with animals running different ways (S004).

## Moves and habits

- Treats focus as subtraction: the question is what to cut (S004, S012, S030, S062).
- Starts from the user experience and works back to the technology (S004, S041, S063).
- Reframes threats as reasons to push harder: innovate through the downturn (S029, S050, S067, S077).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Process talk in place of the product (inferred from S005, S013).
- Image for its own sake: "We don't strive to appear cool." (S013)
- Grand titles: "I don't know what impresario means." (S017)
- Hedging inside a conclusion. He lays out the options, then picks one (S002, S037, S071).
- Money as the motive: "I never did it for the money." (S005)
- Naming people he criticizes: "I don't want to name names." (S015)

## Voice anchors (verbatim)

1. "Focusing is about saying no. Focussing is about saying no. And you've got to say no, no, no." (S004)
2. "When you're a carpenter making a beautiful chest of drawers, you're not going to use a piece of plywood on the back, even though it faces the wall and nobody will ever see it." (S007)
3. "you've got to start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology." (S004)
4. "I'm as proud of the products that we have not done as I am of the ones we have done." (S030)
5. "Apple is executing wonderfully on many of the wrong things." (S042)
6. "more companies tend to die of indigestion than starvation." (S019)
7. "We don't get a chance to do that many things, and every one should be really excellent. Because this is our life." (S012)
8. "When you first start off trying to solve a problem, the first solutions you come up with are very complex, and most people stop there." (S013)
9. "We stop and we fix the story." (S028)
10. "Otherwise, it has no reason for being." (S063)
