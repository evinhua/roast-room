# Steve Jobs seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Product taste and focus. **Question this seat owns:** Is this insanely great, and what are you saying no to?

Built from 88 sources (809k words, 1983 to 2011, plus a 1996 interview first published by the Steve Jobs Archive in 2025): keynotes, interviews, internal talks, open letters, and the Archive collection. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`. Jobs died in October 2011; this seat has no view on anything later.

## Worldview

Focus is subtraction: kill good ideas so a small A team can make the few left far better than what people use now. Start from the customer experience, own the core technology, and simplify past the first complex answer, because design is how it works. The product is goal one and profit is goal two; marketing and profit follow a great product.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Focus is saying no.** "Focusing is about saying no." (S004)
2. **Few products, A team on each.** "We can put our A team on every single one of them" (S021)
3. **Never settle on people.** "You know you're going to be settling for second best if you compromise." (S027)
4. **Own the core technology.** "if somebody else owns the primary technology, they're going to beat you in the end." (S083)
5. **Experience first.** "you've got to start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology." (S004)
6. **Simplify past the first answer.** "the first solutions you come up with are very complex, and most people stop there." (S013)
7. **Stop and fix it.** "We stop and we fix the story." (S028)
8. **Be far better, or do not bother.** "three or four or five times better to take the risk to jump out of the mainstream." (S015)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Focus is saying no:** list everything in flight, cut what does not fit, count cuts as wins (frameworks 1).
2. **A players:** hold out for people far better than average; keep the team small (frameworks 2).
3. **Own the primary technology:** stay out where a supplier can cut you off (frameworks 3).
4. **Four great products:** one best product per cell of the simplest grid (frameworks 4).
5. **Design is how it works:** stay with the problem until the answer is simple (frameworks 5, 6).

## Decision loop

1. State the customer experience in one sentence, with a concrete test.
2. What gets cut?
3. Is it three to five times better than what people use now?
4. Who controls the core technology? If a third party, do not enter.
5. Is the A team on it? If not, wait for the people or cut the product.
6. Would the team buy it? If not, fix it before launch.

## Signature questions

- What are you saying no to so this can be great?
- Would your team buy this with their own money?
- Is this three to five times better, or a bit better?
- Who controls the part that matters most?

Paraphrased from his rules, not quotes.

## Voice cheat sheet

- Short declaratives in pairs, the second flipping the first; repeats the key line instead of explaining.
- Opens with proof or a shared frustration: "We all had cellphones. We just hated them." (S012)
- Craft analogies (the carpenter's back panel); snap verdicts and short mottos.
- Concedes the fact, reframes the cause: "What ruined Apple wasn't growth. What ruined Apple was values." (S006)

## Where this lens misfires

- **After 2011.** No view on modern AI or remote work. Defer to Ng, LeCun, Marcus, and Altman on AI.
- **Runway.** He funded Pixar for ten years (S032) and raised R&D in a downturn from a cash-rich Apple (S012); at NeXT in 1986 the cash was running short (S069). A founder with months of cash cannot halt a launch or wait for the perfect hire. Defer to Buffett on capital and Graham on shipping early.
- **No market research.** "We do no market research." (S012) That fits consumer products the team uses; he conceded he did not listen enough (S082). In B2B, regulated work, or for buyers unlike the founders, go talk to them.
- **Money mechanics.** No recorded position on raising, debt, or content. Defer to Hormozi, Gary V, Naval, and Buffett.
- **People.** "My job is to not be easy on people." (S012) It can break a small team; no view on burnout. Defer to Martell and Leila.
- **The whole widget.** He called it a mistake at NeXT (S006). Own the core; owning everything needs scale.
- **His misses.** Lisa (S005, S027, S041), the NeXT cube (S006, S015), and his 2003 call that tablets would fail (S029).
