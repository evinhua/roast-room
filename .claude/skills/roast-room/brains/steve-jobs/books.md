# Books: Steve Jobs

Built from the source table (89 listed, 88 used) and seventeen batches of notes. Steve Jobs wrote no books. His thinking lives in interviews, keynotes, a commencement speech, and three open letters; one posthumous collection gathers his own words. Summaries are in my own words; no book text is reproduced.

## Make Something Wonderful: Steve Jobs in His Own Words (Steve Jobs Archive, published 2023, posthumous)
- **Core thesis:** An edited collection of his speeches, interviews, emails, and notes from 1976 to 2010. Not a book he wrote, but the closest thing to one: a maker's view that great work comes from caring more, focusing hard, and hiring people better than you.
- **Key frameworks:** cut the product line to four and put the A team on each; the dynamic range of talent and recruiting as the main job; management by shared values (agree on where you are going, then get out of the way); making something great costs time, not money; creative risk on a fiscally conservative base; marketing as values, not specs; liberal arts meeting technology; death as a filter for big choices.
- **Taught in sources:** S001 (the collection itself), with the same ideas in S012, S021, S023, S036, S045, S063, S088.

## Open letters (in place of books)
- **Thoughts on Music (2007):** lays out three options for DRM on iTunes and argues for abolishing it; a model of laying out alternatives with data before concluding. S002.
- **A Greener Apple (2007):** admits Apple had done the work on the environment but kept quiet about it, apologizes for that, and changes the policy of only talking about things already done. A model of owning a communication failure. S087.
- **Thoughts on Flash (2010):** six numbered reasons Apple kept Flash off iPhone and iPad; the case for owning the platform, open web standards, and leaving the past behind. S037.

## Major talks that work like essays
- **Stanford commencement address (2005):** three stories (connecting the dots, love and loss, death); do work you love and do not settle. S036, also in S001.
- **Macworld Boston return (1997):** the turnaround thesis: focus on where Apple is still relevant, find the core assets (the brand and the Mac OS), fix the top first, and drop the idea that Apple wins only if Microsoft loses. S042.
- **Digital hub keynotes (2001 to 2002):** the Mac as the center of cameras, camcorders, and music players, with one app per device that makes it worth far more; the three ages of the personal computer. S048, S049, S050, S074, S077, S078.
- **iPhone (2007) and iPad (2010) introductions:** leapfrog a bad category; a new category must be far better at key tasks; the intersection of technology and liberal arts; an aggressive price goal. S018, S063.
- **WWDC 2011 (iCloud):** his last keynote; moves the digital hub from the Mac to the cloud and admits MobileMe "wasn't our finest hour." S065.

## Books and works he cites
- **The Whole Earth Catalog (Stewart Brand):** he called it one of the bibles of his generation and took "Stay Hungry. Stay Foolish." from its farewell issue as the closing wish of the Stanford speech and as advice to keep risking failure. S036, S089.
- **Books as a model for computers:** he described the printed book as a way to read Aristotle without an intermediary, and hoped computers would one day let a student ask questions of a great thinker's worldview. S031.
