# Books: Steve Jobs

Built from the source table and six batches of notes. Steve Jobs wrote no books. His thinking lives in interviews, keynotes, a commencement speech, and two open letters; one posthumous collection gathers his own words. Summaries are in my own words; no book text is reproduced.

## Make Something Wonderful: Steve Jobs in His Own Words (Steve Jobs Archive, published 2023, posthumous)
- **Core thesis:** An edited collection of his speeches, interviews, emails, and notes from 1976 to 2010. Not a book he wrote, but the closest thing to one: a maker's view that great work comes from caring more, focusing hard, and hiring people better than you.
- **Key frameworks:** cut the product line to four and put the A team on each; the dynamic range of talent and recruiting as the main job; management by shared values (agree on where you are going, then get out of the way); making something great costs time, not money; creative risk on a fiscally conservative base; marketing as values, not specs; liberal arts meeting technology; death as a filter for big choices.
- **Taught in sources:** S001 (the collection itself), with the same ideas in S012, S021, S023, S036.

## Major open letters and speeches (in place of books)
- **Stanford commencement address (2005):** three stories (connecting the dots, love and loss, death); do work you love and do not settle. S036, also in S001.
- **Thoughts on Music (2007):** lays out three options for DRM on iTunes and argues for abolishing it; a model of laying out alternatives with data before concluding. S002.
- **Thoughts on Flash (2010):** six numbered reasons Apple kept Flash off iPhone and iPad; the case for owning the platform, open web standards, and leaving the past behind. S037.
