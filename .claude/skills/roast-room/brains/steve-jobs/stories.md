# Stories: Steve Jobs

20 stories and cases he reuses, built from the verified batch notes, most-used first, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **Cutting Apple from many products to four (1997).** He could not understand the product line after weeks, so he cut it to a grid of four: consumer and pro, desktop and portable. Engineers on cancelled projects came out relieved because they knew where they were going, and the loss turned to profit within a year. Lesson: focus is a financial strategy and it energizes the right people (S001, S011, S021, S023).
2. **Xerox PARC (1979).** He saw a rough graphical interface and within ten minutes knew every computer would work that way, so blinded by it he missed the networking and objects in the room. Xerox never shipped it; Apple had to finish the work and make it cheap. Lesson: seeing it is not enough, you have to make it real (S005, S010, S038).
3. **Pixar stops for the story.** Toy Story halted for months while the team was paid to fix a story that was not good enough, and every later film hit a story crisis and stopped too. Lesson: discipline on the core beats discipline on the schedule (S012, S028).
4. **Fired from Apple at 30.** The board sided with Sculley. He calls it the best thing that happened to him, since it led to NeXT and Pixar, but also says he lacked the experience to run a $2 billion company. Lesson: adversity can reset you, and own your part (S001, S005, S027, S036).
5. **The iTunes deal with the labels.** Apple told the labels their copy protection would not work, got thrown out, kept coming back, called which rival services would fail, and won the 99 cent store. Later he used the labels' own DRM-free CDs to argue for DRM-free downloads. Lesson: be right, be patient, bring evidence (S002, S009, S016, S030).
6. **NeXT: hardware failed, software survived.** The cube sold about 10,000 units and NeXT stopped making computers, but its software became the base of Mac OS X. He later said NeXT should have gone software-only sooner. Lesson: know which part of the bet is the real asset (S006, S015, S027).
7. **Saying no to the PDA to fund the iPod.** Apple passed on the PDA because phones would cover what most users did on the road. The resources went to the iPod, which took 70 percent share after years of single digit Mac share. Lesson: a no is what pays for the big yes (S012, S029).
8. **Making peace with Microsoft (1997).** With Apple near death he called Gates, settled terms on an hour's barefoot walk with Microsoft's CFO, and dropped the idea that Apple needed Microsoft to lose. Lesson: false competitive frames can kill a company (S008, S011).
9. **The Lisa bet and the Mac.** Apple put its best people on Lisa for three and a half years with no backup plan, and it was a mismatch for the company's culture and channels. The Mac was the course correction. Lesson: check the product against who actually buys from you (S005, S027, S041).
10. **Desktop publishing surprise.** Nobody planned desktop publishing for the Mac. Within a year Apple saw it was the Mac's real advantage and moved its whole strategy there. Lesson: watch for the use you did not plan and follow it (S027).
11. **Ten years and $50 million on Pixar.** He funded Pixar for about a decade before Toy Story and the IPO, and later said they should have funded only animation from the start. Lesson: the biggest creative bets need decades of patience, and side businesses dilute them (S001, S032).
12. **Apple Retail.** Retail staff turned over too fast to explain a new product, so Apple built its own stores, which reached a billion dollars in sales faster than any retailer before. Lesson: if the channel fails your product, build your own (S029, S030).
13. **The iPhone enclosure reset.** Weeks before launch he could not love the design, told the team to throw out a year of work, and they agreed. Lesson: a last-minute reset is what separates great from good (S012).
14. **OpenDoc gets cut.** Engineers worked hard on OpenDoc, but it did not fit the strategy and the industry would not adopt it, so he killed it. Lesson: effort does not override fit (S004).
15. **Paul Rand's one logo.** Asked for options for the NeXT logo, Rand said he would solve the problem once and Jobs could take it or leave it. Jobs found that clarity refreshing. Lesson: a real professional sets the terms (S033).
16. **Avi Tevanian and the password prompt.** Tevanian insisted OS X ask for authentication, and Jobs fought him for a year over ease of use before giving in. It became the key security decision in OS X. Lesson: the inconvenient person may be the one who is right (S028).
17. **The LaserWriter's first page.** He held the first printout and knew they could sell it, because the value showed itself without explanation. Lesson: a great product proves itself at a glance (S004).
18. **Attrition from 33 to 15 percent.** On his return a third of staff left each year. Ten months later it was below the Valley average, driven by a visible path to winning and options tied to it. Lesson: people stay when they can see the company matter again (S023, S040).
19. **The Apple II floppy drive.** Apple shipped a cheap, reliable floppy years before rivals, which is why VisiCalc was written for the Apple II. Lesson: early product choices compound (S010).
20. **Lunch with Noyce and Grove.** In his twenties he called Bob Noyce, Andy Grove, and others and asked to buy them lunch each quarter, and they said yes. Lesson: ask the people you want to learn from (S001).
