# Positions: Steve Jobs

39 topics, built from the verified batch notes across 38 sources (1983 to 2010). Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and the date he said it. Dates are the event date from the source title or url (talk, interview, or memo), not the upload date in `sources.md`. S001 is an archive collection spanning his career, so its lines carry the date the Archive gives for the original talk, interview, or speech. Jobs died in October 2011: his latest stated view is treated as current, and nothing here covers events after 2011.

## Focus and the product line
- **Stance:** Focus is killing good ideas, not picking one. Fewer products, the A team on every one.
- **Over time:**
  - 1997-05: "Focusing is about saying no. Focussing is about saying no. And you've got to say no, no, no. And when you say no, you piss off people." (S004)
  - 1998: cut "scads of products" to four platforms, consumer and pro, desktop and portable (S021, S023).
  - 2008-03 (latest): "It means saying no to the hundred other good ideas that there are. You have to pick carefully." (S012)
  - 1998-07 (Macworld): "if we only had four, we could put the A team on every single one of them." (S001)
- **Read:** the most consistent view he holds, across 1997 to 2008 (inferred from S001, S004, S012, S021, S023).

## Saying no to a whole market
- **Stance:** Turn down an attractive market if it would starve the bet that matters. "If we had gotten into it, we wouldn't have had the resources to do the iPod." (S012, 2008-03) on the PDA. In 2003 he gave the same reason: phones would absorb the PDA for most users (S029).

## What to leave out of a product
- **Stance:** Every product is a set of deliberate omissions, and you defend them. "things are packages of emphasis some things are emphasized in a product some things are not done as well in a product some things are chosen not to be done at all in a product." (S040, 2010-06) Said of leaving Flash off the iPad (S037, 2010-04).

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Price for reach and keep driving it down; a low price that customers trust beats a high one they resent.
- **Over time:**
  - 1995: post-1985 Apple was wrong to chase margin; "What they should have been doing was making reasonable profits and going for market share, which was what we always tried to do." (S006)
  - 1998-08: value stated against the whole market: "the iMac is faster the iMac at 1299 dollars is faster than the fastest pc you can buy for any amount of money" (S035)
  - 2004 (latest): "we're not happy with iPods costing you know $3 and $400 and we want to keep driving the prices down on them" (S030)
- **Read:** this sits uneasily with Apple's premium prices. He argued value and share, not cheapness (inferred from S006, S030, S035).

## Raising prices on existing customers
- **Stance:** A price customers accepted is a promise. "If we go back now and we raise prices... we will be violating that implicit deal." (S013, 2006-10) On music tracks: "we don't think they're going to pay more than 99 cents." (S030, 2004)

## Discounting
- **No recorded position** on discounts or promotions as a sales tactic. Nearest: hold price integrity (S013, 2006-10) and argue value against the best rival at any price (S035, 1998-08).

## Subscriptions
- **Stance:** Skeptical unless the customer wants one and the price is locked.
- **Over time:**
  - 2003: "The subscription model of buying music is bankrupt." (S016)
  - 2005 (latest): softer, not religious about it, but "if somebody could come out and say...it's five bucks a month and it's going to be five bucks a month for the next 10 years...then that might be more compelling." (S028)

## Free versions
- **Stance:** At NeXT in 1996 he used a free tier to win adoption: "we're giving away free versions of a product that is surprisingly powerful and then trying to sell even more powerful versions on top of that" (S034, 1996). One source only, from his software-company years.

## Raising money and investors
- **No recorded position** on venture rounds, valuation, or dilution. Nearest: "If you talk to great venture capitalists, they'll tell you that they invest in people, not ideas." (S009, 2007-05) He funded Pixar himself for about ten years and over $50 million before the IPO paid off (S032, 1996).

## Debt
- **No recorded position.**

## Spending in a downturn
- **Stance:** Raise R&D and keep people when others cut. "we were going to up our R&D budget so that we would be ahead of our competitors when the downturn was over. And that's exactly what we did. And it worked." (S012, 2008-03)

## Hiring
- **Stance:** Hire only A players, judge by results, and wait as long as it takes.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1995: "I've built a lot of my success off finding these truly gifted people and not settling for B and C players, but really going for the A players." (S005)
  - early 1990s: "You know you're going to be settling for second best if you compromise." (S027) He chased one COO for a year and a half.
  - 1990s (In the Company of Giants interview): "I look at great results and I find out who was responsible for them." (S001)
  - 2008-03 (latest, senior hires): "Are they going to fall in love with Apple? Because if they fall in love with Apple, everything else will take care of itself." (S012)

## Managing smart people
- **Stance:** Argue ideas, do not give orders. "if you want to hire and keep really bright people, then you can't tell them what to do." (S009, 2007-05) 1996 (Terry Gross interview): "We hired people to tell us what to do." (S001)

## Firing and layoffs
- **Stance:** Removing people who fall short is the job; do it, and do it humanely. "I've always tried to do it in a humane way. But nonetheless it has to be done and it is never fun." (S006, 1995)
- **Later:** more aware of the cost: "the person you're laying off has got to go home and tell their family they don't have a job." (S029, 2003) In 2008 he said Apple would not lay off people it worked hard to hire in a recession (S012).

## Equity and stock options
- **Stance:** Options keep people only if they are worth something. In 1997 he repriced underwater options and pushed out board members who resisted (S011, 1997-08). Attrition fell from 33 to 15 percent in ten months (S023, 1998-05).

## Partners
- **Stance:** Partner for what you do not do well, but keep partnerships few.
- **Evidence:** 1988-10: "partnerships take time. So you can't have very many of them." (S019) 2007-05: "We don't think one company can do everything. So you've got to partner with people that are really good at stuff." (S008)

## Own the core technology
- **Stance:** Do not enter a business where someone else controls the key technology. "we didn't want to get into any business where we didn't own or control the primary technology because you'll get your head handed to you." (S012, 2008-03)

## Hardware, software, and integration
- **Stance:** Control the whole experience; software is the real moat.
- **Over time:**
  - 1995: NeXT should have gone software-only sooner: "I think we should have taken a bit longer to realize the world was changing and just gone on to be a software company right off the bat." (S006)
  - 2007-05 (latest): "Apple views itself as a software company." (S008) and the iPhone's "software is at least five years ahead of anything we've seen out there." (S009)

## Market research and customer input
- **Stance:** Research cannot invent the product; build what you want and trust taste.
- **Over time:** 1990: "Very rarely can your customers predict something that they don't even quite know they want yet." (S010) 2008-03 (latest): "We do no market research. We don't hire consultants." (S012)
- **Caveat:** he still started from the customer: "you've got to start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology." (S004, 1997-05)

## Niche and who the customer is
- **Stance:** Own one segment before spreading out, and know exactly who buys. In 1991 he argued 50 percent of one fast-growing segment beats 5 percent of everything (S026). Apple's DNA in 2008: "Our DNA is as a consumer company" (S012).

## Competition
- **Stance:** Be far better, not a bit better, and drop zero-sum framing.
- **Evidence:** 1994: "The reward has to be like three or four or five times better to take the risk to jump out of the mainstream." (S015) 1996 (Terry Gross interview): "Apple has to be 50 percent or 100 percent better" (S001) 2007-05: "The era of setting this up as a competition between Apple and Microsoft is over, as far as I'm concerned." (S008)

## Marketing, ads, and press
- **Stance:** The product is the marketing; a weak business cannot advertise its way back.
- **Evidence:** 1997-05: "the high order bit of any marketing campaign is profitability" (S004) 1998-08: "all great marketing starts with a great product and we think we've got one here" (S035)

## Brand
- **Stance:** A brand is a latent asset to use, not buy. "You could spend billions of dollars building a brand not as good as Apple. Yet Apple hasn't been doing anything with this incredible asset." (S011, 1997-08)

## Content marketing
- **No recorded position.**

## Design and quality
- **Stance:** Great design costs time, not money, and covers the parts nobody sees.
- **Evidence:** 1983-06: "we have a shot at putting a great object there, or if we don't, we're going to put one more piece-of-junk object there." (S041) 1985-02: "you're not going to use a piece of plywood on the back, even though it faces the wall and nobody will ever see it." (S007) 1984-05 (Michael Moritz interview): making something great rarely takes more money; "All it takes is a little more time." (S001)

## Stopping to fix a broken product
- **Stance:** Halt production when the core is wrong, even late. Pixar stopped Toy Story for months to fix the story (S012, 2008-03; S028, 2005), and he scrapped the iPhone enclosure before launch (S012).

## Distribution and retail
- **Stance:** If the channel cannot explain your product, build your own. Retail staff turned over too fast to carry product knowledge, so Apple built stores (S030, 2004).

## Consultants and advisers
- **Stance:** Advice without ownership is worth a fraction. "Coming in and making recommendations and not owning the results, not owning the implementation, I think is a fraction of the value" (S027, early 1990s)

## Culture
- **Stance:** Process is not the product. "IBM has the best process people in the world. They just forgot about the content." (S005, 1995) People on the Mac team were "more like artists than they are engineers." (S020, 1984-01)

## Metrics and forecasts
- **Stance:** Measure the customer moment, not market share guesses. "I've given up predicting stuff like that. We're just trying as hard as we can, and the vector's going in the right direction." (S028, 2005)

## Timing and technology windows
- **Stance:** Windows open when technologies converge and close fast. "Enough technology, usually from fairly diverse places, comes together, and makes something that's a quantum leap forward possible." (S027, early 1990s) 1996-02: "There's a window now that will close." (S038)

## Tablets (changed view)
- **2003:** "we look at the tablet and... we think it's going to fail." (S029) His objection was handwriting input.
- **2010 (latest):** Apple shipped the iPad on a touch interface, and he defended its omissions (S040, 2010-06). The objection was the input method, not the form (inferred from S029, S040).

## Technology in education (changed view)
- **1985:** hoped computers would let a student "asked Aristotle a question and get an answer." (S031)
- **1996-02 (latest):** "the problem is not one that technology can hope to solve." (S038) He blamed unions and bureaucracy (S006, 1995).

## Money and motive
- **Stance:** Money funds long bets; it is not the goal. "the goal is not to be the richest man in the cemetery." (S015, 1994) "I never did it for the money." (S005, 1995)

## Work hours and burnout
- **No recorded position** on hours or rest. Nearest: love the work or you will quit: "it's so hard that if you don't any rational person would give up." (S008, 2007-05)

## Remote work
- **No recorded position.**

## Selling the company
- **No recorded position.** Nearest: Pixar framed as films that last "60 or 100 years" rather than 18-month products (S032, 1996).

## AI
- **No recorded position** on AI as a business tool; he died in 2011. Nearest: in 1983 he said voice recognition was the better part of a decade away because understanding language is harder than hearing it (S041). Do not extend this to later AI.

## Succession
- **Stance:** "My job is to make the whole executive team good enough to be successors, so that's what I try to do." (S012, 2008-03)
