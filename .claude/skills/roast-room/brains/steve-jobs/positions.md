# Positions: Steve Jobs

40 topics, built from the verified notes of all 17 batches across 88 used sources of 89 listed (1983 to 2011). Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and the date he said it. Dates are the event date from the source title, url, or content (talk, keynote, interview, or memo), not the upload date in `sources.md`. S001 is an archive collection spanning his career, so its lines carry the date the archive gives for the original item where known. Keynote dates (S042 to S081) come from the event named in the title. Jobs died in October 2011: his latest stated view is treated as current, and nothing here covers events after 2011.

## Focus and the product line
- **Stance:** Focus is killing good ideas, not picking one. Fewer products, the A team on every one, and the whole line turned fast.
- **Over time:**
  - 1983-06: bet the company on one product with no backup: "If Lisa had bombed, Apple would be just one more computer company." (S041)
  - 1997-05: "Focusing is about saying no. Focussing is about saying no. And you've got to say no, no, no. And when you say no, you piss off people." (S004)
  - 1998: cut "scads of products" to four platforms, consumer and pro, desktop and portable (S021, S023, S044). 1998-07 (Macworld): "if we only had four, we could put the A team on every single one of them." (S001)
  - 1999-10: the grid was full only when the iBook landed, and the whole line was turned in about 148 days (S071).
  - 2008-03 (latest): "It means saying no to the hundred other good ideas that there are. You have to pick carefully." (S012)
- **Read:** the most consistent view he holds, across 1983 to 2008 (inferred from S001, S004, S012, S021, S023, S044, S071).
- **Execution versus strategy:**
  - **Stance:** Great execution on the wrong plan is waste; fix the plan first. "Apple is executing wonderfully on many of the wrong things. The ability of the organization to execute is really high, though." (S042, 1997-08) In 1998 he said Apple had flipped its goals to profit first, product second, and it took five years to show (S045).

## Saying no to a whole market
- **Stance:** Turn down an attractive market if it would starve the bet that matters. "If we had gotten into it, we wouldn't have had the resources to do the iPod." (S012, 2008-03) on the PDA. In 2003 he gave the same reason: phones would absorb the PDA for most users (S029). In 1994 he warned PDA makers they were building for consumers when the only early buyers were mobile professionals (S015).
- **Also:** at Pixar in 1996 he pulled 25 animators off commercials: "great people are hard to find, and we couldn't afford to have 25 of them making commercials anymore." (S066)

## What to leave out of a product
- **Stance:** Every product is a set of deliberate omissions, and you defend them. "things are packages of emphasis some things are emphasized in a product some things are not done as well in a product some things are chosen not to be done at all in a product." (S040, 2010-06) Said of leaving Flash off the iPad: "Flash was designed for PCs using mice, not for touch screens using fingers." (S037, 2010-04)
- **Also:** the MacBook Air dropped the optical drive because wireless replacements were better (S059, 2008-01). Unibody cut almost 60 percent of major structural parts (S062, 2008-10).

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Price for reach and keep driving it down; price is a design target set with the technical goals, not an afterthought.
- **Over time:**
  - 1995: post-1985 Apple was wrong to chase margin; "What they should have been doing was making reasonable profits and going for market share, which was what we always tried to do." (S006) He repeated it in 2000 about Macs pushed to $2,499 and up (S090).
  - 1998-08: value stated against the whole market: "the iMac is faster the iMac at 1299 dollars is faster than the fastest pc you can buy for any amount of money" (S035)
  - 1999-07: efficiency funds lower prices: "the more efficient we are the more we can save money and pass it on in terms of lower prices of our products" (S046)
  - 2004: "we're not happy with iPods costing you know $3 and $400 and we want to keep driving the prices down on them" (S030)
  - 2008 to 2010 (latest): iPhone cut from $599 to $399 ten weeks after launch (S058, 2007-09), iPhone 3G at $199 (S061, 2008-06), and the iPad staged against the pundits' $999: "the iPad pricing starts not at $999, but at just $499." (S063, 2010-01)
- **Read:** this sits uneasily with Apple's premium prices. He argued value and share against the best rival, not cheapness (inferred from S006, S030, S035, S063, S090).

## Raising prices on existing customers
- **Stance:** A price customers accepted is a promise. "If we go back now and we raise prices... we will be violating that implicit deal." (S013, 2006-10) On music tracks: "we don't think they're going to pay more than 99 cents." (S030, 2004)

## Packaging, versions, and paid upgrades
- **Stance:** One version, one price, everything in it. "we have just one version of leopard it's got everything in it and it cost $129 and we just couldn't be happier" (S057, 2007-06) In 2002 he framed a $129 OS as less than a dollar per major feature (S050).
- **Crippled versions and paid upgrades:**
  - **Stance:** Never throttle a product to sell the unlock. He showed MusicMatch slowing MP3 encoding to sell a paid upgrade as the thing iTunes would not do (S080, 2003-10). "iTunes gives us unrestricted encoding into MP3 it gives us unrestricted encoding quality it gives us unrestricted encoding speed" (S074, 2001)
  - **Early buyers:** do not punish people who bought first: "we absolutely don't want to penalize those pioneers. And so we're just pulling them right along with us at no additional costs." (S020, 1984-01)

## Discounting
- **No recorded position** on discounts or promotions as a sales tactic. Nearest: permanent price cuts announced as product news (S058, S061, S077) and price integrity on a promised price (S013, 2006-10). A price cut is a move to widen the market, not a sale (inferred from S058, S077).

## Subscriptions
- **Stance:** Skeptical unless the customer wants one and the price is locked.
- **Over time:**
  - 2003: "The subscription model of buying music is bankrupt." (S016) 2003-10: "people have told us over and over and over again they don't want to rent their music" (S080)
  - 2004-06: "people don't want to rent their music. They want to own it." (S081)
  - 2005 (latest on music): softer, not religious about it, but "if somebody could come out and say...it's five bucks a month and it's going to be five bucks a month for the next 10 years...then that might be more compelling." (S028)
  - 2008-01: for movies, which you watch once or twice, renting made sense (S059, S030). The test is how often the customer uses the thing, not a rule against recurring revenue (inferred from S030, S059, S081).

## Free versions and platform economics
- **Stance:** Free is a tool for reach, paid for by something else. 1996: "we're giving away free versions of a product that is surprisingly powerful and then trying to sell even more powerful versions on top of that" (S034) 2000-01: free QuickTime and iTools paid back through hardware and partnerships, not upsell (S047). 2008-03: free apps cost the developer and Apple nothing (S060).
- **Limit:** in 2002 he moved free iTools to paid .Mac, saying "the world is really different and we're going to have to reflect that." (S077)
- **Platform economics and app stores:**
  - **Stance:** Line up your interest with the developer's and cover the costs yourself. "the developer gets seventy percent of the revenues right off the top we keep 30 to pay for running the app store" (S060, 2008-03) But curate: "there will be some apps that we're going to say no to" (S060)

## Raising money and investors
- **No recorded position** on venture rounds, valuation, or dilution. Nearest:
  - 2007-05: "If you talk to great venture capitalists, they'll tell you that they invest in people, not ideas." (S009)
  - 2010-07: "we want investors that are in Apple for the long haul and that are investing in Apple because of the character of the company, not the piece of news that just crossed the wire." (S083)
  - He funded Pixar himself for about ten years and over $50 million before the IPO paid off (S032, 1996).

## Cash, burn, and survival
- **Stance:** In 1986 he put $7 million of his own money into NeXT to carry it to launch, and the burn rate made it look short (S069, 1986, narrator). At that NeXT retreat the team framed the real fight as survival: not running out of money before the product ships. The film does not show who said it, so treat it as the room's view, not his words (inferred from S069). In 1997 Apple had about 90 days of cash left when he returned (S040, 2010-06).

## Debt
- **No recorded position.**

## Spending in a downturn
- **Stance:** Raise R&D and keep shipping when others cut.
- **Over time:** 2002-01: "our strategy can be expressed in one word. And that's to innovate." (S049) 2002-07: argued against despair and for being stronger at the recovery (S077). 2008-03 (latest): "we were going to up our R&D budget so that we would be ahead of our competitors when the downturn was over. And that's exactly what we did. And it worked." (S012)

## Hiring
- **Stance:** Hire only A players, judge by results, and wait as long as it takes.
- **Evidence:**
  - 1983-06: some people run circles around five merely good ones; those are the only ones worth hiring (S041).
  - 1995: "I've built a lot of my success off finding these truly gifted people and not settling for B and C players, but really going for the A players." (S005) 1998: Woz was 50 times better than an average engineer (S045).
  - early 1990s: "You know you're going to be settling for second best if you compromise." (S027) He chased one COO for a year and a half.
  - 1996: at Pixar, growing fast without lowering the bar was the hardest job (S066).
  - 2008-03 (latest, senior hires): "Are they going to fall in love with Apple? Because if they fall in love with Apple, everything else will take care of itself." (S012)

## Managing smart people
- **Stance:** Argue ideas, do not give orders; the best idea wins, not the title.
- **Evidence:** 1996 (Fresh Air): "We hired people to tell us what to do." (S001) 1996 (Pixar): "the hierarchy of power inverts. And the CEO is actually at the bottom." (S066) 2007-05: "if you want to hire and keep really bright people, then you can't tell them what to do." (S009) 2010-07: "the best ideas win here, not the highest person in the org chart." (S083)

## Firing and layoffs
- **Stance:** Removing people who fall short is the job; do it, and do it humanely.
- **Over time:** 1995: "I've always tried to do it in a humane way. But nonetheless it has to be done and it is never fun." (S006) 1998: on return he cleared the burnt-out "barnacles," mostly in upper management, so the talent under them could work (S045).
- **Later:** more aware of the cost: "the person you're laying off has got to go home and tell their family they don't have a job." (S029, 2003) In 2008 he said Apple would not lay off people it worked hard to hire in a recession (S012).

## Equity and pay
- **Stance:** Pay in stock everyone shares, not cash bonuses for a few.
- **Evidence:** 1989-04: NeXT's third goal was that its people share in its success (S088). 1996: Pixar chose stock options over Hollywood contracts so everyone shared one goal (S066). 1997: he repriced underwater options and pushed out board members who resisted (S011, 1997-08); attrition fell from 33 to 15 percent in ten months (S023, 1998-05). 2000-01: senior managers should get stock, not cash bonuses (S090).

## Partners
- **Stance:** Partner for what you do not do well, but keep partnerships few and real.
- **Evidence:** 1988-10: "partnerships take time. So you can't have very many of them." (S019) 1997-08: Apple has "to forge some meaningful partnerships, not just partnerships and press releases." (S042) 2007-05: "We don't think one company can do everything. So you've got to partner with people that are really good at stuff." (S008) He gave partners public credit on stage (S059, S051).

## Own the core technology
- **Stance:** Do not enter a business where someone else controls the key technology. "we didn't want to get into any business where we didn't own or control the primary technology because you'll get your head handed to you." (S012, 2008-03)
- **Earlier:** 1991-08: "the way to make your machines unique is with unique software." (S086) He held that US firms lost by not owning displays and memory (S024, 1991-07).
- **Open standards:**
  - **Stance:** Back open standards where nobody can own the layer, and own the layer above. 2002-07: "no one is going to own rendezvous" (S077) 2010-04: "Adobe's Flash products are 100% proprietary." (S037) He still kept Apple's own stack closed (inferred from S012, S037, S077).

## Hardware, software, and integration
- **Stance:** Control the whole experience; software is the real moat.
- **Over time:**
  - 1985-09: "Software is what will distinguish products in the next 10 years." (S082)
  - 1995: NeXT should have gone software-only sooner: "I think we should have taken a bit longer to realize the world was changing and just gone on to be a software company right off the bat." (S006)
  - 2007-05: "Apple views itself as a software company." (S008) and the iPhone's "software is at least five years ahead of anything we've seen out there." (S009)
  - 2011-06 (latest): "if the if the hardware is the the brain and the sinew of our products, the software in them is their soul." (S065)

## Market research and customer input
- **Stance:** Research cannot invent the product; build what you want and trust taste, but stay close to the customer's experience.
- **Over time:**
  - 1989-04: "You can't just ask customers what they want and then try to give that to them. By the time you get it built, they'll want something new." (S088)
  - 1990: "Very rarely can your customers predict something that they don't even quite know they want yet." (S010)
  - 2008-03 (latest): "We do no market research. We don't hire consultants." (S012)
- **Caveat:** he still started from the customer: "you've got to start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology." (S004, 1997-05) In 1985 he conceded he had been accused of not listening to customers enough and that some of it was valid (S082). He did use buyer data after launch: 28 percent of iBook buyers were new to Apple (S047, 2000-01).

## Niche and who the customer is
- **Stance:** Own one segment before spreading out, and know exactly who buys.
- **Evidence:** 1988-10: NeXT served higher education first (S019). 1991: 50 percent of one fast-growing segment beats 5 percent of everything (S026). 2000-01: "A lot of people can't get past the fact that we're not going after the enterprise market." (S090) 2008-03: "Our DNA is as a consumer company" (S012)
- **Limit:** in 2010 he said Apple is not good at selling through large enterprise organizations (S040).

## Competition
- **Stance:** Be far better, measure against the best rival in public, and drop zero-sum framing.
- **Evidence:** 1997-08: Apple plus Microsoft was the whole desktop market, so a deal beat a war (S042). At the same event: "The era of setting this up as a competition between Apple and Microsoft is over, as far as I'm concerned." (S008, clip replayed at D5 in 2007) 1998-05: "we got to stop measuring ourselves relative to ourselves" (S044) 2001: real-app benchmarks over megahertz (S048, S074).
- **Movies are different:** at Pixar the rival is the work itself: "we're competing against can we make a great film that people love." (S032, 1996)
- **Cannibalizing your own hit:**
  - **Stance:** Replace your best product before a rival does, and make the new one plainly better on the same promise. He killed the iPod mini, Apple's best seller, with the nano (S054, 2005-09), and in 2010 replaced every iPod model at once despite dominant share (S064, 2010-09).
- **Is it better at anything?**
  - **Stance:** A new product needs a reason to exist: far better at a few key tasks, not cheaper or a new shape. "The problem is netbooks aren't better at anything. They They're slow. They have low-quality displays, and they run clunky old PC software." (S063, 2010-01)
  - **Earlier:** "The reward has to be like three or four or five times better to take the risk to jump out of the mainstream." (S015, 1994) 1996 (Terry Gross interview): "Apple has to be 50 percent or 100 percent better" (S001)

## Marketing, ads, and press
- **Stance:** The product is the marketing; a weak business cannot advertise its way back.
- **Evidence:** 1997-05: "the high order bit of any marketing campaign is profitability" (S004) and "on page 7 to 14, Apple is spending a million dollars saying we're back, but on page 1, a journalist writes an article saying they're in the tank, who are you going to believe?" (S004) 1998-08: "all great marketing starts with a great product and we think we've got one here" (S035) 2002-07: real switchers telling their own stories drew Windows users to the site (S077).
- **Announcing early and secrecy:**
  - **Stance:** Say nothing until it is nearly ready. "in general, we don't tell people about new products until they're just about ready to go." (S083, 2010-07) 2006-08: "we don't want our friends to start their photocopers any sooner than they have to" (S056)

## Brand
- **Stance:** A brand is a latent asset to use, not buy, and it cannot cover a missing product.
- **Evidence:** 1997-08: "You could spend billions of dollars building a brand not as good as Apple. Yet Apple hasn't been doing anything with this incredible asset." (S011) 1997-08 (Macworld): "We have one of the world's greatest brands and we haven't paid much attention to it in the last several years." (S042) 1998-05: with no good Mac under $2,000, the brand alone could not hold consumers (S044).

## Content marketing
- **No recorded position.**

## Design and quality
- **Stance:** Design is how it works, all the way down, including the parts nobody sees; it costs time and care, not money.
- **Evidence:** 1983-06: "we have a shot at putting a great object there, or if we don't, we're going to put one more piece-of-junk object there." (S041) 1984-05 (Michael Moritz interview): making something great rarely takes more money; "All it takes is a little more time." (S001) 1985-02: "you're not going to use a piece of plywood on the back, even though it faces the wall and nobody will ever see it." (S007) 1989-04: "doing it right the first time was going to be easier than having to go back and fix it." (S088)
- **Latest framing:** 2000-01: "Design is the fundamental soul of a man-made creation that ends up expressing itself in successive outer layers of the product or service." (S090) and customers pay Apple "to sweat all these details so it's easy and pleasant for them to use our computers." (S090) 2010-07: "we try to have our cake and eat it, too." (S083)

## Stopping to fix a broken product, and failures after launch
- **Stance:** Halt production when the core is wrong, even late; rebuild rather than patch.
- **Evidence:** Pixar stopped Toy Story for months to fix the story (S012, 2008-03; S028, 2005): "no amount of technology can turn a bad story into a good story" (S066, 1996), a line he credits to John Lasseter: "John's taught me that." (S066) He scrapped the iPhone enclosure before launch (S012). 2011-06: MobileMe was rebuilt from scratch as iCloud: "It wasn't our finest hour. Let me just say that, but we learned a lot." (S065)
- **When a shipped product fails customers:**
  - **Stance:** Get the data, own your part, and make the customer whole. "we're going to do whatever it takes to make them happy. And if we can't make them happy, we're going to give them a full refund." (S083, 2010-07) and "we were not just innocents in this." (S083) In 1997 he apologized for bad support lines he had called himself (S042).

## Distribution and retail
- **Stance:** If the channel cannot explain your product, build your own.
- **Evidence:** 1997-11: cut distributors from five to two (S043). 2001-07: stores built around asking a genius, not product shelves (S075). 2004: retail staff turned over too fast to carry product knowledge, so Apple built stores (S030). 2002-05: "it's the best buying experience that we know of in the world" (S050)

## Culture
- **Stance:** Culture is set by the work, not the handbook, and product people must stay in charge.
- **Evidence:** 1984-01: people on the Mac team were "more like artists than they are engineers." (S020) 1989-04: "you don't say it. You don't put it in an employee handbook. That stuff is meaningless. All that counts is the product that results from the work of the group." (S088) 1995: "IBM has the best process people in the world. They just forgot about the content." (S005) 2004-10: when the sales guy runs a tech company, product decays: "Right, the sales guy. Case closed." (S091)
- **Consultants and advisers:**
  - **Stance:** Advice without ownership is worth a fraction. "Coming in and making recommendations and not owning the results, not owning the implementation, I think is a fraction of the value" (S027, early 1990s)

## Metrics and forecasts
- **Stance:** Manage the inputs that make great products and the numbers follow; do not forecast share.
- **Evidence:** 1989-04: a maxim passed on to him ("Somebody once told me"), which he adopted: "Manage the top line, and the bottom line will follow." (S088) In 1998 he credited it to "one of the wisest business people I've ever met" (S045). 2005: "I've given up predicting stuff like that. We're just trying as hard as we can, and the vector's going in the right direction." (S028) 2008-06: he did cite satisfaction as proof: "90% customer satisfaction that's off the charts" (S061) 2010-01: on revenue, "I like to forget that cuz that's not how we think about Apple." (S063)

## Timing and technology windows
- **Stance:** Windows open when technologies converge and close fast; check the world has not moved before you copy your last win.
- **Evidence:** early 1990s: "Enough technology, usually from fairly diverse places, comes together, and makes something that's a quantum leap forward possible." (S027) 1986: the film's narrator says he feared that missing spring 1987 would cost NeXT a whole year (inferred from S069). 1996-02: "There's a window now that will close." (S038) 1996 (Fresh Air): on NeXT hardware, "the door to establish a new hardware company really had closed even before we started the company we just didn't know that at the time" (S067)
- **Platform transitions:**
  - **Stance:** Bold transitions keep a platform at the front; prepare them in secret and finish them fast.
  - **Evidence:** 2002-09: OS 9 booting dropped so engineering could focus on one OS (S078). 2005-06: "every release of Mac OS 10 has been compiled for both power PC and Intel this has been going on for the last 5 years just in case" (S053) 2006-01: whole line moved to Intel within the calendar year (S055).

## Tablets (changed view)
- **2003:** "we look at the tablet and... we think it's going to fail." (S029) His objection was handwriting input.
- **2010 (latest):** Apple shipped the iPad on a touch interface and rebuilt every app for it (S063, 2010-01), and he defended its omissions (S040, 2010-06). The objection was the input method, not the form (inferred from S029, S040, S063).

## Technology in education (changed view)
- **1985:** hoped computers would let a student "asked Aristotle a question and get an answer." (S031) He pushed to put a computer in every school (S041, S082).
- **1996-02 (latest):** "the problem is not one that technology can hope to solve." (S038) He blamed unions and bureaucracy (S006, 1995; S045, 1998).

## Money and motive
- **Stance:** Money funds long bets; it is not the goal.
- **Evidence:** 1984-10: "The journey is the reward." (S092) 1994: "the goal is not to be the richest man in the cemetery." (S015) 1995: "I never did it for the money." (S005) 1998-11: "the great companies, well, that's not what they're about." (S089)

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** Do not sell out early; the hard years are the point. 2000-01: "when these people sell out, even though they get fabulously rich, they're gypping themselves out of one of the potentially most rewarding experiences of their unfolding lives." (S090) He framed Pixar as films that can live "60 or even a hundred years" rather than 18-month products (S032, 1996). One source on the founder exit itself.

## Work hours and burnout
- **No recorded position** on hours or rest. Nearest: love the work or you will quit: "it's so hard that if you don't any rational person would give up." (S008, 2007-05) In 1985 he described going to "Siberia" at Apple and deteriorating without a mission (S082).

## Remote work
- **No recorded position.** Nearest: he designed one campus to hold everyone, "a little like a spaceship landed." (S084, 2011-06)

## AI
- **No recorded position** on AI as a business tool; he died in 2011. Nearest: in 1983 he said voice recognition was the better part of a decade away because understanding language is harder than hearing it (S041). In 1984 he expected computers to move from tools to agents that anticipate what you want (inferred from S092). Do not extend this to later AI.

## Succession
- **Stance:** "My job is to make the whole executive team good enough to be successors, so that's what I try to do." (S012, 2008-03) In 1985 he said the people who invent an industry are not always the ones to run it at scale (S082).
