# Stories: Sara Blakely

Built from the batch 01 to 09 notes over 54 usable sources of 60 fetched (about 331,000 words; see `sources.md`), 52 of them with notes of their own. 23 stories and cases she reuses, with the lesson she draws. Source ids in parentheses; most-told first. Where her numbers or details drift between retellings, the entry says so.

1. **The wrong movie.** Seven years into selling fax machines door to door, after a bad day, she pulled off the road, wrote that sales was her one strength, then asked why she was good at it, and wrote the goal of inventing a product she could sell to millions. The idea took two years to arrive: she cut the feet off control-top pantyhose to wear under cream pants. "I'm in the wrong movie like call the director call the writer cut this is not my life" (S007) Lesson: decide the outcome and the why before you know the how, and ask the second question, or you just take another sales job (S001, S004, S007, S014, S015, S029, S032, S045, S053, S054).

2. **The Neiman Marcus bathroom pitch.** She found the buyer's number, kept calling, flew to Dallas with the prototype in a Ziploc bag, a color-copy package, and her lucky red backpack, and when the buyer drifted she took her to the restroom for a live before and after. "I could tell I was losing her. So I said, you know what, Diane? Will you come with me to the bathroom?" (S031) The buyer ordered for seven stores. Lesson: when a product is best proven live, go in person, read the nonverbals, and show it (S001, S007, S013, S022, S023, S031, S040, S053).

3. **Business is war.** Early on, men at a party told her to get ready because business is war. She went home, sat on the floor, cried, and chose a different way: intuition, humor, kindness, and the consumer. "I don't want to go to war. Why does it have to be war? What if there's a different way?" (S033) She later said the same to Kevin O'Leary on a panel while shaking (S037). Lesson: the premises everyone accepts are not laws. The detail drifts: three men (S002, S007, S011) or two (S003, S021, S045) (S002, S003, S007, S011, S021, S033, S037, S045).

4. **Spanx days: a sales force off the payroll.** For about two years after the first orders and Oprah she stood in Neiman, Saks, Nordstrom, and Bloomingdale's from 8:30 am to 7 pm, ran morning rallies, gave free product to associates in shoes, cosmetics, and couture, and drove the California coast store by store. "I was building an entire salesforce that wasn't on my payroll." (S054) Lesson: the order is the start; the founder makes it sell through (S002, S004, S007, S022, S041, S054).

5. **The mill owner's daughters.** Every hosiery mill, all run by men, said no by phone and then in person. "They always asked me the same three questions. They'd say, Sara Blakely, and you're financially backed by, and I'd be like Sara Blakely." (S031) Weeks later one owner called back because his daughters liked the idea. Lesson: the yes came from someone with a stake in the problem, moved by her enthusiasm. The detail drifts: three daughters (S001, S008) or two (S016); she names him Sam Kaplan (S016) and places him near Charlotte (S035) (S001, S002, S008, S016, S018, S031, S035).

6. **Writing her own patent.** Lawyers quoted more than her savings, so she bought a patents and trademarks book at Barnes & Noble, wrote it herself, had her mother draw the figure, and paid a lawyer only for the claims. He later admitted he thought she was a hidden-camera prank. A mill worker said the yarn was 30% "lacquer"; she nearly filed it before learning he meant Lycra. Lesson: do the legwork, buy only the expert piece, and check every detail. The numbers drift: $3,000 to $5,000 (S002), $2,000 to $5,000 (S013, S039), and $750 for the claims (S007) (S002, S007, S013, S016, S035, S039).

7. **Oprah, the sign and the fake staff meeting.** Close to giving up, she caught Oprah on TV showing she cut the feet out of her own pantyhose: "for me that was my sign I was like I'm going for it" (S013). She sent Oprah a gift basket; it reached her wardrobe and Spanx became a favorite product. When the crew asked to film headquarters and a staff meeting, she had an apartment and no staff, so friends sat in a circle on her floor. Lesson: chase free exposure, then fake the polish and do the real work. The Oprah pick came as she was running out of friends and money (S023) (S001, S013, S018, S023, S039, S041, S054).

8. **Paying friends to buy it.** After the seven-store order she called friends in each city, mailed them checks, and gave them a line to say at the counter, and later had friends pose as curious shoppers at her table. "I was Shameless it was like hi do you remember me in fourth grade" (S023) The buyer called a week later to say it was selling out. Lesson: early sell-through is the founder's job, or the buyer gives no second chance (S002, S023, S031, S053).

9. **Father's failure dinners.** Her father asked each week what she had failed at, and was disappointed if she had nothing. "my dad actually encouraged my brother and me to fail" (S029) Lesson: failure means not trying, which is what let her make cold calls, bathroom pitches, and live TV mistakes (S001, S007, S012, S016, S020, S029, S030, S033).

10. **Getting out of the corner.** Spanx sat in the quiet hosiery corner. She bought office-supply stands at Target and set them at every Neiman register without approval, and once taped a bin to a counter and was caught on store video. By the time anyone noticed, it sold well enough that she was told to keep going. "nobody puts Baby in the corner." (S004) Lesson: when placement is killing you, act first and let the numbers make the case (S004, S016, S018).

11. **Naming Spanx.** After a year of bad names, she wanted the hard K sound of Kodak and Coca-Cola, which comedians say gets laughs, and swapped the letters for an X because made-up words trademark and sell better. The runner-up was "Open-toed Delilah's." Lesson: the name is part of the product (S002, S013, S016, S035, S038).

12. **The knockoff ad campaign.** A few months after a dinner with a big company, it launched a lookalike product with an $8 million campaign. She thought she was finished. Instead the ads taught the market and buyers came back to the better product. "they launched an 8 million ad campaign for a very similar item with a girl that looked just like me" (S022) Lesson: a superior product can ride a rival's ad budget, and a patent is only worth the fight you will fund (S013, S020, S022).

13. **QVC at full price.** People warned that QVC would kill a Neiman brand and that everyone discounts there; she went on as her own free ad and held price. "I get on QVC I sell 10,000 pair in six minutes" (S006) In the green room the GoPro founder sold almost nothing. Lesson: find the channel that scales without you standing there, and a channel miss does not prove the product bad (S006, S021).

14. **Sneex: four years, then a sneaker cut in half.** She spent four years with Spanx bra designers on a comfortable stiletto, got it more comfortable but not exponentially so, and stopped through tears. Saying out loud "I just want my high heel to feel like my sneaker" (S045), she cut a sneaker and a heel in half and glued them. Two factories fired her; a Spanish owner who had gone to Florida State took her on. She went direct first and cites repeat buying as proof. Lesson: if it is not 10x better, stop and reinvent; the industry's men still had never worn the product (S004, S006, S009, S019, S021, S038, S044, S045, S059, S060). In a 2025 written Q&A she tells it the same way: "I set out to create the world’s most comfortable stiletto. I did not set out to create a sneaker heel." (S059) Context, not her view: at launch Vogue reported harsh online reaction and its tester found the shoes less comfortable than a sneaker (S060).

15. **Postponing the Sneex launch a year.** Days before launch, with inventory ready and press done, she woke up knowing it was not time and told her small team she meant a year, not a week. She launched a year later when the team said they believed in it, and her children told her "no risk, no story." Lesson: check you are launching for the right reason; timing is a creative decision too (S003, S004, S037).

16. **Oops meetings.** She stands before the company, names her own mistakes for the quarter with a theme song, and invites others to follow. Her own oopses: chasing wholesale buyers over the end consumer ("I want you to want me") and staying in shapewear too long ("Mr. Roboto"). "we stand up and we share what we failed at or an oops or a mistake that we made and everybody claps" (S053) Lesson: leaders go first so the team is not afraid to fail (S017, S020, S053, S054).

17. **9/11, 2020, and the red backpack.** Near launch, after 9/11, advisors told her to stop working empty stores; she kept going and built her deepest ties with associates. In 2020 she ran people, costs, inventory, and sales in three-week plans, and gave 1,000 women $5,000 each, her own starting sum, with a red backpack like the one she carried to Neiman. Lesson: in a crisis keep selling, protect people and cash, and give back at the scale you started (S010, S014, S020, S025, S032, S041, S042, S049).

18. **The wrong leader.** In some stretches she drifted from product into legal and HR while someone else ran the company, came home drained, and burned out, then returned to her lane and to weekly fit sessions. "I stopped honoring my lane in the business and I had the wrong leader in the business and I lost my way a little bit during that time." (S007) Lesson: protect the one or two things only you do best (S007, S009, S019, S041).

19. **Laminated butt photos.** Before she could afford models or ads, she took before and after photos of her own butt in white pants, laminated them at Kinko's, and showed them to shoppers in Neiman all day. "holding a laminated picture of my butt not looking great with a big old panty line" (S006) Lesson: raw personal proof builds trust faster than polish (S006, S015, S020).

20. **The crotch crisis.** Right after landing Neiman she learned the mill had no free machine for gussets. She searched the Yellow Pages, had pieces shipped in, and found a supplier near Atlanta in time. "When you're the driver of your thoughts, crisis, or in my case, crotches, don't derail you. They push you." (S024) Lesson: the big win exposes small problems you never imagined; solve them fast (S001, S024).

21. **Asking why on the factory floor.** She asked why every size got the same waistband and why a rubber cord sat inside each one, and was told it cut cost. She gave each size its own waistband, and later remade the men's undershirt, which had not changed since 1918. Lesson: an outsider's crazy question breaks an industry's autopilot (S001, S002, S018, S022).

22. **Selling the majority of Spanx.** After 21 years as sole owner, with 80% of her mind on Spanx, she asked for a specific sign, sold a majority to Blackstone in 2021, and kept a stake and the chair. She went into grief she could not separate from perimenopause until friends staged an intervention. One of three quotes she collects to describe that time: "you can make the right decision and still be sad." (S003) Lesson: sell for freedom, give up control fully, and expect grief (S003, S004, S005, S009, S040).

23. **Caring the most on the factory floor.** Standing among men who tested her product on plastic forms, with $5,000 and no experience, she decided her one edge would be caring the most about how women feel. "i don't have the most money at five grand i don't have the most experience none but i care the most and let me see what happens if i care the most" (S057) Lesson: when you are outgunned on money and experience, out-care everyone; she says 20 years on the team she hired cares the most too (S057). Related: asking why on the factory floor (21).
