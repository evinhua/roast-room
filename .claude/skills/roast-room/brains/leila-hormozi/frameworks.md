# Frameworks: Leila Hormozi

Built from the 107 sources in sources.md (about 913,000 words: her channel, long interviews, live Q&As, workshops, and Acquisition.com vlogs, dated 2021 to 2026 where a date is known; 59 are undated). 25 frameworks, most-used first. The Sources line counts distinct sources where the idea is taught or applied, across all notes (ids listed). She rarely gives her frameworks formal names, so descriptive short names are marked *(unnamed)*; names without that mark are her own words. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from her stated thresholds, not quotes.

## 1. Reinforcement over punishment (latency over intensity)
- **What it is:** Both punishment and positive reinforcement change behavior. Punishment is faster to start but breeds secrecy, anxiety, resentment, minimum effort, and compliance only while the boss is watching. Reinforcement starts slower and lasts. How fast you respond to a behavior matters more than how big the response is.
- **Parts:** Reinforce the exact behavior right after it happens (a Slack message the moment the work lands beats a year-end bonus). Over-index on rewarding what you want more of; most unwanted behavior then fades on its own. Four kinds of recognition: outcome, effort, milestone, value. The recognition scrambler: pick one *when*, one *who* (manager, company, peers), one *what* (formal or spontaneous), one *how* (written, verbal, reward). Verbal is the most underused. Start with one kind and do it consistently. Formalize it past about five people: what you did naturally when small you must do on purpose when big. Keep negative consequences for immoral, unethical, or illegal acts. Her test of culture: what people do when the leader is not present.
- **When to use:** A team that hides mistakes, a manager who yells, a culture going punitive under one leader, a plan to fix performance with threats, or a company that keeps adding headcount to get the same output.
- **How to score:** 5 if managers recognize specific behavior within a day and punishment is kept for ethics; 3 if recognition is formal and rare, or lives only in the founder's head; 1 if fear is the main tool. Overstaffing is her warning sign of a punishing culture (S074). If one manager made the culture punitive, the fix is that manager changing fast, or leaving (S074).
- **Limits she names:** Punishment does work in organizations that can replace people endlessly (she cites Goldman Sachs and Coinbase); she argues it carries long-term costs, not that it fails (S035, S080). She says she was "all carrots" early and had to build the harder side of feedback on purpose (S005), and admits "i will recognize someone in my head and it's like i won't verbally do it right" (S042).
- **Quote:** "latency matters more than intensity" (S035) "the only time that I use negative reinforcement is when something is somebody does something immoral unethical or illegal" (S012) "one of the telltale signs I can have of a company that punishes is they are overstaffed" (S074)
- **Sources:** 19 (S001, S002, S005, S010, S011, S012, S013, S028, S035, S038, S042, S056, S074, S075, S080, S084, S089, S091, S093)

## 2. Values as the decision filter (her board of directors)
- **What it is:** The founder's values become the company's values, and every hire, fire, deal, partner, and priority runs through them. Values let people decide in situations no rule anticipated. Rules make rule followers; values make leaders.
- **Parts:** Acquisition.com's three values: unimpeachable character, sincere candor, competitive greatness (the last from John Wooden). Keep it to three so people can recall and use them; each one chunks several older values (S070). Define each value as behaviors. Recognize people who live a value and address it when someone breaks one. Repeat mission, vision, and values at annual, quarterly, and monthly meetings and name the people and decisions that lived them (S008). Culture comes from the top and dilutes with each layer, so no one will hold the values more purely than the founder (S008). SOPs supplement values; they do not replace them (S099).
- **When to use:** A deal, partner, or hire that looks good on numbers; a founder tempted to keep a high performer who breaks the culture; a company that has grown past the founder's line of sight.
- **How to score:** 5 if the values are written as behaviors and the company has already turned down money or let a performer go over them; 3 if values are posted but never used to decide; 1 if the founder keeps someone who breaks them because of results. She will not invest in a founder whose values conflict with hers, however strong the financials (S014).
- **Quote:** "I have my values right and I look at my values is they are my board of directors" (S014) "if you lead your company with rules then you create rule followers if you lead your company with values then you create leaders" (S034) "i don't run my business with a boatload of sops you know i run my business by values" (S099)
- **Sources:** 18 (S003, S008, S010, S014, S020, S026, S034, S042, S056, S060, S069, S070, S091, S092, S093, S098, S099, S103)

## 3. Constraint first, then subtract before you add *(unnamed; "earn the right" is her phrase)*
- **What it is:** Fix the one constraint that returns the most this quarter, and solve it by removing things before adding people, tools, or products. You have to earn the right to do more by proving the current thing works.
- **Parts:** Of seven broken things, pick the one that, fixed, gives the highest return, and finish it (S049). The CEO's job is relieving the current constraint and seeing the next one forming (S073). Solve your biggest problem before chasing your biggest opportunity (S026). Exhaust more and better in the current business (more and better marketing, sales, customer success) before anything new (S075). Before a hire, ask whether the need is a person, a process, or a project; default to a process fix or a contractor (S032). Do not hire into inconsistent revenue (S019). Start with the smallest change and escalate only if it fails (S086). Never kill a revenue stream without a replacement (S031). Do not solve problems with money; companies built that way stand on stilts (S008).
- **When to use:** Any plan to add a product, division, location, or hire; any founder whose answer to a problem is more headcount.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder can name the single constraint and shows the current offer is maxed out; 3 if the constraint is known but the plan adds things around it; 1 if the plan adds a new line while the core is still leaking. Hiring full time when the need is a project or a process scores low by her rule (S032).
- **Tension:** She also says to "hire ahead conservatively" so infrastructure is in place before new clients arrive (S105). Read it as: once demand is consistent, hire slightly ahead; before then, do not hire into the inconsistency (inferred from S019, S105).
- **Quote:** "Solving through addition is easy. It feels good temporarily. It is never the right answer. The best CEOs, the best business owners, the best operators solve through subtraction." (S017) "we haven't earned the right to do more here." (S017) "we have not earned the right to scale another one." (S017) "a full-time employee is a one-size fits-all solution to a very intricate problem which is how do you meet the demand of a growing business" (S032)
- **Sources:** 15 (S008, S017, S019, S024, S026, S027, S031, S032, S049, S063, S073, S075, S086, S099, S105)

## 4. A vision big enough to hold everyone's vision *(unnamed; DSS is her term)*
- **What it is:** People stay as long as their own vision fits inside the company's. A small vision loses big talent, and founders usually stall because they run out of vision, not because the market does. The CEO's job is people, vision, and cash (S005, S023).
- **Parts:** A desired superior state (DSS): the measurable and qualitative state of the company in 3 to 5 years (S005). Reverse planning: a 10-year vision, back to year 3, 2, 1, and the next three months, with headcount forecast plus a 10 to 15% buffer (S070). For each leader, show where their personal vision sits inside the company's (S024). Repeat it: she calls herself chief reminder officer, and assumes an employee must hear something seven times to believe it matters (S061, S093, S103). In remote teams, a quarterly vision presentation and a weekly CEO update (S093). Leaders who want a different destination have to go even if they perform (S026).
- **When to use:** Losing or recruiting senior people, setting the next stage, or a leadership team that disagrees on direction.
- **How to score:** 5 if the top three to five people can each name their next role inside the plan; 3 if the vision exists but is said once a year; 1 if the best person has nowhere to go. Executives are rarely let go for performance; they go for misalignment with the vision (S026).
- **Changed over time:** By 2026 she says AI may move faster than a 3 to 5 year plan assumes, so the DSS may need to change (S005). Her goals must be doable with current resources, though a stretch (S086).
- **Quote:** "you have to have a vision big enough that other people's visions for their own lives can fit inside of it" (S003) "most companies stall because the founder runs out of vision not because the company runs out of potential" (S027) "misaligned leaders break companies aligned leaders build legacies" (S026)
- **Sources:** 14 (S003, S005, S006, S008, S011, S023, S024, S026, S027, S038, S061, S070, S093, S103)

## 5. Before you fire: diagnose, then do not carry management debt *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Most "bad employee" problems are misalignment or a gap the leader left, and more coaching does not fix misalignment. Diagnose first. Once the diagnosis is clear, act fast, because avoided people decisions pile up as management debt.
- **Parts:** Three wrong fits (S015): right person in the wrong seat (compare skills, not résumé, to the job; reshape or split an overloaded role; remove admin work), the wrong season (of their life, or of the business: peacetime versus wartime), or the wrong boss (add a dotted line to another leader). Did I do my part: were expectations, metrics, and training clear, and can this stage even train them (S045)? Look for a skill deficit before a motivation failure (S079). Manage up or out: coach them up or they coach themselves out (S045). The extinction curve: how long it takes someone to stop a behavior; if longer than the business can bear, make the call (S031). The enforcement matrix (S056): high skill and high will, promote; low and low, remove; high will and low skill, coach; low will and high skill, usually remove. Management debt (S105): known misfits left in place until the company stands on toothpick stilts.
- **When to use:** Before any firing decision, and whenever a plan assumes an existing person will grow into a bigger role.
- **How to score:** Re-seat if values are strong and the problem is seat, season, or boss; coach if their improvement rate is faster than the business needs; exit if values fail, or if improvement stays too slow after clear expectations. Flag any misfit older than about 90 days, since teams get used to the gap (S022). Do not over-promote; bring someone in above them instead (S026).
- **Tension:** She rejects the rule of hiring slow and firing fast in favor of hiring well and firing well (S094), yet calls not firing people soon enough the biggest mistake she ever made (S098). Newer reading: diagnose properly, then do not delay (inferred from S015, S094, S098).
- **Quote:** "putting in more effort, putting in more hours, putting in more work, coaching somebody more, giving them more feedback will not fix a problem where they are not in the right role or where they are misaligned." (S015) "management debt is when you start off in your small business and then as you continue to scale you know that there's some people that probably aren't the right fit." (S105) "the biggest mistake i ever made was not firing people soon enough" (S098)
- **Sources:** 14 (S001, S011, S015, S022, S026, S031, S045, S056, S079, S082, S094, S098, S102, S105)

## 6. Goal-anchored feedback (critique the do, not the who) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Learn each person's goal, then frame every piece of feedback as the gap between how they acted and how someone in their goal role would act, plus what to do next time. Feedback that does not change behavior was not feedback.
- **Parts:** Anchor to their stated goal (S005, S072). Critique names the gap; an insult ties the person to something negative (S011, S074, S080). Future-focused: give the alternative behavior, not just the stop (S002, S080). A critique ties to behavior the person controls and knows how to improve, comes fast and often, uses individual metrics, and is backed by their own self-report (S080). One piece at a time; a second before the first is fixed overwhelms them (S073, S084). Normalize it: Feedback Fridays, one piece per person per week (S032). Game tape: record calls, rewatch one a week together, one action item per team (S072, S089). A 360 view from manager, peers, reports, and clients each quarter (S089). Ask for feedback with specific, future-paced questions (S083).
- **When to use:** Any performance gap, any manager whose feedback produces no change, any leader who avoids it.
- **How to score:** 5 if the manager can name each report's goal and gives weekly, specific feedback tied to it; 3 if feedback is quarterly or general; 1 if it is about who they are, or absent. If a leader can only say what not to do, the problem is the leader's (S020).
- **Quote:** "you're not giving them feedback about themselves. You're giving them feedback of how to achieve their goal." (S005) "if you want somebody to feel bad insult them if you want them to get better you critique them" (S080) "if you give feedback to somebody the behavior doesn't change the feedback was not feedback because feedback in out of definition means that behavior changes" (S011)
- **Sources:** 13 (S002, S005, S011, S020, S028, S032, S072, S073, S074, S080, S083, S084, S089)

## 7. Accountability = expectation + measurement x feedback
- **What it is:** Accountability does not exist until a person knows the expectation, has a number to judge against, and hears where they stand often enough to change. The leader is a neutral scale, not a judge. She calls the CEO the chief accountability officer.
- **Parts:** Four levels of expectations, each flowing from the one above: core values, brand promise (results times experience, built to be hit 100% of the time), department KPIs, role KPIs (S034, S103). Measurement: KPIs, scorecards, CRM triggers, leaderboards, rubrics. End-of-week reports she reads and answers line by line; if you stop answering, people stop filing (S001, S030). Self-assessment is king: let the person measure and name the gap (S073, S084). State the facts neutrally, ask what they think, have them propose the fix, reinforce it (S084). Tactical accountability (small, frequent, about 20%) versus developmental (habits and character, about 80%, in weekly one-on-ones) (S047). If a performance review surprises anyone, the weekly work was missing (S047). You disseminate accountability; you do not delegate it (S102).
- **When to use:** Underperformance, a founder who says people should just know what to do, or a team past what the founder can see.
- **How to score:** 5 if every role has written results with a number and weekly feedback; 3 if expectations exist but feedback is quarterly; 1 if accountability means telling people they are wrong. Low revenue per head, hidden expenses, weak upsells, and high churn are her signs of low accountability (S102). Executives and experienced hires are held to the same standard (S047, S102).
- **Inconsistency:** The multiplier in the formula is "feedback" in some sources (S061, S102) and "reinforcement" in others (S034, S084).
- **Quote:** "expectations plus measurement times feedback equals accountability." (S102) "you are the scale for your team." (S102) "the most effective way to measure accountability is when the person themselves your employee your teammate is the one doing the measuring not you" (S084)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S003, S025, S030, S034, S047, S056, S061, S073, S084, S102, S103)

## 8. Demonstrate, document, duplicate (then follow up)
- **What it is:** Before handing off any function, do it yourself, have the new owner document what they watched, then have them do it exactly that way. Only then talk about improvements. Delegation is not finished at handoff: the delegator becomes the person who reinforces and checks the work.
- **Parts:** Demonstrate (leadership is caught, not taught, after John Maxwell, S028); document (the person who documents learns most, S002); duplicate until they match the success metric. Delegate and follow up with a living SOP and metrics (S032). Delegation trades doing time for reinforcing time (S075). A system built on memory is not a system; if you cannot explain the process in under 30 minutes you have a magic trick, not a process (S019). When a function fails because of a person, she steps into the role herself until it turns around, then hires (S031). Figure out the playbook before hiring a specialist for something new (S088). Be the primary operator yourself for the first year to eighteen months (S099).
- **When to use:** Any time the founder wants to hand off something they do now, or hire a replacement for their own work.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has shown it, the new owner wrote the playbook, and someone checks quality after handoff; 3 if a document was handed over with no check; 1 if delegated and forgotten. Smart or experienced hires may need less of the full process (S002, S003).
- **Quote:** "you want to demonstrate, you want to document, you want to duplicate." (S025) "I can't just delegate and forget about it I have to delegate and follow up" (S032) Citing psychology studies rather than her own finding: "the person who documents the process learns the most" (S002) "you cannot just offload tasks. You need to create repeatable processes that somebody else can do the thing without you." (S019)
- **Sources:** 12 (S002, S003, S019, S022, S025, S028, S031, S032, S053, S075, S088, S099)

## 9. Character, competency, commitment (and the high potential player)
- **What it is:** Every hire needs character (values shown in behavior), competency (the skills, tested directly), and commitment (what they gave up to be here). For promotion, look for high potential: capacity, character, and motivation, judged by rate of progress.
- **Parts:** Operationalize values: write what behavior proves each value, then ask for past examples and probe for detail; vagueness or a long preamble is a red flag (S002, S069). Competency: break the role into about five core activities and test or reference each (S069). Commitment: ask what they gave up (S069). The high potential player: capacity (ceiling), character, motivation (S076). Rate of progress over rate of performance; humility over arrogance; accept extreme weaknesses that come with extreme gifts and staff around them (S076). Promote people who already have followers; titles do not make leaders (S075). Merit, not tenure or age (S021). Hire and promote for humility; fire for ego and pride (S083).
- **When to use:** Writing a scorecard, choosing between a skilled candidate and a values fit, or picking who gets promoted into management.
- **How to score:** 5 if all three Cs were tested with evidence, not inferred from a résumé; 3 if skills were tested but values were asked about rather than observed; 1 if the hire rests on likability or credentials. A skilled hire who fails on character costs more later: her own example cost about $300,000 (S069).
- **Quote:** "behavior is the proxy for values" (S069) "you don't hire people for experience you hire people for skill experience is only a proxy to skill" (S069) "to spot a high potential leader you look for rate of progress not rate of performance" (S076)
- **Sources:** 12 (S002, S008, S012, S021, S031, S033, S069, S070, S075, S076, S083, S098)

## 10. Employee value proposition (lead with growth) and the growth plan
- **What it is:** Just as the company has an offer for customers, it needs an offer for talent. She leads with growth, then status, then pay. Ascension is part of retention: people who can see their next step stay.
- **Parts:** Three levers: growth, status, money (S002). Five parts of the talent offer: compensation (raise and promotion cycles, pay transparency; the higher the role, the more performance-based), benefits that match the stated values, career development, work environment, and culture (S024). Five pillars of retention: growth, measurable impact (the IMPACT acronym), fair pay at the top of the market, recognition, community (S038). A competency map of levels, skills, and pay (S003). Two tracks: most should be technical tracks, with few management slots (S026). The growth plan: map the future role, rate skills red, yellow, green, start with the reds, and give a real business challenge that forces the red skill; skill grows with frequency, not time (S028, S038). Stretch goals one level up with about 80% odds of success (S038).
- **When to use:** Losing good people, unable to recruit seniors, or a founder who plans to compete for talent on salary alone.
- **How to score:** 5 if each key person has a written growth path with the next role and the skills to get there; 3 if pay is competitive but the path is vague; 1 if the only lever is money. Replacing an employee costs her figure of 90 to 200% of annual salary (S089).
- **Quote:** "you have to know your employee value proposition right just like you have an offer for the marketplace you have to have an offer for the talent Marketplace" (S002) "compensation in general does not just exist to attract the right people but also to repel the wrong ones." (S024) "invest in them as if they will never leave but be happy when they do" (S082)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S002, S003, S012, S024, S026, S028, S033, S038, S076, S082, S094)

## 11. Who to hire at each stage: Swiss Army knife, scalpel, player-coach, operator *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The right hire depends on the stage. Early hires are generalists; later you need specialists. Small companies need player-coaches, not pure coaches. A visionary founder needs an operator whose strengths are the founder's weaknesses.
- **Parts:** Swiss Army knife to scalpel: either hire a generalist and tell them plainly they may be replaced by specialists, or hire one who can build the team around them; keep early titles small (S050). Hire the leader of a function before the doers; more cost now, far less managerial debt later (S050). Player-coach: under about $30 to 50M, a leader must step onto the field (S106). Tiered operators (S099): 0 to 1M a builder; 1 to 10M a builder who delegates; 10 to 50M an incremental improver; 50M plus a builder again. No corporate operators before about 10M (S099). Manage only, or manage and grow: decide which before writing the job (S068, S073). The person great at $3M may be wrong at $50M (S068, S072). Creators should hire an operator, not a manager (S012).
- **When to use:** A first senior hire, a founder replacing themselves, a stalled company run by a loyal early hire, or a visionary who wants to step back.
- **How to score:** 5 if the hire matches the stage, can still do the work, and complements the founder; 3 if they fit the stage but duplicate the founder; 1 if a big-company coach is hired into a small company, or the operator has never scaled past the current level. Her portfolio case: a best-friend operator replaced with someone who had scaled further, and revenue tripled in six months (S037, S095).
- **Quote:** "most people from day one aren't there on day 657" (S050) "in small businesses the coach has to go in when the players hurt and they also sometimes need to be in the game no matter what." (S106) "if your operator's not a 10 out of 10 your business isn't going to be a 10 out of 10" (S099)
- **Sources:** 11 (S012, S037, S050, S068, S072, S073, S095, S098, S099, S105, S106)

## 12. Not-dumb decisions and the power of a decision *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The goal is fewer dumb decisions, not brilliant ones (she takes this from Warren Buffett). A good decision is the highest-leverage thing a leader does, because one decision is executed by the whole team.
- **Parts:** Four steps (S044): ask the right question ("how might I ___ so that I ___"); separate the problem from the symptom; turn beliefs into assumptions; map upside, downside, worst case, and likelihood for each option, then pick the most probable compounding positive outcome with the lowest risk. Watch for net zero decisions, the one bad call that cancels the good ones: wrong co-founder, investor, buyer, or niche (S044). Four multipliers (S016): reach, repetition, duration, reversibility. Three skills (S016): create more options, set deadlines, remove yourself (ask from a third-person or future view). Keep a running list and batch decisions in set time (S016). Pick your problems, not your solutions (S029). Separate execution failure from strategy failure (S014). Short and long aligned: it must work at 10 minutes and 10 years (S096, S097). Decide what is right, not who is right (S083). State facts, not feelings, when disagreeing (S090). Process protects you from emotion (S011).
- **When to use:** Any big, hard-to-reverse call: a key hire, a partner, an investor, a pivot, a sale.
- **How to score:** 5 if the problem is separated from the symptom, options were widened, and the downside is survivable; 3 if the decision is reasonable but rests on untested beliefs; 1 if it was made while emotional or put off with no deadline. Harder-to-reverse calls need more care in both directions (S016).
- **Quote:** "the most probable compounding positive outcome plus lowest risk" (S044) "I have never seen a fast growing high velocity organization with slow decision makers." (S016) "There's no amount of execution that can out execute an entire team executing on your one decision that you made." (S053)
- **Sources:** 11 (S011, S014, S016, S029, S044, S053, S083, S090, S096, S097, S101)

## 13. The leader as the rock: regulation and certainty *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The leader sets the emotional weather. A dysregulated leader makes a dysregulated team, and a leader without certainty makes followers without commitment. Certainty comes from how you react, not from controlling events.
- **Parts:** Her five rules of leadership (S020): emotional regulation (name the emotion, put a buffer between feeling and action, keep a neutral tone), sincere candor, unimpeachable character, clarity (one to three priorities in plain words), ownership. Regulate, do not suppress (S020). Never vent down; vent up, or to your inner circle (S020, S025). The certainty chain: certainty, clarity, speed, progress, momentum, success (S029). A good state means behaving well, not feeling good (S041). Treat staff drama as non-urgent; your calm lowers theirs (S096). Remain neutral when something hard surfaces (S100). Do not have a hard conversation while emotional (S046, S090). Her company is a mirror of her, so care deeply and be ruthless when needed (S023).
- **When to use:** A crisis, a team reacting to a founder's moods, or a founder making people calls under stress.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder can name how they regulate and the team describes them as predictable; 3 if calm in public but venting downward; 1 if the team reads the founder's mood to decide what to do.
- **Quote:** "a disregulated leader just creates a disregulated team." (S020) "leaders who lack certainty create followers who lack commitment" (S029) "a good state doesn't mean you feel good a good state means you behave good" (S041)
- **Sources:** 10 (S020, S023, S025, S029, S041, S046, S070, S090, S096, S100)

## 14. Coach off the court: give decisions, not tasks
- **What it is:** Leadership scales when the founder stops being the star player and transfers judgment. Giving tasks makes doers; giving decisions makes leaders. Rescuing people robs them of the skill.
- **Parts:** The coach never steps on the court when the game is on the line (after John Wooden, S036; Phil Jackson, S059). Four levels of delegation: investigation, informed progress, informed results, complete ownership; match autonomy to experience (S064). The accountability dial, five levels from "needs to be told" to full ownership, trained with a rule that people bring the answer they think she would give (S056). Transfer judgment, not just attention (S013). Build strength in others instead of taking all the strength yourself (S013). Do not solve your team's conflicts; help them script and rehearse the talk and make them run it (S013, S096). In a crisis, hand them the problem and ask for the plan (S059). Schedule your own absence: for each role you play, who could do it next (S059). Growth or control, not both (S060).
- **When to use:** A founder who is the bottleneck on decisions, who jumps in to fix things, or whose team escalates everything.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder can name decisions the team now owns without check-ins; 3 if tasks are delegated but decisions come back up; 1 if more than half the founder's time goes to solving problems directly (S059). When she was out for surgery, her team handled crises she would have taken on and she saw she had been robbing them (S060).
- **Quote:** "If you want to create doers, then give people tasks. If you want to create leaders, give people decisions." (S059) "You need to transfer your judgment to them." (S013) "Codling your team robs them of the opportunity to develop those skills themselves and it robs everybody else around you as well" (S079)
- **Sources:** 9 (S013, S036, S041, S056, S059, S060, S064, S079, S096)

## 15. Onboarding as acceleration *(unnamed; "acceleration" is her word)*
- **What it is:** Onboarding starts when the offer is signed and decides how fast a hire produces and whether they stay. She puts far more effort into it than seems needed, at every level including the C-suite.
- **Parts:** Five Cs (S104): compliance, clarity (30-60-90 day goals by day one), culture, connection (a buddy, cross-team meetings), check back. Communication taper: twice a day for two weeks, then daily, then twice a week, then weekly (S104). An hour-by-hour schedule for the first one to two weeks (S104). Onboarding mirrors the future job: if the role needs autonomy, onboard autonomously (S038). Set expectations lower than reality and tell people the hard parts so they arrive delighted (S038, S093). Batch onboarding for duplicative roles: a 7-day immersion (S068). Fresh eyes: 60-day observation notes on what works, what makes no sense, what drives return, what slows them down (S089). For new senior leaders: overlap a month before and weeks after a transition, then check in monthly (S070). Give critical feedback in the first week; trust is built through adversity (S104).
- **When to use:** Any new hire, especially senior ones, and any company with early churn.
- **How to score:** 5 if a written 30-60-90 and daily contact exist for each new hire; 3 if onboarding is paperwork plus a handoff; 1 if new hires learn by watching. Losing someone in the first year costs her figure of 100 to 300% of salary (S104).
- **Quote:** "onboarding starts the moment that they sign the job offer." (S104) "put uh 10 times more effort into onboarding somebody than I would ever thought was possible" (S031) "every new teammate is a chance to break status quo" (S089)
- **Sources:** 9 (S031, S038, S068, S070, S073, S089, S093, S104, S107)

## 16. Talent acquisition funnel (the mirror of the client funnel)
- **What it is:** Hiring is run like sales: the same funnel, nurture, scripts, and metrics. Clients come in the front door; talent has to keep coming in the back door. Alex runs the client funnel; she runs the talent funnel (S089).
- **Parts:** Applicant generation: the job title is the headline and the description is ad copy; be specific, no insider terms or cute titles (S095). Six channels: network (niche roles only), outbound, paid ads (high volume roles), organic content, recruiters, team referrals (S037, S095). Applicant nurture: speed (same or next day), volume, personalization, omnichannel (S037). Then a scripted interview process (framework 17). Track it like a sales pipeline: applications, screened, qualified, interviews, offers, time to fill, quality of hire (S089). For an executive filling the biggest constraint, the founder does the outreach personally (S068). Sales frameworks such as CLOSER work in recruiting (S089). The job description, interview, onboarding, and retention map to the sales page, sales call, fulfillment, and LTV (S056).
- **When to use:** A founder who says they cannot find good people, relies on their network, or posts a job and waits.
- **How to score:** 5 if roles get high applicant volume, contact is same day, and the funnel is measured; 3 if ads are written but slow or network-only; 1 if hiring is a posted job and a gut call. Her bar: for management roles Acquisition.com gets 500 or more applications, and out of 100 applicants only a few reach a full interview (S037, S095).
- **Quote:** "the reason that we couldn't scale was not due to lack of client acquisition it was actually lack of talent acquisition" (S037) "the top candidates find a job in eight so speed is King" (S037) "if you wouldn't rely on your network to get clients then why would you rely on it to build your team" (S037)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S037, S056, S068, S089, S091, S095)

## 17. Multi-step interview with the CEO as gatekeeper *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A scripted interview process where each step checks one thing, and the founder's step protects the culture and the candidate. The interview is the best a candidate will ever be.
- **Parts:** Five steps (S037, S095): screening; expectations and culture; skill test (give them last quarter's real problem); alignment (career path and pay); CEO interview. An earlier three-phase version (S048): history and values with the hiring manager, skills with the manager and peers, then the CEO gatekeeper who paints the hard parts. A weighted scorecard of must-haves so she is not deciding on bias (S013). Do 20 or more interviews per role to learn what good looks like (S001). Five biases to guard against: confirmation, expectation anchors, halo, similarity, affinity; anyone you feel strongly about gets a second interviewer (S039). When you like someone a lot, add a technical interview (S013). Never ignore a red flag: dig in or pass (S106). Feeling uneasy because someone is unfamiliar, not unqualified, is a reason to hire (S039).
- **When to use:** Any hire for a role you expect to keep long term, and any company whose hires keep failing in the first 90 days.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a scorecard, a real skill test, and a founder or senior gatekeeper step; 3 if interviews are unscripted conversations; 1 if the hire was made on liking the person. Skipping a step predicts a specific failure: no skill step gives you likable but incompetent; no culture step, skilled but misfit; no gatekeeper, someone who leaves (S048).
- **Changed over time:** In 2021 she said founders who leave interviewing before they have a capable C-suite stall at 20 to 30 people (S048). In 2023 she put the CEO interview in every hire below about 1,000 people (S003), and elsewhere only past 30 people (S037). Newer view (2026): she uses a scorecard and transfers her judgment to others rather than holding every interview (S013).
- **Quote:** "present the problem you had last quarter ask them how they would solve it literally you could do that with any role" (S037) "the interview is the best you're gonna get from this person...that is the 120 trying." (S106) "likeness blinds you from incompetence." (S013)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S003, S013, S037, S039, S048, S095, S106)

## 18. Stage ladder (the levels of the scaling roadmap)
- **What it is:** Each stage of a company has one constraint, one skill, and one function to build before it can move up, and the founder has to change at each step.
- **Parts:** The ten levels she taught in 2026 (S019): improvise, monetize, advertise (the rule of 100: 100 lead-generation actions a day for 100 days), stabilize (stop doing, start documenting, first hire), prioritize (5 to 9 people), productize (10 to 19), optimize (20 to 49), categorize (50 to 99), specialize (100 to 250), institution (250 to 500). An earlier four-phase version: market fit to 1M, deliverables to 10M, professionalization to 50M, innovation and talent to 100M (S094). Revenue skills: sell consistently to 1M, manage vendors and a few people to 3M, then duplicate yourself (S011); get other people consistent for you to reach 10M (S005). At about 30 people memory-based systems broke and margins fell 20% (S022). The 3.0 shift: the heroics that built the company stop scaling it (S021). Early, gain control; later, let go (S056).
- **When to use:** Placing a company and founder on the ladder before judging any plan, especially a hire or new product.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan builds the function the current level needs; 3 if it skips one level; 1 if it copies a company several levels up. Find role models only two or three steps ahead (S105). The ladder stops at about 500 people.
- **Quote:** "Your business is never going to outgrow your capacity to lead it." (S019) To reach 10 million you have to get other people to be consistent for you (inferred from S005). "what you do unintentionally when you're small is what you must do intentionally as you get big" (S042)
- **Sources:** 8 (S005, S011, S019, S021, S022, S042, S056, S094)

## 19. Sell, don't tell: rolling out change and transferring authority *(unnamed; "sell don't tell" is her phrase)*
- **What it is:** Change and new leaders are adopted when they are sold to each person, not announced. Transferring authority is a process, not an event. A leader's job is to make change easier, not to protect people from it.
- **Parts:** Compile the reasons into a deck as persuasive as when you first heard them, then hold one-on-ones that tie the change to each person's goals (S072). Before a big change, meet each leader on how it affects them personally (S088). Authority transfer: position the new leader before they arrive, co-lead, edify them publicly, then phase yourself out; when people keep coming back to you, redirect them to the new leader instead of answering (S092, S093). Overlap a new senior leader with the outgoing reporting line for weeks (S070). Add one new system or meeting a month, not all at once (S103). The three Rs to rebuild conviction: remind, resell, reinforce (S089). Treat change as a test first, then decide (S092). Resistance is a choice to focus on the past; intervene at the thought or the behavior (S081). Change on evidence, not feeling (S081).
- **When to use:** A reorg, a new executive, a new reporting line, a pivot, or a new system the team will resist.
- **How to score:** 5 if each affected leader heard the change one-on-one and the new leader has public authority; 3 if announced well but people still route around the new leader; 1 if a change was decreed in a single meeting.
- **Quote:** "I always think sell don't tell" (S072) "your job is not to protect people from change your job is to make it easier for your team to change" (S081) "it's not an event that occurs, it's a process. So, when somebody is If you're transferring authority to somebody else, you truly have to transfer it." (S092)
- **Sources:** 8 (S070, S072, S081, S088, S089, S092, S093, S103)

## 20. Communication cadence: pulse, purpose, preparation
- **What it is:** Communication, not intelligence, predicts high-performing teams, and meetings should fit the company's activity and size. Nobody quits over too much communication.
- **Parts:** Pulse equals activity times size; purpose is strategic or tactical (annual vision and culture, quarterly course-correct, monthly adjust, weekly execute, daily iterate); preparation is context and the right people against an agenda that forces a conclusion (S040). Re-check the cadence monthly; no cookie-cutter cadence (S040). A communication cycle at the bottom of every job description: what that role runs daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly (S093). Communication equals energy times engagement plus emotional control times exploration (S100). Move one-off questions into group channels and go one-to-many as you scale (S064, S100). End each day asking who needs to know what happened (S032). Share goals at least quarterly (S061).
- **When to use:** A founder in meetings all day, silos between departments, or a team that says it hears about things last.
- **How to score:** 5 if every recurring meeting has a stated purpose and each role has a written communication cycle; 3 if meetings exist by habit; 1 if information lives in the founder's head.
- **Quote:** "nobody ever quits a job or a company because someone over communicated" (S100) "the goal is not to have meetings for the sake of saying we checked the box we had a [ __ ] meeting nobody cares about that it's to move the company forward" (S040)
- **Sources:** 8 (S008, S032, S040, S061, S064, S091, S093, S100)

## 21. Producer, manager, leader, owner (the leadership cycle)
- **What it is:** People move up a ladder from doing the work, to managing others, to rallying people to a vision, to allocating people and money. Each rung needs new skills, mostly soft ones. In her book in progress, the same ladder is a cycle: lead yourself, lead others, lead leaders, create leaders, then create leaders who build a culture that creates leaders (S013).
- **Parts:** Producer: autonomous, industrious; stuck by low self-awareness. Manager: reliable, organized; stuck in details. Leader: humble, empathetic, poised, treats the team as the product. Owner: allocates capital and people (S043). Management of time before self before others (S067). Executor to communicator: advocate for your work, operate in now plus tomorrow, reduce your boss's load (S067). Reactive leaders approve tasks; proactive ones develop people (S076). A level one leader does the work; a level five creates leaders, which needs letting go of control (S060). Leadership starts with leading yourself (S052).
- **When to use:** Promoting a top performer into management, or a founder deciding what kind of leader to become next.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person has shown the next rung before getting the title; 3 if they are strong on their current rung only; 1 if they are promoted on output alone. Do 100% of your job and half the job above before expecting a promotion (S043).
- **Quote:** "when i think of a manager i think of someone with two hands when i think of a leader i think of someone with 10 hands" (S043) "You can have growth, you can have control, but you cannot have both at the same time." (S060) "you don't need more technical skills to move up you need more soft skills" (S067)
- **Sources:** 7 (S005, S013, S043, S052, S060, S067, S076)

## 22. Hard conversation formula (six Es, then four As)
- **What it is:** A script for a hard conversation with an employee, so short-term discomfort replaces long-term dysfunction.
- **Parts:** Before they speak, the six Es: establish tone, eliminate assumptions (say whether their job is at risk), explain the goal, explain where the concern came from, emphasize the impact, estimate the severity from 1 to 10. Then the four As: ask (watch for coachable or defensive), advise, agree on the plan and check-ins, assure (close in the tone you opened). Write it down, rehearse it calm, wait if you are emotional, record and review it like game tape (S046). For conflicts between teammates, help the person script and practice it, then let them have the talk (S013, S096). The company you want is on the other side of the talks you are dreading (S060).
- **When to use:** A performance gap that has gone on too long, a values breach, or a founder who has been putting off a talk.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder has written the six Es and named the severity; 3 if they plan to "just talk"; 1 if the talk keeps being postponed.
- **Quote:** "it is totally fine to have short-term discomfort meaning you've kind of hurt someone's feelings they feel kind of shitty they seem down you know those might be short-term side effects to avoid the long-term dysfunction" (S046) "if you feel emotional about something don't have the conversation until it goes away" (S090)
- **Sources:** 5 (S013, S046, S060, S090, S096)

## 23. Jockey over horse (and the founder self-rating gap)
- **What it is:** When investing in or judging a business, bet on the founder's character and adaptability over the idea. Strategy and team can be changed; the person running it cannot.
- **Parts:** A great leader can make an average company succeed and a poor one can sink a great idea (S012, S026). Strong beliefs, loosely held (S007). Founders who cannot manage themselves cannot manage a business (S007). The self-rating gap: in her experience, founders who failed rated themselves higher than she rated them; those who succeeded rated themselves lower (S007). Her CEO self-rating exercise on John Wooden's pyramid traits, run with about 900 CEOs (S041). Pick founders with one near-world-class skill and a strong support system; if you would not hire the founder to run the company as an outside executive, take majority or pass (S049).
- **When to use:** Deciding whether a founder can lead the next stage, and before any partnership or investment.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder rates themselves below what the evidence shows and fixes deficits without shame; 3 if capable but defensive; 1 if they overrate themselves and blame others. Her rule: no jockey is better than the wrong jockey (S026).
- **Quote:** "I'm going to bet on the jockey I'm done betting on horses and if I can't find a jockey that I like no jockey is better than the wrong jockey" (S026) "the ones that had failed had all rated themselves higher than I rated them...the ones that succeeded all rated themselves lower than I rated them" (S007) "strong beliefs loosely held that's what I want in an entrepreneur and in a person that's running a business" (S007) Using the common investing saying: "bet on the jockey not the horse" (S012)
- **Sources:** 5 (S007, S012, S026, S041, S049)

## 24. Exit and layoff protocol *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How you let people go decides how the people who stay see you and how you see yourself as a leader. Go past what feels comfortable.
- **Parts:** For a department shutdown (S030): let the manager go first and separately; meet every person individually, never in a group; severance beyond comfortable; personal letters of recommendation; line up interviews at other companies; own the mistake. Tell the remaining team the truth; silence scares them more (S030). Give the plain business reason (S092). When someone leaves angry, protect the company, give grace, do not retaliate (S001). Keeping someone where they have no path is a disservice; help them land well (S001). Forgive yourself for the hard decision so you can show up for the team (S070).
- **When to use:** A layoff, a department closure, a cut after a bad hire wave, or any exit of a long-tenured person.
- **How to score:** 5 if each person is met one-on-one with severance and help finding work, and the remaining team gets the full reason; 3 if fair pay but a group announcement; 1 if people learn by email or rumor.
- **Exception:** For a pending sale of the company, she says do not tell the team until it closes (S101).
- **Quote:** "how you let them go is going to say it it will it will translate into how you feel about yourself as a leader for the next however long" (S030) "my transparency saved me with launch" (S030) "I have to forgive myself for everybody else who doesn't understand the situation. Otherwise, I'm not my best for them." (S070)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S030, S070, S092, S101)

## 25. Disappear-for-a-month test (seven components of a sellable business)
- **What it is:** A business is worth what it can do without its founder. If you vanished for a month, would it decline, hold, or grow? Grow means a strong team.
- **Parts:** Seven components (S027): growth potential, unique value proposition (price, promotion, or product; about 20% different is enough), revenue diversification, cash flow, recurring revenue, financial performance, strength of team. Know whether the buyer is strategic or financial (S101). The best time to sell is when you do not want to (S027). Keep the option to sell even if you never use it (S027). Do not brag about being out of the day-to-day until a full CEO is in place, and keep quality control over your core strength (S105).
- **When to use:** A founder planning to step back, sell, raise, or take on a big new project that pulls them away.
- **How to score:** 5 if the business grew the last time the founder was away; 3 if it held; 1 if it declined. Team strength is the component she weights most for sale value (S027).
- **Quote:** "if I were to disappear for a month would my business decline maintain or grow and if it grows you have a really strong team" (S027) "creating a strong team is probably the most valuable thing that you can do to sell your business" (S027) "the only way to make business sustainable is to make sure it does not always all rely on you" (S011)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S027, S101, S105)
