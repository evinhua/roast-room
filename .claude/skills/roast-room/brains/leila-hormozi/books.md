# Books: Leila Hormozi

Built from the 107 sources in sources.md (2021 to 2026 where dated). She has no published book of her own in these sources, solo or co-written; her leadership book is a finished first draft, unpublished as of the newest source that mentions it. This file covers that book, the Acquisition.com material she teaches, and the books she cites as shaping how she leads and decides. Book text was not fetched. Summaries are in my own words. Alex Hormozi's books and ideas appear only where she teaches or cites them, not as her own work.

## Her own book in progress

### Her leadership book (unpublished; title not confirmed)
- **Status:** In 2024 an audience member referred to her upcoming book as $100 Million Leadership (S031); she did not confirm the title there. In 2026 she said the first draft was done, she expected it out in Q1 2027, and she would most likely self-publish because she did not like the publishing deals (S013). She plans a free course with a workbook to go with it and says she will not charge for information (S013). Earlier she said the whole book is built from her lessons and failures (S005) and that writing it was awful for her because she dislikes working alone, while Alex loves it (S010). She set aside an earlier book on how to change your life because it was not ready (S013).
- **Core thesis:** Most people want money, status, and influence but not the responsibility of leading people, and leadership advice is too vague to act on ("be honest, have integrity"). The book breaks leadership into steps simple enough that more people think they can do it (S013).
- **Key frameworks:** 12 steps arranged as a flywheel, built on a leadership cycle: lead yourself, lead others, lead leaders, create leaders, then create leaders who build a culture that creates leaders. Written so a reader at any stage gets something. Mostly stories, with concrete how-to steps at the end of each chapter, a structure Alex advised (S013). She would be content if it only became the leadership manual inside Acquisition.com (S013).
- **Taught in sources:** S005, S010, S013, S031.

## Acquisition.com material she teaches

### The scaling roadmap (ten levels of business)
- **Core thesis:** Each stage of a company has one constraint, one skill, and one task to master before it can move up, and the business never outgrows its leader's capacity to lead it.
- **Key frameworks:** Ten levels from improvise to institution, keyed to revenue and headcount (5 to 9, 10 to 19, 20 to 49, 50 to 99, 100 to 250, 250 to 500 people); the rule of 100 (100 lead-generation actions a day for 100 days), which she calls the Hormozi law; stop doing and start documenting at the stabilize level; the founder's shift from operator to architect. The ladder ends around 500 people.
- **Taught in sources:** S019. Earlier stage models: four phases from 0 to $100M (S094), revenue-stage skills (S005, S011), and operator types by stage (S099).

### Value Acceleration Method (VAM)
- **Core thesis:** Founders lack the who, the what, and the how to scale. Acquisition.com recruits the who, gives the what, and shows the how, after which the business scales by default.
- **Key frameworks:** Talent acquisition as the mirror of client acquisition (applicant generation, applicant nurture, scripted interview); six hiring channels; a five-step interview with a CEO interview at the end.
- **Taught in sources:** S037, S095.

### Acquisition.com's values
- **Core thesis:** Three values (unimpeachable character, sincere candor, competitive greatness) act as the company's decision filter for hiring, firing, deals, and recognition. Each replaced several older Gym Launch values.
- **Key frameworks:** Values as a board of directors; a maximum of three values; values defined as behaviors; values over rules; recognition tied to a named value.
- **Taught in sources:** S014, S020, S070, S092, S093, S098, S099.

### Her "high output management practices" series
- **Core thesis:** A multi-part YouTube series on running people: recognition, accountability, and expectations. The series name echoes Andy Grove's book High Output Management, but she does not name Grove or walk through that book in these sources.
- **Key frameworks:** The recognition scrambler (S042); the accountability formula, tactical versus developmental accountability, and "you are the scale" (S102); four levels of expectations and values versus rules (S103).
- **Taught in sources:** S042, S102, S103.

### Seven components of a sellable business
- **Core thesis:** Buyers buy a business, not a person, and team strength is the most valuable thing a founder can build for a sale.
- **Key frameworks:** Growth potential, unique value proposition, revenue diversification, cash flow, recurring revenue, financial performance, and team strength (the disappear-for-a-month test). Her separate lessons from selling her companies cover buyer types, terms, and when to tell the team.
- **Taught in sources:** S027, S101.

### Acquisition.com workshops
- **Core thesis:** Her own team, not professional presenters, teaches the material, because teaching something is how you learn it, and attendees should leave doing something differently.
- **Key frameworks:** Team members as presenters; a live feedback document filled in during the event; work out the playbook yourself before hiring a specialist planner.
- **Taught in sources:** S033, S088.

### Alex Hormozi's books
- **Core thesis:** As she presents them, the business-building basics: offers, leads, and (for gyms) retention.
- **Key frameworks:** She recommends $100M Offers and $100M Leads to beginners (S049). She names the five horsemen of retention from Gym Launch Secrets when answering a retention question (S030). Audience members cite $100M Offers and Gym Launch Secrets to her (S033, S036, S096).
- **Taught in sources:** S030, S049.

## Books she cites

### John Wooden's Pyramid of Success and his book (title not stated)
- **Core thesis:** Success is peace of mind from knowing you did your best, built from traits stacked in a pyramid; effective leaders are first teachers.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Competitive greatness as a company value (S014); a CEO self-rating exercise on each pyramid trait, which she says she has run with about 900 CEOs (S041); industriousness and enthusiasm as the base; the coach who never steps on the court when the game is on the line (S036); the leader as coach of the team (S005); his line on humility (S083); leaders as teachers (S029).
- **Taught in sources:** S005, S014, S029, S036, S041, S083.

### The Hard Thing About Hard Things (Ben Horowitz, 2014)
- **Core thesis:** Nothing prepares you to run a company except running one; the job is mostly hard situations with no clean answer.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Peacetime versus wartime, applied to which people fit which season of the business (S015). She reads it when she feels like a bad CEO after a hard decision and still recommends it to her team (S041, S043). In a crisis she asked how leaders she had read, including Horowitz, Phil Knight, and Bill Graham, handled their darkest hours (S070).
- **Taught in sources:** S015, S041, S043, S070.

### Patrick Lencioni's books
- **Core thesis:** Short, easy reads on teams and leadership.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** None named. She calls his library the best body of work on team dynamics for her team and suggests reading one a month (S043); in 2024 she said she had just read one she called "the naked" (S031).
- **Taught in sources:** S031, S043.

### Jim Collins's books
- **Core thesis:** Researched distillations of what the best companies do.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** None named. She prefers his research-backed approach to one person's opinion and recommends all of his books.
- **Taught in sources:** S031.

### Matt Mochary's CEO book
- **Core thesis:** A tactical manual for running a company.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** None named. She calls it the tactical guide to being a CEO, says she loved it, and says she once asked him to mentor her and he declined. (His published book is The Great CEO Within, subtitled The Tactical Guide to Company Building.)
- **Taught in sources:** S031.

### Scaling People (Claire Hughes Johnson, 2023)
- **Core thesis:** As she frames it, the modern successor to High Output Management.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** None named. In 2023 she said she was pushing it on her team and attributed it to the Stripe CEO (the author was Stripe's chief operating officer).
- **Taught in sources:** S001.

### Pour Your Heart Into It (Howard Schultz, 1997)
- **Core thesis:** Schultz stepped back into Starbucks when it was failing and turned it around.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** The founder returning to run the business when it needs it; she calls Schultz one of her idols (S041). Elsewhere she uses his story of 17 years without profit to argue that money comes late and then all at once (S066), and that the front line, like the barista who came up with the Frappuccino, holds product ideas (S087).
- **Taught in sources:** S041; Schultz also in S048, S066, S087.

### John Maxwell (title not stated)
- **Core thesis:** Leadership is caught, not taught.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Demonstrate before you delegate (S028); the leader adjusts the sails rather than complaining about the wind (S060).
- **Taught in sources:** S028, S060.

### John Mackey's book on Whole Foods (title not stated)
- **Core thesis:** Make a decision only when it is a win for customers, employees, community, and investors.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** The win-win-win test: how the company, the employees, and partners each win, with the long-term win stated openly when the short term cannot deliver it (S002, S093).
- **Taught in sources:** S002.

### A book by the CEO of Blackstone (title not stated)
- **Core thesis:** As she reads it, a big company is as easy to build as a small one.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** A bigger vision recruits better talent, which is why her vision must be big enough for others' visions to fit inside.
- **Taught in sources:** S003.

### The Culture Code (Daniel Coyle, 2018)
- **Core thesis:** The core leadership trait is not love or control but safety; people who feel safe perform better.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Leaders who learned fear as a language must build safety on purpose; it shaped her view that entrepreneurs are comfortable with chaos while their teams are not.
- **Taught in sources:** S005.

### Exponential Organizations (Salim Ismail, 2014)
- **Core thesis:** Fast-growing companies share a massive transformational purpose.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** The idea behind her desired superior state (DSS) for setting a company's 3 to 5 year direction. She says she read it around 2010.
- **Taught in sources:** S005.

### Albert Ellis and Steven Hayes (behavioral psychology)
- **Core thesis:** You can change how you act regardless of why you started, and having anxiety is not the problem; labeling it as wrong is.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Change the behavior, not the root cause; act as the person you want to become before you believe it (credited to Hayes); process over emotion when deciding. She names Ellis's How to Control Your Anxiety Before It Controls You (S014), says she has read nearly all his books (S006), and prefers older psychology texts as less commercial (S070). She reads psychology or behavioral science most days rather than business books (S013).
- **Taught in sources:** S006, S014, S070.

### The Comfort Crisis (Michael Easter, 2021)
- **Core thesis:** Modern comfort makes people weaker, so seek discomfort on purpose.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Comfort creep, and stair-stepping toward bigger discomforts. She describes the author as a friend.
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S062.

### Early reading: Rich Dad Poor Dad (Robert Kiyosaki, 1997), Tony Robbins, and Jim Rohn
- **Core thesis:** Money is a learnable skill, and early in a career you work to learn, not to earn.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** Work to learn when young; reframe a negative association with money; pour time into education rather than entertainment. She now calls Robbins and Rohn probably outdated for a younger audience (S004).
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S005, S008; Robbins and Rohn also in S097, S098.

### Books on meetings (titles not spoken)
- **Core thesis:** Hosting meetings is a learnable skill.
- **Key frameworks she uses:** After realizing she spent most of her time in meetings without ever reading a book on running them, she studied the topic and reduced it to pulse, purpose, and preparation, choosing a useful model over a complete one.
- **Taught in sources:** S040.
