# Positions: Leila Hormozi

Built from the 20 batch notes over 107 video sources (about 913,000 words): 48 dated uploads from 2021-07 to 2026-10 and 59 undated ones (her channel, Build episodes, live Q&As, vlogs, and long interviews). 40 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and upload month; undated sources say "undated" (see `sources.md`). Where she changed her mind or contradicts herself, both versions sit side by side and the newer dated one is marked. Topics with thin evidence say so.

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Pricing is mostly a courage problem. Founders underprice out of guilt or fear of a no, and the fix is usually to raise the price and hold it. Offers and pricing are Alex's lane more than hers (inferred from S051, S055, S062).
- Guilt drives low prices: business owners let the guilt of a few customers who cannot afford a new price set pricing for the whole business (S055, undated, paraphrase).
- A portfolio CEO agreed the price was too low and still sat on it for six weeks because it was not his idea; once he saw it, he raised it in two days and the business doubled (S062, undated).
- Pay tracks difficulty: rule ten of her money rules is that the harder the problem you solve, the more money you make (S066, undated, paraphrase).
- With affiliates or ambassadors, always set a price floor: "you can charge anything above this amount" (S093, undated).

## Discounting
- **Stance:** Discounting is usually playing not to lose. Ask whether the decision comes from strength or fear.
- "When you play not to lose, you feel like you shrink." (S051, undated)
- She treats discounting as a rejection-avoidance habit: if you cannot handle a no, you never learn whether full price works (S051, undated, paraphrase).
- A fully booked hairdresser who complained about the workload but would not raise prices is her example of chosen scarcity disguised as caution (S051).

## Raising money and debt
- **Stance:** Grow to the organic max before using leverage. Money hides undisciplined problem solving. Raising is not a default yes.
- "it takes someone very disciplined to not solve problems with just money. And if you solve problems with just money, the company's basically built on stilts." (S008, 2022-07)
- "our method of growing a company is basically we want to take it to its organic max before we take it to its basically like leveraged maximum" (S008, 2022-07)
- Whether to raise depends on the end goal, whether you want to own 100%, and whether you can manage multiple stakeholders; founders with prior exits do it better (S094, undated, paraphrase).
- Investors buy the team, not the founder: show who runs the company when you are absent (S005, 2026-04, paraphrase).
- **On debt:** She will not ask portfolio founders to grow on debt before the organic max, and she counts the non-cash debt that fast growth piles up.
- Uncomfortable putting portfolio companies through debt-financed growth before organic max; thin-margin companies that take capital early rarely have enough left to reinvest in talent (S008, 2022-07, paraphrase).
- Management debt is her name for the people problems you know about and avoid: "management debt is when you start off in your small business and then as you continue to scale you know that there's some people that probably aren't the right fit." (S105, undated)
- Personal finance first: wipe debt, then build what she calls "an oh [ __ ] fund", then take risks (S066, undated, paraphrase).

## First hires and when to hire
- **Stance:** Get consistent revenue first, ask whether the need is a person, a process, or a project, and never hire out of panic or for launch-week demand.
- "Do not do that. You are just going to add extra overhead and inconsistency." (S019, 2026-02), on hiring before revenue is consistent.
- "Like you need to get consistent lead flow and sales locked in before you hire a bunch of people." (S019, 2026-02)
- "is it a person is it a process or is a project" (S032, 2024-07)
- "they overhire because there's short-term pain that you solve with a long-term solution" (S032, 2024-07)
- "you cannot just offload tasks. You need to create repeatable processes that somebody else can do the thing without you." (S019, 2026-02)
- Her first executive assistant came six months into the consulting model, with four employees and everything falling on her; "my life was immensely more simple" afterward (S107, undated).
- **Contradiction on order:** "I actually didn't hire the executive team first. I hired doers and then I hired managers." (S088, undated) versus hire the leader of each function before the doers because it avoids managerial debt (S050, undated, paraphrase). **Read:** doers first while you can still teach the work; leader first in functions you do not know (S031, S092).

## Founder involvement in hiring
- **Stance:** The founder stays in the loop, including the last interview, until proven leaders can hold the culture. She stayed involved at $40M to $50M.
- **Over time:**
  - 2021-12: the CEO runs the gatekeeper interview until a capable C-suite exists; founders who abdicate below 20 to 30 people stall there (S048, paraphrase).
  - 2022-06: "i am dedicated to making sure that we have the right team and i know how important it is" (S039), after delegating hiring and watching culture crumble within about five months.
  - 2023-01: the CEO takes the last interview at any company under about 1,000 people (S003, paraphrase).
  - Undated: "our company was doing 40 50 million and i'm like i'm still involved in hiring people." (S105)
  - Undated: for the biggest-constraint executive hire, do the outreach yourself: "I'm going to coming like guns blazing and I'm going to message everyone" (S068)
- **Read:** the CEO-as-gatekeeper rule holds; only the scope shrinks with scale (inferred from S039, S048, S105).

## Hiring process, speed, and onboarding
- **Stance:** Run talent like a sales funnel, move within days, test skills rather than read resumes, and treat the interview as the ceiling.
- Her retelling of what her mentor Bob explained to her: "the talent acquisition funnel is the mirror to every business if you want to keep clients coming in the front door then your talent needs to continue to come in the back door" (S095, undated)
- "the average hiring process takes 23 days the top candidates find a job in eight so speed is king during this process" (S095, undated)
- Volume: management roles at Acquisition.com draw at least 500 applications before a hire: "that is how we are so picky minimum of 500" (S095, undated).
- "present the problem you had last quarter ask them how they would solve it literally you could do that with any role" (S037, 2023-08)
- "the interview is the best you're gonna get from this person...that is the 120 trying." (S106, undated)
- "likeness blinds you from incompetence." (S013, 2026-02). Likable candidates get a second, technical interview and a weighted scorecard (S013).
- "if you wouldn't rely on your network to get clients then why would you rely on it to build your team" (S037, 2023-08)
- **On onboarding:** Most early failures are onboarding failures. Put far more into it than feels necessary.
- "onboarding starts the moment that they sign the job offer." (S104, undated)
- "almost every time somebody has not succeeded in one of my companies...it's because they've had poor onboarding." (S104, undated)
- "put uh 10 times more effort into onboarding somebody than I would ever thought was possible" (S031, 2024-05)
- Talk to new hires twice a day for two weeks, set 30-60-90 goals on day one, give critical feedback in week one: "trust is built through adversity." (S104, undated)
- New hires keep 60 days of observations on what works, what does not make sense, what drives ROI, and what slows the team (S089, undated, paraphrase).

## Culture fit versus skill and experience
- **Stance:** Character and values first; train the skill. Judge skills and rate of progress, not age, tenure, or brand-name resumes.
- "it doesn't matter how skilled they are. If they don't fit the values, they just can't work here." (S008, 2022-07)
- "you don't hire people for experience you hire people for skill experience is only a proxy to skill" (S069, undated)
- "it is harder to teach somebody to be honest than it is to teach somebody to film a video" (S070, undated)
- "I'm purely a performance merit-driven CEO. I don't care about your age. I don't care about the years of experience." (S021, 2025-06)
- Her $300,000 lesson: a big-tech CTO hired on resume, never skills-tested (S069).
- **Tension:** where she cannot train the function, she hires for experience: "what roles do I have no effing clue what they require or how to do them that I need to hire for experience." (S092, undated). In small companies: "it makes more sense to get good at picking people than it does training people" (S094, undated).

## Executives and operators
- **Stance:** The operator sets the ceiling. Hire the type the stage needs (builder in chaos, incremental improver at $10M to $50M), tie them to revenue, and remove any operator who undermines the founder.
- "if your operator's not a 10 out of 10 your business isn't going to be a 10 out of 10" (S099, undated)
- "the best operators simplify through elimination" (S099, undated)
- Never hire corporate operators before $10M; they add bureaucracy (S099, undated, paraphrase).
- Under about $30M to $50M, hire player-coaches: "in small businesses the coach has to go in when the players hurt and they also sometimes need to be in the game no matter what." (S106, undated)
- Executive pay: about 1% equity for an inexperienced executive and about 4% for a very experienced one, on a one-year cliff, with a growth-plus-profit incentive (S068, undated, paraphrase).
- Before signing, ask why this person would be fired in 60 days and de-risk each reason (S068, undated, paraphrase).

## Firing
- **Stance:** Diagnose first (your own part, wrong seat, wrong season, wrong boss), then decide fast and say little. Good leaders hold on too long.
- **Over time:**
  - 2021-09: she fired too quickly in the first year and got a reputation for it; now she runs a diagnose-before-fire check (S045, paraphrase). "My outcome is either I coach them up and they rise to the occasion or I coach them out and they cannot tolerate you know the requests I'm making of them" (S045)
  - Undated: "the biggest mistake i ever made was not firing people soon enough" (S098)
  - Undated: "if you're a great leader which i think a lot of people that watch this channel are you probably hold on way too long." (S105)
  - 2026-09 (newest dated): "putting in more effort, putting in more hours, putting in more work, coaching somebody more, giving them more feedback will not fix a problem where they are not in the right role or where they are misaligned." (S015)
- "the goal of a company is not to keep everybody. it is to keep the right people at the right time." (S015, 2026-09)
- Catch fit early: "if you don't catch it in the first like 90 days, it's likely that person can just stay because you just acclimate to their wherever the fit isn't right." (S022, 2025-04)
- Extinction curve: if someone's improvement rate is slower than the business can bear, make the call (S031, 2024-05, paraphrase; the yelling employee had 52 incidents in a year).
- **Read:** her advice cuts both ways. Early on she fired too fast; later she warns that strong leaders fire too slowly. The constant is: diagnose, then act (inferred from S045, S098, S105, S015).

## Layoffs and restructuring
- **Stance:** Own the mistake out loud, meet people one by one, pay more severance than is comfortable, and place them elsewhere.
- "severance package line them up with interviews letters of recommendation meet with everybody individually" (S030, 2024-08)
- "how you let them go is going to say it it will it will translate into how you feel about yourself as a leader for the next however long" (S030, 2024-08)
- Gym Launch went from about 120 people to 55 at the same revenue after she fired nearly the whole leadership team: "you can have the same revenue with half the team and the reason for that is just the standards change." (S102, undated)
- She told those leaders it was her fault: "it's my fault i'm firing you because i'm [ __ ] inexperienced" (S102, undated)

## Business partners
- **Stance:** Shared mission and values, clean lanes, autonomy inside them, and no move without agreement. Complementary skills, not a second copy of you.
- "if you don't agree, don't move forward" (S008, 2022-07), advice she and Alex were given early on. Still her rule in 2025, where she credits it to a preacher who met with them: "if you don't agree don't move forward" (S012, 2025-01)
- "if we weren't in a relationship, how would we run the business? That's just what we did" (S008, 2022-07)
- Contracts follow character: "if I wouldn't do a handshake deal with somebody and I wouldn't trust them to like say I had kids raise my kids babysit my kids to step in and run my company for a day I shouldn't do a deal with them" (S012, 2025-01)
- 2025-07: a president or partner is worth it only when mission, values, and personal trust all line up, which is rare (S023, paraphrase). Chiron took finance, legal, real estate, and tech, lanes unlike hers and Alex's (S013, 2026-02, paraphrase).
- Confirm founders agree on the exit goal before building together (S096, undated, paraphrase).

## Equity
- **Stance:** Equity is for people who create without being managed. Grant it on a cliff, never on day one, and clean up a bad early grant now.
- "Barrels are people who can take something. They can create something out of nothing. They require little instruction and all you need to do is keep giving them ammunition." (S025, 2025-03)
- "this kind of situation only gets worse with time, not better." (S025, 2025-03), on over-allocated equity.
- "I would never bring in an executive and say like you get 4% on D one I'd say like cool we have a one-year cliff" (S068, undated)
- Do not trade equity for one skill: "it's kind of like marrying somebody because because they're good in bed" (S036, 2023-08)

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Maximize what works before adding anything; trade rather than add. Her own Prestige Labs launch is the cautionary case.
- "don't look for the next opportunity until you've maximized the one you're in" (S027, 2024-12)
- "strategy is not saying yes strategy is saying no to 99.9% of opportunities so that you can say yes to the one thing that moves the needle the most" (S075, undated)
- "most companies die by indig gestion not starvation" (S026, 2024-12)
- 2026-10 (newest): "Solving through addition is easy. It feels good temporarily. It is never the right answer. The best CEOs, the best business owners, the best operators solve through subtraction." (S017). And "we haven't earned the right to do more here." (S017)
- On Prestige Labs beside Gym Launch: "it is a completely different business and it was very hard to do both" (S072, undated). She says the combined result would have been two to three times larger with focus (S075, paraphrase).
- A second division is fine only if it feeds the first: "can you create a flywheel right two systems that feed each other and make each other better rather than two separate systems" (S073, undated)
- For a sale, she wants concentration low: a single client at 35% to 40% of revenue is a liability (S027, 2024-12, paraphrase).

## Side projects and new ventures
- **Stance:** Solve the biggest problem before chasing the biggest opportunity, test cheaply, set a kill date, and cut flops without sunk-cost guilt. Leaders with side businesses drift.
- "have I solved my biggest problem before tackling my biggest opportunity" (S026, 2024-12)
- "surefire way to fail is to try and pursue all of them." (S053, undated), on three ideas at once.
- "if it didn't become profitable within 4 months, we would shut it down." (S008, 2022-07)
- Leaders should be fully committed with no side projects; two employees who promised to stop drifted back around month five or six (S069, undated, paraphrase).
- Sunk cost is the main reason leaders avoid decisions; ask what you would do starting from scratch today (S016, 2026-09, paraphrase).

## Niche
- **Stance:** One product, one avatar, one channel until it works; saying no to the wrong customers is the best move at the prioritize stage.
- Phase 1 is one product, one avatar, one channel, with week-over-week unit consistency (S094, undated, paraphrase); the same three-stage rule appears in S005 (2026-04).
- Saying no to customers who are not your avatar is what lifts businesses past $3M to $5M (S019, 2026-02, paraphrase).
- "rather than asking yourself what Market do I start with ask yourself what customer do I know best" (S094, undated)

## Content and personal brand
- **Stance:** Make fewer, useful pieces that build authority. A personal brand bridges zero to one; after that the product has to market itself.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-07: uses social media only to give value and will trade social growth for team priorities (S008, paraphrase).
  - 2023-08: views are a vanity metric; brand means accepting fewer views short term (S001, paraphrase).
  - 2024-05: she avoided content for about four years out of fear of judgment and says it cost her (S007, paraphrase); S062 (undated) puts it at three years and tells the Bear Lake restart.
  - 2025-01: "I look at a personal brand as like Bridging the Gap um or having audiences Bridging the Gap to get from Zer to one but then to get from 1 to 10 I would say the thing needs to Market itself" (S012)
  - 2026-02: after cutting filming to one day every two weeks for health, the channel grew faster (S013, paraphrase).
  - 2026-10 (newest): views and authority are different; without authority content you look like an influencer, not an expert (S017, paraphrase).
- "The job of everybody in here is to help someone make a decision." (S051, undated)
- The cost of entry: "you will get [ __ ] ripped apart online. If you are not okay with that and you want to avoid it, do not do this. You are not going to be cut out for it." (S051, undated)

## Paid ads and lead generation
- **Stance:** Not her lane. Prove volume on one channel first, and never buy your way around a quality problem.
- "100 actions towards lead generation every day for 100 days." (S019, 2026-02)
- At Gym Launch, referrals fell from 45% of sales; running more ads instead of fixing quality pushed referrals to 20% and satisfaction down (S029, 2024-09). Her takeaway, a quote she found and adopted: "you have to get better and let your customers demand you get bigger" (S029)
- Referrals: ask three times, write the message for the customer, and pay a fee at or slightly above CAC (S073, undated, paraphrase).
- **Read:** her lead-gen advice is thin and borrowed from Alex; defer to a marketing seat on channel questions (inferred from S019, S029).

## Product versus marketing
- **Stance:** If the sales and marketing machine works and you still are not growing, the product is the problem.
- "if you have built a sales and marketing machine and you are still not growing fast enough it is probably because of your product not your sales and your Marketing" (S087, undated)
- "you have product Market fit when [ __ ] feels easy" (S087, undated)
- "if you have the right product you barely need to like Market it so like fix the product you know and then it'll mark it for itself" (S097, undated)
- $24,000 in a short test is not product-market fit; real fit takes hundreds of thousands or millions in revenue (S053, undated, paraphrase).
- Copied products cannot have fit because the demand is already met (S087, undated, paraphrase).

## Burnout

- "how so many more people between 1 million to 3 million to to even 10 million burn out than somebody that's has a $50 million business" (S011, 2024-08)
- "a lot of the times people are burned out because they're not feeling the reward from their work in some way" (S030, 2024-08)
- "often times when they're burnt out, I see it's often because of not feeling like you have autonomy over your own destiny." (S010, 2026-06)
- Undated: people burn themselves out by staying in the execution spiral (S067, paraphrase); one head of marketing trained her environment to keep giving her more by always saying yes (S054).

## Selling the company
- **Stance:** Always keep the option, sell from strength, build a team that runs without you, and do not tell the team until it closes.
- "you don't need to sell your business but you need to have the option" (S027, 2024-12)
- "best time to sell your business is often when you don't want to sell your business" (S027, 2024-12)
- "if I were to disappear for a month would my business decline maintain or grow and if it grows you have a really strong team" (S027, 2024-12)
- "most investors do not want to buy a business or invest in a business where one person makes the majority of the decisions" (S094, undated)
- "the terms that you get in the beginning are the best they are ever going to get." (S101, undated). "don't tell your team too early." (S101)
- Regret: with her current skills she would never have sold Gym Launch (S006, 2023-10, paraphrase). The price differs between retellings (see `stories.md`).

## Culture and values
- **Stance:** Culture is the founder's behavior repeated and what the leader tolerates. Keep three values, define them as behaviors, and enforce them.
- "Culture is not what you say, it's what you tolerate, it's what you enforce." (S056, undated)
- "culture is what people do when a leader is not present" (S074, undated)
- "if you lead your company with rules then you create rule followers if you lead your company with values then you create leaders" (S034, 2024-05)
- "behavior is the proxy for values" (S069, undated)
- Three values at Acquisition.com: "unimpeachable character sincere candor competitive greatness like those are the three things that we look for in people" (S098, undated). They took about 12 hours to write (S103, paraphrase).
- "growth actually does not fix broken systems or broken culture, it actually multiplies all of those things." (S056, undated)
- "companies die from the inside out they implode more than they explode" (S060, undated), a line she credits to a mentor.

## Metrics
- **Stance:** Measure so people can see whether they are winning; set incentives only after you know average; leading indicators beat lagging ones.
- "it's understanding that accountability doesn't exist without measurement" (S003, 2023-01)
- "you cannot incentivize people to do better until you know what average is" (S026, 2024-12). She puts that at about 18 to 24 months of history (S026).
- "the leading indicator is like LTV to CAC ratio which is the lifetime value of a customer and the cost to acquire a customer" (S012, 2025-01)
- Low accountability shows up as low revenue per head, hidden expenses, weak upsells, and high churn (S102, undated, paraphrase).
- North-star metrics should be NPS and customer and employee satisfaction, not only leads and conversions (S029, 2024-09, paraphrase).

## Competition
- **Stance:** Collaborate where you can, be about 20% different, never copy, and keep your eyes on your own work.
- "people who would be considered my competition I would rather be friends with them and to a degree I help them at times because I also like having competition because it keeps me sharp" (S012, 2025-01)
- You only need to be about 20% different on price, promotion, or product (S027, 2024-12, paraphrase).
- "the grass is greener where you water it not where you watch it" (S066, undated)
- "the greatest threat to your business is not your competition it's your unresolved conflict with yourself and other people" (S026, 2024-12)

## Timing, the economy, and planning horizon
- **Stance:** Conditions, not dates, decide timing. Plan in short horizons and reassess often. Her stated horizon varies by source.
- "it's not about time it's conditions" (S026, 2024-12)
- "efficiency is important adaptability is imperative" (S043, 2022-01)
- Businesses take three to five years to become predictable (S053, undated, paraphrase).
- **Horizon, by source:** a 3-to-5-year desired state (S005, 2026-04); three years as a stretch with focus on the next quarter (S063, undated); one year built back from a three-year vision, reassessed weekly (S064, undated); two years at most (S051, undated); a 10-year vision worked back to three years and three months (S070, undated). A big 10-year vision repels talent at a small stage; cast a credible 3-year one (S092, undated, paraphrase).
- "ditch rigid 5 10 year plans. Anyone who I know who hits their five or 10 year plans is probably a really [ __ ] lame plan." (S064, undated)

## Compensation
- **Stance:** Anchor pay where top people expect it, trade base for uncapped performance pay as seniority rises, and lead with growth, not money.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-09: "top of the market is the best way to retain people" (S038)
  - 2023-08: "if you pay people now based on the cash you have available now you screw yourself in two and three and four years" (S036)
  - 2025-01: "a flat salary is like lazy way of compensating people" (S012), said of creators and editors.
  - 2025-02 (newer): "great talent knows their value if your compensation doesn't reflect that they won't even show up at the table." (S024). Example: a CTO base of $450,000 with an uncapped bonus tied to growth (S024).
- Watch for misaligned incentives: a profit share tells people to stop investing when you need growth (S086, undated, paraphrase).
- **Read:** her emphasis moved from top-of-market pay (2022) to growth first with performance-weighted pay (2024 to 2025).

## Why talent joins and stays
- **Stance:** Growth is the lead offer, the vision must hold each person's own vision, and most churn is the employer's fault.
- "I actually believe that our number one value proposition is growth the amount of growth that people have personally and professionally within our company is absolutely our competitive advantage." (S024, 2025-02)
- "you have to have a vision big enough that other people's visions for their own lives can fit inside of it" (S003, 2023-01)
- "most churn, it's not good churn. It's just being irresponsible." (S038, 2022-09)
- "57% of employees who quit do so due to their boss management or leadership" (S080, undated)
- Turnover in fast growth is structural: "most people from day one aren't there on day 657" (S050, undated). She tells teams most of them will not be there in five years (S043, 2022-01; S050).

## Promotion and the management track
- **Stance:** Promote for rate of progress and existing followership, keep most people on technical tracks, and under-title so you can hire above.
- "to spot a high potential leader you look for rate of progress not rate of performance" (S076, undated)
- "titles do not make leaders actions do look for those who already have followers in your company and mark them as Leaders or potential leaders" (S075, undated)
- "over promotion turns Talent into a liability and a bad fit in a good role is actually worse than no fit at all" (S026, 2024-12)
- "most tracks in a company should be technical tracks" (S026, 2024-12)
- The common mistake at the executive stage is "promoting say your best operators into leadership roles without teaching them how to lead." (S019, 2026-02)
- "hire and promote for humility fire for ego and pride" (S083, undated)
- Keep early titles small so you can bring someone in above without losing them (S050, undated, paraphrase).

## Career planning
- **Stance changed.** Earlier she built formal ladders and growth plans; by 2026 she says HR-run career planning in a growing company creates problems, and the path belongs to the manager.
- **Earlier:**
  - 2022-09: an employee growth plan with a clear 3-to-5-year path, training, feedback, stretch goals, and promotion criteria (S038, paraphrase).
  - 2023-01: competency maps with levels, skills, and pay per level (S003, paraphrase).
  - 2024-10: red, yellow, green skills mapped against the future role; "red is like this is our area of deficit that we should focus on" (S028)
- **Newer:**
  - 2026-10: after a bigger HR team announced formal career planning, people who had never asked began demanding it; adding the department created a problem that did not exist (S017, paraphrase).

## Feedback and hard conversations
- **Stance:** The conversations you avoid set the company's ceiling. Give one piece of feedback at a time, privately and fast, tied to the person's own goal, and judge it by changed behavior.
- "the gap between where you are and where you want to be in your business is the amount of hard conversations you're willing to have" (S046, 2021-07), a line she credits to Tim Ferriss.
- "avoidance of giving feedback is selfish" (S028, 2024-10)
- "if you give feedback to somebody the behavior doesn't change the feedback was not feedback" (S011, 2024-08)
- "if you want somebody to feel bad insult them if you want them to get better you critique them" (S080, undated)
- "I just tell them what to do instead and I anchor it to the goal you told me you want to be a CEO one day you did this here's what a CEO would do" (S072, undated)
- "if you feel emotional about something don't have the conversation until it goes away" (S090, undated)
- Do not take over a teammate's hard conversation; script and rehearse it with them (S096, undated; S013, 2026-02).

## Punishment versus positive reinforcement
- **Stance:** Both work; punishment is faster and costs loyalty, discretionary effort, and honesty. Negative reinforcement only for the immoral, unethical, or illegal.
- "punishment works better in the short term and positive reinforcement looks works better in the long term" (S035, 2023-11)
- "latency matters more than intensity" (S035, 2023-11)
- "the only time that I use negative reinforcement is when something is somebody does something immoral unethical or illegal" (S012, 2025-01)
- "one of the telltale signs I can have of a company that punishes is they are overstaffed" (S074, undated)
- "scared people hide problems" (S089, undated). Her finance director hid a $9M tax bill until the weekend before it was due (S089).
- **Limits she grants:** high-demand firms can punish and outrun turnover; the cost is still there (S035; S080, undated, Coinbase versus Neutrogena tenure).

## Accountability and micromanagement
- **Stance:** The CEO is the chief accountability officer. Weekly reports and follow-up are management, not micromanagement. Accountability is disseminated, never delegated.
- "the accountability formula is expectations plus measurement times reinforcement equals accountability" (S084, undated). Other versions use "times feedback" (S061, S102, undated).
- "you don't delegate this you disseminate this." (S102, undated)
- "this word needs to be burned to the [ __ ] ground you're managing people you're not micromanaging micromanaging would be if you followed up with them 10 times a day" (S032, 2024-07)
- "if you don't respond to the reports they will stop filling them out that's just the science of behavior" (S030, 2024-08)
- "the most effective way to measure accountability is when the person themselves your employee your teammate is the one doing the measuring not you" (S084, undated)
- Executives get held as accountable as frontline staff (S047, 2021-07, paraphrase).

## Delegation and the founder's own job
- **Stance:** Delegate decisions, not just tasks; follow up; and at scale transfer your judgment instead of taking problems back.
- "If you want to create doers, then give people tasks. If you want to create leaders, give people decisions." (S059, undated)
- "I can't just delegate and forget about it I have to delegate and follow up" (S032, 2024-07)
- "delegation is trading the time you spent doing the task for the time it takes to reinforce and reward it many forget this crucial step do not be one of them" (S075, undated)
- 2026-02 (newer): "You need to transfer your judgment to them." (S013), after people problems grew from one hour a day to four.
- "Your business is never going to outgrow your capacity to lead it." (S019, 2026-02)
- "if your leadership team needs constant managing, you don't have a leadership team. You have a liability team." (S064, undated)
- When a function fails because of a person, she goes in and runs it until it turns around, then hires (S031, 2024-05, paraphrase).

## The CEO seat and stepping back
- **Stance changed.** She once tied her goal to staying CEO and argued founders make the best CEOs; by 2025 to 2026 she added a partner, split the load, and moved herself to executive chairwoman.
- **Over time:**
  - 2022-05: replacing a founder with a professional CEO: "there's a lot of evidence to support that that's actually a really terrible idea" (S041)
  - Undated: founder-led companies have much higher success rates; being CEO is painful but she would not delegate it (S070, paraphrase).
  - Undated: do not step out too soon; the CEO stays in weekly leadership meetings, monthly all-hands, and monthly key check-ins (S105, paraphrase).
  - 2025-07: "What's the job of CEO? It's like vision, people, money." (S023). With every division rolling up to her, she brought in Chiron (S023).
  - 2026-09 (newest): "I moved from CEO to executive chairwoman, and it wasn't because I hated being CEO...it's just seasons." (S015)
- **Read:** she applies her own wrong-season test to herself, which makes her credible on succession; but her founder-led argument predates it.

## Meetings, communication, and transparency
- **Stance:** No fixed cadence; set it by activity and headcount, send information ahead, and over-communicate because the risk only runs one way.
- "pulse there's purpose and there's preparation" (S040, 2022-05)
- "you don't need to follow a cookie cutter system you can [ __ ] think for yourself" (S040, 2022-05)
- "nobody ever quits a job or a company because someone over communicated" (S100, undated), a line someone told her years ago that she repeats.
- "the average employee has to hear something seven times before they believe it before they believe it's important to the company" (S093, undated)
- Every job description ends with a communication cycle: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly (S093, undated, paraphrase).
- "communication itself was a greater predictor of success than individual intelligence or skill level of the team" (S100, undated), her summary of a study's conclusion.
- **On transparency:** Share context and numbers widely and own mistakes publicly. The one exception is a pending sale.
- "I think the only thing that builds trust quickly is transparency transparency and honesty" (S072, undated)
- She taught every manager to read the P&L against advisors' warnings; ownership rose and nobody demanded raises (S064, undated, paraphrase).
- "imagine if everybody else in the company had as much context as you do you think they could make better decisions for the company heck yeah" (S093, undated)
- "my transparency saved me with launch" (S030, 2024-08)
- **Exception:** "don't tell your team too early." (S101, undated), after telling a team about a sale two weeks early left them in limbo for two months.

## Decision speed and reversibility
- **Stance:** Unmade decisions are the risk. Test what is reversible, think hard only about what is not, and never let more time stand in for a better answer.
- **Over time:**
  - 2021-12: "inaction is a decision in itself" (S044). She credits Buffett: avoid dumb decisions rather than chase brilliant ones (S044).
  - 2024-09: "every unmade decision is an attention suck" (S029), a line from an early mentor she repeats.
  - Undated: "I was not decisive enough I caved based on all the opinions of other people my job is to decide things." (S072)
  - 2026-09 (newer): "an unmade decision is a huge area of risk in my company." (S016). "I have never seen a fast growing high velocity organization with slow decision makers." (S016). On layoffs, pivots, and failed AI-first moves at big companies: "that were hard to reverse and arguably bad decisions." (S016)
- Undated: "rushed actions create mistakes so we have to be very careful about the decisions we do make" (S097). **Read:** speed on reversible calls, care on foundations; she holds both.

## Change management
- **Stance:** Sell change, do not announce it; roll it out person by person and one piece at a time.
- "I always think sell don't tell" (S072, undated)
- "your job is not to protect people from change your job is to make it easier for your team to change" (S081, undated)
- "if I have a change I want to make in my company it's not about how it's going to affect the company it's about how it's going to affect every individual person" (S088, undated)
- Add one new system per month; adding everything at once guarantees rejection (S103, undated, paraphrase).
- Authority transfer is a process, not an event: pre-position the new leader, co-lead, edify them in public, then phase out (S092, undated, paraphrase).
