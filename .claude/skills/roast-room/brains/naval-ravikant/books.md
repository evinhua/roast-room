# Books: Naval Ravikant

Built from 19 extract batches over 86 usable sources (2005 to 2026). Summaries are in my own words; no book text was fetched.

Naval has written no books of his own. The Almanack of Naval Ravikant (2020) was compiled and edited by Eric Jorgenson from his tweets, podcasts, and interviews; it is not in the corpus, and its material lives in the sources below. In 2026 he says part of why he has not written a book is that it might feel fraudulent, since he does not see himself as enlightened, and part is laziness (S032). He gives his ideas away on purpose and says he will never charge for content, because good ideas should spread as far as possible (S069, S017). His thinking lives in the essay and podcast series below, then in the books he recommends.

He is wary of business books as a genre: he calls business biographies useless for building a great business, good only for inspiration, and says how-tos do not work for creative work beyond the basics (S039). He prefers original texts over summaries and old solutions for old problems (S001, S074), and treats books as disposable, reading many at once and dropping any without guilt (S074).

## His essays and podcast series

### How to Get Rich (tweetstorm, then podcast series; transcript dated 2019-12-28)
- **Core thesis:** Wealth comes from owning assets that earn while you sleep, built by applying specific knowledge with accountability and leverage, steered by judgment. You will not get rich renting out your time.
- **Key frameworks:** wealth versus money versus status; specific knowledge, accountability, leverage, judgment; labor, capital, and code and media as leverage; permissionless leverage; productize yourself; inputs versus outputs; aspirational hourly rate; long-term games with long-term people; escape competition through authenticity; four kinds of luck; principal-agent problem; avoid ruin. He calls it the bedrock he is sure about, with a Silicon Valley and tech bias (S001).
- **Taught in sources:** S001 (the full series), with episode cuts in S004, S005, S041, and S043; restated in S017, S030, S032, S063, S091, and S034.

### How to Angel Invest, Parts 1 and 2 (2019-11-17 and 2020-06-12), and the earlier "How to be an angel investor" (2009)
- **Core thesis:** Early-stage returns follow a power law, so access to the few winners and judgment about founders matter more than anything else. In 2009 he also framed angel investing as patronage of the next generation of founders (S056).
- **Key frameworks:** capital, judgment, dealflow; dealflow versus access; a brand as an authentic expression of who you are; power-law portfolio math; non-consensus and right; weird deals with one fatal flaw; first-time versus repeat founders; intelligence, energy, integrity; never forward a deal you are not in.
- **Taught in sources:** S024, S025, S056; echoed in S062, S080, S087.

### Venture Hacks and early nav.al essays (2005 to 2013)
- **Core thesis:** The internet cut the cost of starting a company so far that leverage, not talent, explains the speed of new fortunes, and the funding industry is restructuring around that. Founders should understand terms and keep control.
- **Key frameworks:** returns to entrepreneurship come from leverage; the 80-hour myth and measuring outputs; unquantifiable adoption risk for network businesses; VC as a bundle of advice, control, and money, and its unbundling; investors herd rationally; funding markets develop in reverse; seed is the new Series A; early hires as late founders; one way to start a startup (product-market fit as the gate); passion-market fit; how to pick a cofounder; conflicts of interest and NDAs; the fundable startup; a venture SLA; written outreach before meetings. AngelList grew out of Venture Hacks as a public service (S093).
- **Taught in sources:** S018 to S023, S044 to S061, S065.

### Naval Podcast short episodes (2019 to 2021)
- **Core thesis:** Short takes on compounding, building and selling, feedback from reality, and knowledge as the real resource.
- **Key frameworks:** long-term games with long-term people; learn to sell, learn to build; groups never admit failure, so prefer for-profits; knowledge makes resources effectively infinite; credibility moving from institutions to individuals; happiness as a learnable skill.
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S005, S013, S014, S015, S026, S036, S041, S042, S043.

### The Beginning of Infinity series and talks with David Deutsch (2021 to 2023)
- **Core thesis:** Good explanations are hard to vary; all progress comes from conjecture and error correction; wealth is the set of physical transformations you can bring about, and knowledge is most of it.
- **Key frameworks:** good explanations; error-correcting progress across science, evolution, and markets; rational optimism; ideas as the new oil; do not destroy the means of error correction; real-world tests over social approval.
- **Taught in sources:** S037, S038, S040, S072; applied to business in S028, S032, S080, S084.

### Naval Podcast, AI and building series (2025 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** AI is a motorcycle for the mind that hands huge leverage to people with taste, judgment, and agency. Small teams of geniuses can now build what once took companies, and pure software is no longer a moat.
- **Key frameworks:** curate people; the four things a founder cannot delegate; minimum-sized groups; good products are hard to vary; life in the arena and learning loops; waste tokens, save time; always the smartest model; humans as verifiers; pure software is uninvestable; sell the truth and walk from bad deals.
- **Taught in sources:** S002, S003, S006, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S012, S028, S029, S035 (S027 is a hiring notice with little content of his).

### Long interviews (2016 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** The same ideas in conversation, plus how he decides, spends time, and lives.
- **Key frameworks:** if you cannot decide, no; explore then exploit; iterations, not hours; asymmetric bets; three ways to retire; desire as a contract to be unhappy; change it, accept it, or leave it; aspirational hourly rate and low burn rate; zone of genius; the CEO's three jobs; judgment to taste.
- **Taught in sources:** Tim Ferriss (S016, S062, S063, S074), Joe Rogan (S017), The Knowledge Project (S031), BeerBiceps (S033), Modern Wisdom (S030), Internapalooza with Cory Levy (S034), Eric Jorgenson's megasode (S032), Pando (S088), the CEO talk (S089), AngelList and conference firesides (S080, S081, S085, S087), Balaji Srinivasan (S076, S084), Chris Dixon (S069), Clubhouse sessions (S090, S091), and podcasts on AI, politics, and risk (S066, S067, S070, S071, S035, S092).

## Books he recommends (business and decisions first)

- **The Beginning of Infinity and The Fabric of Reality (David Deutsch, 2011 and 1997):** Humans are universal explainers, all evils come from lack of knowledge, and good explanations are hard to vary. The one book he says changed his thinking, decision-making, and outlook in adult life. He takes from it his later definition of wealth, the hard-to-vary test for products, and conjecture and criticism as the learning loop. He says to start with the first three chapters and treat it like a year-long course. (S028, S029, S032, S037, S040, S070, S072, S092)
- **Skin in the Game (Nassim Taleb, 2018):** Those who take risk should bear the consequences, and systems stay honest only when they do. He calls it required reading and rereads it; it underpins his accountability and ruin rules. (S001, S032) Antifragile does not stick for him for lack of practical examples (S032); Fooled by Randomness is his reminder about survivorship bias (S001).
- **Influence (Robert Cialdini, 1984):** The six levers of persuasion. He says everyone should know them but stay honest using them, and uses them rarely himself; he got the point of the sequel Pre-Suasion without finishing it. (S016, S031, S062)
- **The Intelligent Investor (Benjamin Graham, 1949):** Cited through Buffett's apprenticeship with Graham as the model for paying to learn from the best. (S001)
- **How to Get Rich (Felix Dennis, 2006):** His favorite on the topic when young, credible because Dennis made his money elsewhere. (S001)
- **The Strategy of Conflict (Thomas Schelling, 1960):** Source of Schelling points, which he uses for coordination and negotiation. (S001)
- **Poor Charlie's Almanack (Charlie Munger):** Collected speeches and mental models; he loads up on models from evolution, game theory, and Munger but does not run checklists. (S031, S062)
- **The Company (a short history of the limited liability company):** Recommended by Chris Dixon and affirmed by Naval; limited liability unlocked capital formation at scale, which he compares to tokens. (S069)
- **The Art of Manipulation:** A study of con men and their tells: overselling, pushing deals too hard, talking about their own honesty. He uses it to read counterparties. (S031)
- **Original sources over summaries:** The Wealth of Nations and On the Origin of Species over any business or biology book; Feynman's stories (Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman); Thing Explainer and Thinking Physics as models of explaining from first principles. (S001, S031, S062, S074)
- **History:** The Lessons of History (Will and Ariel Durant), the short book he rereads instead of the full Story of Civilization (S016, S029, S031, S032); Sapiens (S031, S062); everything by Matt Ridley, which feeds his positive-sum view (S016).
- **Books he argues against or reads with care:** Guns, Germs, and Steel, which he calls neat but mostly false because creativity beats geography (S072); Superintelligence, to be read with the rebuttals (S031, a 2019 view); Gödel, Escher, Bach, which he admits he never finished (S037).
- **Science fiction:** Snow Crash, for how much it predicted (S062).
- **Off-topic for this seat (philosophy and inner life), listed for completeness:** Meditations, Seneca's letters, Schopenhauer's essays, Krishnamurti (Total Freedom, The Book of Life), Daniel Ingram's Mastering the Core Teachings of the Buddha, The Prophet, Siddhartha, Jed McKenna, Sam Harris's Lying, Bruce Lee's Striking Thoughts, Anthony de Mello, The Untethered Soul, Kapil Gupta's Direct Truth, Finite and Infinite Games, and Seven Brief Lessons on Physics. (S016, S029, S031, S032, S062, S063, S074)
