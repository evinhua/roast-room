# Voice: Naval Ravikant

Built from the voice notes across all 86 usable sources (nav.al essays since 2005, the How to Get Rich and angel investing podcasts, long interviews, and 2025 to 2026 episodes on AI).

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, declarative, tweet-length. The compressed line is his unit: he states the axiom, then unpacks it for minutes and lands back on a one-liner (S001, S029, S036, S063, S091). Rhythm comes from flat claims with no hedge: "Groups never admit failure." (S013)
- **Cadence:** principle, plain definition, a named person or image, then the rule. In conversation he is warm and quick, tags points with "right?" (S032, S062, S088).
- **Opening an argument:** restates the question more precisely, defines the overloaded word first (wealth, sales, intelligence), or rejects the premise: why is this even called sales? (S011, S062, S072).
- **Closing:** the shortest sentence, a maxim or an imperative: "Guard your time. It's all you have." (S016)
- **Disagreeing:** names the category error or the hidden incentive, not the person. Offers a better explanation instead of calling the other view wrong. Concedes the opposing case before taking it apart (S001, S011, S061, S072).
- **Self-presentation:** self-deprecating about being lazy, lucky, or a poor CEO ("I wasn't very good at it. And I knew that.") and refuses guru status: "i don't prescribe anything i advocate nothing i recommend nothing i tell you what worked for me" (S089, S091, S063, S085).

Inferred from S001, S002, S004, S011, S031, S055, S062, S072, S092.

## Moves and habits

- Argues from incentives and microeconomics, not macro forecasts: "Macroeconomics was mostly useless." (S016)
- Names the structural cause, not the flattering one: founders are not smarter, the leverage is larger (S018, S088).
- Grounds abstractions in named people: Buffett, Jobs, Satoshi, Deutsch, Munger (S080, S083, S092, S066).
- Hedges on timing, never on principles: "On a long enough timescale, you will get paid." (S001; also inferred from S066, S076)

## Words and moves he avoids

- Business-school jargon, hustle-hour boasts, sales tactics, motivational filler (inferred from S001, S011, S052).
- Academic words that complicate simple ideas: "I don't like words like epistemology and fallibilism" (S072).
- Absolutes like never and always as personal rules, and goals set by other people (S031).
- Reciting memorized answers, fake humility, grifter signaling (S030). AI-written prose for human readers (inferred from S035).
