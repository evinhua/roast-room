# Positions: Naval Ravikant

40 topics, built from the batch notes on the deep corpus (nav.al and startupboy posts 2005 to 2013, the How to Get Rich and angel investing series 2019 to 2020, nav.al essays and the Naval podcast 2021 to 2026, and podcasts, fireside chats, and Clubhouse rooms 2013 to 2026). Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (post date, or upload date for videos; see `sources.md`). Many video sources (S065 to S093) are undated in the table; where the title names a year it is given, otherwise the date says "undated". Where his view moved, the versions sit side by side with dates and the newer one is marked **(newer)**. Topics with no evidence say so. Auto-caption sources keep caption spelling. He warns that "the problem is everything is contextual. It's very hard to sit up here and say always do X, right?" (S088, undated), so treat these as his defaults, not a playbook.

## Leverage, equity, and the seat's question

### 1. Renting out your time versus owning equity
- **Stance:** You do not get rich selling hours. Own a piece of a product, a business, IP, or a brand. This is the most repeated line in the corpus, 2009 to 2026.
- **Evidence:**
  - His 2018 tweet, "You are not going to get rich renting out your time. You must own equity, a piece of the business to gain your financial freedom." (tweet read by Nivi, S001, 2019-12; Tim Ferriss reads it in S063, 2020-10; Naval quotes it himself in S024, 2019-11)
  - "you have to own a piece of a business you need to have equity either as an owner an investor shareholder or a brand that you're building that accrues to you to gain your financial freedom" (S017, 2019-06)
  - "you must own a piece of a business if you want a path to financial freedom there's literally no other way" (S091, 2021-01)
  - Why owners win: "the owners actually don't get anything guaranteed the owners do not get anything in front of insurance or in the form of predictability but they get all the upside" (S091, 2021-01)
  - "The ideal would be to make money with your mind, not with your time." (S063, 2020-10)
  - **(newer)** He upgraded his own definition of wealth. The old one: "my definition of wealth was assets that earn while you sleep" (S032, 2026-01). The new one, from David Deutsch: "knowledge is the big multiplier. It's not the capital." (S032, 2026-01)
- **Read:** Consistent for 17 years. The 2023 to 2026 version adds that the asset that matters most is know-how, with capital as stored potential: "wealth is a byproduct of knowledge" (S080, 2023) and "the vast, vast majority of wealth is actually knowledge, right?" (S084, undated).

### 2. Services, consulting, and high-paid hourly work
- **Stance:** Renting time is fine early, but only while you learn and save. A high rate is still rented time. Consulting puts your ideas through someone else.
- **Evidence:**
  - "You will need to rent your time to get started. This is only acceptable when you are learning and saving." (his tweet, read by Nivi, S041, 2020-01) His spoken gloss: "Try to learn something that people haven't quite figured out how to teach yet." (S041)
  - "Early on, find things that interest you and allow you to take on accountability. Don't worry about short-term compensation." (S041, 2020-01)
  - Repetitive work is a warning: "You want to avoid repetitive drudgery" (S041, 2020-01)
  - On the nine-to-five: "it is a modern trimmed down form of slavery it's wage slavery" (S091, 2021-01)
  - "the way to earn in the modern world is through knowledge and leverage it's not through work" (S091, 2021-01)
- **Read:** He calls consulting a trade-off that costs you direct accountability and visibility (S041). His ladder from laborer to contractor to developer to tech founder adds specific knowledge, accountability, and leverage at each rung (S001). He asks what a services business leaves you owning, not whether it is wrong (inferred from S001, S041).

### 3. Leverage: which kind
- **Over time:**
  - 2011-12: "The computer is the first tool since maybe the stone axe, that an individual can use to gain massive leverage, without permission from anyone else." (S055)
  - Undated (PandoMonthly): "there's just so much embedded leverage in the system now that a single person with a lever can truly move the earth" (S088)
  - 2019-06: "we live in an age of infinite leverage" (S017)
  - 2019-12: "Product and media are the new leverage." (S001)
  - 2021-01: "labor is the shittiest form of leverage" (S091)
  - 2025-08: the ladder adds AI on top: "Leverage is just force multiplier for your work." (S034) and "it's not that the rich are getting richer. It's that the smart and leveraged are getting richer." (S034)
  - **(newer)** 2026-02: "Software engineers, even the ones who are not necessarily tuning or training AI models, these are now among the most leveraged people on earth." (S002)
- **Read:** Permissionless leverage (code, media, now agents) first; capital second; labor last. With leverage, the size of each decision matters more than hours: "In an age of infinite leverage, judgment becomes the most important skill." (S001, 2019-12)

### 4. Specific knowledge and niche
- **Stance:** Be number one at something narrow that is authentic to you, and keep redefining the something until it is true. If it can be trained, it can be replaced.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Specific knowledge is found much more by pursuing your innate talents, your genuine curiosity, and your passion." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "If you really want to get paid in this world, you want to be number one at whatever you do. It can be niche" (S043, 2019-05)
  - "You want to be the best in the world at what you do, but keep redefining what you do until that's true." (S029, 2025-10; he cites his old tweet again in S002, 2026-02)
  - "one of the hallmarks of specific knowledge is that it will feel like play to you but it will look like work to others" (S091, 2021-01)
  - "you find your specific knowledge through action, by doing" (S029, 2025-10)
  - **(newer)** "There is no demand for average. The average app, nobody wants it." (S002, 2026-02)
- **Read:** In the AI era he adds that knowledge an AI can recite has no scarcity premium; only knowledge at the frontier pays (S084, undated, paraphrase in notes).

### 5. Accountability and putting your name on it
- **Stance:** Take public risk under your own name. Accountability is the one input you can take without permission.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Productize has specific knowledge and leverage. Yourself has uniqueness and accountability." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "you want to productize yourself into a business, and then you want to own that business." (S063, 2020-10)
  - "You can literally get paid for just being you." (S043, 2019-05)
  - He says you never get paid what you are worth without accountability (S091, 2021-01) and that accountability is permissionless, so take it first (S041, 2020-01).

## AI, code, and venture capital over time

### 6. AI
- **Over time:**
  - 2016-05: general AI far off, narrow AI such as computer vision real (S016, paraphrase in notes).
  - 2019-08: the singularity "is religion for nerds you know it's got all the same characteristics it's unfalsifiable until it happens" (S031)
  - 2020-10: GPT-3 completes text but does not understand it (S063, paraphrase).
  - 2023-02: of image and text models, "Of course, you are doing the intelligent part there by picking that one out." (S040)
  - 2023: "they're not actually intelligent they're just interpolating between existing data they aren't creating new knowledge" (S085)
  - Undated: "I call it a patchwork plagiarist" (S071) and "I think AI is a little over-named, but you know, it's natural language computing." (S084)
  - 2025-03: "every poem ever written by an LLM is garbage" (S030)
  - 2026-02: "Now you can literally program in English." (S002) and "I don't really worry about unaligned AI. I worry about unaligned humans with AI." (S002)
  - 2026-06: "human plus AI, we're in that era, and I'm betting we stay there longer than people think." (S003)
  - **(newer)** 2026-07: on his own podcast he appears to admit he was late: he assumed AI was like fusion and would never come, then it came fast (inferred from S035; the captions have no speaker labels and four people talk).
- **Read:** From AGI skeptic (2016 to 2024) to daily AI builder who will not forecast AGI (2026): "I don't want to be in the futurist business." (S009, 2026-05). The constant is that judgment, taste, and desire stay with the human.

### 7. Code and software as a business
- **Over time:**
  - 2009-11: "What used to cost $1M-$2M to set up, now costs $10K." (S018)
  - 2020-01: "This is why I encourage people to learn to code or produce media, even if it's just nights and weekends." (S041)
  - 2025-08: software is being commoditized by AI; deep tech has fewer competitors and better moats but lower odds (S034).
  - 2026-02: the 5 to 20 person software company filling an enterprise niche gets blown apart by vibe coding (S002).
  - 2026-04: "You truly can have one-person, two-person software companies now that can scale to millions upon millions of users and make billions upon billions of dollars." (S006)
  - **(newer)** 2026-04: "pure software is uninvestable. I would just full stop right there." (S006) and 2026-07: AI has now eaten software the way software ate the world, so software is a commodity (inferred from S035)
- **Read:** Code went from the best permissionless leverage (2009 to 2021) to a commodity as a product (2026), while knowing how software works got more valuable. Both hold: engineers are the most leveraged people, and a pure software company has no moat.

### 8. Using AI inside the business
- **Stance:** Use the smartest model, spend tokens to save your time, and stay the final gate. Do not send AI prose to humans.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Waste tokens, save time. Don't look at the tokens either as inputs or outputs. Just look at your time, and look at the final output." (S003, 2026-06)
  - "I think intelligence is an unalloyed good. You always want more intelligence." (S008, 2026-05)
  - He will pay for the smartest model on matters of judgment, because you are always applying leverage and your time is valuable (inferred from S035, 2026-07).
  - "This combo right now of human operator combined with state-of-the-art coding model can yield incredible results." (S006, 2026-04)
  - On writing: good writing comes from good thinking, so anything an AI drafts for a human reader should be compressed down by you first (inferred from S035, 2026-07).
  - "Humans are becoming verifiers." (S003, 2026-06)
- **Limit:** He cross-checks: "I actually run most of my queries, almost all actually, through four AIs and I'll always fact-check them against each other." (S002, 2026-02)

### 9. Raising money: whether and when
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-11: with unquantifiable adoption risk and no track record, bootstrap until adoption is proven, find a proxy, or find a solo angel who believes (S053).
  - 2010-04: "Iterate like crazy until you find product/market fit . If you don't find it, do not raise money, do not pass go. Start over." (S057)
  - Undated (PandoMonthly): "there are a lot of great businesses that can be built without funding but the moment you add funding it ruins them" (S088)
  - 2025-11: he will not take money from VCs he does not respect, and calls most VCs money managers (S007, paraphrase).
  - 2026-01: Apple, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Facebook, Tesla, and SpaceX were self-financed or broke out before needing much VC; round after round is a warning sign (S032, paraphrase).
  - **(newer)** 2026-05: "I measure my own excitement level, and when my own excitement level gets above a certain threshold, then I know I'm ready to go raise the money." (S011)
- **Read:** The gate is fit and conviction, not the calendar. **(newer)** In 2025 to 2026 he founds again with his own money and treats venture as optional (inferred from S007, S011, S032).

### 10. Investor terms, control, and boards
- **Stance:** Judge money by its terms, not its brand. Keep control. Pick the partner, not the firm.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Cash: you want this on the best terms possible. The brand is irrelevant here." (S049, 2005-12)
  - "The individual VC partner on your Board matters a lot more than the firm's name does, so write the partner's name into the termsheet." (S049, 2005-12)
  - "If you're moving around Powerpoint formats to please your Board members, you've already lost this one." (S049, 2005-12)
  - "whose money you're investing is less relevant than on what terms it comes on." (S022, 2010-12)
  - "If you have found product/market fit, raise money from high-quality people that you trust . Keep control ." (S057, 2010-04)
  - Angels give the help "with much less dilution and much less capital and, most importantly, much less control over your company." (S056, 2009-03)
  - His 2013 founder-friendly term sheet: 1x liquidation preference, no veto on arms-length deals, no option pool shuffle, and 10 percent of a round for founder liquidity (S051, 2013-06).
- **Valuation guardrail:** do not take money at a valuation above about a third of a modest exit, because investors can block exits below their return bar (S049, 2005-12).

### 11. How to approach investors
- **Stance:** Writing first, meetings last. No NDAs. Be exceptional at one thing.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Written always beats a video or screencast, since most intelligent people can read a lot faster than they can listen." (S023, 2010-04)
  - "Ask an investor to sign an NDA, and you've just filtered out all but the most desperate investors." (S061, 2010-07)
  - "ideas do not have the merit that we were all raised believing. You do have to pick the right space, but after that, execution is everything." (S061, 2010-07)
  - Be exceptional in one of traction, team, product, social proof, or pitch, rather than adequate at all (S065, undated summary).
  - The founder does it personally: his August 2025 tweet, "Founders can delegate everything except recruiting, fundraising, strategy, and product vision." (tweet read by Nivi, S007, 2025-11) He adds: "Recruiting is the most important thing because you need creativity; you need motivated people." (S007)

### 12. Debt
- **Stance:** Avoid it personally. In the capital stack, creditors get a fixed return and no upside.
- **Evidence:** An old tweet of his lists debt first: "Avoid debt, jail, addiction, disgrace, shortcuts, and media" (old tweet read by the host, S032). Naval's only change on hearing it was to one word: "I think I replaced shortcuts with sugar" (S032, 2026-01). In his claims hierarchy, creditors get fixed interest while owners get all the upside (S091, 2021-01). He tells nations to lever up with tools, not debt (S055, 2011-12, paraphrase).
- **Read:** No recorded position on venture debt or borrowing to fund a company. His ruin rule (topic 13) suggests he would refuse debt that can wipe you out (inferred from S001, S032).

### 13. Risk, ruin, and asymmetric bets
- **Stance:** Take many capped-downside bets with huge upside. Never risk ruin, and never through ethics.
- **Evidence:**
  - "The number one way people get ruined in modern business is not by betting too much; it's by cutting corners and doing unethical or downright illegal things." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "you don't want to invest money that you're not OK losing." (S025, 2020-06)
  - "Bitcoin for example it's an asymmetric bet if you lose you just lose your money if you win if it becomes digital gold you can make 50 times or 100 times your monies" (S083, undated)
  - "Your outcome scales more with the number of bets than the size of the bets." (S045, 2007-08)
  - "In 1,000 parallel universes, you want to be wealthy in 999 of them." (S001, 2019-12)
- **Read:** He admires Musk putting nearly all his PayPal money into SpaceX, Tesla, and SolarCity (S030, S032). His rule is about ruin relative to what you have, not about size (inferred from S001, S025, S030).

### 14. Pricing and discounting
- **Stance:** Thin evidence. He refuses to charge for ideas he wants to spread, and avoids pricing that puts you in a zero-sum game with your users.
- **Evidence:**
  - "if you have good ideas you want to spread them as far and wide as possible that's why i'll never charge for a piece of content" (S069, undated)
  - AngelList did not charge on the fundraising side because it would set up a zero-sum game with users and adverse selection (S087, 2013, paraphrase).
  - On negotiation: "money has karma, too. And uh if you treat it badly, it will treat you badly." (S088, undated) He leaves something on the table (S088).
- **Read:** No recorded position on raising prices, premium versus cheap positioning, or discounts.

### 15. Business size, market size, and lifestyle businesses
- **Stance:** For venture-scale work, pick a big market and expect power-law returns. But a great lifestyle business is a good outcome, not a failure, and should not take funding.
- **Evidence:**
  - Pick a big market; small markets cap upside regardless of execution (S057, 2010-04).
  - "The upside can be 100x, 1,000x, 10,000x bigger. So there's no point in fighting over the small pie before it's fully baked." (S011, 2026-05)
  - "99 out of 100 start ups effectively fail" (S081, 2019)
  - "you learn very quickly that all the returns in this business are made with a huge, huge outcomes, at least for an investor." (S062, 2018)
  - Lifestyle businesses: "We would all love to have great lifestyle businesses" (S088, undated). See topic 9 on funding ruining them.
- **Read:** The fork he puts to a founder is honest: a venture bet needs a market where 100x is possible; a business you love that throws off cash should stay unfunded (inferred from S057, S088).

### 16. What to build and where the moat is
- **Over time:**
  - 2006-02: near-zero cost plus network effects plus category critical mass, on Craigslist: "It's a supercharged monopoly in the making." (S046)
  - 2019-12: network effects, zero marginal cost of replication, and scale economies compound leverage (S001, paraphrase).
  - Undated: prefer technical risk over market risk: "you want to build something that if people get it if you can deliver it you know people will want it and it's just hard to build" (S070)
  - Undated: in winner-take-all markets, "when I win I own the whole market and I can raise prices" (S070)
  - 2025-09: "Good products are hard to vary ." (S028)
  - **(newer)** 2026-04: "If you're a venture investor now, you're looking for hardware, you're looking for network effects, you're looking for AI models." (S006)
- **Read:** As software got cheap, he moved the moat to hardware, network effects, design taste, and model training. Product simplicity is part of it: "you just cannot make a product that's simple enough." (S007, 2025-11)

## People

### 17. Co-founders
- **Stance:** Two founders with a history, one builder and one seller, aligned motives, and no compromise on intelligence, energy, and integrity.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Picking a co-founder is your most important decision. It's more important than your product, market, and investors." (S059, 2009-11)
  - "The ideal founding team is two people, with a history of working together, of similar age and financial standing, with mutual respect." (S059, 2009-11)
  - "You wouldn't marry someone you'd just met. Date first." (S059, 2009-11)
  - "If one founder wants to build a cool product, another one wants to make money, and yet another wants to be famous, it won't work." (S059, 2009-11)
  - "If you can't see yourself working with someone for life, then don't work with them for a day." (S001, 2019-12)
  - **(newer)** "the best way to match I think honestly is through work. It is not through uh shaking hands or through talking." (S034, 2025-08)
- **Breakups:** "If you're going to fall out with your co-founder, do it early, recover the equity into the option pool to keep the company going, and recruit someone else great to fill the missing slot." (S059, 2009-11)

### 18. Equity for early people
- **Over time:**
  - 2009-03: the best advisors are already successful and need a meaningful stake, not token grants (S056).
  - 2010-12: engineer equity expectations rise as their opportunity cost rises (S044, paraphrase).
  - 2011-12: "Your first two engineers? They're just late founders. Treat them as such." (S019) and "Close the equity gap, and hiring will get a lot easier." (S019)
  - Undated: recruit founders who failed through no fault of their own and be very generous with equity (S089, paraphrase).
  - **(newer)** 2025-11: "We break every rule." (S007) His 19-year-old at the new startup is paid and treated the same as senior people (S034, 2025-08).

### 19. Hiring bar and recruiting
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-11: "Nothing damages a startup like a mediocre and reliable performer." (S052)
  - 2019-12: "I almost won't start a company, or hire a person, or work with somebody if I just don't think they're into what I want them to do." (S001)
  - 2023: "people who are low ego take agency solve problems don't have to thump their chest about it and are just professionals they just get stuff done" (S080)
  - Undated: "Don't hire good people who make you miserable." (S089)
  - 2025-08: "I fire people much much faster. uh hire people much much more slowly." (S034)
  - **(newer)** 2025-11: "Collect geniuses. Warehouse them. You'll never regret it." (S007) and "Recruiting is the most important thing because you need creativity; you need motivated people." (S007)
- **Limit he names:** "You're lucky if you can hire one genius a month." (S007, 2025-11)

### 20. Pay
- **Stance:** Pay well and expect far more.
- **Evidence:** "pay twice as much but but expect 10 times the output" (S034, 2025-08). In creative work, he says, the right decision is worth far more than the wrong one, so the premium is cheap (S034).

### 21. Firing
- **Over time:**
  - 2009-11: break up with a co-founder early (S059).
  - Undated: "capitalism works because it makes you fire your best friend" (S071)
  - 2025-08: he fires much faster now (S034, see topic 19).
  - **(newer)** 2026-05: he admits he is historically bad at firing, leans on co-founders, and only fires once convinced the person will be more effective elsewhere (S011, paraphrase in notes).
- **Read:** He preaches fast firing and admits he is bad at doing it himself.

### 22. Team size, culture, and management
- **Over time:**
  - Undated (PandoMonthly): "the actual efficient size of a company is shrinking very rapidly. And so the future will be almost all startups" (S088)
  - 2025-11: he endorses Vinod Khosla's line: "I think Vinod's phrase is absolutely correct: the team you build is the company you build" (S007) and "Scale is your enemy." (S007)
  - 2025-11: "Early teams do look like cults. They are monomaniacal; they are weird; but they're all kind of weird in a similar way." (S007)
  - 2026-05: "I actually hate organizational management because I hate organizations." (S009) He runs a "fully interconnected graph" with a light hub (S009).
  - 2026-05: "Management is telling people what to do, and leadership is making them want to do it." (S011)
  - **(newer, economy level)** 2026-06: "you don't need to spend twenty years learning one thing before you can contribute. That barrier going down means generalists are having a field day." (S003)
- **Read:** Small, flat, founder-shaped, and everyone a "barrel" who sets direction rather than a "bullet" who takes orders (S034, 2025-08).

### 23. Integrity, partners, and deals
- **Stance:** Integrity is what you do despite the money. Walk from bad deals. Play long-term games.
- **Evidence:**
  - "if you have someone who's high intelligence and high energy but a low Integrity what you've got is a hardworking smart Crook" (S074, undated)
  - "if they can do it to them they can do it to me" (S074, undated), on founders who offer to shortchange another shareholder to please him (also S062, 2018).
  - "I've just learned over the years that compromise is the enemy of building a great business." (S011, 2026-05)
  - On contracts: "You're closing down your options. And the world is a big place. Optionality is very powerful." (S011, 2026-05)
  - "So, it's important to pick an industry where you can play long-term games, and with long-term people." (S004, 2019-03)
  - "one of the things that I'm now very wary of is people who are overoptimizing for the moment because they're signaling they're not a long-term player." (S088, undated)

## Focus and time

### 24. The CEO job and delegation
- **Stance:** Spend your time in your zone of genius and recruit for the rest. CEO is a burden, not a prize. Keep the four jobs you cannot hand off.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I think of a CEO, has at least three jobs and those three jobs are raise money and keep cash in the bank, set and communicate the strategy and recruit and retain the team." (S089, undated)
  - "it really ends up being a recruiting problem, not a execution problem." (S089, undated)
  - "CEO was a high stress people management job that you did because you had no other choice, because you wanted to make your baby successful." (S089, undated)
  - "If you can outsource something, or not do something, for less than your hourly rate, outsource it or don't do it." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "Guard your time. It's all you have." (S016, 2016-05)
- **Read:** He stepped down as AngelList CEO and rated his life far higher after (S089). Founders should stay engaged at the board or strategy level after stepping down (S089).

### 25. Meetings, calendars, and travel
- **Over time:**
  - 2010-04: "your high-value targets don't have time for meetings between un-screened parties, and since you're busy building a company, you shouldn't have time for them either." (S023)
  - 2019-12: "I don't do non-transactional meetings. I don't do meetings without a strict agenda." (S001)
  - Undated: "a busy calendar is the death of creativity. It might be good for productivity, if you're completely in exploitation mode." (S089)
  - Undated: "I don't travel for conferences and neither probably should you" (S083)
  - **(newer)** 2025-03: "The uh the overscheduled life is not worth living." (S030)

### 26. Focus, saying no, and diversification
- **Stance:** Explore widely, then commit and say no by default. One big desire at a time. Diversify only to cover what you do not know.
- **Evidence:**
  - "if you can't decide, the answer is no" (S030, 2025-03)
  - "if you're making a four-year decision, spend a year thinking it through" (S030, 2025-03)
  - "you can basically get anything you want out of life as long as it's one thing that's my learning" (S093, undated)
  - "If I'm not excited about something, I'm not going to do it." (S016, 2016-05)
  - In investing, diversification is a hedge against lack of knowledge (S025, 2020-06, paraphrase).
- **Read:** Exploration first, then compounding commitment; skipping either phase costs years (S032, 2026-01, paraphrase).

### 27. Side projects and obsessions
- **Stance:** Feed obsessions instead of seeking balance. A business should grow out of one.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I go out of my way to indulge in it. I don't look for balance. I look to feed my obsessions." (S011, 2026-05)
  - "the only way you're likely to find product-market fit is if you're almost irrationally obsessed with the market and if you've been working on it for a long time." (S058, 2011-07)
  - "where I was most successful was when I was uh when I did projects either because I was genuinely curious about them" (S034, 2025-08)
  - AngelList started as a public service, Venture Hacks, and grew into a business by following demand (S093, undated).

### 28. Work hours
- **Over time:**
  - 2005-11: "Your best work was probably done in tremendous, focused bursts, surrounded by long periods of dullness and inactivity." (S052)
  - 2019-12: "It's more like a lion hunting and less like a marathoner running. You sprint and then you rest." (S001)
  - Undated: "a great engineer can create a billion dollars worth of value look at Satoshi Nakamoto and a bad engineer can cost you value it has nothing to do with the amount of time they put in" (S083)
  - **(newer)** 2025-10: on his tweet that self-employment ends weekends and work alike: "You are basically only measured on your output." (S029)
- **Read:** Hours are not the variable he measures. Output, direction, and intense sprints are.

### 29. Burnout
- **Stance:** Burnout means the work is wrong or not working, so a break does not fix it. Sustainability is what lets you reach the compounding.
- **Evidence:**
  - "burnout to me is a clear case that like you're not enjoying this anymore and if you're not enjoying this anymore you should probably stop working on it" (S034, 2025-08)
  - "all the returns in life, whether in money or relationships or friendships or colleagues or whatever, or product development. It's compound interest, it happens at the end." (S089, undated)
  - "Busy is a death of productivity and happiness." (S016, 2016-05)
- **Read:** If you burn out you sell early and forfeit the compounding (S089, paraphrase). He reads "I'm burned out" as "I want to quit" (S007, 2025-11, paraphrase).

### 30. Location and remote work
- **Over time:**
  - 2010-04: step one is to move to Silicon Valley (S057).
  - 2010-12: outside a funding hub, bootstrap to real returns or relocate (S021, paraphrase).
  - 2019: "Silicon Valley is not a place that creates entrepreneurs or wealth or technology. It is a place that attracts entrepreneurs, who create wealth and technology" (S081)
  - 2023: the Bay Area is still best for early creative teams, and scaling can happen elsewhere; crypto companies may need to leave the US (S080, paraphrase).
  - 2024-10: "where you live is the single most important decision you make" (S033)
  - **(newer)** 2023: "the scene is more Dynamic more high energy more conducive to early stage startups than I may have ever seen" (S085)
- **Read:** Work is atomizing for individuals (S017, S088), but for a founder starting something, he still says go to the hub.

## Market and growth

### 31. Competition
- **Stance:** Do not copy and do not watch competitors. Escape competition through authenticity.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Basically, when you're competing with people it's because you're copying them." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "You're playing a stupid game. You're going to win a stupid prize." (S001, 2019-12)
  - "the best entrepreneurs display a lot of chutzpah. They aren't fazed by the competition, nor do they see shadows in every corner. They are their own biggest competition." (S061, 2010-07)
  - "You escape competition through authenticity" (S030, 2025-03)
  - Define your competitive space as "realistically narrow." (S061, 2010-07)

### 32. Content, writing, and building in public
- **Stance:** Content is permissionless leverage and brand, but make it for its own sake and write it yourself.
- **Evidence:**
  - Writing is a learnable form of selling for technical people (S005, 2019-03, paraphrase).
  - Twitter's character limit forced him to choose words like a poet and built taste he did not plan (S034, 2025-08).
  - "I don't even want followers I only want peer relationships" (S033, 2024-10)
  - Pick a marketing medium you love almost pathologically or you will drop off (S029, 2025-10, paraphrase).
  - **(newer)** 2026-07: if it is meant for a human reader, he wants to understand the thing himself and respect the reader's time; he uses AI to brainstorm, not to write the output (inferred from S035).

### 33. Paid ads and marketing
- **Stance:** He does not discuss buying ads. He prefers products that market themselves.
- **Evidence:** Craigslist built a giant business with no VC, no marketing, and no patents (S046, 2006-02). Magazine covers and awards are a bad sign for a builder (S085, 2023, paraphrase).
- **Read:** No recorded position on paid acquisition spend or CAC.

### 34. Brand and fame
- **Over time:**
  - 2016-05: build an independent personal brand around what you love, not a company name (S016, paraphrase).
  - 2019-05: an eponymous brand is the goal; Oprah gets paid for being Oprah (S043, paraphrase).
  - 2019: "you wanna be rich and anonymous, not poor and famous" (S081) and "fame is a curse. I don't recommend it for anybody" (S081)
  - Undated: asked if being recognized is a problem, he calls himself a minor celebrity and says for him the harms outweigh the benefits, though he admits he may not be self-aware enough to know (inferred from S077).
  - Undated: "I would rather have the respect of ten people whom I respect rather than the respect of the masses." (S084)
  - **(newer)** 2025-03: "the kind of fame that's earned because you did something useful uh why dodge that" (S030)
- **Read:** A personal brand is leverage. Fame for its own sake is a status game he regrets playing.

### 35. Sales
- **Stance:** Credibility over technique. If it feels like selling, you are selling the wrong thing. Learn both building and selling.
- **Evidence:**
  - "I don't sell. I just try to figure things out, and then if there's something I believe in, I try to convey that as accurately and honestly as possible to the other person." (S011, 2026-05)
  - "If it feels to you like you're selling, then you're probably selling the wrong thing." (S011, 2026-05)
  - "I think the real giants in any field are the people who can both build and sell." (S005, 2019-03)
  - "I'd rather teach an engineer marketing, than a marketer engineering." (S005, 2019-03)
  - When someone says no, ask for the reason twice: "usually whatever the last thing they say is, is the real truth." (S089, undated)
- **Limit he names:** he is not the person for volume quota selling (S011, 2026-05, paraphrase).

### 36. Metrics, traction, and feedback
- **Stance:** Measure outputs, not inputs. Trust markets and nature over people.
- **Evidence:**
  - "First, measure outputs, not inputs, in yourself and your organization." (S052, 2005-11)
  - "As a result, judgment and accountability matter much more." (S041, 2020-01) "People need to be able to tell what role you had in the company's success." (S041)
  - For consumer startups he wants to see traction before investing; for a consumer app, about 100,000 users before a $500,000 to $1 million angel round, and in enterprise perhaps five paying beta customers (S087, 2013).
  - "It is impossible to fool Mother Nature." (S029, 2025-10)
  - "nature and free markets are the gold standards" (S092, 2024)
  - "If you want to change the world to a better place, the best way to do it is a for-profit because for-profits have to take feedback from reality." (S013, 2021-12)
- **Read:** No recorded position on specific operating metrics like churn or LTV.

### 37. Timing and trends
- **Stance:** Do not chase hot markets. Be early, be obsessed, and expect ten years.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Anyone in this business who's chasing hot markets gets killed." (S025, 2020-06)
  - "trend chasing is a lot like macro chasing uh and often it's too late" (S080, 2023)
  - "the lesson of history is that product-market fit is very precise, one wrong tweak or slightly bad timing and you can miss the whole thing." (S058, 2011-07)
  - "it takes 10 years to build a career in anything." (S093, undated)
  - "the great new companies always look really strange. They don't look very much like the previous companies." (S062, 2018)
  - "anecdotal evidence aside, entrepreneurship favors the young." (S045, 2007-08)

### 38. Selling the company, exits, and liquidity
- **Stance:** As an investor, a team likely to sell early is a red flag, and returns come from holding winners. As a founder, he shut down what did not work and made investors whole.
- **Evidence:**
  - A founding team that hints or appears likely to sell early is "a strong negative indicator" (S025, 2020-06).
  - "the highest returns are made by taking a whole portfolio and holding it till the end" (S080, 2023)
  - He allows founder liquidity: 10 percent of a round in his 2013 term sheet (S051, 2013-06).
  - On Airchat: "I built a product that I loved that I was proud of but it didn't catch fire." He found the team homes and returned investor money (S070, undated).
  - "if you're going to fail, fail fast, face fail early, acknowledge reality, and then try again" (S088, undated)
- **Read:** No recorded position on when to sell a company that is working, beyond the warning that burnout drives early sales (S089).

### 39. Advice, experts, and courses
- **Stance:** Reject most advice, check the advisor's incentives, and never buy wealth advice from people who sell it.
- **Evidence:**
  - "nobody who's wealthy is going to give you actionable advice in a public forum and how to get wealthy this is nonsense it doesn't work" (S091, 2021-01)
  - On people who sell wealth advice: "they wouldn't be selling courses on how to get rich" if they were rich (S091, 2021-01). In 2025 he agreed when a host read back his line that people who know how to make money do not sell courses (inferred from S030).
  - "great founders aren't that coachable. They listen to lots of advice, but they follow very little of it." (S025, 2020-06)
  - "I've found business biographies to be useless for building a great business. They're good for inspiration." (S039, 2020-05)
  - "If there's an easy way to get rich, it's already been exploited." (S001, 2019-12)
  - On his own limits: "it's silly to use them as a source of truth." (S081, 2019)

### 40. Regulation, regulated markets, and crypto
- **Stance:** Regulators can only say no, so innovation runs to unregulated domains. Be ready to move. Crypto is real but speculative.
- **Evidence:**
  - "Regulators are basically cops with the ability to say no and not the ability to say Yes" (S080, 2023)
  - "The two biggest advancements in tech in Silicon Valley in the last decade, AI and, before that, crypto, they're both in the math domain, because that's the last unregulated domain." (S010, 2026-05)
  - "this is why there's so little innovation in medical right now. The FDA approval process is a nightmare." (S010, 2026-05)
  - "Crypto is the native currency of the internet." (S063, 2020-10)
  - He warns crypto is volatile and most NFTs will go to zero (S069, S076, paraphrase).
- **Read:** For a founder in health, aviation, or finance he expects regulation to be the binding constraint and favors jurisdiction moves and legal challenges (S080, S092, paraphrase).
