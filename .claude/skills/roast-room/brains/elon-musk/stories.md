# Stories: Elon Musk

Built from the 27 batch notes over 94 sources (2003 to 2026). 20 stories and cases he reuses, most-used first, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **Falcon 1's fourth launch, 2008.** He gave SpaceX under a 10% chance, the first three launches failed, and the fourth was the last one the money could pay for. "if the fourth one had not succeeded SpaceX would not exist so it was a very close call" (S089) A NASA contract followed, and he says "fate liked us that day." (S036) Lesson: write the odds down, stay in the game to the last dollar, and know how thin the margin was (S021, S028, S036, S047, S064, S077, S089, S091).
2. **Tesla's Christmas Eve round, 2008.** With SpaceX failing and Tesla's financing falling through, he split his last money across both companies instead of saving one. "we closed the financing round at 6 p.m. on Christmas Eve 2008. Uh it's the last hour of the last day that it was possible." (S076) Payroll would have bounced days later, and paying suppliers on time would have killed both companies (S017). Lesson: put everything in only when the alternative is certain death, and hold reserves the rest of the time (S017, S021, S022, S023, S028, S057, S076, S079, S096).
3. **Twitter: the plane in a nosedive.** He describes the takeover as "like being teleported into a plane that's in a nosedive with engines on fire and the controls don't work" (S058). Cash flow was about $3 billion negative against about $1 billion in the bank (S029, S046), so he cut roughly 80% of staff in weeks and says the site improved (S048, S050). Lesson: in a cash crisis find the runway number and cut immediately; accept that some cuts are wrong (S029, S030, S046, S048, S050, S058, S060).
4. **Model 3 production hell.** He says they got almost every part of the line wrong, lived in the Fremont and Nevada factories, and slept on the floor so both shifts could see him: "there were three years of hell" (S024). Failed automation had to be ripped out (S069). Needing 50% more output in three weeks, they built a line in a tent on a sloped parking lot: "So we actually started the production line at the top" (S076). Lesson: prototypes are easy, volume is the hard part, and the leader belongs at the constraint (S024, S042, S052, S069, S072, S076).
5. **Mars Oasis and the Russian ICBMs.** In 2001 and 2002 he planned a small greenhouse on Mars to stir public support for NASA, went to Russia three times to buy rockets, and was priced out (S056, S059, S064). Working the math on the flight home, he found the raw materials were tiny next to the price: "it's like 2% of what a rocket costs. So clearly it would be in how the atoms are arranged." (S064) Lesson: the stated problem (public will) was not the real one (launch cost), so attack cost from first principles (S022, S056, S059, S061, S064, S079, S089).
6. **The Roadster's two false premises.** Tesla planned to license AC Propulsion's drivetrain and lightly modify a Lotus Elise. The battery made the car about 30% heavier, broke the crash structure, and in the end almost nothing carried over (S008, S096). "It would have been better to just build the house from the beginning." (S023) An investor audit then found the car's cost had been understated by more than 2x (S056), and he calls trying not to be CEO "the actual moral error here was me trying to have my cake and eat it too" (S017). Lesson: test the premise early, start from scratch when you would rebuild anyway, and check unit costs yourself (S008, S017, S022, S023, S056, S096).
7. **Model 3 battery mats: the five steps done backwards.** Fiberglass mats on the pack were the top bottleneck. He automated the cell, sped it up, and optimized it before asking what the mats were for. The battery team said noise, the noise team said fire safety, a test showed no difference, and deleting them bypassed a $2 million robot cell (S014, S052). A laser weld that stopped the factory turned out to do nothing for crash safety (S052). Lesson: question the requirement and delete before you optimize; every requirement needs a named owner (S014, S052, S055, S093).
8. **OpenAI as the counterweight that turned.** Larry Page called him a speciesist for caring about AI risk while Google held most AI talent and compute, so he funded and named OpenAI as an open nonprofit and recruited Ilya Sutskever (S019, S029, S047). He compares the result to funding a rainforest charity that became a lumber company (S026, S051). Lesson: money without governance buys no control; he now builds his own lab (S019, S026, S029, S045, S047, S051, S081).
9. **Steel instead of carbon fiber.** Carbon fiber Starship work was slow. "this is happen by accident by the way it may sound like some great insight but it actually happened because we're moving too slowly on composite and I was like we cannot move this slowly or we'll go bankrupt" (S078) At cryogenic temperatures steel matches composite strength to weight at about 2% of the cost (S066), and "It was dumb not to do steel." (S002) Lesson: when progress is too slow, change the material or the design, not the schedule (S002, S066, S078, S094).
10. **Raptor sheds its parts.** Raptor 1 looked like a Christmas tree covered in spaghetti; Raptor 2 deleted and merged subsystems and "costs about half as much as rafter 1 despite having much more thrust" (S067). Raptor 3 looks so clean that industry experts thought it was unfinished (S037). Mid-interview he realized Starship could steer with spare ullage gas instead of separate thrusters: "It occurred to me while I was explaining to you" (S095). Lesson: the best part is no part, and the right direction is fewer parts with more performance (S009, S012, S037, S067, S093, S095).
11. **The Space Shuttle that could not change.** Crew on board from day one froze the design, so known O-ring and foam problems stayed because it had flown before: "Look, I've pulled the trigger so many times. There must be no bullets in this gun." (S009) It took about nine months to refurbish and cost about as much as a Saturn V for a quarter of the payload (S011, S091). Lesson: punishing change kills iteration, and reuse only counts if it is rapid and complete (S009, S011, S016, S025, S091).
12. **Landing a rocket took 14 tries.** Grasshopper and early Falcon 9 boosters tipped over and exploded, and SpaceX was naive about parachutes (S063, S066). It took 14 or 15 attempts to land the first booster (S067), and the team made a blooper reel (S066). Lesson: count failures as tuition, and expect fewer next time because the experience exists (S025, S049, S063, S066, S067).
13. **Colossus in months, not two years.** Suppliers quoted 18 to 24 months for a large GPU cluster. He broke it into building, power, cooling, and power smoothing, took an old Electrolux factory in Memphis, rented generators and about a quarter of US mobile cooling, added Megapacks, and ran four shifts around the clock (S021). Grid power at the site would take a year, so they ganged gas turbines (S035), and in 2026 he was living in a trailer there racking servers (S018). Lesson: decompose an "impossible" quote into parts and attack each, and be at the constraint (S002, S018, S021, S035, S051).
14. **Optimus with nothing off the shelf.** Tesla tried to use existing motors, gearboxes, and sensors for the robot and none could be bought at any price because they did not exist (S016). "with Optimus, there's there is no supply chain." (S088) Lesson: when no supplier exists, build the supply chain yourself, and expect a long flat start to the ramp (S016, S031, S071, S083, S088).
15. **PayPal's dashboard versus email payments.** The elaborate all-in-one financial dashboard drew a "ho hum"; the email money transfer, built in about a day, drew a "wow", so the company followed the customers (S059, S064, S065). Lesson: react to what the environment tells you, not to your original plan (S059, S064, S065).
16. **Zip2's hired CEO and media board.** After VC funding, investors installed a professional CEO and newspaper customers pushed the product toward their old model. "the company succeeded in spite of that person not because of them" (S003) Before that, the brothers ran it on almost nothing, sleeping in the office: "we had just an absurdly tiny burn rate" (S059). Lesson: keep burn tiny, do not cede control to people whose interests pull the product elsewhere, and go direct to the customer (S003, S023, S056, S059).
17. **The GM EV1 vigil.** GM repossessed and crushed its electric cars while owners held a candlelight vigil at the junkyard (S003). "if somebody is holding a candlet vigil for the rest because they love your product so much maybe you should make more of it" (S089) Lesson: when an incumbent kills a category customers love, there is room for a new entrant (S003, S089).
18. **Bill Gates and the electric Semi.** Gates said a long-range electric semi could not work. Musk asked which number was wrong, the battery energy per kilogram or the truck's energy per mile. "he didn't know any of the numbers." (S019) Lesson: put numbers on any claim and argue the numbers (S019).
19. **Giga casting from toys.** He took the idea of casting large body sections in one piece from how cheap toys are made and asked six casting machine makers. Five said no and one said maybe: "i'm like i'll take that as a yes" (S017). Lesson: ask whether physics forbids it, then find the one supplier who will try (S017).
20. **The Starlink team reset.** He let a Starlink team in Redmond run for years and tracked it in weekly engineering reviews. When he concluded it could not get there, he replaced it: "I'll take drastic action only when I conclude that success is not in a set of possible outcomes." (S002) Lesson: treat each review as a data point, and act hard only when success is out of the set (S002).
