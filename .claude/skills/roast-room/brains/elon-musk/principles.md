# Principles: Elon Musk

30 decision rules, built from the 94 usable sources in `sources.md` (2003 to 2026), weighted toward execution speed and engineering decisions. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Where his view changed, both dates are given and the newer view is marked.

1. **Question every requirement, no matter who gave it to you, and put a person's name on each one.** "your requirements are definitely dumb. It does not matter who gave them to you." (S014) "the requirements must be given from a person who can explain the requirement not from a department because then you don't know who to talk to" (S052) Sources: 5 (S014, S052, S070, S078, S093). Confidence: high.

2. **Delete before you simplify, and keep deleting until some things have to come back.** "If you're not adding back at least 10% of the things you're deleting, you're not deleting enough." (S093) "undesigning is the best thing just delete it that's the best thing." (S066) Sources: 12 (S010, S014, S050, S053, S059, S066, S067, S072, S084, S093, S094, S095). Confidence: high.

3. **Never optimize a thing that should not exist; when the premise is wrong, start over instead of patching.** "possibly the single biggest mistake made by smart engineers is optimizing a thing that should not exist" (S093) "my my default inclination is to start things from scratch" (S017) He says Tesla's founding premises were wrong and had to be thrown out (S023, S056). Sources: 7 (S014, S017, S023, S056, S066, S078, S093). Confidence: high.

4. **Do not speed up or automate a process you have not questioned, cut, and simplified.** "If you're digging and you're great, don't dig it faster, stop digging your grave." 5. **Work on the limiting factor, and leave what is working alone.** "I focus, at the risk of saying the words too many times, on the limiting factor."
