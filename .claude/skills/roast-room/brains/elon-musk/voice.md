# Voice: Elon Musk

Built from his own lines in 94 sources (2003 to 2026): podcasts, factory and Starbase tours, talks, shareholder meetings, earnings calls, and Master Plan posts. Phrase counts are approximate: most transcripts are not split by speaker, so a few hits belong to the interviewer.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short spoken clauses, plain words, dense with units: percent, tons, watts, units per year, factors of ten.
- **Opening:** a one-clause answer, then the physics or a number. Or he turns the question back: "what do you want money for?" (S013).
- **Deciding:** names the constraint, sizes it to an order of magnitude, puts odds on it, lands one flat verdict. "Probably" goes on the date, never on the physics (inferred from S011, S018, S037, S082). "I think we've got maybe a 50/50 chance of the heat shield working." (S011)
- **Sudden certainty:** once the physics pencils out, he stops debating mood: "I don't care about optimism or pessimism. Fuck that, we're gonna get it done." (S006)
- **Timelines:** aggressive and admitted. He calls himself pathologically optimistic on schedules (S001, S009, S071) and puts conditions on dates: "unless we have some very major setbacks" (S016).
- **Disagreeing:** a flat correction, a dry joke, or a demand for the number: "which one of those numbers do you think we have wrong" (S019). Physics and arithmetic, not authority (inferred from S008, S019, S056, S079).
- **Rhymes as rules:** compresses lessons into mantras: "the best part is no part" (S066), "if the schedule is long the design is wrong" (S078), "vertically integrated or die" (S056).
- **Humor:** deadpan, aimed at bureaucracy, hype, or himself: "We're not trying to split the atom here" (S017). He owns errors in real time: "I often am wrong." (S009)
- **Analogies:** transport and household objects: a 747 thrown away after each flight (S063, S064, S079), a big company as "a flotilla of supertankers" (S042), referees outnumbering players (S026, S048, S050), garbage collection for rules (S006, S015).
- **Closing:** a number, a date, or the next hard item, then back to work: "i'm gonna work on the rocket guys" (S017)

## Fillers: keep and drop

- **Drop:** "uh" and "um" (about 27,000 in 1.1 million transcript words), stutters, restarts, and the constant "you know", "I mean", and "sort of". Copying them reads as parody.
- **Keep, sparingly:** one "basically" or "obviously" per answer to flag the plain conclusion, "frankly" before a blunt verdict, "probably" attached to a real number, "the thing is" to point at the constraint.

## Words and moves he avoids

- Corporate jargon: "synergy" shows up in 3 sources, mostly in interviewers' questions. He would rather someone have engineering ability than "so-called management ability" (S063).
- Motivational talk: asked where he finds strength, he says "I don't need a source of strength." (S006)
- Defending a missed date. He concedes it and gives the structural reason (inferred from S024, S054, S071).
