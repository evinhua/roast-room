# Principles: Jensen Huang

Built from eighteen batches of notes on 104 sources (2011 to 2026; S066 to S105 are undated in the table). 30 decision rules, roughly most-supported first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources across all notes that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Grow the install base and never break compatibility.** "that's the only unnegotiable rule in our company" (S004) "we support software and we fine tune software for as long as we shall live." (S087) Sources: 14 (S001, S004, S008, S011, S018, S029, S038, S039, S040, S042, S043, S046, S047, S087). Confidence: high.

2. **Create the market instead of fighting for share in one.** "Nvidia is a market maker not share taker... not one day does this company talk about market share." (S008) "We chose a market with no customers a zero billion dollar market and it was robotics." (S034) Sources: 9 (S004, S008, S016, S017, S024, S034, S035, S038, S070). Confidence: high.

3. **Do only what others cannot do, or what would not happen without you; stay out of commodity markets.** "You build a great company by doing things that other people can't do." (S013) "If I don't do this, it won't get done." (S017) "we naturally selected ourselves out of commodity markets" (S029) Sources: 10 (S002, S011, S013, S017, S024, S028, S029, S047, S048, S066). Confidence: high.

4. **Endurance matters more than intelligence; the one who can suffer longest wins.** "intelligence is not top of that list my ability to endure pain and suffering my abil my ability to work on something for a very very long period of time" (S034) "the person who's willing to suffer the longest wins." (S029) Sources: 10 (S018, S021, S024, S027, S028, S029, S034, S042, S071, S076). Confidence: high. He also says he would not start NVIDIA again knowing the cost: "It's too scary. It's too painful. Too much sacrifice." (S076)

5. **When things are hard or uncertain, go back to first principles, not to the stock price.** "When I'm uncertain about the future, when things are moving too fast, you always go back to first principles." (S021) "The stock price changed, but did something else change? Did physics change, did gravity change?" (S017) Sources: 9 (S003, S005, S006, S016, S017, S021, S073, S076, S079). Confidence: high.

6. **Build ahead of demand when you have reasoned the market will come.** "if you build it, they might not come. But if you build it, if you don't build it, they cannot come." (S040) "if you don't build it, they can't come. And that's usually how we look at the world." (S005) Sources: 6 (S005, S020, S034, S040, S041, S059). Confidence: high. **Tension:** on chip supply at scale he says the opposite: "we're at the end of the supply chain, right? And so, we respond to demand." (S007, 2025), and "With demand everything will be resolvable." (S069) The newer lines are about capacity at a company already at scale; the older rule is about creating a new platform. Use the first for a new category and the second for capacity planning.

7. **Compete on output per scarce unit and lifetime cost, not on price.** "Our TCO is so good that even when the competitor's chips are free it's not cheap enough." (S018) "Your performance per watt translates directly absolutely directly to your revenues." (S046) "Choosing the wrong architecture just because the chips are cheaper doesn't translate. Doesn't make sense." (S096) Sources: 12 (S006, S007, S014, S018, S037, S038, S042, S045, S046, S074, S091, S096). Confidence: high.

8. **Stop doing the wrong thing now, even before you know the right strategy; take the hard medicine fast.** "We don't know what the right strategy is, but we know what the wrong technology is. So, let's stop doing it the wrong way" (S003) "when it finally happens to you, just take the hard medicine and get it behind you." (S048) Sources: 7 (S003, S020, S035, S048, S057, S070, S076). Confidence: high.

9. **Hard problems that take a long time are a feature, because they filter out the less committed.** "if it takes a long time to do... a lot of people who are less committed probably won't do it." (S016) "If you want to do extraordinary things, it shouldn't be easy." (S050) Sources: 6 (S016, S024, S028, S029, S040, S050). Confidence: high.

10. **Work the supply chain far upstream and tell it what is coming.** "I spend a lot of my time informing, directly or indirectly, our supply chain, partners, and ecosystem about the opportunity in front of us." (S002) "none of the bottlenecks last longer than a couple of years, two, three years, none of them." (S002) Sources: 9 (S002, S037, S041, S046, S054, S062, S074, S084, S086). Confidence: high. He adds that a strong balance sheet is strategic because it buys supply priority: "A strong balance sheet today is not only helpful, it's strategic." (S074)

11. **Before you bet the farm, pull the risk forward and simulate it away.** "When you bet the farm you're saying I'm going to take everything in the future all the risky things and going to pull it in advance." (S004) Sources: 7 (S003, S004, S037, S041, S043, S098, S101). Confidence: high.

12. **Believe something unconventional, but only once it is informed and reasoned.** "let it be informed and let it be reasoned then dedicate yourself to making it happen." (S034) He wants the reasoning in words, not cells: "I love words for that reason, words are reasoning." (S016) Sources: 5 (S005, S016, S020, S034, S076). Confidence: high.

13. **Fund the long bet with a large market, and pick forms that can reach volume.** "having it being a large market is important because the technology is complicated and if we had a large market, our R&D budget could be large" (S005) "technology needs scale. Most of the robotic systems we've had so far are too low volume." (S041) Sources: 7 (S005, S021, S028, S029, S038, S041, S079). Confidence: high.

14. **Do as much as needed, as little as possible; partner for the rest.** "We should do as much as needed, as little as possible." (S002) "We should not be the world's fifth cloud service provider." (S013) Sources: 7 (S002, S011, S013, S028, S047, S048, S066). Confidence: high.

15. **Decide what to give up, and do not enter a market where someone else owns the chokepoint.** "strategic retreat sacrifice deciding what to give up is at the core the very core of success" (S035) "Going into that market in the first place was a waste of time." (S071) Sources: 5 (S020, S034, S035, S071, S073). Confidence: high. The 2023 telling makes the phone exit a choice (S035); the later one calls entering it a mistake (S071). The newer view is stricter.

16. **Win developers first; if a developer needs your product, the answer is yes.** "winning developers is what creates the future platform." (S073) "if a developer reaches out to you a needs a GPU, the answer is yes." (S098) Sources: 5 (S049, S070, S073, S098, S099). Confidence: high.

17. **Give everyone the same information at the same time; reason out loud in front of them.** "No conversation is ever one person. That's why I don't do one-on-ones. We present a problem and all of us attack it." (S001) "I believe that when you give everybody equal access to information, it empowers people." (S016) Sources: 7 (S001, S004, S008, S016, S017, S068, S079). Confidence: high.

18. **Cannibalize your own product before someone else does.** "if you want to be a market leader you have to take the initiative to cannibalize your own products" (S020) "when Blackwell starts shipping in volume you couldn't give Hoppers away" (S037) Sources: 4 (S020, S037, S038, S087). Confidence: high.

19. **Run as if you are 30 days from going out of business, at any size.** "the phrase 30 days from going out of business I've used for 33 years" (S003) "for us for you know for 30 years we're always in a perpetual state of going out of business" (S024) Sources: 4 (S003, S024, S035, S077). Confidence: high.

20. **Do not concede a market; you rarely replace the one you abandon.** "Our market is not his to seed. We ought to go compete for the world's market." (S031) Sources: 6 (S007, S022, S024, S031, S075, S084). Confidence: high. Most of his evidence is the China export case, which is policy, not a founder choice, and he has also left markets (rule 15). Inferred from S022, S035, S071: he concedes markets that are off mission or where someone else owns the chokepoint, not ones that are on mission. He also notes that once customers learn you are unreliable, they move to local rivals and trust is slow to rebuild (S084).

21. **Track early indicators, not results; margin is a result.** "KPIs are hard to understand... gross margins. That's not a KPI, that's a result." (S017) Sources: 4 (S016, S017, S035, S048). Confidence: high.

22. **When AI makes the company more productive, hire more, not fewer.** "when companies become more productive using artificial intelligence it is likely that it manifests itself into either better earnings or better growth or both" (S008) "join companies where they have more ideas than they can afford to fund so that when AI automates their work, it's going to shift." (S029) Sources: 6 (S008, S023, S028, S029, S030, S096). Confidence: high.

23. **Invest where you know the space and can see the demand; the regret is investing too little.** "you got to invest in things, you know... And it turns out we happen to know this space." (S007) "I think the only regret that I have is that I didn't invest more and sooner." (S054) Sources: 5 (S002, S007, S025, S046, S054). Confidence: high. He names misses: too little in OpenAI early (S007), not backing Anthropic early (S002). He also refuses to pick winners among customers: "we don't pick winners. We need to support everyone." (S002)

24. **Use what exists off the shelf; build your own only where you must.** "I would I would just continue to use use off-the-shelf whenever you can and build your own super agent whenever you must." (S069) Sources: 5 (S047, S051, S069, S071, S083). Confidence: high. Caveat in S083: "don't forget, you have to invest in building your own intelligence." Off the shelf for most work, your own where your data and craft are the edge.

25. **Speed is performance; never do twice what you can do once.** "why should you do things twice if you can do it once why tape out a chip seven times if you could tape it out one time" (S004) "This is your time to market. This is technology leadership. This is your pricing power." (S097) Sources: 4 (S004, S070, S085, S097). Confidence: high.

26. **Fear of failure is a better fuel than the wish to succeed; keep expectations low and resilience high.** "I have a greater drive from not wanting... to fail than the drive of wanting to succeed" (S003) "people with very high expectations have very low resilience and unfortunately resilience matters in success." (S018) Sources: 3 (S003, S018, S077). Confidence: high.

27. **Make the right decision first; deal with the consequences second.** "Make the right decision first. The consequence you will think about second." (S070) He used it in the 1995 Sega crisis, when contracts, money, and team feelings were tangled. Sources: 1 (S070). Confidence: low.

28. **Treat constraints as a gift; scarcity forces the best choice.** "I love constraints. And the reason for that is because in a world of constraint, you have no choice but to choose the best." (S074) Sources: 1 (S074). Confidence: low.

29. **Hold your price; be dependable rather than auction scarce supply.** "You set your price and then people decide to buy it or not." (S002) "I prefer to be dependable, to be the foundation of the industry." (S002) Sources: 1 (S002). Confidence: low.

30. **Do not start a company for money, and only with people you deeply trust.** "Money is the only singular reason not to start the company because starting companies are it's a very very unlikely probability for success." (S020) "don't ever go into business with uh anyone you don't deeply trust" (S020) Sources: 1 (S020, from 2011). Confidence: low.
