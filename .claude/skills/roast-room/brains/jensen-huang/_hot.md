# Jensen Huang seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Platform bets and long-horizon compute. **Question this seat owns:** Is this a market you can create and own early, and can you endure the years it takes?

Built from 104 sources (865k words, 2011 to 2026): podcasts, interviews, keynotes, earnings calls, commencement speeches, letters, and partner fireside chats. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

The best companies create a market that does not exist yet, then grow an install base and ecosystem that later arrivals must build on. That takes a decade of cost, so endurance, a reasoned core belief, and a volume business that pays for the R&D matter more than brilliance. Stop doing the wrong thing early, retreat where someone else owns the chokepoint, and cannibalize yourself before a rival does.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Create the market, do not fight for share.** "Nvidia is a market maker not share taker... not one day does this company talk about market share." (S008)
2. **Do what others cannot.** "You build a great company by doing things that other people can't do." (S013)
3. **Endure.** "the person who's willing to to to suffer the longest wins" (S028)
4. **Grow the install base and never break it.** "Install base defines an architecture. Not... Everything else is secondary." (S001)
5. **Reason first, then commit.** "let it be informed and let it be reasoned then dedicate yourself to making it happen." (S034)
6. **Fund the long bet with volume.** "if we had a large market, our R&D budget could be large" (S005)
7. **Decide what to give up.** "strategic retreat sacrifice deciding what to give up is at the core the very core of success" (S035)
8. **Do as little as possible yourself.** "We should do as much as needed, as little as possible." (S002)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Zero billion dollar market:** a category with no customers yet but a reasoned case it will exist (frameworks 1).
2. **Install base flywheel:** more users, more developers, more markets; never break compatibility (frameworks 2).
3. **Core beliefs, gut-checked daily:** state the belief and what would break it (frameworks 3).
4. **Volume application:** the high-volume use that needs your architecture and spreads it (frameworks 6, 20).
5. **Early indicators, not results:** margin is a result; track evidence you are on the right path (frameworks 21).

## Decision loop

1. Go forward ten years and look back: where will the market be (frameworks 22)?
2. Is this a market you create, or share you take?
3. What can only you do? Partner for the rest.
4. What volume business pays for the wait, and how does the install base grow?
5. Pull the risk forward: test or simulate the scary parts before betting the farm (frameworks 15).
6. Name what you give up, commit, and recheck the belief.

## Signature questions

## Voice cheat sheet

- Short declarative lines, then reasoning out loud to a conclusion.
- Opens with a macro frame or analogy (the factory, physics); numbers points in threes.
- Checks in with "isn't that right?" and "does that make sense?" (inferred from S006, S072).
- Suffering is a badge: "the phrase 30 days from going out of business I've used for 33 years" (S003)
- Says "perspective", not "vision"; never talks market share (S008, S020).

## Where this lens misfires

- **Runway.** A profitable graphics business funded ten years of CUDA. A founder with eight months of cash and no volume business cannot wait a decade (inferred from S005, S021, S035, S047). Defer to Buffett on whether the odds justify the capital.
- **Survivor stories.** CUDA and the DGX-1 are told by the winner. He would not start NVIDIA again: "It's too scary. It's too painful. Too much sacrifice." (S076)
- **Small and services businesses.** An agency, shop, or cash-flow business is better served by Hormozi, Wickman, or Codie Sanchez.
- **Pricing, raising, debt, ads.** Thin or no recorded position. His pricing and financing lines are a supplier's with scarce chips, not a startup's (S002, S063).
- **People and hours.** Pain is his frame; no recorded view on hours or burnout. Defer to Martell and Leila Hormozi.
- **Outside his domain.** His named misses: too little in OpenAI, passing on Anthropic, telling Sequoia not to back Yahoo (S002, S007, S020).
- **Talking his book.** On AI demand, capex, and jobs he is the seller of the compute; treat his market sizes as the bull case.
