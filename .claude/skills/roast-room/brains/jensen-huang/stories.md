# Stories: Jensen Huang

Built from the extraction notes on 31 sources. 20 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Most retold first. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **CUDA on GeForce.** In 2006 and 2007 Nvidia put CUDA on every consumer card. No customer asked for it, it ate the gross margin, and the market value fell to a few billion dollars. Applications came one field at a time for about ten years until AlexNet in 2012. Lesson: a platform bet means years of cost before the market sees it, so build the install base and hold on. "we risked everything to pursue deep learning" (S035) (S001, S003, S011, S016, S035, S038)
2. **The radiologist prediction.** A leading AI scientist (Hinton) said radiologists would be obsolete in five years. Now nearly all radiology uses AI and there are more radiologists, because they read more scans and hospitals see more patients. Lesson: automating a task is not eliminating a job when the purpose of the job grows (S001, S009, S018, S022, S025, S026).
3. **Sega and the wrong architecture.** Nvidia's first chip got three big architecture choices wrong, and the Sega console deal could not be finished. Jensen flew to Japan, told Sega's CEO the truth, asked to be let out of the contract and still be paid, and Sega agreed. The money built the Riva 128. Lesson: admit the mistake early and ask for help; a strategic retreat saved the company. "Intellectual honesty and humility saved our company and how a retreat a strategic retreat was one of our best strategies." (S034) (S002, S003, S035, S036)
4. **The Riva 128 tape-out.** With months of cash and dozens of rivals, he spent half the remaining money on an emulator from a company going out of business, tested the whole chip and its drivers in software, and sent it straight to production without a prototype. It worked. Lesson: when failure is the default, take the big bet, but simulate the downside away first (S003, S004).
5. **The DGX-1 nobody bought.** In 2016 he built the first deep learning supercomputer, announced it to silence, found no buyers, and hand-delivered the first unit to a small San Francisco nonprofit called OpenAI. Lesson: the market rarely sees a platform shift at launch; you have to believe first. "I delivered the first one to a startup in San Francisco a startup nobody knew anything about" (S034) (S003, S005, S034)
6. **The deep learning researchers.** Andrew Ng, Geoffrey Hinton, and Yann LeCun reached out for GPU help. Nvidia then went looking for every AI researcher on the planet and built cuDNN for them, though they had no money. Lesson: watch the people doing important work at the edge; they show you the next market. "find every single AI researcher on the planet" (S004) (S004, S017, S021)
7. **AlexNet and how far it can go.** Seeing AlexNet (and later a blurry 32 by 32 GAN cat), he asked not how good it was but how far it could scale. The answer led Nvidia to rebuild its whole computing stack. Lesson: judge a breakthrough by whether it scales, not by its current quality. "if it can do this, how far can it go?" (S005) (S004, S005, S017, S021)
8. **The textbook fix.** Facing the wrong technology and dozens of competitors, he bought the Silicon Graphics or OpenGL books, handed one to each architect, and said to read them and save the company. "And we implemented the OpenGL pipeline like nobody had ever implemented the OpenGL pipeline." (S017) Lesson: start from the best known art, then rethink it from first principles (S017, S020).
9. **Leaving the phone market for a zero billion dollar one.** Nvidia's phone chips were an early hit, then rivals swarmed and Nvidia was pushed out of market after market. He chose to leave and go into robotics, a market with no customers. "to retreat from a giant phone market to create a zero billion dollar robotics Market" (S035) Lesson: retreat from fights outside the mission and use the time to create the next market (S034, S035).
10. **The Don Valentine meeting.** The plan needed technology, a market, and an ecosystem that did not exist, and his pitch was poor. Wilf Corrigan of LSI Logic called Valentine and told him to fund the kid anyway. Lesson: your track record does the selling (S017, S028).
11. **Intel left Nvidia alone.** Intel watched for years and rejected parallel computing because its CPU business was so successful. Lesson: success blinds incumbents, and being ignored is time to build the ecosystem. "people left us alone for a long time" (S024) (S024)
12. **The 80 percent drop.** After the financial crisis Nvidia lost about 80 percent of its value. He went back to work the next day the same way. "you go back to just doing your job, woke up at the same time, prioritize my day in the same way." (S017) Lesson: anchor on first principles, not the stock price (S017).
13. **Reinventing at the peak.** While Hopper was selling at its prime, Nvidia bet the next system on NVLink 72, which partners called too hard. The first full-size NVLink design years earlier had been too big; he kept the idea and reworked it. Lesson: reinvent while you are winning, and keep an architecture idea that is right even if the first build is wrong (S015, S037, S038).
14. **Mellanox.** He saw that a data center is set apart by its networking, not its processor, and bought Mellanox. He spent years shaping belief inside the company first, so the call got full buy-in on the day. Lesson: reason from what the whole system needs, and prepare people long before the announcement (S001, S004, S015).
15. **Misuse as demand.** Researchers at Mass General were tricking graphics cards into CT reconstruction by framing it as a graphics problem. That helped inspire CUDA. Lesson: watch how people misuse your product; it shows the real demand (S005).
16. **The investments he missed.** He was invited into OpenAI early and put in too little, and passed on Anthropic early because he assumed VCs would fund it. Years before, he told Sequoia not to back Yahoo because he could not see how free search made money. Lesson: deep insight in one domain does not make you right in others, and it is fine to have regrets (S002, S007, S020).
17. **Keyhole.** He invested in Keyhole when it had no revenue because he believed in 3D navigation and nobody else would fund it. It became Google Earth. Lesson: a view ahead of the market often has to fund and sell itself (S020).
18. **The Wall Street forecast.** In early 2023 analysts expected about $26 billion in revenue; Nvidia did $60 billion. He calls it "the single greatest failure of forecasting the world has ever seen." (S008) Lesson: analysts model straight lines and cannot model compounding platforms (S007, S008).
19. **The moss gardener.** At a Kyoto temple he met a gardener picking dead moss with bamboo tweezers in a huge garden. The gardener said he had cared for it for 25 years and had plenty of time. Lesson: do the most important work first, every day, and you will have time for a long project (S034).
20. **The H20 write-off.** When export rules banned the H20, Nvidia wrote off $5.5 billion and walked away from $15 billion in sales. "no company in history has ever written off that much inventory." (S014) Lesson: outside shocks can hit without warning; build the company to survive them (S014, S024).
