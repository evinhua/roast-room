# Books: Jensen Huang

Built from eighteen batches of notes on 104 sources (2011 to 2026). He has written no books. His thinking lives in talks, keynotes, earnings calls, and a few short written letters and posts, so the main series in the corpus are listed below, followed by the books he points others to. Summaries are in my own words.

## His major series (no books)

### NVIDIA keynotes: GTC, COMPUTEX, CES, SIGGRAPH, and regional GTCs (2021 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** General-purpose computing has run out of road and is giving way to accelerated computing and AI; the data center is now an AI factory that turns power into tokens; NVIDIA ships a new full system every year on a roadmap shown years ahead so the world can plan land and power. The later keynotes add agents and physical AI as the next waves.
- **Key frameworks:** AI factory and tokens per watt; compute equals revenue; the install base flywheel and CUDA compatibility; extreme co-design; scale up before scale out; vertically integrated, horizontally open; domain-by-domain libraries to break the chicken-and-egg problem; digital twin before physical build; three computers for robotics (train, simulate, deploy); waves of AI (perception, generative, agentic, physical); the five parts of an agent.
- **Taught in sources:** S037, S038, S039, S040, S041, S042, S043, S044, S095, S096, S097, S098, S099, S100, S101, S102, S103, S104, S105.

### Long-form interviews and podcasts (2022 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** How he decides: create zero billion dollar markets, reason from first principles, build the install base, endure years of disbelief, and do only what would not get done otherwise. The newer ones (2025 to 2026) spend more time on AI demand, financing, China, safety, and jobs.
- **Key frameworks:** zero billion dollar market; do as much as needed, as little as possible; prefetch risk before betting the farm; five-layer cake; task versus purpose; conviction with agility; flat org with no one-on-ones; fungible and durable infrastructure; speed of light; shaping belief before the move; zero trust for agents.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S013, S014, S015, S033, S047, S048, S066, S067, S075.

### Stanford, business-school, and university talks (2011 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** Company building is mostly endurance and character; perspective matters more than vision; the CEO allocates scarce talent, holds a unifying theory, and reasons in front of the team; the 1990s near-death years taught him to make the right decision first.
- **Key frameworks:** perspective over vision; cannibalize yourself; early indicators of future success; go forward in time and look backward; org designed like the machine it builds; choose work that is hard, new, and yours; strategic retreat (with the later admission that entering mobile was a mistake).
- **Taught in sources:** S016, S017, S018, S020, S024, S025, S028, S029, S070, S071, S073, S076, S079.

### Commencement speeches (2023 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** Three stories carry his lessons: Sega (honesty and humility in failure), CUDA (endurance through years of poor results), and leaving the phone market for robotics (strategic retreat). The 2026 speech adds that societies that retreat from technology only give up the chance to shape it.
- **Key frameworks:** strategic retreat; zero billion dollar market; do the highest priority work first; setbacks as openings; believe something unconventional but reasoned.
- **Taught in sources:** S034, S035, S036.

### Earnings calls (2018 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** Demand exceeds supply; the platform shift is structural, not one application; NVIDIA wins because it runs every model, in every cloud, across the whole AI life cycle, and because its supply chain trusts its orders.
- **Key frameworks:** compute equals revenue; revenue per gigawatt by generation; three platform shifts; three business segments that grow beyond hyperscaler spend; performance per watt as revenue; supply commitments ahead of the curve.
- **Taught in sources:** S045, S046, S054, S055, S056, S057.

### Policy talks, TV interviews, and partner fireside chats (2018 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** AI is national infrastructure built in five layers; conceding markets and slowing open source weakens the platform; regulate real harms, not imagined ones; you will lose your job to someone who uses AI, not to AI; every company should treat agents like employees.
- **Key frameworks:** five-layer cake as a competitive scorecard; sovereign AI; task versus purpose; apply current law before new law; zero trust and containment for agents; off the shelf where you can, your own where you must; your data is your source code.
- **Taught in sources:** S019, S021, S022, S023, S026, S027, S031, S032, S049, S050, S051, S052, S053, S068, S069, S072, S077, S078, S080, S081, S082, S083, S084, S085, S086, S087, S088, S089, S090, S091, S092, S093, S094.

### Written letters and posts (2016 to 2026)
- **Core thesis:** Short written statements of the same theses: deep learning as a new computing model (2016), the Mellanox merger as the answer to the next bottleneck (2020), AI as a five-layer cake (2026), and AI factory compute as a financeable asset class (2026).
- **Key frameworks:** end-to-end AI platform; accelerated-disaggregated infrastructure; five-layer cake; land, power, and shell as a strategic resource; productive, fungible, durable compute.
- **Taught in sources:** S058, S059, S060, S061, S062, S063, S064, S065.

## Books he cites

### The Innovator's Dilemma and sequels, Clayton Christensen (1997 on)
- **Core thesis:** Successful incumbents lose to new technologies because serving today's best customers blinds them to cheaper or different products that start small.
- **Why it matters to this seat:** The book he names most. He calls the series intuitive and approachable (S004), says it teaches how industries evolve and how to set expectations for an emerging technology (S067), and tells people to read all of them (S073). His account of Intel ignoring accelerated computing (S024) and his rule to cannibalize yourself (S020) follow the same logic.
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S067, S073.

### Positioning, Al Ries and Jack Trout (1981)
- **Core thesis:** A product wins by owning a clear place in the customer's mind, usually by being first in a category.
- **Why it matters to this seat:** He reads it as a strategy book about how people see products and how to present them (S067, S073). It fits his habit of naming categories (AI factory, zero billion dollar market) before the market exists.
- **Taught in sources:** S067, S073.

### Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach, John Hennessy and David Patterson (1990)
- **Core thesis:** Computer design should be driven by measurement and quantitative tradeoffs, not intuition.
- **Why it matters to this seat:** He praises it for turning an abstract field into engineering (S067). It matches his speed-of-light habit of benchmarking against hard limits.
- **Taught in sources:** S067.

### Zero to One, Peter Thiel (2014)
- **Core thesis:** Real value comes from making something new, not copying what exists, and from building a monopoly in a market you create.
- **Why it matters to this seat:** He says he only skimmed it but that it reads like the NVIDIA story of choosing work nobody thought possible. It matches his zero billion dollar market framework. "Peter Teal's 0ero to1 book. In a lot of ways is kind of a story about Nvidia 2." (S024)
- **Taught in sources:** S024.

### Andy Grove's books (he names no title; Only the Paranoid Survive is the best known)
- **Core thesis:** Managers must spot strategic inflection points early and act with paranoia about survival.
- **Why it matters to this seat:** He recommends all of them (S004). His "30 days from going out of business" habit (S003, S024) is the same instinct.
- **Taught in sources:** S004.

### Crossing the Chasm, Geoffrey Moore (1991)
- **Core thesis:** New technology products stall between early adopters and the mainstream unless they win one niche completely first.
- **Why it matters to this seat:** Named in passing as a good book (S073). The idea fits his rule of narrowing to one audience (gamers) before expanding (S003).
- **Taught in sources:** S073.

### Others named in passing
- Sapiens, Yuval Noah Harari, and "everybody's first calculus book," which he calls his favorite (S073). Neither shapes how he decides in the corpus.
