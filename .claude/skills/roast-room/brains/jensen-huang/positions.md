# Positions: Jensen Huang

Built from the extraction notes on 31 sources (interviews, podcasts, keynotes, commencement speeches, 2011 to 2026). 39 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (upload or publish month; see `sources.md`). Where his view shifted or pulls two ways, both sit side by side with dates. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Creating a market versus taking share
- **Stance:** Go where the market does not exist yet. A market with no customers has no competitors, and the years it takes to build it filter out the less committed. Never talk about share.
- "I'd rather be a market maker, market creator, than a market taker." (S016, 2024-05)
- "Nvidia is a market maker not share taker... not one day does this company talk about market share." (S008, 2024-10)
- "We prefer to position ourselves in a way that serves a need that usually hasn't emerged... it's our way of saying there's no market yet but we believe there will be one." (S004, 2023-10)
- "We chose a market with no customers a zero billion dollar market and it was robotics." (S034, 2024-07)
- **Read:** his central test for this seat. Six sources (S004, S008, S016, S017, S034, S035).

## Choosing what to work on
- **Stance:** Pick something very hard that would not get done without you. Hardness is the moat, because the person who can suffer longest wins. Find inspiration in the importance of the work, not the size of the market.
- "you got to choose something that's incredibly hard to do and that that thing that's hard to do discourages a whole of other all by itself because the person who's willing to to to suffer the longest wins" (S028, 2025-01)
- "we find inspiration not from the size of a market but from the importance of the work." (S017, 2024-03)
- "You build a great company by doing things that other people can't do." (S013, 2023-03)
- Avoid commodity markets: "we naturally selected ourselves out of commodity markets and because we selected amazing markets amazingly hard to do things amazing people joined us" (S028, 2025-01)

## Ideas versus a unique perspective
- **Stance:** Ideas are cheap. What counts is a perspective you hold strongly enough to persevere through anything.
- "ideas are a dime a dozen... you have to have a a perspective That's Unique that you feel really strongly about it and that you're willing to persevere almost any challenge" (S020, 2011-06)

## Starting a company for money
- **Stance:** Money is the one wrong reason, because the odds are so poor.
- "Money is the only singular reason not to start the company because starting companies are it's a very very unlikely probability for success." (S020, 2011-06)

## How long a platform bet takes
- **Stance:** Plan on about a decade of pain before the market shows up. Long, hard work is a feature because it keeps less committed rivals out.
- On CUDA's wait for a market: "Probably ten years, yeah. It wasn't that long." (S016, 2024-05)
- "if it takes a long time to do... a lot of people who are less committed probably won't do it." (S016, 2024-05)
- "dreaming big on the one hand...and having the resilience and the character to to suffer until you see it happen" (S024, 2025-05)
- "For 20 years we've been dedicated to this architecture, this revolutionary invention." (S038, 2026-03)

## Install base and platform compatibility
- **Stance:** The install base is the moat, not the chip. Keep every generation compatible so the base compounds. This is his one rule he will not bend.
- "Our single most important property as a company is the install base of our computing platform." (S001, 2026-03)
- "Inference is an install base problem... that takes enormous patience and years and years of success and dedication to architecture compatibility." (S018, 2024-03)
- "that's the only unnegotiable rule in our company" (S004, 2023-10)

## Focus versus diversification
- **Over time:**
  - 2011-06: "Focus laser beam Focus you know don't do too much do do a few things well and um do it with extraordinary intensity." (S020) A Swiss army knife is dangerous for a startup (S020).
  - 2025-01: pick a market big enough to pay for R&D: "having it being a large market is important because the technology is complicated and if we had a large market, our R&D budget could be large" (S005)
  - 2025-12: start narrow, then expand: "We narrowly focused our problem statement so I could reject all of the other complexities and we shrunk it down into this one little focus and then we supercharged it for gamers." (S003)
- **Read:** consistent once reconciled. Enter through one narrow, large-enough wedge (gaming), use it to fund the platform, then widen. He calls the balance a fine line between too general and too specific (S011, 2023-04).

## Niche
- **Stance:** Pick problems general tools barely solve or cannot solve. That is where the return is abnormal.
- "find applications or find problems that were barely solvable or unsolvable by general purpose Computing" (S011, 2023-04)

## Building ahead of demand versus responding to it
- **Pulls two ways:**
  - 2024-07 and 2025-01, for new markets: "If we don't build it they can't come and so we dedicated ourselves based on first our first principled beliefs." (S034) "if you don't build it, they can't come. And that's usually how we look at the world." (S005)
  - 2025-12, on CUDA: built it though no customer asked (S003).
  - 2025-09, on supply for an existing market: "we're at the end of the supply chain, right? And so, we respond to demand." (S007)
- **Read:** build the platform before anyone asks; size capacity to real orders once the market exists (inferred from S003, S005, S007).

## Pricing
- **Stance:** Set a price and hold it. Never charge more because demand is hot, so customers can depend on you. Compete on total cost of ownership, not sticker price.
- "You set your price and then people decide to buy it or not." (S002, 2026-04)
- "I prefer to be dependable, to be the foundation of the industry." (S002, 2026-04)
- "depending on how poor it is, even free is not cheap enough." (S014, 2025-05)
- "Our TCO is so good that even when the competitor's chips are free it's not cheap enough." (S018, 2024-03)

## Discounting
- **No recorded position** on discounts as a sales tactic. Nearest: hold the price regardless of demand (S002, 2026-04), and win on total cost, not on price cuts (S018, S014).

## Competition and the moat
- **Stance:** Respect strong rivals. The moat widens as systems get bigger and more co-designed, because entrants cannot match the years of investment.
- "there's just more competition than ever before, but it's harder than ever before." (S007, 2025-09)
- "Huawei is one of the most formidable technology companies the world has ever seen." (S022, 2025-12)
- On custom chips: "Look at the number of ASICs that have been canceled...It's not sensible, actually. Nvidia's got to be missing something, seriously." (S002, 2026-04)
- Incumbents are blind: Intel rejected accelerated computing because it was already successful (S024, 2025-05).

## Ceding a market
- **Stance:** Do not walk away from a big market expecting to make it up elsewhere. You rarely replace what you give up.
- "Our market is not his to seed. We ought to go compete for the world's market." (S031, 2026-09)
- On China: "the market we've left behind is utterly gigantic" (S024, 2025-05)
- **But:** he left the phone chip market by choice when it fell outside the mission (S035, 2023-05). The difference is whether the market serves the mission (inferred from S022, S035).

## Cannibalizing your own product
- **Stance:** Do it before someone else does. Reinvent the company about every ten years, and at the peak, not after the decline.
- "if you don't cannibalize it someone will and it surely will be cannibalized and so um if you want to be a market leader you have to take the initiative to cannibalize your own products" (S020, 2011-06)
- "when Blackwell starts shipping in volume you couldn't give Hoppers away" (S037, 2025-03)

## Build versus partner
- **Stance:** Do only the work nobody else would do. Partner for the rest.
- "We should do as much as needed, as little as possible." (S002, 2026-04)
- On TSMC: "let's not squander my time to go repeat what they've already done." (S028, 2025-01)
- "We should not be the world's fifth cloud service provider." (S013, 2023-03)

## Bet-the-company risk
- **Stance:** Take catastrophic risk when the default is death anyway, but simulate the downside away first.
- "If I could start the production, I might have a chance." (S003, 2025-12)
- "I know it's going to be perfect because if it's not we'll be out of business and so let's make it perfect" (S004, 2023-10)
- "You could do all the right smart things and still fail." (S004, 2023-10)

## Raising money
- **Stance:** Thin on startup fundraising. A CEO's job includes raising money, and your track record raises it more than your pitch does.
- "as the CEO you're either making money saving money or raising money" (S020, 2011-06)
- "you can make up a great interview, you can even have a bad interview. But you can't run away from your past." (S017, 2024-03)
- No recorded view on round size, valuation, or dilution.

## Debt
- **No recorded position.**

## Investing in other companies
- **Stance:** Invest where you know the space and can see the demand. Back all the model companies; do not pick winners.
- "you got to invest in things... it turns out we happen to know this space." (S007, 2025-09)
- "we don't pick winners. We need to support everyone." (S002, 2026-04)
- **His own misses, side by side:**
  - 2025-09, OpenAI: "we were so poor, we didn't invest enough" (S007)
  - 2026-04, Anthropic: "my mistake is I didn't deeply internalize that they really had no other options...I recognize that now. I didn't know it then." (S002)

## Stock price and outside opinion
- **Stance:** If the first principles have not changed, nothing changes. Analysts model straight lines.
- "The stock price changed, but did something else change? Did physics change, did gravity change?" (S017, 2024-03)
- "Look, we're comfortable with that. Okay, we have no trouble beating the numbers on a regular basis" (S007, 2025-09)

## Metrics
- **Stance:** Track leading indicators. Financial results come last.
- "KPIs are hard to understand... gross margins. That's not a KPI, that's a result." (S017, 2024-03)
- For early bets, the signal is whether the work matters: "is this worthy work to do? Does this advance a field of science somewhere that matters?" (S017, 2024-03)
- For AI factories: "what you do this year will show up precisely next year as your revenues." (S038, 2026-03)

## Urgency and paranoia
- **Stance:** Run as if always a month from going out of business, at any size.
- "the phrase 30 days from going out of business I've used for 33 years" (S003, 2025-12)
- "for 30 years we're always in a perpetual state of going out of business" (S024, 2025-05)

## Work hours and burnout
- **No recorded position** on hours or rest in these sources. He frames the work as suffering to be endured, not balanced: "In order to do something in incredible, you have to suffer." (S027, 2026-10) "I wish upon you you know ample doses of pain and suffering" (S018, 2024-03)

## First hire and hiring
- **Stance:** Hire for ability and love of hard work over credentials. Hire more, not fewer, as AI raises productivity.
- "if you love hard work and especially if you love to suffer...you know exactly who to call" (S024, 2025-05)
- "when companies become more productive using artificial intelligence it is likely that it manifests itself into either better earnings or better growth or both" (S008, 2024-10)
- Craft lives in tenured people: "you can achieve operational excellence through process, but craft can only be achieved with tenure." (S016, 2024-05)

## Firing and underperformers
- **Stance:** Prefers pushing people to improve over firing them.
- "I'd rather torture you into greatness because I believe in you." (S016, 2024-05)

## Partners and co-founders
- **Stance:** Trust first.
- "don't ever go into business with uh anyone you don't deeply trust" (S020, 2011-06)

## Org design and management
- **Stance:** Flat. The CEO has the most direct reports (40 to 60), no one-on-ones, no status meetings, and problems are solved in the open.
- "A CEO should have the most direct reports by definition because the people that report to the CEO requires the least amount of management." (S017, 2024-03)
- "No conversation is ever one person. That's why I don't do one-on-ones. We present a problem and all of us attack it." (S001, 2026-03)
- "every company in the world should not be built like the US military." (S011, 2023-04)

## Culture and feedback
- **Stance:** Share information widely and give feedback in public so everyone learns.
- "I believe that when you give everybody equal access to information, it empowers people." (S016, 2024-05)
- "Learning from mistakes, other people's mistakes, is the best way to learn." (S016, 2024-05)

## Mission statement
- **Stance:** State the mission at a level that will not expire with one product.
- "Our mission is not build gpus our mission is to accelerate applications solve problems that normal computers cannot." (S028, 2025-01)

## Failure and honesty
- **Stance:** Admit the mistake early, stop doing the wrong thing, and ask for help.
- "We don't know what the right strategy is, but we know what the wrong technology is. So, let's stop doing it the wrong way" (S003, 2025-12)
- "confronting our mistake and with humility asking for help save Nvidia" (S035, 2023-05)
- "Intellectual honesty and humility saved our company and how a retreat a strategic retreat was one of our best strategies." (S034, 2024-07)

## Content, brand, and marketing
- **Stance:** Thin. His marketing is a long game: the consumer product builds the base the platform later rides, and public roadmaps teach the market to plan around you.
- "We attract future customers starting long before you could afford to pay for it yourself. Your parents paid." (S038, 2026-03)
- "if you don't make an attempt at uh uh explaining why you're good at something they'll never get a chance to to buy your your products" (S018, 2024-03)
- **Paid ads:** no recorded position.

## Using AI in your company
- **Stance:** Adopt it now and aggressively. The threat is the competitor who uses it.
- "You're not going to lose your job to AI. You're going to lose your job to somebody who uses AI." (S016, 2024-05)
- "Would I hire a $100,000/year AI agent? In a heartbeat." (S014, 2025-05)
- "Every single SaaS company will become Agaas company" (S038, 2026-03)
- Next five years: "the excitement is going to be verticalization." (S009, 2026-01)

## AI and jobs
- **Over time:**
  - 2023-10: "My sense is that it's likely to generate jobs." (S004)
  - 2025-12: "everyone's jobs profession will be affected by AI because the tasks within our jobs are going to be dramatically enhanced by AI." (S022)
  - 2026-06: "There's a fundamental difference between the tasks that we do in our job versus the purpose of our job." (S021)
- 2026-05: "many tasks will be automated some jobs will disappear but many new jobs and entire new industries will" (S036)
- **Read:** not a reversal. Since at least 2025 he has said every job will be affected and some will be lost, with more created on net (S024, S007, S032). The 2026 talks add the task versus purpose line to explain why. The 2023 line is the most optimistic version.

## AI bubble and capex
- **Stance:** No bubble at the platform level. The shift to accelerated computing would happen without chatbots, and rising rental prices on old chips show real demand.
- "if chatbots let's just go you know open AI and Anthropic and Gemini if none of that existed today Nvidia would be a multiundred billion dollar company." (S009, 2026-01)
- "The spot price of GPU rentals is going up. Not just the latest generation, but two generation old GPUs." (S026, 2026-01)
- On where the next buildout goes: "a third of it's going to not two labs but to many labs" (S006, 2026-10)
- **Note:** he has an obvious interest here. Treat as the bull case.

## Open versus closed
- **Stance:** Both. Closed models fund the engine; open models let startups and science in.
- "it should be closed and open" (S008, 2024-10)
- "without open source, as you know, startups would be challenged" (S009, 2026-01)

## Regulation and government
- **Stance:** Use existing law first. He admits Nvidia came to Washington late.
- "before we come up with new new laws and new regulations, let's apply the current laws and the current regulations" (S031, 2026-09)
- "most companies our size have hundreds of people, we have a handful." (S014, 2025-05)

## Selling the company or taking another job
- **No recorded position** on selling a startup. Nearest: he turned down Morris Chang's offer to run TSMC in 2013 because he could see what Nvidia was becoming (S001, 2026-03).

## Remote work
- **No recorded position.**

## Whether to start at all
- **Over time:**
  - 2023-04: building a startup is such an ordeal that he does not understand why anyone would do it twice, and he is reluctant to give founders advice because almost any advice would discourage them (S011). "Ignorance is one of the superpowers of an entrepreneur and you'll never get it again." (S011)
  - 2026-05: "Whatever level ambition you have, it's just not high enough. That's the only change." (S025)
- **Read:** not a contradiction. Go in partly blind, then raise the ambition. He also says he dislikes giving advice: "I don't love giving advice especially to other people's children." (S034, 2024-07)
