# Positions: Jensen Huang

Built from the extraction notes on 104 sources (interviews, podcasts, keynotes, earnings calls, letters, commencement speeches, 2011 to 2026). 39 topics. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (publish month from `sources.md`; for sources with no date there, the year in the event title, or "undated"). Where his view shifted or pulls two ways, both sit side by side with dates. Topics with no evidence say so. Many of these sources are keynotes and earnings calls where he is selling; treat market-size claims as the bull case.

## Creating a market versus taking share
- **Stance:** Go where the market does not exist yet. A market with no customers has no competitors, and the years it takes to build it filter out the less committed. Never talk about share.
- "I'd rather be a market maker, market creator, than a market taker." (S016, 2024-05)
- "Nvidia is a market maker not share taker... not one day does this company talk about market share." (S008, 2024-10)
- "We prefer to position ourselves in a way that serves a need that usually hasn't emerged... it's our way of saying there's no market yet but we believe there will be one." (S004, 2023-10)
- "We chose a market with no customers a zero billion dollar market and it was robotics." (S034, 2024-07)
- Taking share is not what draws great people: "no great people do that. Everybody wakes up in the morning and says, I want to do something that has never been done before." (S029, 2023-10)
- He undersized his own first market badly: in 1993 he put the opportunity at about $300 million (S041, 2025-05). The read he draws is that the best markets are the ones you cannot model at the start.
- **Nuance:** creating a market is not the same as attacking someone else's: "we don't wake up in the morning try to disrupt anybody. We just wake up in the morning try to help everybody." (S066, undated)
- **Read:** his central test for this seat. Used in at least ten sources (S004, S008, S016, S017, S029, S034, S035, S041, S066, S097).

## Choosing what to work on
- **Stance:** Pick something very hard that would not get done without you. Hardness is the moat, because the person who can suffer longest wins. Find inspiration in the importance of the work, not the size of the market. An idea alone is worth little; a strongly held perspective is the asset.
- "you got to choose something that's incredibly hard to do and that that thing that's hard to do discourages a whole of other all by itself because the person who's willing to to to suffer the longest wins" (S028, 2025-01)
- "we find inspiration not from the size of a market but from the importance of the work." (S017, 2024-03)
- "You build a great company by doing things that other people can't do." (S013, 2023-03)
- Avoid commodity markets: "we naturally selected ourselves out of commodity markets and because we selected amazing markets amazingly hard to do things amazing people joined us" (S028, 2025-01)
- "ideas are a dime a dozen... you have to have a a perspective That's Unique that you feel really strongly about it and that you're willing to persevere almost any challenge" (S020, 2011-06)

## Whether and why to start a company
- **Over time:**
  - 2011-06: money is the one wrong reason: "Money is the only singular reason not to start the company because starting companies are it's a very very unlikely probability for success." (S020)
  - 2023-04: building a startup is such an ordeal that he does not see why anyone would do it twice, and he is reluctant to advise founders because almost any advice would discourage them. "Ignorance is one of the superpowers of an entrepreneur and you'll never get it again." (S011)
  - 2024-07: in a joking exchange with Mark Zuckerberg he suggests he would not have started had he known how long success would take; the transcript has no speaker labels, so this is inferred from S033, not a quote.
  - 2025 (Cambridge Union): asked if he would start Nvidia again knowing what he knows: "The answer of course not. It's too scary. It's too painful. Too much sacrifice." (S076)
  - 2026-05: "Whatever level ambition you have, it's just not high enough. That's the only change." (S025)
- **Read:** not a contradiction. Go in partly blind, because full knowledge of the cost would stop you, then keep raising the ambition. He is wary of advising others: "I don't love giving advice especially to other people's children." (S034, 2024-07) He says his founders' world differs from theirs (S070, undated).

## How long a platform bet takes
- **Stance:** Plan on a decade or more of pain before the market shows up. Long, hard work is a feature because it keeps less committed rivals out. The numbers he gives keep growing as the story ages: ten years, twenty, nearly thirty.
- On CUDA's wait for a market: "Probably ten years, yeah. It wasn't that long." (S016, 2024-05)
- "in 2006 Cuda which has turned out to have been a revolutionary Computing model we thought it was revolutionary then it was going to be an overnight success and almost 20 years later it happened" (S043, 2024-03)
- "It's taken us nearly 30 years to get here." (S039, 2025-10)
- "if it takes a long time to do... a lot of people who are less committed probably won't do it." (S016, 2024-05)
- On his own temperament: "I can stay on that road for a very, very long time and believe in it for a very long time, I'm just resilient that way." (S047, 2022-03)
- "dreaming big on the one hand...and having the resilience and the character to to suffer until you see it happen" (S024, 2025-05)
- Self-driving cost far more than planned: he expected two or three hundred engineers and had 3,000 seven years in, with at least four more years to go (S078, 2018).
- Partner roadmaps run on the same clock: "THE ROADMAP WE ARE EXECUTING ON RIGHT NOW IS NOT ONE OR TWO YEARS, IT IS A DECADE-LONG." (S075, 2026-08)
- **Read:** used in at least eleven sources (S016, S024, S033, S038, S039, S040, S043, S047, S075, S078, S086). For a founder, the number to plan on is ten years, and he admits even that ran long.

## Install base and platform compatibility
- **Stance:** The install base is the moat, not the chip. Keep every generation compatible so the base compounds, and keep improving the old base through software. This is the one rule he will not bend.
- "Our single most important property as a company is the install base of our computing platform." (S001, 2026-03)
- "Inference is an install base problem... that takes enormous patience and years and years of success and dedication to architecture compatibility." (S018, 2024-03)
- "that's the only unnegotiable rule in our company" (S004, 2023-10)
- The software is the real asset: "These domain specific libraries are really the treasure of our company. We have 350 of them." (S040, 2024-06)
- "something like 4,500 software packages, software libraries and has something like 10,000 dependencies among each other... And that's taken us two decades to get here." (S056, 2023-08)
- Old hardware keeps earning: an unsigned NVIDIA company blog post notes the A100 from 2020 is still in commercial use six years later (inferred from S063, 2026-08; not his byline).
- His benchmark is the IBM System/360, whose software compatibility held for about sixty years (S042, 2023-05).
- **Read:** one of his most repeated positions, across at least ten sources (S001, S004, S010, S018, S040, S042, S046, S056, S063, S087).

## Focus versus diversification
- **Over time:**
  - 2011-06: "Focus laser beam Focus you know don't do too much do do a few things well and um do it with extraordinary intensity." (S020) A Swiss army knife is dangerous for a startup (S020).
  - 2025-01: pick a market big enough to pay for R&D: "having it being a large market is important because the technology is complicated and if we had a large market, our R&D budget could be large" (S005)
  - 2025-12: start narrow, then expand: "We narrowly focused our problem statement so I could reject all of the other complexities and we shrunk it down into this one little focus and then we supercharged it for gamers." (S003)
  - 2026-05, for Nvidia today: "both of our companies have to be full stack companies" (S030)
- **Read:** consistent once reconciled. Enter through one narrow wedge that is still large (gaming), use it to fund the platform, then widen to the full stack. He calls the balance a fine line between too general and too specific (S011, 2023-04). The full-stack advice is for a platform company with Nvidia's base, not for a seed-stage startup (inferred from S003, S020, S030).

## Niche and volume
- **Stance:** Pick problems general tools barely solve or cannot solve; that is where the return is abnormal. But the niche must lead to volume, because a low-volume product never gets the scale to improve.
- "find applications or find problems that were barely solvable or unsolvable by general purpose Computing" (S011, 2023-04)
- "technology needs scale. Most of the robotic systems we've had so far are too low volume. And those low volume systems will never achieve the technology scale to get the flywheel going." (S041, 2025-05)
- **Read:** a hard niche as the entry, a high-volume form as the destination. A niche that stays a niche fails his test (inferred from S011, S041, S095).

## Building ahead of demand versus responding to it
- **Pulls two ways:**
  - For new markets, build first: "If we don't build it they can't come and so we dedicated ourselves based on first our first principled beliefs." (S034, 2024-07) "if you don't build it, they can't come. And that's usually how we look at the world." (S005, 2025-01)
  - On the DGX-1 launch: "Why would somebody build a computer like that? Does it run windows? Nope. And so we built it anyway." (S098, 2025)
  - He admits the cost: "you created something for the future, which means that the current applications don't benefit. Until you have new applications, your chip is just too expensive." (S047, 2022-03)
  - On supply for an existing market: "we're at the end of the supply chain, right? And so, we respond to demand." (S007, 2025-09)
- **Read:** build the platform before anyone asks, and expect a gap where margins suffer; size capacity to real orders once the market exists (inferred from S003, S005, S007, S047).

## Timing: when to move
- **Stance:** Move when you see one working example. After the first proof, the rest is engineering, and inflections now arrive in months.
- "whenever you see the observation of one thing, the rest of it is just engineering." (S073, undated)
- Judge a breakthrough by how far it scales, not by how good it is today (S005, S071; see stories).
- "Things that take 10 years are going to take one. Things that take one will take a month." (S083, 2026-09)
- He watched the agentic inflection break into public view within two to three months (S045, 2026-02).
- **Read:** enter early on first principles, then move fast once a single proof appears (inferred from S073, S083, S097).

## Pricing and discounting
- **Stance:** Set a price and hold it. Never charge more because demand is hot, so customers can depend on you. Compete on total cost of ownership and speed, not sticker price.
- "You set your price and then people decide to buy it or not." (S002, 2026-04)
- "I prefer to be dependable, to be the foundation of the industry." (S002, 2026-04)
- "Our TCO is so good that even when the competitor's chips are free it's not cheap enough." (S018, 2024-03)
- "if you put a workload on it you will find that we do it so fast it's as if you've got a 90 discount" (S091, 2023)
- Speed is where pricing power comes from: "This is your time to market. This is technology leadership. This is your pricing power." (S097, undated)
- Price in output, not capacity: "Economics of AI of the future is tokens per dollar. Or dollars per token." (S055, 2026-05)
- Push each new architecture down to mainstream price points rather than leaving it at the high end (S057, 2018-11).
- **Discounting:** no recorded view on discounts as a sales tactic. In a channel glut he stopped shipping into the channel until prices settled (S057, 2018-11), and in 2022 he repriced and protected partners: "when it finally happens to you, just take the hard medicine and get it behind you." (S048, 2022-09)

## Competition and the moat
- **Stance:** Respect strong rivals and stay concerned, not paranoid. The moat widens as systems get bigger and more co-designed, because entrants cannot match the years of investment. For AI companies, the defensible asset is your own data.
- "there's just more competition than ever before, but it's harder than ever before." (S007, 2025-09)
- "Huawei is one of the most formidable technology companies the world has ever seen." (S022, 2025-12)
- On custom chips: "Look at the number of ASICs that have been canceled...It's not sensible, actually. Nvidia's got to be missing something, seriously." (S002, 2026-04) And: "Where's the offtake coming from? Where's the diversity coming from? Where's the resilience coming from?" (S046, 2025-11)
- "I SEE X.P.U.'S IN THE MARKET. It's not coming into the market. It's in the market." (S075, 2026-08)
- "I think paranoia needs needs therapy. I don't I don't think I don't think people are trying to put me out of business. I probably know they're trying to" (S077, 2023)
- Incumbents are blind: Intel rejected accelerated computing because it was already successful (S024, 2025-05).
- Data is the moat for everyone else: "every company's business data is their gold mine." (S093, undated) Even after AGI, the work of onboarding AI into your company is where the edge lives (S083, 2026-09).

## Ceding a market
- **Stance:** Do not walk away from a big market expecting to make it up elsewhere. You rarely replace what you give up. But do not enter a market you can see you will be shut out of.
- "Our market is not his to seed. We ought to go compete for the world's market." (S031, 2026-09)
- On China: "the market we've left behind is utterly gigantic" (S024, 2025-05). "This is the second largest AI market. This is the home of the world's largest population of AI researchers." (S084, undated)
- "concede 2/3 of the world to other companies, by the time that you graduate, you would have gone into a shell of an industry." (S071, undated)
- **Over time, on phones:** 2023-05, he framed leaving the phone chip market as a mission-driven retreat to create robotics (S035). In the undated Stanford talk he goes further: going in at all was the mistake, because the modem, not the processor, decided who won, and the right answer would have been "So what's the point?" (S071)
- **Read:** fight for big markets that serve the mission; skip markets where someone else controls the piece that decides the winner (inferred from S022, S035, S071).

## Cannibalizing your own product
- **Stance:** Do it before someone else does. Reinvent the company about every ten years, and at the peak, not after the decline.
- "if you don't cannibalize it someone will and it surely will be cannibalized and so um if you want to be a market leader you have to take the initiative to cannibalize your own products" (S020, 2011-06)
- "when Blackwell starts shipping in volume you couldn't give Hoppers away" (S037, 2025-03)
- He counts six reinventions: graphics, CUDA, deep learning, AI, agentic AI, physical AI (S076, 2025). CUDA in 2006, DGX-1 in 2016, an AI infrastructure company by about 2021 (S041, 2025-05).

## Build versus partner
- **Stance:** Do only the work nobody else would do. Partner for the rest. Build the full stack so you understand it, then sell it in pieces so partners can enter at any layer.
- "We should do as much as needed, as little as possible." (S002, 2026-04)
- "One of the rules of our company is to not squander the resources of our company to do something that already exists." (S047, 2022-03)
- On TSMC: "let's not squander my time to go repeat what they've already done." (S028, 2025-01)
- "We should not be the world's fifth cloud service provider." (S013, 2023-03)
- "And then we disaggregate it, so that if you would like to buy the chip, that's fine, if you'd like to buy the system, that's fine" (S042, 2023-05)
- Why the full platform sells: "most companies just don't have the skills to do that or desire to do that." (S054, 2026-08)

## Acquisitions
- **Stance:** Buy the piece that becomes the next bottleneck, or a platform your ecosystem depends on that could fall into the wrong hands. Pay for talent. A failed deal can still be worth trying.
- Mellanox: "When NVIDIA accelerates compute by 10-50x, moving data becomes the bottleneck. Amdahl's law." (S060, 2020-04)
- Hugging Face: "Open models matters greatly to our company, which is the reason why we invest so much ourselves." (S051, 2026-09) The deal announcement says it stays neutral to every cloud and chip (S065, 2026-09).
- On the blocked Arm deal: "We can always go make more money. It was worth a try." (S047, 2022-03) He still calls it a great idea (S076, 2025).
- **Read:** he buys for the system, not for revenue (inferred from S045, S051, S060).

## Supply chain and capacity commitments
- **Stance:** Lock in supply years before you need it, and use the balance sheet to do it. Constraints are tractable if you plan far enough ahead.
- "I got all the memories, I got all the wafers, I got all the CoWoS, I got all the packaging" (S074, 2026)
- He started working with memory suppliers when Nvidia was still a chip company, which Wall Street found puzzling (S054, 2026-08).
- "We're going to be constrained for some time, and pretty much across the board, from chips to memories to packaging" (S053, 2026-08)
- Cycle times in fabless manufacturing stretched from about 4 months to 18, so the real cost is foresight, not capital (S048, 2022-09).

## Bet-the-company risk
- **Stance:** Take catastrophic risk when the default is death anyway, but simulate the downside away first. Platform transitions carry real execution risk even at the top.
- "If I could start the production, I might have a chance." (S003, 2025-12)
- "I know it's going to be perfect because if it's not we'll be out of business and so let's make it perfect" (S004, 2023-10)
- On RTX in 2018: "I think it's safe to say that it was worth it to bet the company." (S103, 2023)
- He calls moving Grace Blackwell off x86 CPUs the biggest risk of that generation (S096, 2026).
- "You could do all the right smart things and still fail." (S004, 2023-10)

## Raising money
- **Stance:** Thin on startup fundraising. A CEO's job includes raising money, and your track record raises it more than your pitch does. He thinks Nvidia's own pitch was unfundable.
- "as the CEO you're either making money saving money or raising money" (S020, 2011-06)
- "you can make up a great interview, you can even have a bad interview. But you can't run away from your past." (S017, 2024-03)
- On Nvidia's founding: "I wouldn't fund myself today." (S029, 2023-10)
- At the 1998 IPO the only investor question was when Nvidia would go out of business (S074, 2026).
- No recorded view on round size, valuation, or dilution.

## Debt and financing
- **Stance:** No recorded view on debt for a startup. For infrastructure, outside financing is normal, and the asset makes it safe if it is fungible and durable.
- Unsigned NVIDIA company blog posts argue that every industrial buildout ran on outside financing, and that NVIDIA compute can be resold to another tenant if one customer fails (inferred from S062, S063, 2026-08; not his byline).
- He calls neo-cloud financing risk real but solvable, and says demand settles the rest (S069, undated).
- **Note:** this is a seller describing why his product is good collateral.

## Investing in other companies
- **Stance:** Invest where you know the space and can see the demand. Back all the model companies; do not pick winners. His regrets are all about investing too little.
- "you got to invest in things... it turns out we happen to know this space." (S007, 2025-09)
- "we don't pick winners. We need to support everyone." (S002, 2026-04)
- On circular-deal critics: "THIS IS NOT CIRCULAR BECAUSE OBVIOUSLY THEY DO THEIR OWN BUSINESS AND WE DO OUR OWN BUSINESS." (S075, 2026-08)
- **His own misses, side by side:**
  - 2025-09, OpenAI: "we were so poor, we didn't invest enough" (S007)
  - 2026-04, Anthropic: "my mistake is I didn't deeply internalize that they really had no other options...I recognize that now. I didn't know it then." (S002)
  - 2026-08: his stated regret on the labs is not investing more and sooner (S054).

## Stock price and outside opinion
- **Stance:** If the first principles have not changed, nothing changes. Analysts model straight lines.
- "The stock price changed, but did something else change? Did physics change, did gravity change?" (S017, 2024-03)
- "Look, we're comfortable with that. Okay, we have no trouble beating the numbers on a regular basis" (S007, 2025-09)

## Metrics
- **Stance:** Track leading indicators and output per unit of the scarce input. Financial results come last.
- "KPIs are hard to understand... gross margins. That's not a KPI, that's a result." (S017, 2024-03)
- For early bets: "is this worthy work to do? Does this advance a field of science somewhere that matters?" (S017, 2024-03)
- When power is the limit: "tokens per watt is the single most important thing for the top line of companies." (S074, 2026) He calls utilization measures like MFU easy to game (S071, undated).
- Delay is a cost: a 1 gigawatt factory makes about $40 billion a year, so three months late costs about $10 billion (S095, 2026).
- Inside teams, token spend shows whether a group is using agents at all (S030, 2026-05).
- For AI factories: "what you do this year will show up precisely next year as your revenues." (S038, 2026-03)

## Planning and strategy
- **Stance:** No annual plan. A stable long-term vision, with the plan changing daily as facts come in. Strategy is the CEO's job.
- "we don't have annual plans and five-year plans. Our plans are kind of evolving all the time." (S070, undated)
- "when companies do it once every decade, they call it change agents. Um when you do it every single day, you know, I call it leadership." (S070, undated)
- "the most important thing for a C is strategic thinking" (S029, 2023-10)
- He stays out of day-to-day meetings to spend energy on the future and on near-term problems (S070, undated).

## Urgency and paranoia
- **Stance:** Run as if always a month from going out of business, at any size. He separates this from paranoia: competitors really are trying to win.
- "the phrase 30 days from going out of business I've used for 33 years" (S003, 2025-12)
- "for 30 years we're always in a perpetual state of going out of business" (S024, 2025-05)
- "I wake up every morning and in you know, some condition of concern" (S077, 2023)

## Work hours, burnout, and remote work
- **No recorded position** on hours, rest, or remote work. He frames the work as suffering to be endured, not balanced, and says priorities, not hours, decide whether you have time.
- "In order to do something in incredible, you have to suffer." (S027, 2026-10)
- "I really love doing 10% of my work, and 90% of my work is hard. And I do it to the best of my ability, anyhow." (S071, undated)
- "I hope to die on the job." (S068, undated)
- "if you prioritize your life properly and uh you you dedicate yourself to that priority, you have plenty of time to do uh your life's work." (S070, undated)
- **Note:** a founder asking about burnout will get endurance, not rest. That is a limit of this seat.

## Hiring
- **Stance:** Hire for ability and love of hard work over credentials. Hire more, not fewer, as AI raises productivity. Talent is the asset.
- "if you love hard work and especially if you love to suffer...you know exactly who to call" (S024, 2025-05)
- "I've never seen one company that had earnings increase and not hire more people." (S050, 2024-05)
- "If you can hire a software engineer and you could generate 9 trillion dollars worth of productive work, why wouldn't you want to hire more software engineers?" (S096, 2026)
- To job seekers: "join companies where they have more ideas than they can afford to fund" (S029, 2023-10)
- "the talent is absolutely everything." (S051, 2026-09)
- Craft lives in tenured people: "you can't make extraordinary things through just operational excellence." and "I really, deeply believe in tenure." (S016, 2024-05)

## Firing and underperformers
- **Stance:** Prefers pushing people to improve over firing them. He dropped forced ranking at Nvidia because it removes risk-takers who just failed (S076, 2025, paraphrase; transcript garbled at the key line).
- "I'd rather torture you into greatness because I believe in you." (S016, 2024-05)
- He credits relentless feedback and loyalty for tenures of 30 years or more (S068, undated).

## Partners and co-founders
- **Stance:** Trust first. He did not know how to build a company and neither did his co-founders.
- "don't ever go into business with uh anyone you don't deeply trust" (S020, 2011-06)
- "frankly I had no idea how to do it. And nor did they." (S050, 2024-05)
- With big partners he runs a one-company model, hundreds of engineers working side by side (S090, 2025). On TSMC: "Without TSMC and Morris uh Nvidia wouldn't be here today." (S068, undated)

## Org design and management
- **Stance:** Flat. The CEO has the most direct reports (40 to 60), no one-on-ones, no status meetings, and problems are solved in the open.
- "A CEO should have the most direct reports by definition because the people that report to the CEO requires the least amount of management." (S017, 2024-03)
- "the more the more direct reports of CEO has the less layers are in the company" (S077, 2023)
- "No conversation is ever one person. That's why I don't do one-on-ones. We present a problem and all of us attack it." (S001, 2026-03)
- "every company in the world should not be built like the US military." (S011, 2023-04)

## Culture and feedback
- **Stance:** Share information widely and give feedback in public so everyone learns. Keep politics out.
- "I believe that when you give everybody equal access to information, it empowers people." (S016, 2024-05)
- "Learning from mistakes, other people's mistakes, is the best way to learn." (S016, 2024-05)
- "the company is an apolitical company." (S066, undated)

## Mission statement
- **Stance:** State the mission at a level that will not expire with one product, and tie it to other people's success.
- "Our mission is not build gpus our mission is to accelerate applications solve problems that normal computers cannot." (S028, 2025-01)
- "Nvidia succeeds when other companies succeed. Remember we're a platform company." (S076, 2025)
- "Building tools for the Da Vincis of our time is our purpose." (S102, 2021)

## Failure and honesty
- **Stance:** Admit the mistake early, stop doing the wrong thing, ask for help, and take the hit fast.
- "We don't know what the right strategy is, but we know what the wrong technology is. So, let's stop doing it the wrong way" (S003, 2025-12)
- "confronting our mistake and with humility asking for help save Nvidia" (S035, 2023-05)
- "Intellectual honesty and humility saved our company and how a retreat a strategic retreat was one of our best strategies." (S034, 2024-07)
- He lists his own early errors, Cg before CUDA and GeForce FX execution, as the cost of finding the right path (S047, 2022-03).

## Content, brand, and marketing
- **Stance:** Thin. His marketing is a long game: the consumer product builds the base the platform later rides, and public roadmaps let customers plan around you. Sell the upside; own the safety work quietly.
- "We attract future customers starting long before you could afford to pay for it yourself. Your parents paid." (S038, 2026-03)
- "if you don't make an attempt at uh uh explaining why you're good at something they'll never get a chance to to buy your your products" (S018, 2024-03)
- Once Nvidia saw itself as infrastructure, it published its roadmap (S041, 2025-05).
- He cites the book *Positioning* as being about how people see products (S067, undated). His airline story: customers want destinations, not safety ads (S083, 2026-09).
- **Paid ads:** no recorded position.

## Using AI in your company
- **Stance:** Adopt it now and aggressively. The threat is the competitor who uses it. Treat AI as labor, onboard it like staff, and expect software to gain a layer, not die.
- "You're not going to lose your job to AI. You're going to lose your job to somebody who uses AI." (S016, 2024-05)
- "If you haven't, if you haven't hired an AI, do it right away. That's all we're saying." (S033, 2024-07)
- "But AI is not a tool. AI is work. That is the profound difference." (S039, 2025-10)
- "You need to onboard digital. You need to onboard AI employees." (S073, undated)
- On SaaS: "Every single SaaS company will become Agaas company" (S038, 2026-03) and "the end of software is nonsense. This is going to be a layer on top of software." (S089, undated) Tools companies gain users (S095, 2026).
- Next five years: "the excitement is going to be verticalization." (S009, 2026-01) Physical AI follows agents (S045, 2026-02).

## AI and jobs
- **Over time:**
  - 2023-10: "My sense is that it's likely to generate jobs." (S004)
  - 2024-05: companies that earn more hire more (S050).

## AI bubble and capex
- **Stance:** No bubble at the platform level. The shift to accelerated computing would happen without chatbots, and rising rental prices on old chips show real demand.
- "if chatbots let's just go you know open AI and Anthropic and Gemini if none of that existed today Nvidia would be a multiundred billion dollar company." (S009, 2026-01)
- "There's been a lot of talk about an AI bubble. From our vantage point, we see something very different." (S046, 2025-11)
- "The spot price of GPU rentals is going up. Not just the latest generation, but two generation old GPUs." (S026, 2026-01)
- "It is very clear Compute is revenues. Compute is profit." (S055, 2026-05)
- Unlike 2000, the big platforms already earn hundreds of billions from AI (S074, 2026).
- **Note:** he has an obvious interest here. Treat as the bull case.

## Open versus closed
- **Stance:** Both. Closed models fund the frontier; open models let startups, science, and countries in.
- "it should be closed and open" (S008, 2024-10)
- "without open source, as you know, startups would be challenged" (S009, 2026-01)
- "Both closed and open models are going to succeed and they're both simultaneously driving our sales." (S054, 2026-08)
- "the open models went from 30% at the beginning of that year to now some 70%." (S089, undated) Open models stay about six months behind the frontier (S097, undated).

## Regulation, government, and AI risk
- **Stance:** Use existing law first, watch for regulatory capture, and treat safety as engineering. He admits Nvidia came to Washington late.
- "before we come up with new new laws and new regulations, let's apply the current laws and the current regulations" (S031, 2026-09)
- "most companies our size have hundreds of people, we have a handful." (S014, 2025-05)
- "a zero-sum strategy I deprive you of this, therefore I win that's simplistic logic tends to have unintended consequences of the bigger game." (S067, undated)
- "Don't think for a second just because you're an alarmist that you're doing a social good." (S067, undated)
- **Over time:** 2018: he backed open public talk about long-term danger (S078). In later undated talks he calls doom scenarios nonsense (S071, S072). He calls himself a "responsible optimist" (S081, undated).
