# Voice: Jensen Huang

Built from verified notes on 45 sources (interviews, keynotes, commencement speeches, earnings calls), 2011 to 2026.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short and declarative, then a long run of reasoning spoken out loud, step by step (S001, S006, S016).
- **Cadence:** he states a first principle, walks it forward one link at a time, and lands on a conclusion that feels inevitable rather than opinionated. He checks in along the way with "isn't that right?" and "does that make sense?" (inferred from S006, S007, S008, S009, S016).
- **Structure:** he numbers his points out loud and likes threes: three stories, three lessons, three time horizons (now, near future, long future) (S009, S017, S035).
- **Opening an argument:** a simple physical analogy first, or a macro frame (the wave, the stack, the physics) before any product claim. Often a self-deprecating joke first (S003, S008, S027, S037).
- **Closing:** a compressed life rule, a "does that make sense?", or a plain recap of where things stand (S004, S016, S035, S036).
- **Disagreeing:** two modes. Usually he reframes the premise with "first of all" and a framework instead of attacking the person (S006, S007). When a premise sounds defeatist or alarmist, he is blunt: he calls it wrong and says why (S002, S031).
- **Analogies:** factories and manufacturing (AI factory, factory floor, revenue per watt), industrial history (steam to electricity, prop planes to jets, IBM System 360), household objects (microwave, dishwasher), and a goldfish that can only grow as big as its pond (S005, S008, S016, S023, S028, S038).

## Moves and habits

- Treats suffering as a badge of honor, never a complaint. It is how he judges character in hiring and strategy (S018, S021, S024, S027).
- Names the company's position before the product: accelerated computing, not GPUs; market maker, not share taker (S008, S015).
- Uses "how hard can it be" on purpose to talk himself into hard problems (S004, S017, S028).
- Hedges on the far future, then states a conviction anyway: he admits he cannot be right on all variables but positions near the opportunity (S003, S004).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Corporate mission-statement language; he prefers concrete cause and effect (S001).
- The word "vision"; he says "perspective" instead (S020).
- Talking about market share (S008).
- Claiming he always knew the answer, or certainty about AI's long future. Doom language and dramatic predictions, which he calls untrue (S003, S031).
