## Customers and invention

1. **Center the company on customers, not competitors, because customers are never satisfied and keep pulling you forward even when you lead.**
   "customers are always unsatisfied. They're always discontent. They always want more." (S003) / "they should be afraid not of our competitors but they should be afraid of our customers because our customers are the only ones who are going to ever give us money" (S011)
   Sources: 14 (S003, S004, S011, S012, S024, S030, S037, S039, S044, S047, S060, S082, S085, S098). Confidence: high.

2. **Invent what customers cannot ask for; listening and market research are necessary but never enough.**
   "customer obsession is also inventing on their behalf because it's not their job to invent for themselves" (S014) / "No customer was asking for Echo. This was definitely us wandering." (S066)
   Sources: 9 (S009, S014, S021, S023, S025, S041, S064, S066, S078). Confidence: high.

3. **Put most of your energy into the product and the experience, not into shouting about it; word of mouth online is a megaphone.**
   "I believe online you invert those it's still important to shout but I think you should put thirty percent of your energy into shouting and seventy percent of your energy into building the best possible customer experience" (S082)
   Sources: 6 (S004, S021, S037, S082, S098, S101). Confidence: high.

4. **Do not enter a market with a me-too offer; enter only when you can say how it would be different.**
   "we don't want to do a me-too product" (S037)
   Sources: 6 (S003, S037, S044, S054, S083, S088). Confidence: high.

## Time horizon and capital

5. **Run on a five to seven year horizon; most people will not defer gratification, and that is your edge.**
   "If everything you do needs to work on a three-year time horizon, then you're competing against a lot of people. But if you're willing to invest on a seven-year time horizon, you're now competing against a fraction of those people." (S070)
   Sources: 8 (S014, S024, S037, S038, S054, S070, S098, S108). Confidence: high.

6. **Separate the stock from the company and manage the company.**
   "the stock is not the company and the company is not the stock." (S044) / "so if the stock is the company somebody forgot to tell the customers" (S027)
   Sources: 8 (S003, S008, S018, S023, S027, S044, S048, S060). Confidence: high.

7. **Choose future cash flows over good-looking accounting; earnings growth can destroy value.**
   "When forced to choose between optimizing the appearance of our GAAP accounting and maximizing the present value of future cash flows, we'll take the cash flows." (S038) / "a company can actually impair shareholder value in certain circumstances by growing earnings." (S052)
   Sources: 6 (S038, S048, S049, S050, S052, S060). Confidence: high.

8. **Price to earn trust; lower prices with scale and never raise them just because you can.**
   "so if you're waiting for us to raise prices give up now but we are going to lower prices" (S009) / "Our pricing objective is to earn customer trust, not to optimize short-term profit dollars." (S056)
   Sources: 8 (S009, S037, S049, S050, S051, S053, S056, S070). Confidence: high.

9. **Prefer low margins on a large customer base; high margins hide waste.**
   "There are two ways to build a successful company. One is to work very, very hard to convince customers to pay high margins. The other is to work very, very hard to be able to afford to offer customers low margins." (S070)
   Sources: 4 (S009, S029, S051, S070). Confidence: high.

10. **Chase growth before efficiency; optimize only once one percent of revenue is a meaningful number.**
    "you should instead be trying to build a company of the scale where one percent matters" (S027)
    Sources: 2 (S009, S027). Confidence: medium.

11. **Tell people which kind of company you are running so the right investors self-select.**
    "you can hold a rock concert and that's okay and you can hold a ballet and that's okay just don't hold a rock concert and advertise it as a ballet" (S018)
    Sources: 3 (S018, S027, S085). Confidence: high. He credits the line to Warren Buffett.

## Risk, bets, and failure

12. **Take many bold bets, but never one that can sink the company.**
    "I don't believe in bet the company bets that's when you're desperate that's that's the last thing you can do" (S018) / "the winners when you're doing these experiments winners pay for thousands of losers" (S025)
    Sources: 6 (S018, S025, S036, S063, S066, S086). Confidence: high (the no-bet-the-company line itself is one source, S018).

13. **Failure comes with invention; accept it, and grow the size of your failures as the company grows.**
    "you cannot invent and pioneer if you cannot accept failure" (S014) / "Amazon is the best place in the world to fail" (S074)
    Sources: 7 (S014, S018, S041, S063, S074, S086, S106). Confidence: high.

14. **Be realistic about the odds and use early capital to remove risk, not to seek it.**
    "good entrepreneurs don't like Risk they seek to reduce risk" (S029) / "I told them that I thought there was a 70 percent chance that they would lose their whole investment." (S043)
    Sources: 6 (S001, S029, S041, S043, S089, S108). Confidence: high.

15. **Double down when something works; give up on a bet only when the last high-judgment champion folds.**
    "when something works you should double down on it and that's what we're doing" (S086) / "I'm willing to keep funding things if if if there's one high judgment champion who still wants to keep trying" (S010)
    Sources: 5 (S010, S025, S036, S074, S086). Confidence: high.

16. **Anything new means being misunderstood, often for years; accept it or do not start.**
    "If you're going to do anything new or innovative, you have to be willing to be misunderstood." (S003) / "you've got to be willing to be misunderstood for long periods of time if you're going to invent" (S083)
    Sources: 7 (S003, S010, S037, S047, S070, S083, S091). Confidence: high.

17. **Credit luck honestly; every startup that became substantial had a lot of it.**
    "any startup company that turns into a substantial company over the years never lose track of the fact that there was a lot of luck involved in that" (S108)
    Sources: 4 (S009, S020, S043, S108). Confidence: high.

## How to decide

18. **Speed is a cultural trait; protect it by deciding reversible things fast.**
    "decisiveness is about speed, and speed matters in business." (S007) / "decision-making speed is the most important piece of nimbleness" (S073)
    Sources: 6 (S007, S024, S045, S064, S073, S091). Confidence: high.

19. **Go slow on one-way doors and never skip steps; shortcuts only look faster.**
    "I find myself often at Amazon I'm the chief slowdown officer" (S073) / "Skipping steps slows you down. It's seductive, but wrong." (S032)
    Sources: 7 (S006, S017, S025, S032, S035, S073, S078). Confidence: high.

20. **Be stubborn on the vision and flexible on the details.**
    "you really can't accomplish anything important if you're not stubborn on Vision because things that are important take a long time" (S106) / "We're stubborn on vision and flexible on details." (S070)
    Sources: 6 (S010, S023, S062, S070, S083, S106). Confidence: high.

21. **People who are right a lot change their minds a lot; hunt for evidence against your firmest beliefs.**
    "I've noticed that people who are right a lot change their mind a lot." (S024) / "you should never let anybody trap you with anything you've said in the past" (S106)
    Sources: 5 (S001, S023, S024, S036, S106). Confidence: high.

22. **Use analysis whenever it can decide; the biggest calls still come down to judgment and intuition.**
    "if you're only looking at your data, you also will not win or at least not win big." (S023) / "Math-based decisions command wide agreement, whereas judgment-based decisions are rightly debated and often controversial, at least until put into practice and demonstrated." (S053)
    Sources: 4 (S011, S023, S044, S053). Confidence: high.

23. **When the data and the anecdotes disagree, suspect the metric; metrics and process are proxies.**
    "when the anecdotes and the data disagree the anecdotes are usually Right?" (S003) / "Good process serves you so you can serve customers." (S064)
    Sources: 3 (S003, S045, S064). Confidence: high.

24. **Fix the root cause so the fix works for every customer, not just the one who complained.**
    "you don't just fix the symptom you have to fix the root cause" (S022) / "We really obsess over small defects. That's what drives up costs. Because the most expensive thing you can do is make a mistake." (S070)
    Sources: 7 (S022, S029, S033, S049, S070, S076, S098). Confidence: high.

25. **Lean into big trends; stability favors incumbents and rapid change favors startups.**
    "never ever ever lean away from the future" (S030) / "Stability favors incumbents and rapid change favors small, dynamic, nimble startup companies." (S023)
    Sources: 3 (S023, S030, S064). Confidence: high.

26. **A startup should be laser focused and should not chase whatever category is hot.**
    "startup companies need to be absolutely laser focused" (S027) / "pick something you think is interesting, that has the intersection of genuinely creating real customer value and just stay right there and let the wave catch you" (S089)
    Sources: 5 (S022, S027, S085, S089, S108). Confidence: high.

## People and leadership

27. **Trust is earned slowly by doing hard things well, over and over; you cannot ask for it.**
    "the way you earn trust the way you develop reputation is you do hard things well over and over and over" (S073) / "Customer trust is hard to win and easy to lose." (S041) / "You earn trust slowly, over time, by doing hard things well" (S041)
    Sources: 6 (S037, S041, S047, S060, S073, S088). Confidence: high.

28. **The hiring bar is the most important lever; every hire should raise the group's average.**
    "Setting the bar high in our approach to hiring has been, and will continue to be, the single most important element of Amazon.com's success." (S038) / "A's hire A's and B's hire C's and C's hire D's." (S021)
    Sources: 9 (S011, S021, S037, S038, S039, S048, S060, S074, S086). Confidence: high.

29. **Back missionaries over mercenaries, in hiring, partners, and acquisitions.**
    "missionaries build better products." (S055)
    Sources: 7 (S028, S037, S044, S055, S073, S083, S091). Confidence: high.

30. **Senior leaders make a few high-quality decisions on a few big ideas, and repeat those ideas like a broken record.**
    "if I make, like, three good decisions a day, that's enough. And they should just be as high a quality as I can make them." (S044) / "senior leaders need to be broken records on the things that are important to the organization" (S025)
    Sources: 5 (S001, S014, S025, S044, S074). Confidence: high.
