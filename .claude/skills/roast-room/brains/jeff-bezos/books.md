## Invent and Wander: The Collected Writings of Jeff Bezos (2020, introduction by Walter Isaacson)

- **What it is:** A collection of his Amazon shareholder letters from 1997 to 2020, plus speeches and interviews, published as he handed over the CEO role.
- **Core thesis (our words):** Invention, driven by customer obsession and long-term thinking, is the root of durable value. A company stays alive by staying in Day 1: experimenting, accepting failure, deciding fast, and refusing to let process and proxies replace judgment. Distinctiveness has a cost, and it is worth paying.
- **Key frameworks it carries:** Day 1 versus Day 2 and its four defenses; Type 1 and Type 2 decisions; disagree and commit; working backwards versus skills forward; what's not going to change; the cost, price, and growth flywheel; controllable inputs and free cash flow per share; math-based versus judgment-based decisions; long-tailed bets and the best place to fail; the new business tests and the dreamy business; wandering versus efficiency; the four elements of high standards and the six-page memo; create more than you consume. See `frameworks.md` for each.
- **Sources that teach it:** The letters themselves (S038 to S040, S048 to S068), the Princeton baccalaureate (S020), and the 2021 note to employees (S042), which ends with "Remember to wander."

## Books and authors he cites (not his own)

- **Benjamin Graham:** the voting machine versus weighing machine line, used to tell employees and shareholders to ignore the stock (S003, S018, S048, S060).
- **Warren Buffett (sayings, not a book):** the rock concert versus ballet line on telling investors what kind of company you run (S018, S027, S085), and the get-rich-slowly scheme as the reason few copy long-term strategies (S024).
- **Gerard O'Neill, *The High Frontier*:** read in high school; behind his view that humans should expand into free-space colonies rather than planetary surfaces, and that heavy industry should move off Earth (S013, S031, S035, S090).
- **Richard Dawkins, *The Blind Watchmaker*:** quoted in his last letter as CEO on the energy it takes to stay distinctive and not be smoothed into your surroundings (S068).
- **Nassim Taleb, *The Black Swan*:** raised by an interviewer as a book he is fond of, on how people build narratives and underrate randomness; he agreed and tied it to anecdotes revealing what metrics miss (S003).
- ***Mr. Pine's Purple House*:** a children's book his mother read to him many times, used to frame Amazon's wish to do things a little differently (S088).
- **Kazuo Ishiguro, *The Remains of the Day*:** named as his favorite novel (S089). Not tied to how he decides.
