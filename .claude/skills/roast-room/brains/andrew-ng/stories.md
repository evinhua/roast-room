# Stories: Andrew Ng

20 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws, built from the verified notes on 56 sources. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **Bearing AI and the ships.** Mitsui brought a ship-routing idea that he says he "would never have come up with myself" (S008). AI Fund validated it, recruited a CEO, and built the company. He has put the fuel saving at about $450,000 per ship per year (S008, 2023) and later at about $500,000 across 600 plus ships (S051, 2025). Lesson: domain experts plus AI people build what neither could alone (S008, S020, S051).
2. **The ten-minute loan.** A bank can automate one underwriting step and save an hour of staff time, which is fine but small. Redesigning the whole flow to sell "approved in 10 minutes" drives growth. Lesson: value comes from redesigning the workflow, not speeding up one step (S007).
3. **GPT-3.5 beats GPT-4.** On a coding benchmark, the older model wrapped in an agentic loop scored far above the newer model used zero-shot. Lesson: a better workflow can matter more than a better model, so do not wait for the next model (S002, S018).
4. **The kids' game and the eighteen prototypes.** He built a game in about ten minutes, then spent an hour tracking down kids to test it. To find two ideas worth scaling you build about eighteen that go nowhere, so drive the cost of each toward zero. Lesson: feedback, not code, is now the slow step, and cheap failure is the price of finding winners (S004).
5. **Two headcount requests.** In one week one person asked to hire a human and another asked for budget to use AI. He said no to the first and yes to the second. Lesson: knowing when AI can do the job is now a core management skill (S012).
6. **Engineers turned into PMs.** Early in his career he trained good engineers to be product managers. They felt bad and still made weak PMs. Lesson: product instinct comes from empathy with users, not from engineering skill (S012).
7. **Google Brain's first project.** Friends told him scaling up neural networks was a bad career move. He did it anyway, and picked speech recognition rather than ads as the first project to get a win and gain allies. Lesson: make the controversial bet, but sequence early projects to earn credibility (S054, S055).
8. **Coursera.** He spent about five years obsessing over online education before cofounding Coursera, and filmed courses late at night because 100,000 people had signed up before a video existed. Lesson: years of domain study make a founder's gut fast and reliable, and commitment to learners comes first (S001, S019, S047).
9. **The group-watching feature.** His team built a way for two learners to share one browser and earn credit together. Nobody used it, since online learners want their own pace. Lesson: clever features can get zero traction, so test and iterate (S019).
10. **Steel defects and the inspector.** Teams spent weeks hunting better model architectures and gained nothing over a 76.2% baseline; a data-centric approach reached 93.1% in two weeks. Elsewhere a factory inspector labeled the same part a defect in the morning and fine in the afternoon. Lesson: when the model is good enough, fix the data and the labeling rules before collecting more (S023, S024).
11. **Refund calls.** His team found 10% of contact center calls were refund requests, built a classifier, and routed them automatically. Lesson: one narrow, well-chosen task can pay off right away (S057).
12. **The farm that was wrong about itself.** An agriculture company expected to automate field work, but the real opportunity was moving harvested goods. Lesson: break jobs into tasks, because the best target is rarely the obvious one (S057).
13. **The 300-idea spreadsheet.** A financial firm sent him 300 AI ideas to rank. Getting to a handful of high-impact bets took both technical and business judgment. Lesson: more ideas is not better; disciplined selection is the work (S007).
14. **Morgan Stanley's challenge labs.** The CEO set the tone, trained everyone, and paired business and tech leaders to find use cases. Lesson: top-down adoption with structured discovery beats scattered bottom-up pilots (S013).
15. **Lensa, the flashlight app.** A popular photo app that he called a good idea people liked, but a thin layer on someone else's API. Lesson: thin wrappers are not lasting businesses (S008).
16. **The thirty-second decision.** Renate Nyborg, former Tinder CEO, settled a major architecture question in thirty seconds on a call; they later built Amorai together. Lesson: most people have never seen real startup speed, and rare expertise pairings face little competition (S008, S020).
17. **Tariff compliance to a company.** After tariffs came up in a presidential debate, his team built an agentic workflow for tariff documents, and it became the portfolio company Gaia Dynamics. Lesson: domain pain plus fast AI building turns a headache into a product (S049).
18. **Non-engineers who build.** His CFO scripted document checks that replaced hours of clicking, and a marketer built a feedback app in two days. Lesson: people who can build with AI get better data and move faster, whatever their title (S010, S011, S049).
19. **Killing good businesses.** AI Fund has killed several projects with sound economics because the team did not want them to exist. Lesson: making money is not enough; the work has to leave people better off (S001, S008).
20. **Self-driving's broken promise.** He says the self-driving industry, which he was part of, set poor expectations on timelines. Lesson: set and keep resetting customer expectations for AI products (S021, S041).
