# Principles: Andrew Ng

Built from 56 sources (talks, interviews, podcasts, and The Batch letters, 2017 to 2026). 28 decision rules. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Build at the application layer, not the model or infrastructure layer.** "at the application layer, I'm seeing a lot of exciting opportunities that seem to have a very large market, but where the competition sets is very light, relative to the magnitude of the opportunity" (S008) "if anything the application layer feels underinvested." (S017) Sources: 9 (S001, S003, S008, S013, S017, S018, S034, S035, S057). Confidence: high.

2. **Speed is the first test of a startup; technical depth is the second.** "the number one predictor of a startup success is speed" (S006) "A good technical understanding, too, gets you speed!" (S028) Sources: 6 (S001, S006, S012, S020, S028, S033). Confidence: high.

3. **Use AI to grow, not just to cut cost; redesign the whole workflow.** "growth has almost no practical ceiling" (S007) "Growth is more interesting to most businesses than cost savings, and if there are loops in your business that, when sped up, would drive growth, AI might be a tool to unlock this growth." (S029) Sources: 7 (S007, S013, S016, S027, S029, S049, S051). Confidence: high.

4. **Work only on concrete ideas, and have the courage to be wrong.** "Articulating a concrete idea ... takes more courage." (S030) "I like to engage only when there's a concrete idea." (S008) Sources: 5 (S001, S008, S028, S030, S031). Confidence: high.

5. **Pursue one hypothesis hard, then pivot fast when data says no.** "Rather than hedging, it is often better to pursue one hypothesis (for example, build one concrete product) but also be willing to switch quickly to a different hypothesis." (S028) Sources: 3 (S001, S028, S030). Confidence: high.

6. **Drive evals and error analysis before trying the next shiny tool.** "use error analysis to figure out where it's falling short, and focus on that." (S036) Sources: 6 (S006, S009, S012, S036, S037, S054). Confidence: high.

7. **Start evals early and rough; improve them as you go.** "I think of evals as something I'm going to throw together really quickly, you know, in 20 minutes and it's not that good, but it starts to complement my human eyeball evals" (S006) Sources: 3 (S006, S036, S037). Confidence: high.

8. **Make prototypes so cheap that most can fail.** "I would rather focus on driving the cost of those 18 proof of concepts to a zero." (S004) Sources: 5 (S003, S004, S020, S029, S050). Confidence: high.

9. **Getting user feedback is now the bottleneck, so make it fast.** "the bottleneck is increasingly getting user feedback" (S004) "when building consumer products, I routinely approach strangers (in a respectful way) in public places to ask if they're willing to give feedback on a prototype I'm working on." (S028) Sources: 5 (S001, S004, S011, S012, S028). Confidence: high.

10. **Prototype fast and loose, then harden before you ship.** "if it buys you speed, it is okay to hard-code parameters, compute key variables in a Jupyter notebook, use local copies of data, and operate with lightweight code review or versioning processes." (S046) Sources: 5 (S001, S028, S039, S046, S019). Confidence: high.

11. **Move fast and be responsible, not move fast and break things.** "I tend to tell my teams to move fast and be responsible" (S001) Sources: 3 (S001, S004, S010). Confidence: high.

12. **Decide reversible things fast; slow down only for one-way doors.** "just make a decision if it's wrong change it a week later it's fine." (S012) Sources: 4 (S001, S010, S012, S042). Confidence: high.

13. **Use the best model first; optimize cost only after it works and the bill hurts.** "the first most important thing is let's build a product that users love." (S049) Sources: 5 (S032, S033, S049, S052, S018). Confidence: high.

14. **Never lock in to one model vendor.** "I can't forecast in a year or even six months what is going to be the top model. So as we build our businesses, one of the most important things to do is to preserve optionality." (S013) Sources: 5 (S006, S007, S013, S017, S032). Confidence: high.

15. **AI automates tasks, not jobs; plan around the 30 to 40% it can do.** "maybe AI could do, you know, 30-40% of many jobs. And what that means is, well, that 60% that a human does has become even more valuable" (S011) Sources: 5 (S010, S011, S051, S057, S058). Confidence: high.

16. **Buying a chatbot license is not a strategy; finding the use case is.** "no company ever gain competitive advantage just by buying a CH GP or Microsoft co-pilot license. The problem is finding the right use cases." (S013) Sources: 3 (S013, S027, S059). Confidence: high.

17. **Measure AI in business outcomes, not AI metrics.** "I find that the business outcome of AI is more function of the business than the function of the AI." (S011) Sources: 3 (S011, S019, S007). Confidence: high.

18. **Start small inside a company to earn allies, then scale.** "the first step is actually to start small" (S019) Sources: 3 (S019, S051, S054). Confidence: high.

19. **Let applications pull data work forward; do not rebuild data for years first.** "let the applications pull your data infra forward" (S054) Sources: 1 (S054). Confidence: low.

20. **Pair AI skill with deep domain knowledge.** "a lot of the most exciting projects I get to work on is when I, as a AI person, get to collaborate with someone with deep domain knowledge" (S010) Sources: 5 (S008, S010, S020, S031, S051). Confidence: high.

21. **Everyone on the team should code with AI, and hiring should test for it.** "frankly today when I hire a marketer or HR professional I would strongly prefer to hire someone that knows how to use AI to the level of being able to write software." (S016) Sources: 8 (S001, S004, S006, S011, S016, S049, S050, S055). Confidence: high.

22. **Buy what already exists; build only what you want to own.** "if you can buy I often advise Partners you just buy it" (S051) Sources: 1 (S051). Confidence: low.

23. **Good data beats big data.** "for a lot of problems it's much easier to go in and find your 60 mislabeled examples and find a systematic way to clean that up" (S024) Sources: 3 (S022, S023, S024). Confidence: high.

24. **Keep a human in the loop wherever the human knows something the AI does not.** "so long as the human knows something the AI does not, human in the loop is needed to" (S038) Sources: 4 (S011, S038, S058, S010). Confidence: high.

25. **The customer is the final judge.** "ultimately we don't get to vote if we succeed or fail is only the customer that the only one that gets a thumbs up or thumbs down" (S019) Sources: 2 (S019, S012). Confidence: medium.

26. **Set customer expectations about AI early and keep resetting them.** "It's best to set expectations with customers clearly before starting a project and keep reminding them throughout the process." (S041) Sources: 2 (S041, S019). Confidence: medium.

27. **Kill a profitable idea if it fails the ethics test.** "we have multiple times killed projects that we assess to be financially sound, based on ethical grounds." (S008) Sources: 2 (S001, S008). Confidence: medium.

28. **Re-examine anything you still do the way you did it in 2022.** "is there anything we're doing that today that we're also doing in 2022? And if so, let's take a look and see if it still even makes sense today." (S012) Sources: 2 (S012, S058). Confidence: medium.

---

Where he is uneven (inferred from S001, S006, S009, S012, S049): his best practices change every few months by his own account, so older advice in this brain (2017 to 2022, such as data moats and the one-second rule) can be stale. He says "working hard probably correlates to personal success." (S012) But he offers little on pacing, burnout, or managing people beyond hiring. His playbook is built for application startups and enterprise AI adoption; it says little about pricing, sales, or businesses where AI is not central.
