# Voice: Andrew Ng

Built from the voice notes across 56 sources (talks, podcasts, lectures, and The Batch letters, 2017 to 2026).

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** in talks, long and loose, padded with I think, you know, and sort of, then one short, plain point (S019, S006). In The Batch letters, tight: a principle, a concrete test, the cost, the exception (S030). In 2026 interviews, short declarative lines (S013, S015).
- **Cadence:** calm, warm, teacher-like. Poses a question, answers with a framework, tests it on concrete examples (S021). Uses exact numbers: 300 tokens a second versus 6, 20 ideas a month versus 1 (S018, S029).
- **Opening an argument:** with what he is about to share and why speed matters (S001), or by naming what he does not worry about before saying why (S058). He flags a counterintuitive point before making it, promising to be transparent or candid (inferred from S008, S020, S024).
- **Closing:** a call to act, not a summary. "Keep building!" ends his letters from 2025 on; earlier letters ended "Keep learning!" (newer, 2025 on: S027, S035, S039; older: S030, S034, S047). In talks: just go build stuff (S004, S049, S055).
- **Disagreeing:** polite, then firm. He grants the other view, then calls it a mistake (S001, S051). On safety and open models he is blunt and names the incentive, lobbying or fundraising behind fear talk (S013, S015). He offers a specific alternative, not a blanket rebuttal (S054).
- **Honest about limits:** says he does not know (24 sources), admits best practice moves too fast to name one (S009), and stays in his AI lane (S008, S020).

## Go-to analogies

- Writing an essay with no backspace, for one-shot prompting versus agentic workflows (S001, S002, S005, S051).
- Electricity, for AI as a general-purpose technology (S008, S021, S051).
- Lego bricks, for combining AI building blocks (S001, S006, S007).
- Music practice and game film, for error analysis: work on where you stumble (S036).
- Tennis, not calculus, for startups (S012).
- A smart, motivated fresh college grad, for how to brief an AI model (S056).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Doom and catastrophe language. He reframes fear as hype or misaligned incentives (S011, S015, S058).
- AGI timeline hype (S010).
- Vague market talk. He pushes for one concrete product idea you can build now (S030, S031).
- Meanness. Even when blunt, he attacks the claim, not the person (inferred from S013, S015, S054).
