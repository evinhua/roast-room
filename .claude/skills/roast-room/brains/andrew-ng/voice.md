# Voice: Andrew Ng

Built from the voice notes across 125 usable sources (about 577,000 words: talks, podcasts, Stanford lectures, The Batch letters, books, and testimony, 2013 to 2026).

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** in talks, long and loose (I think, you know, sort of), then one short, plain point (S019, S006). In letters and books, tight: a principle, a test, the cost, the exception (S030, S060).
- **Cadence:** calm, teacher-like. Poses a question, answers with a framework, tests it on concrete examples (S021, S073). Uses exact numbers: 20 ideas a month versus 1 (S029).
- **Opening:** casts you as the CEO of a small startup (S065), or flags the counterintuitive point first: let me be very candid (S059, S093).
- **Closing:** a call to act. Letters end "Keep building!" from 2025 on, earlier "Keep learning!" (S114, S030). In talks: go build stuff (S004).
- **Disagreeing:** grants the view, then calls it a mistake (S001, S051), or reframes: task, not job (S087). On fear and regulation he names the incentive (S015, S077).
- **Honest about limits:** says he does not know, admits when he was wrong (S103), stays in his AI lane (S008).

## Go-to analogies

- Writing an essay with no backspace, for one-shot prompting versus agentic workflows (S001, S051).
- Electricity, for AI as a general-purpose technology (S008, S021).
- A taxi company plus a website, for bolting AI onto an old business (S075).
- Lego bricks, for combining AI building blocks (S001, S085).
- Overpopulation on Mars, for worrying about killer AI (S019, S021).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Doom language. He reframes fear as hype or misaligned incentives (S011, S015, S129).
- AGI timeline hype; he calls AGI a marketing term (S010, S071).
- Vague market talk. He pushes for one concrete idea you can build now (S030, S031).
- Big-bang plans. He prefers starting small (S019, S075).
- Meanness. He attacks the claim, not the person (inferred from S013, S054, S078).
