# Books: Andrew Ng

Built from the 56 sources in sources.md. He has no mainstream business book. His written work is short free guides and a weekly letter, and only the letters are in the corpus; book text was not fetched. Summaries are in my own words.

## AI Transformation Playbook (2018, Landing AI guide)
- **Core thesis:** A company becomes good at AI by doing a few small, winnable projects fast, using those wins to build allies, then scaling to bigger projects and training the whole workforce. The technology is the easier part; bringing people along is the hard part.
- **Key frameworks:** start with pilot projects that need not be the biggest win but should succeed quickly; build an in-house AI team; company-wide AI training; an AI strategy that follows early wins, not the other way round.
- **Taught in sources:** he describes the playbook and says it still holds after generative AI (S054, 2025); he cites it as the long-term journey for companies and repeats its first step, start small (S019, 2020). The Google Brain sequence, speech first, then Maps, then Ads, is his worked example (S019, S054).

## Machine Learning Yearning (2018, free draft book)
- **Core thesis:** A guide to structuring machine learning projects: how to set up dev and test sets, read bias and variance, compare to human-level performance, and use error analysis to pick what to fix next.
- **Key frameworks:** error analysis on a sample of failures; human-level performance as a benchmark; deciding whether more data, a bigger model, or better labels is the next move.
- **Taught in sources:** the book is not named in the corpus. Its ideas run through his talks on error analysis and human-level performance (S036, S037, S044) and data-centric AI (S023, S024).

## How to Build Your Career in AI (2022, free ebook)
- **Core thesis:** Career advice for AI builders: learn foundations, build projects, and choose jobs by the team you will work with.
- **Key frameworks:** T-shaped skills (broad, then deep in one area); a few great projects over many weak ones; consistent learning habits over cramming; pick the 10 to 30 people you work with over the company brand.
- **Taught in sources:** the book is not named in the corpus. The same advice is in his Stanford CS230 career lecture (S025, 2019) and the Lex Fridman interview (S019, 2020).

## The Batch letters (2020 to 2026, weekly newsletter series)
- **Core thesis:** His most direct written advice to founders and builders, one idea per letter, closing with "Keep building!" or "Keep learning!".
- **Key frameworks:** concrete ideas (S030); brainstorming startup ideas broadly and with explicit criteria (S031); speed as the startup virtue (S028, S029); falling token prices and building first (S032); three themes for founders, persistence, fast iteration, and community (S033); application layer as LLMs commoditize (S034); the three-bucket bubble view (S035); evals and error analysis (S036, S037); three loops of software building (S038); matching tactics to project maturity (S039); hard tech (S040); setting customer expectations (S041); Ready, Fire, Aim (S042); how AI products differ from software and how to check feasibility (S043, S044); what to do in a downturn (S045); proof of concept versus production (S046); founder success factors (S047); decision matrices (S048); workflow redesign (S027).
- **Taught in sources:** S027 to S048.

## Books he cites
- *The Lean Startup* (Eric Ries): he says he has read it many times and was rereading it in 2026 for its lessons on pace of iteration under uncertainty (S017).
- *Ideaflow*: cited for the point that teams that generate more ideas reach better solutions (S031).
- Benjamin Graham's voting machine and weighing machine line, used to argue for building on fundamentals through hype cycles (S035).
