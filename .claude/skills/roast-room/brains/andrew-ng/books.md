# Books: Andrew Ng

Built from the notes on the 125 usable sources in sources.md. He has no mainstream business book. His written work is free guides, a draft book, and a weekly letter. Machine Learning Yearning (S060) and the AI Transformation Playbook (S061) are now in the corpus, along with The Batch letters; the career ebook and his courses are known only through talks and letters. Summaries are in my own words; no book text is reproduced.

## AI Transformation Playbook (2018, Landing AI guide; corpus copy dated 2020)
- **Core thesis:** A company becomes good at AI by doing a few small, winnable projects fast, using those wins to build allies, then building a team, training the workforce, and only then writing a strategy. The technology is the easier part; bringing people along is the hard part. Aim to be the AI leader in your own sector, not to out-Google Google.
- **Key frameworks:** five steps: pilot projects for momentum, an in-house AI team, broad AI training, an AI strategy, internal and external communications; first projects should succeed more than they should matter; tiered training by role (executives, leaders of AI projects, engineer trainees, with rising hour counts); strategy after hands-on experience; bring the AI team in while deciding what data to collect; unify data instead of siloing it by division; the virtuous circle of product, users, and data.
- **Taught in sources:** the guide itself (S061); he walks through it on CxOTalk (S075) and at TWIMLcon (S079); he says it still holds after generative AI (S054, 2025) and repeats its first step, start small (S019, 2020). The Google Brain sequence, speech first, then Maps, then Ads, is his worked example (S019, S054).

## Machine Learning Yearning (2018, free draft book)
- **Core thesis:** A guide to running machine learning projects so a team picks the right next move. Build a basic system in days, measure it on data that looks like the future, and let error analysis, not intuition or fashion, decide what to fix.
- **Key frameworks:** dev and test sets drawn from the distribution you care about; one optimizing metric plus satisficing thresholds, set up in under a week; error analysis on about 100 failures to estimate the ceiling of each fix; avoidable bias versus variance, and what to do about each; an eyeball and blackbox split of the dev set; error analysis by parts for pipelines; a training dev set to spot data mismatch; when to use end-to-end learning versus a pipeline.
- **Taught in sources:** the book itself (S060). The same ideas run through his Stanford CS230 and CS229 lectures (S065 to S070, 2018 and 2025), his talks on error analysis and human-level performance (S036, S037, S044, S121), and data-centric AI (S023, S024, S095).

## How to Build Your Career in AI (2022, free ebook)
- **Core thesis:** Career advice for AI builders: learn foundations, build projects, then search for a job, with a community around you at every step.
- **Key frameworks:** three steps of learning, projects, and job search; T-shaped skills (broad, then deep in one area); start with small projects and step up, picking ones that stretch you without being out of reach; generate ideas by reading and talking to domain experts; Ready, Fire, Aim to avoid analysis paralysis; pick the people you will work with over the company brand.
- **Taught in sources:** the book is not named in the corpus. Its chapters appear as Batch letters (S125, S126, 2022); the same advice is in his Stanford CS230 career lecture (S025, 2019) and the Lex Fridman interview (S019, 2020).

## The Batch letters (2020 to 2026, weekly newsletter series)
- **Core thesis:** His most direct written advice to founders and builders, one idea per letter, usually closing with "Keep building!" or "Keep learning!".
- **Key frameworks:** concrete ideas (S030); brainstorming startup ideas broadly with explicit criteria (S031); speed as the startup virtue (S028, S029); falling token prices and building first (S032); persistence, fast iteration, and community (S033); the application layer as LLMs commoditize (S034); the three-bucket bubble view (S035); evals and error analysis (S036, S037, S114); three loops of software building (S038); matching tactics to project maturity (S039); hard tech (S040); setting customer expectations (S041); Ready, Fire, Aim (S042); how AI products differ from software and how to check feasibility (S043, S044); downturn moves (S045); proof of concept versus production (S046); founder success factors (S047); decision matrices (S048); workflow redesign (S027); sandboxes for large companies (S102); the product management bottleneck (S103); cutting scope (S104); everyone builds at AI Fund (S105); forward deployed engineers (S106); generalists and PM ratios (S107); which coding tasks speed up most (S108); iterative specs, "AI tokens are cheap; human tokens are gold" (S109); platform trust (S110); trust and hype (S111, S113); data silos (S112); when to fine-tune (S115); the 10x professional (S116); the GenAI application engineer (S117); iteration as risk reduction (S118); consistency over human noise (S119); generative AI opportunity by task (S120); building ML as debugging (S121); prompting and test sets (S122, S123); four agentic design patterns (S124); career letters (S125, S126); the Four Ps for AI-native engineering teams (S127); the jobs narrative (S128); existential risk (S129).
- **Taught in sources:** S027 to S048 and S102 to S129.

## Courses (not books; listed because he cites them as tools)
- **AI for Everyone:** a non-technical course for executives and whole workforces; he cites companies putting every employee through it and a national cabinet taking it (S059, S075).
- **AI Python for Beginners:** the course every non-engineer at AI Fund takes before building with AI (S105).
- **Prompting course:** context-rich prompting, outlines before drafts, rubric-based critique, and neutral questions to avoid flattery (S056, 2026).

## Books and ideas he cites
- *The Lean Startup* (Eric Ries): he says he has read it many times and was rereading it in 2026 for its lessons on pace of iteration under uncertainty (S017).
- *Ideaflow*: cited for the point that teams that generate more ideas reach better solutions (S031).
- *Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment* (Kahneman and coauthors): used to argue that AI's consistency is an underrated reason to adopt it (S119).
- Fred Brooks on essential versus accidental complexity: better tools cut the accidental part, but deciding what problem to solve stays hard (S079).
- Benjamin Graham's voting machine and weighing machine line, used to argue for building on fundamentals through hype cycles (S035).
