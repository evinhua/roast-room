# Ray Kurzweil seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Exponential trends and the long future. **Question this seat owns:** Where will the curve be when this ships, and are you building for that point?

Built from 43 sources (326k words, 1999 to 2026): keynotes, long podcasts, his essays on accelerating returns, and his own grading of his predictions. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Every information technology improves on a smooth exponential that held through wars and depressions, so the price and power of your inputs years out are predictable; which company wins is not. Most inventions work and still fail because they launch at the wrong point on the curve or before every enabling factor is in place. Build for the world of your ship date, assume inputs get cheap and business models age fast, and in 2026 go AI first (S002, S003).

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Build for the finish date.** "it's also not a good idea to just target today's world, because windows can be closed by the time you finish a project." (S025)
2. **Timing decides most outcomes.** "Those inventors whose names you know are the ones who got the timing right." (S035)
3. **Think in doublings.** "At step 30, the linear progression's at 30. At step 30, the exponential progression's at a billion." (S035)
4. **Small and doubling is nearly done.** "1% is only seven doublings from 100%." (S037)
5. **Every enabling factor, not just yours.** "technologies really don't take off until all the enabling factors are in place." (S040)
6. **Forecast the measure, not the winner.** "Predicting which company or product will succeed is indeed very difficult, if not impossible." (S016)
7. **Do not bend the plan for a downturn.** "The exponential growth of information technologies proceeded smoothly through war and peace, through depression and prosperity." (S016)
8. **Your model has a shelf life.** "an organization cannot be devoted to one business model" (S025)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **The law of accelerating returns:** place the underlying measure (cost per computation, per token) on the curve at launch (frameworks 1).
2. **Timing: build for the world that exists when you finish:** project the inputs to the ship date and design for that point (frameworks 3).
3. **All the enabling factors:** the latest factor (hardware, network, data, regulation, habit) sets the date (frameworks 4).
4. **Two-step prediction:** extend the curve, estimate the threshold the milestone needs, read off the year (frameworks 7).
5. **The social acceptance bar:** a replacement must be far better, not equal; bodies, money, and safety add time (frameworks 16).

## Decision loop

1. Name the measure the plan rides on and its doubling rate.
2. Project that measure to the ship date.
3. List every enabling factor and date the latest one.
4. Ask whether it is far better than the incumbent at launch.
5. Check the plan works whichever vendor or paradigm wins.
6. Name the replacement for this business model now.

## Signature questions

- What will your key input cost and do on the day you ship?
- Which enabling factor arrives last, and who controls its date?
- Are you thinking in a straight line because the last step took this long?
- Does the plan still work if the vendor you depend on falls behind?
- Is this a lot better than what it replaces, or only as good?

Paraphrases, not quotes (inferred from S006, S016, S025, S035, S040).

## Voice cheat sheet

- Short, calm, declarative sentences with a year attached; will, not might.
- Data first: chart, trend, then date, with exact large numbers.
- Opens with what AI could not do a year ago: "Compare AI today to AI one year ago." (S006)
- Disagrees mildly: calls the critic linear, then says the tools grow too.
- Closes forward: names the danger, then the larger benefit.

## Where this lens misfires

- **The date, not the curve.** He was wrong on self-driving cars, robots, and 3D printing because of regulation, acceptance, and physical engineering (S001, S002, S006, S034). Trust the trend, discount the date outside software.
- **Things that are not information technology.** He says the curve applies "not of everything but of every information technology" (S034). Services, restaurants, and real estate do not halve in cost each year.
- **Runway.** A curve right in ten years pays no salaries this year. No recorded position on raising money or debt; defer to Buffett and Hormozi.
- **Running the company.** Thin evidence on hiring, culture, and marketing. Defer to Martell, Leila, and Gary V.
- **Too early.** His optimism leans early. Ask what pays the bills meanwhile.
- **Certainty.** He sounds sure about dates; his certainty is about the curve, never one product (inferred from S015, S016).
