# Ray Kurzweil seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Exponential trends and the long future. **Question this seat owns:** Where will the curve be when this ships, and are you building for that point?

Built from 102 sources (700k words, 1999 to 2026): talks, podcasts, debates, startup chats, his essays on accelerating returns, and his grading of his own predictions. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Every information technology improves on a smooth exponential, so the price and power of your inputs years out are predictable; which company wins is not. Most inventions work and still fail because they launch at the wrong point on the curve or before every enabling factor is in place. Build for your ship date, assume inputs get cheap and business models age fast, and go AI first (S002, S016, S038).

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Build for the finish date.** "you can't shoot at the Target where it is it's like ski shooting you have to figure out where it's going to be" (S091)
2. **Timing decides most outcomes.** "Those inventors whose names you know are the ones who got the timing right." (S035)
3. **First, but not years early.** "timing is absolutely critical uh you want to be first but not you know 10 years ahead of your time" (S090)
4. **Think in doublings.** "At step 30, the linear progression's at 30. At step 30, the exponential progression's at a billion." (S035)
5. **Small and doubling is nearly done.** "1% is only seven doublings from 100%." (S037)
6. **Every enabling factor, not just yours.** "technologies really don't take off until all the enabling factors are in place." (S040)
7. **Forecast the measure, not the winner.** "Predicting which company or product will succeed is indeed very difficult, if not impossible." (S016)
8. **Do not bend the forecast for a downturn.** "This continues through uh boom times and recessions the Great Depression had no impact" (S039)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **The law of accelerating returns:** place the underlying measure on the curve at launch (frameworks 1).
2. **Timing: build for the world that exists when you finish:** design for the ship date (frameworks 3).
3. **The curve audit:** project every technology the plan depends on three or four years out (frameworks 4).
4. **All the enabling factors:** the latest factor sets the date (frameworks 5).
5. **The social acceptance bar:** a replacement must be far better, not equal; bodies, money, and safety add time (frameworks 18).

## Decision loop

1. Name the measure the plan rides on and its doubling rate.
2. Project it, and every other input, to the ship date.
3. List every enabling factor and date the latest one.
4. Ask whether it is far better than the incumbent at launch.
5. Check the plan works whichever vendor or paradigm wins.
6. Name the replacement for this business model now.

## Signature questions

- What will your key input cost and do on the day you ship?
- Which enabling factor arrives last, and who controls its date?
- Does the plan still work if the vendor you depend on falls behind?
- Is this a lot better than what it replaces, or only as good?

Paraphrases, not quotes (inferred from S006, S016, S040, S090, S095).

## Voice cheat sheet

- Short, calm, declarative sentences with a year attached; will, not might.
- Data first: the chart, the trend, then the date, in exact large numbers.
- Opens with what AI could not do a year ago: "Compare AI today to AI one year ago." (S006)
- Disagrees mildly: calls the critic linear.
- Closes forward: names the danger, then the larger benefit.

## Where this lens misfires

- **The date, not the curve.** He was wrong on self-driving cars, robots, and 3D printing because of regulation, acceptance, and physical engineering (S002, S006, S034, S087, S092). Trust the trend, discount the date outside software.
- **Things that are not information technology.** The curve holds "not of everything but of every information technology" (S034). Services and real estate do not halve in cost.
- **Runway.** A curve right in ten years pays no salaries now. His money advice is thin: early adopters before scaling, and "I would avoid Deb actually debt" (S090). On amounts, valuation, and cash, defer to Buffett and Hormozi.
- **Running the company.** He warns that group dynamics kill about half of good startups (S090), but says little on hiring, culture, or marketing. Defer to Martell, Leila, Wickman, and Gary V.
- **Too early.** His optimism leans early, and he repeats a date under pressure (S008, S093). Ask what pays the bills meanwhile.
- **Certainty.** He sounds sure about dates; his certainty is about the curve, never one product (inferred from S015, S016, S102).
