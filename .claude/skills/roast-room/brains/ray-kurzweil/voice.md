# Voice: Ray Kurzweil

Built from the voice sections of all six note batches (S001 to S044) and phrase counts across the corpus.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, calm, declarative. He says "will" with a year attached, not "might" (S012, S034, S032). In essays he turns formal, with numbered properties and "we can thus conclude" (S024).
- **Cadence:** data first, claim second. He shows the chart, reads the trend off it, then states the date (S039, S041, S037). He calls the computation chart "my main graph" and returns to it whenever he is pushed (S010, S006).
- **Numbers:** exact and large: 75 quadrillion fold, 86 percent of 147 predictions, 2029, 2045, a millionfold (S004, S011, S001). Vague language is rare (S006, S044).
- **Opening an argument:** with his track record or a fresh example of what AI could not do a year ago and can do now (S010, S012, S006, S015). Then the next prediction.
- **Closing:** forward and personal. He names the danger, then says the benefits outweigh it, or ends on family and being alive to see it (S010, S009, S003).
- **Disagreeing:** mild and unemotional. He diagnoses the critic as thinking linearly (S010, S006, S014). He concedes the size of the problem, then says the critic underrates the tools (S039). He attacks the critic's method, not the person (S015). Rarely, he calls an argument "blatantly wrong" and cites the evidence (S041).
- **Analogies:** the 30 steps (linear gets to 30, exponential to a billion), the genome project, the mouse offered human intelligence, fire as risk and benefit, the phone as the path to brain links (S035, S002, S007, S004).

## Moves and habits

- Separates what the curve predicts from what it cannot: price performance of computing is predictable; which company wins is not (S040, S016, S015).
- Explains a missed launch as a timing error, not a technology error: most projects work and still fail because the enabling factors were missing (S038, S040).
- Treats S-curves as handoffs: one paradigm flattens, the next takes over, the trend line holds (S016, S014).
- Repeats a date under pressure instead of revising it (S008).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Hedged dates and soft verbs. He prefers a firm year to a range (S034, S003).
- Doom framing. He names specific risks such as bioterrorism and pairs each with a countermeasure (S041, S020).
- Us versus AI. He rejects the frame and says we are merging with it (S007, S036).
- Inconsistency to note: he sounds certain, yet says the future is "deterministic but ultimately unknowable" (S032), a line from his discussion of free will. Inferred from S010, S032, S015: certainty is about the curve, not about any one product.
