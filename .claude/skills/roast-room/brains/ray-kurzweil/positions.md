# Positions: Ray Kurzweil

36 topics, built from the batch notes on all 43 sources. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (essay month, or YouTube upload date for talks; see `sources.md`). Where he shifted, the versions sit side by side with dates. Topics with no evidence say so. He speaks far more about technology timing than about running a company, so many classic founder topics have thin or no evidence.

## Timing a product or company
- **Stance:** Timing is the main reason inventions win or lose. Almost every inventor gets the gadget to work; most still fail because they launch at the wrong point on the curve. This is the center of his business view.
- **Over time:**
  - 2009-11: "most inventors fail because their timing is wrong" (S039)
  - undated, about 2010: "Most inventions and inventors fail, not because they are unable to get their gadgets to work, but because their timing is wrong" (S015)
  - 2012-11: "Those inventors whose names you know are the ones who got the timing right." (S035)
  - 2022-09: futurism was a side effect of this: "i wasn't inherently a futurist" (S033); the real goal was to know when things are feasible.
- **Read:** consistent for 40 years; inferred from S015, S033, S034, S035, S037, S039, S041.

## Build for the ship date, not today
- **Stance:** Plan the product for the world that will exist when you finish it. Project the cost and power curves forward to your launch date.
- 2003-05: "it's also not a good idea to just target today's world, because windows can be closed by the time you finish a project." (S025)
- 2012-11: "my project has to make sense when I finish the project, and the world will be a different place two, three, four years from now." (S035)
- undated, about 2010: "My goal was...to time my own technology projects so that they will be appropriate for the world that exists when I complete the projects" (S015)

## Too early versus too late
- **Stance:** Both kill you. He does not say which error is more common; he says timing, not engineering, is what sinks most projects. "Most projects fail, not because the R&D department can't get it to work, but because the timing is wrong." (S025, 2003-05)
- **Note:** the line "being too early is often the same as being wrong" in S032 is the interviewer's, not his. Do not attribute it to him.

## Enabling factors and market readiness
- **Stance:** A product only takes off when every enabling piece is in place at once: the core technology, the supporting devices, the network, the content. Check all of them, not just your own part.
- 2010-02: "technologies really don't take off until all the enabling factors are in place." (S040) E-books existed a decade before tablets, wireless, and digitized books arrived (S040).
- 2011-04: "90% of the projects the R&D teams are do exactly what they say in terms of technological achievement and 95% of the projects still fail" (S038)

## Pricing
- **Stance:** In an information technology market, value decays fast. Last year's version sells at a fraction of the price, and two generations back sells for nothing. Price against the curve and keep shipping the newest version. "we would be able to sell the version prior to the latest version for perhaps a quarter of the price of the current version." (S014, 2025-01 posting of a 2001 essay)
- **Earlier:** price-performance doubles yearly while total spending still rises: "we've had 18% growth in every form of Information Technology in constant currency over the last 50 years" (S039, 2009-11)

## Discounting
- **No recorded position** on discounts as a sales tactic. Nearest: falling cost is normal and good. "there's nothing wrong with deflation" (S044, 1999 interview, uploaded 2007-01)

## Cost curves and deflation
- **Stance:** Every information technology gets about half as expensive per unit of capability each year. Build on the assumption that your inputs get cheap and your product must too.
- 2009-11: "a 50% deflation rate in every form of Information Technology not just computation not just Electronics." (S039)
- 2005 Q and A, posted 2024-02: "Technologies start out affordable only by the wealthy, but at this stage, they actually don't work very well" (S016)
- 2024 to 2026: AI capability for the same cost is now growing about tenfold a year. "right now it's actually multiplying about 10 each year" (S006, 2025-06)

## Raising venture money
- **No recorded position** on how much to raise, from whom, or at what valuation. Nearest: he warns that capital markets run ahead of real adoption. "The investment got ahead of itself, and people were willing to invest in unproven ideas." (S025, 2003-05) His son works in venture and "all of his investments are in AI" (S003, 2026-01).

## Hype cycles and bubbles
- **Stance:** A boom and bust is a capital markets timing error, not a sign the technology failed. Adoption keeps compounding underneath.
- 2010-06: "the adoption was going on at an exponential pace" (S041)
- 2011-04: "that was strictly a capital markets phenomenon" (S038) about the dot com crash.
- 2003-05: the internet was a real revolution, "but that doesn't mean that there isn't a pace for that" (S025)

## Recessions and macro conditions
- **Stance:** Do not change a technology plan because of a recession. The price-performance curve did not bend in the Great Depression, the world wars, or 2008.
- 2009-11: "the Great Depression had no impact the current economic downturn has no impact it's continuing unabated." (S039)
- 2005 Q and A, posted 2024-02: "The exponential growth of information technologies proceeded smoothly through war and peace, through depression and prosperity." (S016)

## First hire and team
- **Stance:** Thin evidence. Innovation is interdisciplinary, so hire an expert for each area. "one thing that you need is experts in each of those areas." (S025, 2003-05) He hired from the users he served: "I worked with the National Federation of Blind to hire eight blind engineers" (S012, 2025-10)

- **No recorded position.**

## Cofounders, partners, and equity
- **No recorded position.**

## Focus versus diversification
- **Stance:** Do not tie the company to one business model. Business model lifespans are shrinking, so measure how long yours will last and plan the next one.
- 2003-05: "an organization cannot be devoted to one business model." (S025) He credits Microsoft's stability to Gates's paranoia about relying on any one model (S025).

## Incumbents and disruption
- **Stance:** Incumbents struggle because the old model holds the power. "the old-line business model has all of the political power because it is producing the profits and the revenue." (S025, 2003-05) He contrasts the music industry, which clung to its old model, with publishers who moved to ebooks early (S040, 2010-02).

## Choosing a niche or first customer
- **Stance:** Find the people who need the thing most and let them tell you what to build. Inferred from two S012 stories: the blind reader he met on a plane, and Stevie Wonder asking for a real instrument sound. No stated rule on niche size.

## Side projects
- **No recorded position.**

## Content, marketing, and paid ads
- **No recorded position.** He launched the reading machine with a live Today Show demo (S012), but draws no marketing rule from it.

## Brand and reputation
- **Stance:** Reputation and liability make large vendors more trustworthy. "if you use a large language model it should be from a larger company uh so they're concerned about their reputation and their liability." (S011, 2025-03) Same view in S007 (2025-05).

## Using AI in the business
- **Stance:** Every business should be AI first, and the opening for new AI businesses is the widest it has been.
- 2010-02: "every product you pick up now has ai at many different levels in its design and its manufacturing" (S040)
- 2026-01: "the opportunities to create a new business and do things uh that have not been done before is actually uh is higher than it's ever been before." (S003)
- 2026-06: "AI first, robot first." (S002)

## Which AI vendor or chip to bet on
- **Stance:** Bet on the curve, not on one vendor. Leaders change, and there will be more model choice, not less.
- About 2024, after his 2024 book (S032 is undated): "[Nvidia is] very well positioned, but we didn't hear about them ten years ago, and other people may overtake them." (S032) Open models matter most: "The public ones are the most impactful because everybody can use them" (S032)
- 2025-06: "I think there'll be even more choice than there is today" (S006)

## AI reliability and hallucinations
- **Stance:** A solvable problem that shrinks every year as models get more data and learn when they do not know.
- 2024-03: "the way to fix hallucinations is to actually give it more more capabilities to memorize things" (S013); at that point a model "can't do that" when asked to admit it does not know (S013).
- 2025-03 (newer): "you compare hallucinations today to one year ago is dramatically better." (S011)

## AGI and the timeline
- **Stance:** Human-level AI by 2029, a date he has held since 1999. Recently he calls it conservative.
- **Over time:**
  - 1999 to 2010: 2029 for Turing-level AI (S015, S022).
  - 2024-10: "it's going to keep happening faster and faster each year so 2029 for AGI is uh conservative" (S004)
  - 2026-06 (newest): "Now it's a conservative number. Now there are people who go beyond 2029." (S001)
- **Read:** he did not move his date; the consensus moved toward it (inferred from S002, S010, S033).

## Large language models arriving
- **Stance:** LLMs came a little ahead of his own schedule. "I was expecting this that to happen like a couple of years later." (S008, 2024-03) Data, not compute, is the current limit: "I think we have the calculations. We don't have the data." (S032)

## Robotics
- **Stance:** Behind software AI, catching up soon.
- 2026-01: "I think that's going to begin to take place in 2026, 2027." (S003)
- 2026-06: "The robots were a little bit behind on. It's going to catch up rapidly." (S001)

## Quantum computing
- **Stance:** Skeptical; errors are unsolved. "I'm not a big fan of quantum computing" (S006, 2025-06) Same in S001 (2026-06).

## Social acceptance of new technology
- **Stance:** A new product has to be much better than the incumbent, not equal, before people accept it. He names this as his biggest forecasting error.
- 2025-06: "It has to be a lot better than human beings. If it's really just equal, maybe a little bit better, we're not going to accept it." (S006)
- 2025-06: "I didn't take into consideration uh the amount of additional uh positivity that would be required to accept them." (S006)
- 2026-06: on self-driving cars, "that was incorrect." (S002) Regulation around the body moves slowest (S008, 2024-03).

## Regulation, pauses, and risk
- **Stance:** Manage specific dangers with targeted rules; never ban a whole field, which only drives it underground.
- 2000 essay, posted 2024-01: "Abandonment of broad areas of technology will only push these technologies underground" (S020)
- 2023-01: against the AI pause letter: "this criterion is too vague to be practical." (S017)
- 2025-03: "threats of AI are real and serious. Um, but it's not an alien invasion." (S011)

## Competition
- **Stance:** You cannot predict which company wins, only the capability curve everyone rides. "Predicting which company or product will succeed is indeed very difficult, if not impossible." (S016, posted 2024-02) In speech recognition, survivors kept pace with each other and the ones who fell behind went out of business (S014).

## Metrics and forecasting
- **Stance:** Measure the underlying price-performance numbers (cost per computation, per base pair, per bit), fit the curve with data, and do not trust intuition.
- 2010-06: "being an engineer I gathered a lot of data" (S041) and he says he can give a likely correct figure for the cost of computing years out (S041).
- 2005 Q and A, posted 2024-02: "Predicting specific projects is indeed not feasible." (S016)
- Grade the whole record, not the misses: "it is easy to focus on the few predictions that were incorrect" (S015)

## Speed of decisions and agility
- **Stance:** Decide fast and build to change fast.
- 2026-06: "I said yes right away cuz I make important decisions very quickly." (S002)
- 2026-06: "you got to be able to take changes very, very quickly." (S002)

## Remote work and geography
- **Stance:** Geography matters less every year. "geography matters less and less because we have virtual communication that allows people to work anywhere" (S043, 2012-03); his own team is spread across the country and the world (S043).

## Work hours, sleep, and burnout
- **Stance:** No rule on hours. Sleep is creative work time, not lost time. "I assign myself a problem before I go to sleep every night" (S043, 2012-03) On burnout, the nearest is resilience: "you have to build-up emotional resilience." (S027, 2011-01)

## Failure and optimism
- **Stance:** Optimism is a working tool, and failure is a delay, not a verdict.
- 2011-01: "failure is just success deferred" (S027)
- 2025-10: "Optimism is not an idle speculation. It's a self-fulfilling prophecy." (S012)

## Selling the company
- **No stated rule.** He has sold companies and took Google's offer for Patterns Inc. two weeks after starting it, when Larry Page offered to buy rather than invest (S004, 2024-10). He draws no general lesson on when to sell.

## Culture
- **Stance:** Thin evidence. Hire people who anticipate change, question every assumption, and give internal disruptors power, checked against a realistic model of how long each business model will last (S025, 2003-05). Inferred from S025, S027.

## What to work on (career advice)
- **Stance:** Follow what excites you, not what pays. Unchanged for 40 years.
- 2024-03: "I'd be less concerned about what will make money and much more concerned about what turns them on." (S008)
- 2025-06: "follow the same advice that I'd give like 40 years ago, which is follow your passion" (S006)

## Jobs, automation, and UBI
- **Stance:** Automation removes jobs and creates better ones; a baseline income arrives in the 2030s.
- 2024-10: "80% of people uh worked in food production uh 200 years ago today there 2% so we've lost all those jobs" (S004)
- 2026-06: "I think we'll have UBI." (S001)
