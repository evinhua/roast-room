# Principles: Ray Kurzweil

28 decision rules, built from 43 sources (talks, interviews, and essays, 1999 to 2026), roughly most-used first and weighted toward business decisions about exponential trends and timing. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Build for the world that will exist when you finish, not the one that exists when you start.** "it's also not a good idea to just target today's world, because windows can be closed by the time you finish a project. So you really have to catch the wave at just the right time." (S025) "My goal was...to time my own technology projects so that they will be appropriate for the world that exists when I complete the projects" (S015) Sources: 7 (S015, S025, S032, S033, S035, S038, S039). Confidence: high.

2. **Most projects fail on timing, not on whether the thing works.** "Most inventions and inventors fail, not because they are unable to get their gadgets to work, but because their timing is wrong" (S015) "I realized 30 years ago that the key to being successful is timing. Those inventors whose names you know are the ones who got the timing right." (S035) Sources: 7 (S015, S025, S034, S035, S037, S039, S041). Confidence: high.

3. **Think in doublings; linear intuition underestimates the long run.** "It has to do with thinking exponentially. Uh and people are not used to that. They're thinking uh linearly think if it took 10 years in the past, it'll take 10 years in the future." (S003) Sources: 12 (S001, S003, S006, S007, S010, S014, S016, S034, S035, S037, S039, S041). Confidence: high.

4. **A small number on a steady doubling curve is close to done, not close to nothing.** "We're almost done. Uh 1% is only seven doublings from 100%." (S037) Sources: 8 (S002, S009, S034, S035, S037, S038, S039, S015). Confidence: high.

5. **Do not change a technology forecast for a recession, a war, or a bad news cycle.** "This continues through uh boom times and recessions the Great Depression had no impact" (S039) "The exponential growth of information technologies proceeded smoothly through war and peace, through depression and prosperity." (S016) Sources: 8 (S015, S016, S025, S033, S034, S035, S039, S041). Confidence: high.

6. **The end of a paradigm is not the end of growth; look for the next paradigm already in the lab.** "what happens is it creates research pressure to create the next Paradigm." (S039) Sources: 8 (S010, S014, S015, S024, S038, S039, S041, S042). Confidence: high.

7. **Every enabling factor must be in place, not just your technology.** "90% of the projects the R&D teams are do exactly what they say in terms of technological achievement and 95% of the projects still fail because only enabling factors that are needed uh would not be in place" (S038) Sources: 4 (S015, S038, S040, S041). Confidence: high.

8. **Forecast the underlying measure, not which company wins.** "[Nvidia is] very well positioned, but we didn't hear about them ten years ago, and other people may overtake them." (S032) "Predicting which company or product will succeed is indeed very difficult, if not impossible." (S016) Sources: 6 (S015, S016, S032, S035, S040, S041). Confidence: high.

9. **Use data and models, not intuition, to place a bet on the future.** "being an engineer I gathered a lot of data I now have a team of 10 people and we gather data in many different fields and try to uh build mathematical models." (S041) Sources: 4 (S025, S038, S041, S015). Confidence: high.

10. **Separate the capital markets cycle from the adoption curve.** "that was strictly a capital markets phenomenon the capital markets Wall Street looked at the internet" (S038) Booms and busts in funding say little about whether usage is growing (inferred from S025, S034, S038, S041). Sources: 4 (S025, S034, S038, S041). Confidence: high.

11. **Assume the input gets cheap and build for the market that the lower price opens.** "these technologies by the time they work well are extremely inexpensive." (S040) Sources: 6 (S016, S034, S035, S039, S040, S041). Confidence: high.

12. **Assume your business model has a shelf life, and build its replacement.** "an organization cannot be devoted to one business model" (S025) In his own speech recognition company, versions two generations old "we couldn't even give those away." (S014) Sources: 4 (S002, S014, S025, S040). Confidence: high.

13. **A new product has to be much better than the incumbent, not just equal, before people adopt it.** "It has to be a lot better than human beings. If it's really just equal, maybe a little bit better, we're not going to accept it." (S006) He calls underestimating this his biggest forecasting error: "I didn't take into consideration uh the amount of additional uh positivity that would be required to accept them." (S006) Sources: 4 (S002, S003, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

14. **Be AI-first and apply AI where it has not been applied yet.** "AI first, robot first." (S002) "the opportunities to create a new business and do things uh that have not been done before is actually uh is higher than it's ever been before." (S003) Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S003, S040). Confidence: high.

15. **Manage danger with targeted fixes and defenses, not bans or pauses.** "I believe we can address the signers' safety concerns in a more tailored way that doesn't compromise these vital lines of research." (S017) Sources: 7 (S014, S016, S017, S020, S037, S041, S042). Confidence: high.

16. **Data, not compute, is the binding constraint now.** "I think we have the calculations. We don't have the data." (S032) "the way to fix hallucinations is to actually give it more more capabilities to memorize things and and give it more information so it knows the answer." (S013) Sources: 3 (S010, S013, S032). Confidence: high.

17. **When you see a curve pointing at a future you believe in, build toward it yourself.** On his 1990 e-book prediction: "in order to make that true i thought i better get in gear and contribute to that technology myself." (S040) Sources: 3 (S033, S039, S040; inferred from S033, S039). Confidence: high.

18. **There is an idea that solves the problem; find it and then implement it.** "No matter what quandaries we face... business problems, health issues, relationship difficulties... there's an idea that can enable us to prevail." (S012) Sources: 3 (S012, S026, S028). Confidence: high.

19. **Follow what excites you over what looks like the bigger market.** "I'd be less concerned about what will make money and much more concerned about what turns them on." (S008) "it takes passion to create knowledge that has value" (S043) Sources: 3 (S006, S008, S043). Confidence: high.

20. **Read the curve, not the consensus, and accept looking crazy for a while.** "People thought that was crazy... but that is in fact what happened." (S037) Experts at a Stanford conference said 100 years for AGI; he held 2029 (inferred from S002, S010). Sources: 4 (S002, S010, S027, S037). Confidence: high.

21. **Do not picture one big change in an otherwise unchanged world.** "A common mistake that people make when considering the future is to envision a major change to today's world... as if nothing else were going to change." (S016) Critics assume today's fixes are all we will have, "without realizing that that's also going to expand" (S004) Sources: 2 (S004, S016). Confidence: medium.

22. **Optimism is a tool, not a forecast.** "Optimism is not an idle speculation. It's a self-fulfilling prophecy. If we assume everything is getting worse, it will." (S012) Sources: 2 (S012, S027). Confidence: medium.

23. **Expect multiples, not increments.** "Innovators seek to improve things by multiples. Innovation is multiplicative, not additive." (S024) His 10x a year figure for AI applies the same idea (inferred from S001, S006). Sources: 3 (S001, S006, S024). Confidence: high.

24. **The curve applies to information technology only.** "30 steps exponentially that's the reality not of everything but of every information technology gets you to a billion" (S034) Sources: 2 (S015, S034). Confidence: medium.

25. **Pick AI vendors that have a reputation and liability to protect.** "if you need if you use a large language model it should be from a larger company uh so they're concerned about their reputation and their liability." (S011) Sources: 2 (S007, S011). Confidence: medium.

26. **Judge a forecast by the whole record and by the decade, not by its worst misses.** "it is easy to focus on the few predictions that were incorrect...and craft a biased analysis" (S015) He grades himself strictly, correct only if within a year (S002). Sources: 2 (S002, S015). Confidence: medium.

27. **Make important decisions fast and stay able to change quickly.** "I said yes right away cuz I make important decisions very quickly." (S002) "you got to be able to take changes very, very quickly." (S002) Sources: 1 (S002). Confidence: low.

28. **Find the user who needs the product most and let them tell you what to build.** His reading machine came from a blind man on a plane, and the Kurzweil 250 from Stevie Wonder's request (S012). Sources: 1 (S012). Confidence: low.

Where these rules are known to fail him: the curve was right but the date was wrong on self-driving cars, robots, and 3D printing, because of regulation, acceptance, and physical engineering (S001, S002, S003, S006, S034). Rules 1 to 8 are strongest on pure information products and weakest on anything physical, clinical, or regulated.
