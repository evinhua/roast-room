# Principles: Ray Kurzweil

30 decision rules, built from the notes on 102 of the 103 corpus sources (talks, interviews, essays, debates, and testimony, 1999 to 2026), roughly most-used first and weighted toward business decisions about exponential trends and timing. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Build for the world that will exist when you finish, not the one that exists when you start.** "it's also not a good idea to just target today's world, because windows can be closed by the time you finish a project. So you really have to catch the wave at just the right time." (S025) "you can't shoot at the Target where it is it's like ski shooting you have to figure out where it's going to be" (S091) Sources: 17 (S015, S025, S032, S033, S035, S038, S039, S045, S046, S052, S073, S076, S078, S091, S096, S101, S102). Confidence: high.

2. **Most projects fail on timing, not on whether the thing works.** "Most inventions and inventors fail, not because they are unable to get their gadgets to work, but because their timing is wrong" (S015) "I realized 30 years ago that the key to being successful is timing. Those inventors whose names you know are the ones who got the timing right." (S035) Sources: 21 (S010, S015, S025, S034, S035, S037, S039, S041, S045, S048, S052, S056, S083, S084, S087, S089, S091, S094, S101, S102, S103). Confidence: high.

3. **Think in doublings; linear intuition underestimates the long run.** "It has to do with thinking exponentially. Uh and people are not used to that. They're thinking uh linearly think if it took 10 years in the past, it'll take 10 years in the future." (S003) He says his own gut is still linear and he is still surprised by things he predicted (S083, S085). Sources: 26 (S001, S003, S006, S007, S010, S014, S016, S034, S035, S037, S039, S041, S045, S054, S056, S064, S067, S083, S085, S091, S093, S094, S097, S100, S101, S102). Confidence: high.

4. **A small number on a steady doubling curve is close to done, not close to nothing.** "We're almost done. Uh 1% is only seven doublings from 100%." (S037) "one percent is only seven doublings from a hundred percent" (S102) He applies it to solar at a few percent of world energy and to any "fringe" player at 1% or 2% (S088, S094, S095). Sources: 18 (S002, S009, S015, S034, S035, S037, S038, S039, S051, S086, S088, S091, S094, S095, S097, S100, S101, S102). Confidence: high.

5. **Be first, but not years early; too early fails the same way too late does.** "timing is absolutely critical uh you want to be first but not you know 10 years ahead of your time" (S090) He says you always find people who tried the same thing too early, and those who came later missed the boat (S094); start a few years before feasibility so you finish first (S102). Sources: 5 (S010, S037, S090, S094, S102). Confidence: high.

6. **Write down the technologies your business depends on and project each one three or four years out.** "I continually see plans that assume the world's three or four years from now will be the same as we see today." (S095) At his e-reader company the team tracked display resolution, communication speed, and mobile computation a year or two ahead (S090). Sources: 6 (S039, S090, S095, S096, S101, S102). Confidence: high.

7. **Do not change a technology forecast for a recession, a war, or a bad news cycle.** "This continues through uh boom times and recessions the Great Depression had no impact" (S039) "recessions including the Great Depression can be modeled as a fairly weak cycle on top of the underlying exponential growth." (S050) Sources: 15 (S015, S016, S025, S033, S034, S035, S039, S041, S050, S073, S076, S081, S089, S091, S101). Confidence: high.

8. **The end of a paradigm is not the end of growth; look for the next paradigm already in the lab.** "what happens is it creates research pressure to create the next Paradigm." (S039) "specific paradigms do run out of steam at a certain point but they create research pressure to create the next paradigm the next method" (S102) Sources: 16 (S010, S014, S015, S024, S038, S039, S041, S042, S045, S052, S061, S064, S073, S091, S092, S102). Confidence: high.

9. **Every enabling factor must be in place, not just your technology.** "90% of the projects the R&D teams are do exactly what they say in terms of technological achievement and 95% of the projects still fail because only enabling factors that are needed uh would not be in place" (S038) Sources: 11 (S015, S038, S040, S041, S046, S048, S052, S083, S089, S101, S102). Confidence: high.

10. **Forecast the underlying measure, not which company wins.** "[Nvidia is] very well positioned, but we didn't hear about them ten years ago, and other people may overtake them." (S032) "Predicting which company or product will succeed is indeed very difficult, if not impossible." (S016) Sources: 11 (S015, S016, S032, S035, S040, S041, S054, S091, S095, S101, S102). Confidence: high.

11. **Use data and models, not intuition, to place a bet on the future, and read the data on a log scale.** "being an engineer I gathered a lot of data I now have a team of 10 people and we gather data in many different fields and try to uh build mathematical models." (S041) On a linear chart, exponential progress looks like nothing until it looks like a wall (S084). Sources: 9 (S015, S025, S038, S041, S050, S052, S084, S087, S102). Confidence: high.

12. **Assume the input gets cheap and build for the market that the lower price opens.** "these technologies by the time they work well are extremely inexpensive." (S040) "it's a mistake to just measure economies in dollars" (S102) Sources: 12 (S016, S034, S035, S039, S040, S041, S054, S057, S075, S083, S100, S102). Confidence: high.

13. **Separate the capital markets cycle from the adoption curve.** "that was strictly a capital markets phenomenon the capital markets Wall Street looked at the internet" (S038) Booms and busts in funding say little about whether usage is growing, and a hype phase usually comes before the real change (inferred from S025, S041, S088, S092, S095). Sources: 9 (S025, S034, S038, S041, S071, S088, S092, S095, S097). Confidence: high.

14. **Assume your business model has a shelf life, and build its replacement.** "an organization cannot be devoted to one business model" (S025) In his own speech recognition company, versions two generations old "we couldn't even give those away." (S014) Sources: 6 (S002, S014, S025, S040, S045, S086). Confidence: high.

15. **A new product has to be much better than the incumbent, not just equal, before people adopt it.** "It has to be a lot better than human beings. If it's really just equal, maybe a little bit better, we're not going to accept it." (S006) He calls underestimating this his biggest forecasting error: "I didn't take into consideration uh the amount of additional uh positivity that would be required to accept them." (S006) Sources: 8 (S002, S003, S006, S008, S055, S086, S087, S092). Confidence: high.

16. **Be AI-first, use AI openly, and apply it where it has not been applied yet.** Asked what a startup built for the coming change should focus on, the reply was AI first and robots first, and able to change in weeks, not years (inferred from S002). "I think to avoid AI is uh really a mistake because the way in which our people will be valued is the ability to use AI." (S077) Sources: 8 (S001, S002, S003, S006, S036, S040, S077, S094). Confidence: high.

17. **Manage danger with targeted fixes and defenses, not bans or pauses.** "I believe we can address the signers' safety concerns in a more tailored way that doesn't compromise these vital lines of research." (S017) "Relinquishing technological advancement would be economic suicide for individuals, companies and nations." (S020) Sources: 17 (S010, S014, S016, S017, S020, S037, S041, S042, S045, S053, S054, S060, S067, S085, S087, S095, S100). Confidence: high.

18. **Expect regulation and objections to slow a technology, not stop it.** "Objections such as those expressed against stem-cell research end up being stones in the water: the stream of progress just flows around them." (S054) He also says anything touching the human body draws far more caution than software (S008). Sources: 4 (S008, S048, S050, S054). Confidence: high.

19. **Never argue that something is impossible because it does not exist today.** "Denying the feasibility of an impending technological transformation is a short-sighted strategy." (S053) "don't listen to people who tell you that things are impossible" (S103) Critics assume today's fixes are all we will have, "without realizing that that's also going to expand" (S004) Sources: 5 (S004, S016, S053, S101, S103). Confidence: high.

20. **Data, not compute, is the binding constraint now.** "I think we have the calculations. We don't have the data." (S032) He says the fix for hallucinations is to give models more information to remember, so they know the answer (inferred from S013). Older view (2010s): the gap was software, with hardware nearly solved (S093). Sources: 5 (S010, S013, S032, S084, S093). Confidence: high.

21. **When you see a curve pointing at a future you believe in, build toward it yourself.** On his 1990 e-book prediction: "in order to make that true i thought i better get in gear and contribute to that technology myself." (S040) Sources: 3 (S033, S039, S040; inferred from S033, S039). Confidence: high.

22. **There is an idea that solves the problem; find it and then implement it.** "No matter what quandaries we face... business problems, health issues, relationship difficulties... there's an idea that can enable us to prevail." (S012) Sources: 5 (S012, S026, S027, S028, S081). Confidence: high.

23. **Treat failure as success deferred, and confidence as a working assumption.** "failure is just really success deferred if you have the right Vision" (S090) "Optimism is not an idle speculation. It's a self-fulfilling prophecy. If we assume everything is getting worse, it will." (S012) Sources: 7 (S012, S027, S084, S090, S094, S095, S103). Confidence: high.

24. **Follow what excites you over what looks like the bigger market.** "I'd be less concerned about what will make money and much more concerned about what turns them on." (S008) "it takes passion to create knowledge that has value" (S043) Sources: 7 (S006, S008, S043, S077, S078, S084, S096). Confidence: high.

25. **Read the curve, not the consensus, and accept looking crazy for a while.** "People thought that was crazy... but that is in fact what happened." (S037) Experts at a Stanford conference said 100 years for AGI; he held 2029 and says the consensus moved to him (inferred from S002, S010, S033, S087). Sources: 6 (S002, S010, S027, S033, S037, S087). Confidence: high.

26. **Do not picture one big change in an otherwise unchanged world.** "A common mistake that people make when considering the future is to envision a major change to today's world... as if nothing else were going to change." (S016) Sources: 3 (S004, S016, S079). Confidence: high.

27. **Expect multiples, not increments.** "Innovators seek to improve things by multiples. Innovation is multiplicative, not additive." (S024) His 10x a year figure for AI applies the same idea (inferred from S001, S006). Sources: 5 (S001, S006, S024, S048, S050). Confidence: high.

28. **The curve applies to information technology only.** "30 steps exponentially that's the reality not of everything but of every information technology gets you to a billion" (S034) He says the law explains price-performance, not elections or revolutions (S096). Sources: 4 (S015, S034, S096, S102). Confidence: high.

29. **Before you scale, prove it with enthusiastic early adopters, avoid debt, and watch the team.** "I would avoid Deb actually debt it's very uh is dangerous if that can lead to a premature termination of your company" (S090) He says about half the companies he saw with a good idea, a market, and the skills were "really destroyed by group dynamics" (S090). He tells founders to talk about the project openly to attract people and investors, because execution, not competition, decides the outcome (inferred from S086).. Sources: 2 (S086, S090). Confidence: medium.

30. **Judge a forecast by the whole record and by the decade, not by its worst misses.** "it is easy to focus on the few predictions that were incorrect...and craft a biased analysis" (S015) He grades himself strictly, correct only if within a year (S002). Sources: 3 (S002, S011, S015). Confidence: high.

Lower-evidence rules worth knowing: make important decisions fast and stay able to change in weeks, "I said yes right away cuz I make important decisions very quickly." (S002), one source, low; pick AI vendors with a reputation and liability to protect (S007, S011), medium; find the user who needs the product most, as with the blind man and Stevie Wonder behind his reading machine and the Kurzweil 250 (S012), low.

Where these rules are known to fail him: the curve was right but the date was wrong on self-driving cars, robots, and 3D printing, because of regulation, acceptance, and physical engineering (S001, S002, S003, S006, S034, S087, S092). His longevity dates have slipped as they approached (S034, S089, S010, S006). Rules 1 to 12 are strongest on pure information products and weakest on anything physical, clinical, or regulated.
