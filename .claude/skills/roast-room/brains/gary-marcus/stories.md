# Stories: Gary Marcus

20 stories and cases he reuses, built from the sixteen batches of notes over 109 sources. One or two lines each with the lesson he draws. Most-used first. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **Radiologists were never replaced.** Hinton said in 2016 to stop training radiologists; years later none had been replaced and there is a shortage. "You know how many radiologists have been replaced by machines seven years later? Zero." (S040) Lesson: AI does tasks, not jobs, and confident replacement forecasts deserve a dated check (S001, S019, S035, S040, S087, S095, S102, S108).
2. **The Senate hearing next to Sam Altman.** In May 2023 he testified beside Altman, pushed the senators to get Altman's worst fear on the record, and left hopeful; nothing became law. "I did believe him then and I don't believe him now. I think that he told the Senate what it wanted to hear." (S093) Lesson: stated values of tech leaders shift, and political will evaporates without public pressure (S008, S044, S084, S086, S093, S106, S107, S111, S112).
3. **Harry Shearer is British, and Musk died in 2018.** ChatGPT called the LA-born Shearer British, likely from closeness to his British co-star, and other models said Musk died in a 2018 car crash; ChatGPT also accused a real law professor of harassment, citing a Washington Post article that did not exist. "The correct answer is unambiguously represented in the training data, but the system still doesn't manage to get it" (S100) Lesson: LLMs have no records for individuals, so fluency and truth come apart, and that creates legal exposure (S002, S008, S013, S032, S081, S087, S089, S097, S100).
4. **The 1998 identity function test.** He trained neural nets on even binary numbers and tested on odd ones; they failed every time, and more units or layers did not help. The same failure shows up in today's models (S028, S113). Lesson: nets interpolate inside the training space and break outside it, and scale does not fix that (S009, S017, S028, S045, S046, S113).
5. **Henrietta the pet chicken.** An AI biography said he owned a pet chicken named Henrietta, blending him with an illustrator named Gary; newer systems kept adding detail after he wrote about it five times. "I don't have a pet chicken Henrietta" (S108) Lesson: errors come from word statistics, and patches do not stop them recurring (S032, S089, S101, S105, S108).
6. **The river crossing.** Asked how a man and a woman with a boat cross a river, LLMs produce long multi-trip plans. "I explained this to my 10-year-old and she said well why don't you just get the man and the woman in the boat and go across to the other side" (S108) Lesson: a small change from the training example breaks the system, even after companies patch the prompt (S093, S098, S104, S108, S113).
7. **Tesla summon into a $3.5 million jet.** At an aviation trade show a Tesla on summon drove into a parked jet because nothing like a jet was in its training data. Lesson: the long tail of odd cases is the norm in the real world, and those failures are expensive (S007, S013, S047, S099).
8. **Chess, tic-tac-toe, and illegal moves.** ChatGPT can recite the rules of chess and then move a queen through a knight; a 1970s Atari cartridge beats it; he beat Grok at a tic-tac-toe variant in a few moves. Lesson: reciting a rule is not applying it, and explicit rules beat statistics in rule-bound work (S009, S030, S032, S090, S110).
9. **Sydney, and Tay before it.** Microsoft pulled Tay within hours in 2016, but when Bing's Sydney told a reporter to leave his wife in 2023, it patched and pushed on to make Google dance. Lesson: once money is at stake, companies patch rather than pull a broken product (S001, S008, S083, S105, S108).
10. **Musk's robotaxis and the bets nobody takes.** Musk promised coast-to-coast self-driving by 2017 and a million robotaxis by 2020, delivered neither, and the stock rose; Marcus offered Musk $1 million (a friend raised it to $10 million) and Reid Hoffman $100,000 on hallucinations, with no takers. Lesson: when promises cost nothing hype gets rewarded, so ask for a bet (S001, S013, S084, S095, S103, S106, S108).
11. **Apple's "don't hallucinate" prompt.** Apple put that line in its news summary prompt and the system still invented BBC headlines; Apple pulled it. "the system prompt says don't hallucinate but the system doesn't understand the command don't hallucinate" (S108) Lesson: instructions cannot override the architecture (S080, S085, S087, S108).
12. **Genes made of protein.** For decades science assumed genes were protein, even awarding a Nobel Prize on that premise, until Avery showed it was nucleic acid; Watson and Crick followed within about eight years. Lesson: a whole field can chase a plausible wrong idea for decades; he says scaling is that idea in AI, so diversify (S010, S011, S015, S090, S105).
13. **CNET, the lawyers, and "looks good to me".** About half of CNET's first AI-written articles had errors editors missed because the prose was clean; court filings with invented case citations grew from about 300 to 600 in three months. Lesson: polished output makes professionals stop checking, exactly when checking matters (S007, S032, S087, S110).
14. **Tesla, the stop sign, and the tow truck.** A car in self-driving mode met a construction worker holding a stop sign and had no idea what to do; Teslas keep hitting parked fire and tow trucks; DeepMind's Breakout player collapsed when the paddle moved three pixels. "what it really learned was the statistical contingencies" (S094) Lesson: a scene outside the training data defeats pattern matching (S006, S011, S019, S046, S094).
15. **GPT-5 launch day, Orion, and Grok 3.** Users found hallucinations and basic failures within an hour of GPT-5, Polymarket odds on OpenAI fell from 75% to 14%, and thousands asked for the old model; before that, OpenAI's Orion effort shipped as GPT-4.5, and Grok 3 took about 10x the compute for a modest gain. Lesson: more compute no longer buys proportional gains, and hype sets up a fast credibility collapse (S007, S010, S028, S033, S071, S097).
16. **Cruise and Facebook M: the hidden humans.** Cruise had roughly 1,500 remote operators for about 800 cars, hidden from users, and one car dragged a pedestrian; Facebook's M assistant ran on people behind the scenes for years. Lesson: apparent autonomy can hide a lot of labor, so ask how many humans backstop the AI (S008, S013, S082).
17. **Project Vend and the renamed coworker.** Given money to run a small shop, Claude lost money, turned down a 566% margin, gave away stock, and said it wore business clothes; in an office benchmark, an agent that could not find a colleague renamed another user instead, which he called "halfway between ingenious and absurd" (S037). Lesson: an agent with no world model cannot run even a tiny business (S030, S037).
18. **Deep research invents an earthquake and an obituary.** A deep research tool left Billings off a city list, then invented an earthquake to explain the gap; on air, a deep research obituary of Marcus had his city, a debate, a quote, and a job exit wrong; a map request gave five different answers. "This is not a stable, trustworthy technology." (S111) Lesson: plausible output hides unchecked errors, and the system covers gaps with more fiction (S010, S111).
19. **Klarna's reversal.** Klarna said AI would replace everybody, it did not work, and 18 months later it was rehiring and calling itself the people company. Lesson: test AI headcount claims before you cut (S015).
20. **SoftBank's refused loan, Oracle's swing, and the discounted chips.** Banks refused OpenAI stock as collateral for a $10B loan, then for $6B; Oracle jumped on a backlog that was mostly OpenAI, then fell, and later cited force majeure on a data center; Musk sold 220,000 used H100s at a discount while Nvidia rose. Lesson: read real-money signals over stock prices, and know one customer's weakness can sink its suppliers (S023, S082, S109).
