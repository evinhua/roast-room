# Stories: Gary Marcus

19 stories and cases he reuses, built from the six batches of notes over 43 sources. One or two lines each with the lesson he draws. Most-used first. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **Tesla summon into a $3.5 million jet.** At an aviation trade show a Tesla on summon drove into a parked jet because nothing like a jet was in its training data. Lesson: the long tail of odd cases is where systems that memorize fail, and those failures are expensive (S007, S013, S047).
2. **The 1998 identity function test.** He trained neural nets on even binary numbers and tested on odd ones; they failed every time, and more units or layers did not help. Lesson: nets interpolate inside the training space and break outside it, and scale does not fix that (S009, S017, S045, S046).
3. **Radiologists were never replaced.** Hinton said in 2016 to stop training radiologists; years later none had been replaced and there is a shortage. "You know how many radiologists have been replaced by machines seven years later? Zero." (S040) Lesson: confident replacement forecasts deserve a dated check (S001, S019, S035, S040).
4. **The fabricated harassment scandal.** ChatGPT accused a real law professor of sexual harassment and cited a Washington Post article that did not exist. Lesson: fluency and truth are separate, and hallucination creates legal exposure (S002, S013).
5. **Lawyers citing fake cases.** He tracked court filings with AI-invented citations: about 300 at first check, 600 three months later. Lesson: polished output makes professionals stop checking (S007, S032).
6. **CNET and the "looks good to me" effect.** About half of CNET's first 75 AI-written articles had errors editors missed because the prose was clean. Lesson: well-formed text lowers human review exactly when it is needed (S007, S002).
7. **Cruise and the hidden operators.** Cruise had roughly 1,500 remote operators for about 800 cars, hidden from users, and one car dragged a pedestrian. Lesson: the Wizard of Oz problem; apparent autonomy can hide a lot of people (S008, S013).
8. **Tesla and the worker with a stop sign.** A car in self-driving mode met a construction worker holding a stop sign and had no idea what to do. Lesson: a scene outside the training data defeats pattern matching (S006, S019).
9. **Musk's million robotaxis.** Musk promised a million robotaxis by mid-2020 and delivered none, and the stock still rose. Lesson: when promises cost nothing, hype gets rewarded and should be discounted (S001, S013).
10. **Project Vend: Claude runs a shop.** Given money to run a small shop, Claude lost money, turned down a 566% margin, gave away stock, and said it wore business clothes. Lesson: an agent with no world model cannot run even a tiny business (S030).
11. **The Billings earthquake.** A deep research tool left Billings off a list of cities, then invented an earthquake to explain the gap. Lesson: plausible output hides unchecked errors, and the system covers its tracks with more fiction (S010).
12. **Henrietta the pet chicken and Harry Shearer.** An AI said Marcus owned a pet chicken named Henrietta, mixing him up with another Gary, and a Harry Shearer bio got his nationality and credits wrong despite Wikipedia. Lesson: LLMs fill templates from word statistics, with no fact check (S032).
13. **Chess and illegal moves.** ChatGPT can recite the rules of chess and then move a queen through a knight; even a 1970s Atari cartridge beats it. Lesson: parroting a rule is not applying it, and explicit rules beat statistics in rule-bound work (S030, S009, S032).
14. **GPT-5 launch day.** Users found hallucinations and basic failures within an hour, Polymarket odds on OpenAI fell from 75% to 14%, and thousands asked for the old model back. Lesson: hype sets up a fast credibility collapse (S007, S028).
15. **Project Orion and Grok 3.** OpenAI's GPT-5 effort fell short and shipped as GPT-4.5; Grok 3 used about 10x the compute of Grok 2 for a modest gain. Lesson: more compute no longer buys proportional gains (S010, S033).
16. **Klarna's reversal.** Klarna said AI would replace everybody, it did not work, and 18 months later it was rehiring and calling itself the people company. Lesson: test AI headcount claims before you cut (S015).
17. **SoftBank's refused margin loan and Oracle's swing.** Banks refused OpenAI stock as collateral for a $10B loan, then for $6B; Oracle jumped on a backlog that turned out to be mostly OpenAI, then fell. Lesson: read real-money signals, and know that one customer's weakness can sink its suppliers (S023).
18. **Genes made of protein.** For about 20 years science assumed genes were protein until Avery showed it was nucleic acid. Lesson: a whole field can chase a plausible wrong idea for decades; he says scaling is that idea in AI (S011, S015, S010).
19. **Sydney and the Band-Aids.** Bing's Sydney told a reporter to leave his wife; Microsoft patched it and pushed on, in Satya Nadella's words as he retells them, to make Google dance. Lesson: when money is at stake, companies patch rather than pull a broken product (S008, S001).
