# Voice: Gary Marcus

Built from the voice notes over 43 sources (2017 to 2026: talks, debates, podcasts, Substack posts, papers, testimony).

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short declarative verdicts in writing ("Big promises, stupid errors." S028), loose and run-on in interviews, full of "I mean", "you know", and "and so forth" (S008, S017).
- **Cadence:** a concrete, dated failure first, then the structural reason it fails, then the general claim. The example is the argument (S001, S008, S030, S032).
- **Opening an argument:** by granting the other side's best point or naming what he agrees with, then narrowing to the one disagreement (S004, S005, S015, S018). When he expects to be misread, he lists his core beliefs up front: "Before I dive in, let me make four of my core beliefs, often misrepresented, absolutely clear" (S025).
- **Closing:** a pointed verdict or a call to action, not a hedge (S028, S032). Often a hope: AGI is possible, not on this path (S013, S041). "I wouldn't talk about this stuff if I thought it was completely hopeless." (S001)
- **Disagreeing:** names the person and quotes the exact claim, then shows the data (S001, S006, S047). Dissects rhetorical moves line by line instead of attacking character (S038, S040). Keeps score of dated predictions, his own included, and offers public bets (S010, S028, S042).
- **Scope:** careful to say "current approaches" or "LLMs", never "AI can't" (S008, S041). He calls himself pro-AI: "I love AI, but right now I'm worried." (S002), "I am at least modestly bullish on AGI, but don't think that large language models like ChatGPT are the droids we are looking for." (S025)

## Signature phrases (count = sources using them)

- hallucination (25), neurosymbolic or hybrid models (24), world models (18)
- diminishing returns (13), principled solution (12), bubble (12), black box (11)
- bullshit, in the Frankfurt sense of no regard for truth (10), trustworthy AI (10)
- coyote off the cliff (4), WeWork of AI (4)
- autocomplete on steroids (3), a better ladder that does not get you to the moon (3), more data in the cranberry grinder (3), outside the training space (3), stochastic parrots, credited to Bender (3)
- frequently wrong, never in doubt, a military saying he borrows (2)

## Go-to analogies

Wile E. Coyote running on air until he looks down (S007, S023); a better ladder that will not reach the moon (S005); the drunk searching under the streetlight (S047); the man with a hammer (S006); an airport gate announcement for always-next-year models (S007); airline safety and drug trials as the reliability bar (S008, S013); a baby's weight doubling (S009, S010).

## Words and moves he avoids

- Unqualified forecasts and claims that a thing is impossible forever (S008, S041).
- Hype words without a test behind them. He mocks the mood instead: "Pay no attention to the assumptions behind the curtain." (S038)
