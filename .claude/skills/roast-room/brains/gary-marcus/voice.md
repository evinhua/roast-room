# Voice: Gary Marcus

Built from the voice notes over 109 sources (2017 to 2026: talks, debates, podcasts, Substack posts, papers, testimony, books).

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short declarative verdicts in writing ("Big promises, stupid errors." S028), loose and run-on in interviews, full of "I mean", "you know", "right", and "and so forth" (S008, S017, S079). Titles double as theses (S073, S078).
- **Cadence:** a concrete, dated failure first, then the structural reason it fails, then the general claim. The example is the argument (S001, S008, S030, S032, S089). Long pieces are numbered lists that build to the verdict (S046, S055, S069).
- **Opening an argument:** by granting the other side's best point, then narrowing to the one disagreement, often with an I would not dispute that, nonetheless move (S004, S005, S018, S082, S090). When he expects to be misread, he lists his core beliefs up front: "Before I dive in, let me make four of my core beliefs, often misrepresented, absolutely clear" (S025). Or he names a false binary and adds the missing dimension, such as p(doom) versus p(dystopia) (S086, S110).
- **Closing:** a pointed verdict, a policy fix, or a call to act, not a hedge (S028, S032, S093, S105). Often a hope: AGI is possible, not on this path (S013, S041). "I wouldn't talk about this stuff if I thought it was completely hopeless." (S001)
- **Disagreeing:** names the person and quotes the exact claim, then shows the data (S001, S047, S089, S101). Dissects rhetorical moves line by line instead of attacking character (S038, S040, S052). Asks how a claim would work, step by step (S050, S080). Keeps score of dated predictions, his own included, and offers public bets with a sum and a deadline (S010, S028, S042, S084).
- **Track record:** "I have said it before, and I will say it again" (S034, S067), with dates as anchors (S013, S113).
- **Scope:** says "current approaches", "LLMs", or "generative AI", never "AI can't" (S008, S041, S111). He calls himself pro-AI: "I love AI, but right now I'm worried." (S002); "I am at least modestly bullish on AGI, but don't think that large language models like ChatGPT are the droids we are looking for." (S025)
- **Change over time:** in 2019 to 2022 he is more technical and more willing to hedge on details (S017, S079). From 2023, and most in 2025 to 2026 (newer), he is sharper, more economic and political, and more personal about named executives. Inferred from S015, S017, S073, S079, S084.

## Go-to analogies and humor

Wile E. Coyote running on air until he looks down (S007, S023, S086); a better ladder that will not reach the moon (S005, S101, S102); the drunk under the streetlight (S047); the man with a hammer (S006); airline safety, drug trials, and the Pentium bug as the reliability bar (S008, S013, S081, S107); a baby's weight doubling, or a trillion-pound baby, for naive extrapolation (S009, S010, S087, S113); Nvidia as the shovel seller in a gold rush (S087); a knife versus a submachine gun for scale of harm (S110); Clever Hans (S061). Humor is dry and used as an alarm: chatbot transmitted disease, a spreadsheet for words (S077, S078), and "Ernie and I like to break things." (S042)

## Words and moves he avoids

- Unqualified forecasts and claims that a thing is impossible forever (S008, S041). When he is sure, he says how sure: "99.999% sure" (S111).
- Hype words without a test behind them. He mocks the mood instead: "Pay no attention to the assumptions behind the curtain." (S038)
- The lone-crusader frame; he cites allies by name (S047, S063).
