# Principles: Gary Marcus

Built from six batches of extraction notes over 43 sources (2017 to 2026). 26 decision rules, roughly most-used first. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Where his view moved over time, the newer view is marked.

1. **If there is no principled fix, more data and more scale will not fix it.** "you need an actual solution to that problem. More data is just hand-waving." (S008) "these neural network models that we have right now they're not reliable and they're not truthful ... just because you make them bigger doesn't mean you solve that problem." (S047) Sources: 7 (S001, S006, S008, S013, S017, S026, S047). Confidence: high.

2. **Treat hallucination as part of the product, not a bug that will be patched.** "hallucinations are not a bug in that sense right hallucinations are inherent to the way that the system works" (S013) "The lack of reliability will continue to haunt generative AI." (S035) He says some newer reasoning models hallucinate more, not less (S010, S032). Sources: 8 (S010, S011, S013, S025, S028, S032, S035, S036). Confidence: high.

3. **Match the reliability you need to the stakes before you hand over authority.** "if you want to delegate authority to a machine the machine needs to work right" (S013) "When the stakes are higher...we need to be much more cautious about adopting deep learning." (S019) Sources: 6 (S008, S009, S013, S019, S045, S047). Confidence: high.

4. **Fluency is not truth; assume reviewers will be fooled by polish.** "The information that they make is so fluid and so grammatical that even professional editors sometimes get sucked in and get fooled by this stuff." (S002) Sources: 6 (S002, S007, S032, S040, S042, S047). Confidence: high.

5. **Ask where it fails, not only where it works.** "understanding exactly why an error happens any kind of cognitive error um is very informative in order to understand how the system as a whole works" (S013) "In the next bit, Pelley and Hinton show an example in which ChatGPT succeeds at reasoning, but they never consider any of those in which it fails." (S040) Sources: 6 (S006, S010, S013, S018, S040, S042). Confidence: high.

6. **No denominator, no disclosure, no trust: a result without them is marketing.** "that's just advertising that's not science" (S006) "we need a full accounting for example of what data is used to train the current generation of models we don't have that" (S001) Sources: 4 (S001, S006, S042, S044). Confidence: high.

7. **A better score is not a different kind of system.** "Real progress on some dimensions, but stuck in place on others." (S028) "is a better ladder but a better ladder doesn't necessarily get you to the moon" (S005) Sources: 6 (S005, S012, S025, S026, S028, S042). Confidence: high.

8. **Fixing the viral examples is not fixing the problem; test nearby variants.** "The real question is not about the replicability of the specific strings that Ernie and I tested, but about the replicability of the general phenomena." (S042) Sources: 2 (S012, S042). Confidence: medium.

9. **Benchmarks are not your job; measure on your own messy tasks.** "Benchmark success is a limited indicator of general intelligence." (S022) "anybody who's worked in the field of psychometrics ... knows it's very difficult to make a test actually measure what you want it to do." (S009) Sources: 5 (S007, S009, S010, S018, S022). Confidence: high.

10. **Judge what has shipped, not what is promised.** "The cards Sam is holding are not as great as he lets on." (S038) "Silicon Valley just traffics in lies Silicon Valley says we're going to fix this in X time and they've been doing this for a long time" (S013) Sources: 5 (S001, S013, S031, S038, S039). Confidence: high.

11. **Discount forecasts from people who profit from belief.** "industry has a vested interest in making people believe that AGI is imminent" (S033) Sources: 5 (S007, S026, S029, S033, S038). Confidence: high.

12. **Watch what people do with real money, not what they say.** "They may say it, but they do not act as if they believe it anymore." (S011) "we can infer that the banks don't really think that OpenAI is worth its current valuation. They don't want OpenAI stock as collateral." (S023) Sources: 4 (S007, S011, S012, S023). Confidence: high.

13. **Early exponential curves do not continue forever.** "Anyone assuming that everything is simultaneously rising exponentially, and that it will continue to do so indefinitely, is a fool." (S025) "most things that look exponential initially don't continue that way." (S009) Sources: 6 (S001, S009, S010, S019, S025, S035). Confidence: high.

14. **Remember the record of broken AI promises when you forecast.** "The failure of the AI 2027 team to reckon with the immense history of broken promises and delays in the AI field is, in a team that styles itself as forecasters, inexcusable." (S031) Radiologists, robotaxis, and promises that hallucinations would be fixed within months are his standing examples (S001, S019, S040). Sources: 5 (S001, S019, S031, S035, S040). Confidence: high.

15. **Use LLMs where errors are cheap and a skilled person checks the work.** "next-token prediction is great for a kind of fancy autocomplete for coding, for example." (S032) "A lot of these tools I think are best used in the hands of sophisticated people who understand their limits." (S010) Newer view (2026): he grants coders really do gain and you should not compete without the tools (S007). Sources: 5 (S001, S007, S008, S010, S032). Confidence: high.

16. **Automate tasks, not whole jobs.** "AI is pretty good at many, but not all tasks. It's not very good at many complete jobs." (S009) Sources: 4 (S009, S015, S035, S037). Confidence: high.

17. **Give agents as little power as the job needs.** "Giving the keys to the castle to an LLM, at our current level of technological readiness, seems to me to be batshit insane." (S039) "The best defense would be not using agentic coding altogether." (S027) Sources: 4 (S027, S030, S039, S047). Confidence: high.

18. **Do not give a system rope until it shows it understands what you asked.** "we shouldn't be giving it much rope we can't really be giving it that much opportunity to do things until we can be confident that it does understand the things that we're asking." (S047) Sources: 3 (S009, S014, S047). Confidence: high.

19. **If everyone follows the same recipe, nobody has a moat.** "They don't have a moat, right? Everybody knows how to build what they're doing." (S012) Sources: 6 (S007, S012, S025, S034, S035, S038). Confidence: high.

20. **The business has to work at the quality customers need, at the price they will pay.** "The economics make no sense. And the hype around the technical stuff also makes no sense. And those two are coming together." (S015) "to make a reliable version of the product you either have to charge more than customers want to pay, or the providers have to take a loss" (S023) Sources: 4 (S007, S015, S023, S025). Confidence: high.

21. **Do not go all in on one idea; count the opportunity cost.** "we have an intellectual monoculture in which almost all of the research dollars and energy goes towards Transformer models and almost nothing else and that's insane" (S001) "In seeking to grab vast resources for an approach that is deeply flawed, Altman is diverting resources from alternative approaches from AI that might be more trustworthy, reliable, and interpretable." (S038) Sources: 5 (S001, S007, S026, S038, S041). Confidence: high.

22. **Multiply the odds of every step in a story before you believe it.** "the AI 2027 scenario almost certainly underestimates how much time we have to prepare for general intelligence, by years if not decades." (S031) Sources: 2 (S031, S039). Confidence: medium.

23. **Spotting a bubble is not timing it.** He cites the old saying "The market can remain irrational longer than you can remain solvent." (S015) "I don't I mean I can't fully predict the future" (S001) Views over time: in 2018 he warned hype could bring "another AI winter" (S046); in 2024 he said "I think we're heading for an AI winter" (S001); newer (2025 to 2026) he frames it as a financial bubble at its peak, with OpenAI as the likely first domino (S015, S023, S036). He also admits he did not expect OpenAI's 2024 raise (S035). Sources: 6 (S001, S015, S023, S035, S036, S046). Confidence: high.

24. **"Trust us" is not a safety plan; regulation can build trust.** "The big tech companies' preferred plan boils down to trust us. Why should we? The sums of money at stake are mind-boggling." (S044) "the notion that, like, you can't have innovation in an industry where there's regulation is just absurd on its face" (S008) Sources: 6 (S001, S008, S013, S014, S015, S044). Confidence: high.

25. **Write your predictions down with dates and own the misses.** "I honestly didn't think OpenAI would burn the brand name on something so mid. I was wrong." (S028) Sources: 6 (S009, S025, S028, S035, S036, S042). Confidence: high.

26. **Skepticism is not the goal; build the better thing.** "It's not about my skepticism. It's about the positive agenda that I've been pushing for a long time." (S016) "I want AI to succeed. It's just that I think we've gone down this terrible rabbit hole where the people who are running AI now are not researchers, they're marketing type people." (S008) Sources: 5 (S008, S012, S016, S041, S047). Confidence: high.

## Where these rules bend
- He is strongest on what fails and weakest on how fast adoption spreads anyway. He admits he underestimated ChatGPT's uptake: "I just didn't think it was gonna become that popular ... I should have seen it." (S009)
- His rules are built around reliability in open-world, high-stakes use. For low-stakes products where a person checks every output (drafts, brainstorms, code with tests), several rules above loosen sharply, by his own cost-of-error logic (inferred from S008, S032, S007).
- He mostly argues about frontier labs, AGI claims, and policy, not about running a small company; applying his market-level bubble calls to one startup's timing is a stretch (inferred from S015, S023, S035).
