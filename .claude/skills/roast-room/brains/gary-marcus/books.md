# Books: Gary Marcus

Built from what his talks, essays, and papers in the corpus say about his books; book text was not fetched. Six books are discussed in the sources, plus three long papers that work like books for this seat. Summaries are in my own words.

## Taming Silicon Valley (2024)
- **Core thesis:** AI companies cannot be trusted to regulate themselves, and governments have been captured by lobbying, so citizens have to push for real oversight, including boycotts if needed.
- **Key frameworks:** layered oversight modeled on aviation; FDA-style approval and a safety case before wide release; independent auditors and pre-release access for scientists; full disclosure of training data; liability for harms; national and global AI agencies; licensing for copyrighted training data (the Napster to Apple path); "to socialize the consequences and privatize the profits" (S008), a phrase he says he did not coin, as the failure to prevent.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S008, S010, S013 (with related testimony in S044 and policy arguments in S014, S015).

## Rebooting AI: Building Artificial Intelligence We Can Trust (2019, with Ernest Davis)
- **Core thesis:** Deep learning alone will not give trustworthy AI. Machines need common sense, cognitive models of the world, and real reasoning, and the field is overhyped relative to what it can do.
- **Key frameworks:** the gullibility gap (people overrate machines from small samples); common sense about objects in space and time as the central unsolved problem; failure-mode probes across physical, biological, social, and practical reasoning and individual tracking; hybrid systems that pair learning with structured knowledge.
- **Taught in sources:** S001, S012, S013, S037, S041, S042, S047 (mentioned in many more, including S009, S017, S019, S030).

## The Algebraic Mind (2001)
- **Core thesis:** Human thought uses something like mental algebra: variables, operations over variables, structured representations, and a way to track individuals separately from kinds. Neural networks of the time could not do this, so they could not generalize outside their training space.
- **Key frameworks:** interpolation versus extrapolation (training space); symbol manipulation as variables, bindings, instances, and operations; individual tracking, whose absence he says predicted hallucination; the case for combining neural networks with classical symbolic machinery. He says the four gaps it listed are still the four gaps in LLMs 25 years later.
- **Taught in sources:** S004, S009, S011, S029, S033, S045 (mentioned in S005, S007, S008, S013, S015, S019, S035).

## Kluge: The Haphazard Evolution of the Human Mind (2008)
- **Core thesis:** The human mind is a patchwork built by evolution, which explains our cognitive biases and quirks of reasoning.
- **Key frameworks:** none used directly in business arguments in the corpus. It shows his view that human cognition is a useful but flawed benchmark: AI should learn from it, not copy it blindly.
- **Taught in sources:** mentioned in S009, S018, S019.

## The Birth of the Mind (2004)
- **Core thesis:** A small genome builds a complex brain by acting like a library of reusable subroutines laid down over evolution, which is why some knowledge can be innate.
- **Key frameworks:** innate structure as an enabler of learning, not its opposite; his later idea that AI might progress faster by evolving or learning on top of principled high-level primitives rather than from a blank slate.
- **Taught in sources:** S009 (mentioned in S005, S013, S018, S019).

## Guitar Zero (2012)
- **Core thesis:** An account of learning guitar at about 40 and what the science says about how adults learn skills and how practice should be structured.
- **Key frameworks:** none applied to business decisions in the corpus.
- **Taught in sources:** mentioned in S009, S019.

## Long papers that act as books for this seat

### Deep Learning: A Critical Appraisal (2018, arXiv)
- **Core thesis:** Deep learning is a statistical technique for classifying patterns that excels on closed problems with lots of data resembling the test set, and needs to be supplemented, not abandoned.
- **Key frameworks:** ten concerns (data hungry, shallow transfer, no hierarchical structure, weak open-ended inference, not transparent, poor use of prior knowledge, correlation not causation, assumes a stable world, outputs not fully trustworthy, hard to engineer); deep learning as one tool among many; overhype risks another AI winter.
- **Taught in sources:** S046 (the paper itself), discussed in S004, S026, S029.

### The Next Decade in AI: Four Steps Towards Robust Artificial Intelligence (2020, arXiv)
- **Core thesis:** Aim for robust AI, which reliably applies what it knows across many problems, before chasing superintelligence.
- **Key frameworks:** narrow versus pointillistic versus robust AI; four steps (hybrid architecture, large-scale abstract and causal knowledge, reasoning, cognitive models); some innate structure is needed to constrain learning.
- **Taught in sources:** S045 (the paper itself), discussed in S001, S013, S016, S017, S018, S030.

### Deep Learning Is Hitting a Wall (2022, Nautilus essay)
- **Core thesis:** Scaling is not enough; reliability, compositionality, and long-tail failures remain, and the field must bring back symbol manipulation.
- **Key frameworks:** stakes-calibrated deployment (fine for rough results, not where one error costs a life); scaling laws as observations, not laws; hybrid AI. He later treats GPT-5 and the 2025 scaling slowdown as vindication (newer view, S010, S026, S029).
- **Taught in sources:** S019 (the essay itself), S006 (the talk), discussed in S007, S010, S012, S026, S029, S035, S047.
